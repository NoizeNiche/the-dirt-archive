## Pass 17: 1990s DOD/DigiTech crossover and the first boutique-generation markers

This pass extends the 1990s corridor into the late FX-series and early-2000s boutique crossover. Seventeen reconciliation records were added, with context-only digital platform records kept separate from dedicated dirt products.

### Newly documented

- **DOD FX69 Grunge**: official DigiTech history anchor for the 1990s Lamb Series.
- **DOD FX86 / FX86B Death Metal**: mid-1990s model and later-generation relationship retained.
- **DOD FX59 Thrash Master / FX70 Metal X**: late FX-series high-gain survivors added from current DigiTech manuals.
- **DOD FX51 Juice Box Overdrive / FX91 Bass Overdrive**: later FX-series overdrive branches added.
- **DigiTech Grunge / Death Metal**: DOD-to-DigiTech rebrand states separated from original DOD retail identities.
- **DigiTech Screamin Blues / Hot Head / Bad Monkey**: 2000s XS Series analog branch documented, with the Bad Monkey anchored to its 2004 development and later discontinuation history.
- **ProCo SOLO / 25th Silver Anniversary RAT**: post-Deucetone RAT branch and commemorative 2003 edition separated from ordinary RAT2 production.
- **Fulltone Full-Drive**: early boutique overdrive chronology anchored to Fulltone's 1991 founding and the 1990s Full-Drive lineage.
- **ZVEX Fuzz Factory**: boutique fuzz identity added with conservative late-1990s/2000s dating pending a sharper launch document.
- **Love Pedal FAB 50**: early boutique overdrive/fuzz identity added from the builder's own history.

### Key research findings

1. **DOD became a bridge into DigiTech rather than disappearing cleanly.** DigiTech's own history says popular DOD analog pedals such as Grunge and Death Metal were later shifted under the DigiTech brand, while new analog XS Series products followed under DigiTech branding.
2. **The Lamb Series deserves its own archival treatment.** FX69 Grunge, FX86 Death Metal, FX59 Thrash Master and FX70 Metal X show how the late DOD line combined aggressive product naming, distinct graphics, and niche genre targeting.
3. **The RAT family now has clear post-1990 branches.** Deucetone, 25th Anniversary RAT, and SOLO should remain separate objects with explicit lineage links.
4. **Boutique history needs different evidence standards.** Builder self-history can establish founding and broad chronology, but exact first-sale dates should be corroborated with dated catalogs, ads, interviews, or surviving early specimens before promotion to VERIFIED.
5. **The archive should preserve brand transitions as historical events.** DOD-to-DigiTech is not merely a logo change: it marks a change in retail presentation and corporate product organization while preserving lineage.
6. **Digital and analog are converging, not replacing each other.** DigiTech's platform history shows DSP growth alongside continued analog distortion products, making the 1990s/2000s a mixed technology ecosystem rather than a simple analog-to-digital handoff.

### Evidence discipline

- **VERIFIED** is reserved for manufacturer or contemporary documentary support.
- **RESEARCH** remains appropriate for specialist dates, self-reported boutique chronology, regional states, and unresolved first-release windows.
- Builder history, branded reissues, regional production and product-format changes are recorded as separate evidence layers.
- Context-only digital records do not inflate the dedicated dirt count.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries, or cloning instructions are added.

### Current lineage position

The research corridor now runs from 1960s fuzz pioneers through Japanese OEM networks, 1970s/1980s landmark overdrive and distortion, the 1980s DOD/BOSS/Ibanez compact expansion, the 1990s high-gain boom, and the late-1990s/2000s crossover into boutique pedals and DSP-based systems.
