# Early Foundational Handoff 79

## Pass 32: HBE, T-Pedals and Catalinbread second-pass consolidation

This pass consolidates the 2002-2010 boutique corridor while preserving a separate later bridge lane. Nine records sharpen HomeBrew Electronics and T-Pedals chronology, add an early Catalinbread distortion anchor, and document D*A.M Fuzz Sound succession into later numbered states.

### Newly documented

- **HomeBrew Electronics Lorba Fuzz**: August 14, 2007 documented fuzz state with Jordan Boss Tone family lineage preserved only as contextual evidence.
- **HomeBrew Electronics FMJ Full Metal Jacket**: January 27, 2008 documented dedicated distortion state.
- **T-Pedals Nuke Booster**: November 29, 2006 documented Italian point-to-point boost/preamp context record, intentionally excluded from the main dirt count.
- **T-Pedals T-Fuzz**: late-2002 prototype lineage anchor preceding the 2006 full-time retail era.
- **Catalinbread Super Charged Overdrive**: September 21, 2005 documented early commercial overdrive.
- **D*A.M Fuzz Sound MkIII**: July 29, 2009 documented production state and D*A.M interpretation of the MkIII/MkIV family.
- **D*A.M Fuzz Sound MkIV**: 2011 bridge state tied to FS-75 numbering and changed presentation.
- **D*A.M FS-75 Fuzz Sound Jr.**: 2012-2013 compact limited bridge state.
- **D*A.M FZ-673**: March 2011 ten-unit limited bridge with explicit unit allocation and auction documentation.

### Key research findings

1. **HBE now has a clean mid-2000s fuzz-to-distortion chain.** Lorba anchors 2007 fuzz production and FMJ anchors 2008 distortion production.
2. **Prototype chronology and retail chronology remain separate.** T-Pedals' late-2002 T-Fuzz history predates the company's 2006 full-time retail phase and is not promoted to a false launch date.
3. **Catalinbread had meaningful dirt before its later expansion wave.** Super Charged Overdrive provides a 2005 commercial overdrive anchor alongside the founding Super Chili Picoso line.
4. **D*A.M Fuzz Sound naming is an evolving historical layer.** The 2009 MkIII record is explicitly D*A.M's interpretation rather than a direct Sola Sound replica; the later MkIV/FS-75 state changes labeling and presentation.
5. **Later bridge records can be unusually strong evidence.** FZ-673 has explicit ten-unit documentation, while FS-75 Jr has period comparison footage and a compact-enclosure identity. These remain outside the 1998-2010 core.
6. **The boost boundary remains intentional.** Nuke Booster illuminates T-Pedals' production identity and chronology without inflating the dedicated OD/fuzz/distortion count.

### Evidence discipline

- Builder timelines, specialist database dates, period videos, and later product records remain distinct evidence classes.
- Family resemblance does not become a claim of identical circuitry or identical manufacture.
- Exact first-sale dates are only asserted when directly supported.
- Limited-run counts remain tied to explicit documentary statements.
- Later bridge states stay visibly separate from the core production corridor.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries, or cloning instructions are added.

### Current lineage position

The archive now has enough depth to compare **product chronology, physical form, branding, manufacturing context, and edition scarcity** as parallel layers. That is the foundation for reliable historical specimen identification without reducing the archive to circuit analysis.
