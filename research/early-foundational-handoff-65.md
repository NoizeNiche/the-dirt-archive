# Early Foundational Handoff 74

## Pass 26: generation boundaries and founding-object evidence

This pass deepens the 2004-2010 boutique corridor around D*A.M, MI Audio, Catalinbread, Black Arts Toneworks, and Keeley. Fifteen reconciliation records were added, with special attention to first batches, precursor objects, documented version boundaries, limited germanium states, graphic/specimen clues, and later-generation bridges.

### Newly documented

- **D*A.M Red Rooster**: 2004-2008 early production window, a dedicated germanium booster with a strong Rangemaster-family identity.
- **D*A.M Sonic Titan ST-07**: early 2007-2008 production state.
- **D*A.M Sonic Titan 2009-on**: separate later-family state retained without inventing an exact transition date.
- **MI Audio Blues Prototype**: 2005 pre-commercial liquidation/prototype object derived from unused Blue Boy-development PCBs and sold in bare aluminium enclosures.
- **MI Audio Blues Pro**: 2006-2008 commercial successor in a professional die-cast enclosure.
- **MI Audio Tube Zone**: V1/V2 documented family, with 1995 design origin kept separate from commercial-pedal chronology.
- **MI Audio Crunch Box V1**: early 2006 first-batch state, approximately 500 units and sold out within three weeks.
- **MI Audio Crunch Box V2**: brighter revision with internal presence control.
- **Catalinbread Formula N°5**: February 2010 documented production state with multiple later graphic/reissue labels.
- **Catalinbread WIIO**: 2009-2010 first-batch/early production state.
- **Black Arts Toneworks Pharaoh**: founding/early production object, with a 2009 company anchor but no invented exact first-sale date.
- **Black Arts Toneworks Pharaoh early graphic state**: specimen-facing standard production evidence kept separate from later Supreme development.
- **Keeley Java Boost**: 2005 limited early germanium state using scarce Mullard OC44/CV7003 stock.
- **Keeley Fuzz Head**: 2005 documented early production state, with contemporary June 2005 recognition preserved as context rather than a fake launch date.
- **Black Arts Toneworks Black Forest**: 2011 bridge record, deliberately retained outside the strict 1998-2010 core because it clarifies the next BAT generation.

### Key research findings

1. **The Blues Prototype is an important object in its own right.** MI Audio’s account explicitly separates the bare-aluminium prototype/liquidation run from the later Blues Pro commercial product.
2. **Crunch Box V1 has unusually strong rarity evidence.** The first commercial batch is documented as 500 units, sold within three weeks. That is valuable specimen chronology without requiring teardown evidence.
3. **Version labels can encode meaningful physical identification clues.** Michael Ibrahim distinguishes the darker V1 from the brighter V2 with an internal presence control, and V3 later becomes a component/layout refinement.
4. **Commercial date and design-origin date must stay separate.** Tube Zone was first built in 1995 but later became MI Audio’s first commercial pedal. The archive preserves both facts without collapsing them.
5. **D*A.M Sonic Titan demonstrates a real production-family split.** The database links an early 2004-2008 state to a separate 2009-on state, so the catalog should not pretend all Titans are one undifferentiated run.
6. **Keeley’s early Java Boost shows why editions matter.** The scarce OC44/CV7003 germanium state is a distinct historical object rather than merely a colorway.
7. **Black Arts Toneworks has a documented founding-object story.** Mark Wentz’s modified Black Russian Big Muff project became Pharaoh and then the basis for the company’s identity. The 2009 founding anchor is useful, but exact retail launch remains unresolved.
8. **Later bridge records can improve earlier identification.** Black Forest’s 2011 footprint helps establish that Pharaoh/LSTR-era BAT chronology led directly into a broader second generation, without back-projecting later products into the 2009-2010 core.

### Evidence discipline

- Builder statements, specialist databases, interviews, and contemporary forum evidence remain distinct classes.
- Publication dates are not automatically promoted to first-sale dates.
- Prototype, first-batch, version, graphic, limited-run, and later-generation labels remain separate states.
- Specimen clues are expressed through enclosure, labeling, control layout, batch size, naming, and documentary chronology rather than circuit reconstruction.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries, or cloning instructions are added.

### Current lineage position

The early boutique corridor is now increasingly capable of answering a harder question than “what pedal is this?”: **which historical state of this pedal is this?** MI Audio provides unusually clean prototype-to-commercial and V1/V2 boundaries; D*A.M provides split production families; Keeley provides limited component-stock editions; Catalinbread supplies graphic and first-batch chronology; and Black Arts Toneworks supplies a founding-object narrative.
