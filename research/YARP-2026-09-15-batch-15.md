# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 15

## Scope

This batch continues historical builder/catalog completion across obscure 1970s American, British, German, and Italian manufacturers already present in the census. The emphasis is on model existence, builder identity, product families, and evidence-qualified relationships rather than circuit reconstruction or specimen teardown.

## 1. Blackfield Orchester-Elektronik

Blackfield now has two separate dirt-related catalog states worth tracking. Effects Database identifies the Blackfield Fuzz as an OEM version of the Schaller Fuzz produced in West Germany in the late 1960s and early 1970s. Tonehome separately documents the much rarer Blackfield Flying Sound, a 1971 German floor unit combining fuzz, wah, and phaser.

The archive should therefore keep Blackfield as a marketed/manufacturer entity with at least two family records rather than treating the whole company as simply a Schaller clone label. The Blackfield Fuzz can carry a Schaller relationship, while Flying Sound remains a distinct Blackfield product requiring its own catalog and ownership research.

Sources:
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/blackfield/fuzz
- https://www.tonehome.de/blackfield/

## 2. Lemon StudioSound

Lemon StudioSound is upgraded from a generic German candidate to a documented historical manufacturer in Karlsruhe. Tonehome identifies Gerard Daleiden as founder in the 1970s and shows a period product range including Sound Stretcher A, Super-Phase, and the Zuzz Thainer. The Zuzz Thainer is specifically dated to 1978 and is described as a fuzz, sustainer, and noise-gate combination.

The archive should record Zuzz Thainer as central dirt/hybrid catalog inventory while retaining Lemon's modulation products as scope-boundary records. A surviving Fachblatt 02/77 product-range document and Fachblatt 01/79 Zuzz Thainer review provide useful period catalog anchors for future catalog completion.

Sources:
- https://www.tonehome.de/lemon-studiosound/
- https://www.tonehome.de/lemon-studiosound/zuzz-thainer/

## 3. Top Gear, United Kingdom

Top Gear's Fuzz Sustain is now upgraded from an unresolved builder signal to a model-level catalog record. Guitar FX Layouts dates the UK-made pedal broadly to 1975-1977 and describes it as a combined Fuzz and Treble Boost design. The source explicitly marks the model as verified within its own historical/layout research.

The archive should keep Top Gear separate from Colorsound and Eurotec even where period similarities are discussed. The Fuzz Sustain is a documented Top Gear product first; technical resemblance is not proof of shared manufacture.

Source: https://tagboardeffects.blogspot.com/2016/08/top-gear-fuzz-sustain.html

## 4. fOXX / Ridinger Associates

fOXX's Clean Machine is upgraded to a strong catalog record. Tonehome identifies Ridinger Associates Inc. in Chatsworth, California as the maker and dates a surviving example to 1973, with a 1974 retail price of $69.95. The product is explicitly described as an overdrive/fuzz unit despite the "Clean Machine" name.

Effects Database additionally records Footsystem FS300 as a rebadged fOXX Clean Machine and identifies Turtle Clean Machine as another marketed state. These remain retail/relationship nodes under the fOXX family rather than duplicate manufacturers.

Sources:
- https://www.tonehome.de/foxx/clean-machine/
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/foxx/cleanmachine
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/footsystem/fs300

## 5. Moreschi

Moreschi is upgraded with a model-specific 1970s Italian Wah-Fuzz record. Amplified Parts documents the Italian accordion maker's connection with Gary Hurst, identifying the Moreschi Wah-Fuzz as a Hurst-designed product based on the Electronic Sounds UFO Wah Fuzz Swell family. The product therefore sits at an important boundary between designer lineage, Italian manufacture/marketing, and the broader Gary Hurst fuzz-wah ecosystem.

The archive should not collapse Moreschi into CBS/Arbiter or Electronic Sounds. Instead, it should represent Gary Hurst as designer/lineage context and Moreschi as the distinct marketed Italian product entity. Further evidence is required before assigning physical assembly to a particular Italian factory.

Source: https://www.amplifiedparts.com/tech-articles/moreschi-wah-fuzz-teardown

## 6. Earth Sound Research

Earth Sound Research remains a particularly important independent American anomaly. The 1976 Graphic Fuzz is supported by a dated surviving specimen in Tone Machines Blog and by a dedicated Effects Database record. The manufacturer is identified as a New York amplifier/effects company active in the 1970s and later associated with ISC Audio and Aucorp Sound.

The archive should continue treating Graphic Fuzz as a genuine ESR product rather than a generic op-amp distortion descendant. The model's unusual design history is a later deep-archaeology topic; the present census goal is to secure the builder and catalog node first.

Sources:
- https://www.tonemachinesblog.com/2011/10/earth-sound-research-graphic-fuzz.html
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/earthsound/graphicfuzz

## 7. Bell Electrolabs

Bell Electrolabs receives a stronger identity and designer record. A Black Guitars interview with former Bell designer Tony Koorlander states that Bell was started by Mike Bell, describes the company's Stokenchurch workshop, and identifies Stan Wilson as the designer of the Bell Fuzz. Koorlander describes the Fuzz as an improvement on the Big Muff Pi.

The archive should preserve Big Muff as design ancestry, not manufacturer identity. Bell's Fuzz is a distinct British product and Stan Wilson should be represented in a designer role. The broader Bell catalog remains incomplete and is still a priority for future enumeration.

Source: https://www.blackguitars.com/bell-electrolabs.html

## 8. Kay / Uecks caution retained

The 2026 Tone Machines research on Kay Fuzz Tone strengthens the existence of a Japanese-market Uecks relationship. Uecks is tied to Ueki Gakki and a June 1976 buyer's guide confirms that Uecks pedals were sold in Japan. The same research emphasizes that the actual manufacturer of the Kay Fuzz Tone remains unknown despite the Japanese component evidence.

This remains a canonical unresolved OEM case. The archive should not promote Ueki Gakki from marketed/distribution evidence to physical manufacturer without additional documentary proof.

Source: https://www.tonemachinesblog.com/2026/07/kay-fuzz-tone-what-we-know.html

## Cross-census observations

1. Schaller, Blackfield, and Höfner should remain separate entities with relationship edges rather than a single German builder node.
2. fOXX, Footsystem, and Turtle are a clean marketed-brand/rebadge cluster under Ridinger Associates evidence.
3. Moreschi illustrates why designer lineage and physical manufacture must be separate fields.
4. Lemon StudioSound and Top Gear are useful examples of rare independent European builders whose famous models are not represented by large surviving catalogs.
5. Earth Sound Research and Bell Electrolabs are high-value independent products whose design ancestry should not erase manufacturer identity.

Catalog-completion batch only. No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries, cloning instructions, or circuit-reconstruction material included.
