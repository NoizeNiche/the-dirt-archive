# Catalog Completeness Phase 66

## Scope

This pass adds four builders/catalog families that were not yet represented in the discovery layer:

- **Musitronics** — 15 catalog records, including Mu-Tron filter, modulation, delay, octave, control and power products, plus the Beigel Sound Lab Envelope Controlled Filter and Gizmotron products surfaced by the Effects Database lineage page.
- **Montgomery Appliances** — 15 records from David Gill's one-person Montgomery County, Maryland operation, including the early flanger work and a notably fuzz-heavy later catalog.
- **DenTone Electronics** — 19 records from Dennis Menard's one-person Vermont operation, preserving its broad family of fuzz, boost and drive experiments.
- **GoosoniqueWorx** — 10 records from Ravi Goose's Singapore one-person operation, preserving discontinued local-market products as well as the later Seventheaven/7thvn and Kult lineage.

## Historical notes to preserve

### Musitronics
Effects Database identifies Musitronics Corporation of Rosemont, New Jersey as formed in 1972 by Mike Beigel and Aaron Newman. Beigel had been working on a Guild synthesizer project before Guild dropped the project after Al Dronge's death. The resulting Musitronics work became central to the Mu-Tron lineage. The archive should preserve the relationship between Guild's abandoned synthesizer work and Musitronics rather than treating Mu-Tron as an isolated product family.

### Montgomery Appliances
David Gill reports beginning personal gear construction in summer 2007, followed by a small run of FSH MkII pedals for local musicians. His next substantial project was an A/DA Flanger replica with approximately 50 units made, after which the operation shifted toward fuzz. This is useful evidence of a one-person builder moving from replicas and one-offs into a recognizable catalog.

### DenTone Electronics
Dennis Menard describes DenTone as a one-person, kitchen-table operation and explicitly discusses experimentation with resistor and capacitor substitutions as part of his design process. The archive should document this as historical builder context, but should not reproduce circuit recipes or cloning instructions.

### GoosoniqueWorx
Ravi Goose's timeline begins with mods and repairs in 2002. MangoJuice appeared in 2004; Pixie, Rapture and Citrus followed in 2005 and were local-market/discontinued products; Fudge 73 appeared in 2007; Tremor followed later in 2007; Seventheaven appeared in 2008 with a 50-unit silver run and a 100-unit black run; in 2012 the improved Seventheaven was renamed 7thvn and Kult added a footswitchable dual-channel format. These production counts and name changes are high-value identification evidence.

## Product-level research priorities

1. Musitronics Mu-Tron III, Micro V, VII and Octave Divider: chronology, enclosure/graphics, production-era identifiers, and relationship to Beigel's earlier Guild work.
2. Montgomery FSH, A/DA Flanger lineage, Beast Egg, Badlands, Supa Bender and Tone Bender MkIII/Quattro: document one-person production and any confirmed period/edition differences.
3. DenTone Buzzaround, FY-2 Companion, Fuzzrite, SupaFuzz and Purple Octavepus: investigate builder attribution, source inspirations, production periods and surviving specimen photos.
4. GoosoniqueWorx MangoJuice, Pixie, Fudge 73 and Seventheaven/7thvn: preserve the discontinued/local-market timeline and the 50-unit/100-unit Seventheaven production distinction.

## Editorial rule

These records are discovery-grade until individual product claims are independently verified. The archive should preserve the existence of obscure products even when dates, internal construction details or production quantities remain uncertain. Component information should remain at the high-level historical distinction needed for identification, not circuit reconstruction.
