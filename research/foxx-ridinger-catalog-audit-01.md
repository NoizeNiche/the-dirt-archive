# fOXX / Ridinger Associates Catalog Audit 01

## Scope
Builder/product-level audit for the early-1970s fOXX / Ridinger Associates effects line. The archive treats Ridinger Associates as the manufacturer entity and preserves fOXX, licensed, private-label, and OEM names as distinct marketed identities until evidence supports a stronger relationship.

## Manufacturer / identity
ToneHome currently identifies fOXX effects as made by Ridinger Associates Inc. in California in the early 1970s. A surviving 1971 primary-period advertisement identifies the seller as FOXX / RIDINGER ASSOCIATES, INC., North Hollywood, California. This gives the archive a strong manufacturer/brand anchor. Sources: https://www.tonehome.de/foxx/ and https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-All-Music/DownBeat/70s/71/DB-1971-03-18.pdf

## Primary-period anchors
A March 1971 DownBeat advertisement explicitly announces the FOXX Tone Machine, gives FOXX / Ridinger Associates, Inc. in North Hollywood as the ordering entity, and lists five plush colors. This is stronger evidence for public-market existence than relying only on later collector databases.

A July 1975 Beat Instrumental "Music Mart" directory lists FOXX Guitar Synthesizer, Foot Phaser, Wa + Volume, Tone Machine, Clean Machine, Loud Machine and Down Machine together. This is especially valuable for defining the broader fOXX catalog corridor rather than treating Tone Machine as the whole company.

Source: https://www.worldradiohistory.com/UK/Beat-Instrumental/Beat-Instrumental-1975-07-S-OCR.pdf

## OEM / alternate-brand map
The 1970s Foxx Fuzz Wah Volume appears under several marketed names, including Foot System, Paraclete Productions, Univox, Emmons and Sears. These should be modeled as marketed/OEM relationship records, not duplicated physical manufacturers. The surviving evidence specifically describes these as versions of the Foxx Fuzz Wah Volume.

## Tone Machine chronology
The Tone Machine is consistently anchored to 1971 in period/retrospective sources. Surviving examples and specialist coverage indicate multiple finish/color states. The archive should preserve those as specimen and production-state records rather than calling each color a separate model.

## Catalog boundary
The fOXX catalog is broader than its fuzz identity. The 1975 directory gives evidence for multiple pedals, while current specialist records identify Clean Machine, Down Machine, Tone Machine and Fuzz & Wa & Volume. The archive should therefore maintain a complete product census before diving into individual Tone Machine specimen generations.

## Public-archive boundary
This audit records product identities, manufacturing/marketing relationships, chronology and evidence states only. No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries, cloning instructions or circuit-reconstruction material are included.
