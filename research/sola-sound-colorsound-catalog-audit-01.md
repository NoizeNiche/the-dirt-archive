# Sola Sound / Colorsound Catalog Audit 01

## Scope
Builder/product-level chronology for Sola Sound and its Colorsound line, concentrating on historically documented overdrive, distortion, fuzz and closely related dirt/booster products. The archive keeps physical manufacturer, marketed brand, and OEM labels distinct.

## Builder and brand relationship
Sola Sound was the London manufacturer associated with Macari's Musical Exchange. Around 1970, the Colorsound name appears in period advertising as Sola Sound's newer branded effects line. Period histories and surviving advertisements show Sola Sound continuing to manufacture products for other marketed brands while developing the Colorsound line.

This means **Sola Sound** should remain the canonical manufacturer entity, while **Colorsound**, **Vox**, **Rotosound**, **Park**, **Carlsbro**, Barnes & Mullins and other marketed names remain relationship nodes unless independent manufacturing evidence supports a different canonical entity.

Sources:
- Big Muff Page Tone Bender timeline: https://www.bigmuffpage.com/The_Tonebender_Timeline_3.html
- Premier Guitar, Stompbox Classics: Tone Bender, Part 2: https://www.premierguitar.com/gear/stompbox-classics-tone-bender-part-2

## Important chronology corrections

### Power Boost → Overdriver is a lineage, not a duplicate model
Historical sources place the Colorsound Power Boost in the early Colorsound period, with the original orange enclosure and 18V operation. Around 1971 the product was repackaged in a gray enclosure, changed to 9V operation, and marketed as the Overdriver. Later Power Boost-branded 9V products also existed. The archive therefore records **Power Boost** and **Overdriver** as separate retail identities linked by product lineage, not as unrelated pedals and not as a single timeless model.

Sources:
- Kitrae, Colorsound Power Boost and Overdriver history: https://www.kitrae.net/music/Music_mp3_Colorsound_Sound.html
- Gilmourish, Colorsound Power Boost history: https://www.gilmourish.com/?page_id=7748

### Supa Tone Bender and Jumbo Tone Bender are related but not interchangeable
Period and historical sources place the Supa Tone Bender in the early-1970s Colorsound line and the Jumbo Tone Bender shortly afterward. Later summaries sometimes describe them too casually as the same product. The archive keeps their retail identities separate while recording their close family relationship and production succession.

Sources:
- Guitar Center Tone Bender history: https://www.guitarcenter.com/riffs/gear-tips/amplifiers--effects/history-tone-bender
- Premier Guitar, Tone Bender Part 2: https://www.premierguitar.com/gear/stompbox-classics-tone-bender-part-2
- Tone Bender historical timeline: https://thetonebender.blogspot.com/2011/12/tone-bender-timeline.html

### OEM relationships must not be collapsed into one builder
Sola Sound manufactured Tone Bender and other dirt products for multiple marketed names. A Vox-branded Tone Bender, for example, is not automatically a separate physical builder. Likewise, Park, Rotosound, Carlsbro and Barnes & Mullins relationships should remain searchable at the marketed/OEM layer until model-specific evidence changes the attribution.

Premier Guitar explicitly notes Sola Sound manufacture for British companies including Barnes & Mullins and Carlsbro while reserving the Tone Bender name for Sola Sound's own products. The historical Tone Bender timeline also identifies a Rotosound OEM relationship for the Mk III corridor.

## Catalog boundary and evidence hierarchy

The present surviving evidence gives strong named-product anchors for the Sola Sound / Colorsound dirt universe, but exact year-by-year retail availability is not equally well documented for every model. The archive should therefore distinguish:

- period catalog/advertisement evidence
- manufacturer retrospective evidence
- specialist historical reconstruction
- specimen-level evidence
- unresolved dates or relationships

No single secondary timeline should override contradictory primary paperwork.
