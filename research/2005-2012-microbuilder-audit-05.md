# 2005-2012 Microbuilder Audit 05

## Purpose
This pass expands the boutique/microbuilder census around the 2005-2012 transition from pedal modification culture into documented original-product businesses. The emphasis is on dirt catalog breadth and the identity of the maker rather than reverse-engineering individual circuits.

## XAct Tone Solutions (XTS)
XAct Tone Solutions is historically useful because its dirt story begins with modification work before moving into ground-up designs. Premier Guitar reports that Brad Walton began modifying pedals around 2001 and by 2006 had decided it was easier to build pedals from the ground up. Precision Overdrive is identified as the first model. By 2009 the business had grown enough that some PCB and engineering work was subcontracted to Nashville's Kingdom Amplification, while Walton retained the design role. The archive therefore separates XTS as designer/brand from physical manufacturing support. Atomic Overdrive, Imperial Overdrive, Tejas Overdriver and the composite XTS Preamp extend the dirt catalog beyond Precision Overdrive.

## Paul Trombetta Design
Paul Trombetta's chronology is unusually well documented. He began guitar wiring/rehousing work young, was modifying Big Muffs and Rats by 2003, and founded Paul Trombetta Design in 2005. The Donita is an early high-gain distortion antecedent. Mini-Bone appears as a later dynamic fuzz platform, followed by Bone Machine and the Tornita! collaboration with David Torn. Trombetta's own store also preserves later Rotobone and Copper Penny dirt families. The archive keeps J. Rockett's Trombetta WTF as a separate collaboration/marketed identity rather than collapsing it into PTD's original catalog.

## Bob Burt Cabinets & Pedals
Bob Burt's own timeline establishes the Overdrive as a core early pedal, with Blues Angel Music distribution and expansion into Japan in 2009. By 2010 Burt had created a dedicated pedal production shop. The 2011 GR8T Multi Distortion is a clear later dirt anchor, and the AH-1 Allen Hinds signature distortion belongs in the subsequent catalog layer. Clean Boost is retained as dirt-adjacent rather than inflated into the central OD count.

## Area 51
Area 51's Fuzz is a strong small-builder record because its development story is explicit. Daniel Albrecht says development began roughly six years before the October 2008 publication and followed examination/rebuilding of an original Arbiter Fuzz Face. Production is in-house, with board stuffing/soldering shared between Daniel and Anita Albrecht, while powder coating and screen printing are performed locally. The builder also documents in-house wah inductor winding. The Fuzz should be treated as its own product lineage even though its historical inspiration is a Fuzz Face.

## Guitarsystems / Paul Lenders / N.E.R.D.
The FuzzTool is a documented July 2008 anchor. It is explicitly based on the Dallas Arbiter Fuzz Face while adding practical operating features. Effects Database indexing also exposes FuzzTool CS MkII, FuzzTool Junior, Tony's BenderTool, TrebleTool, DriveTool and other tool-family names. The catalog is therefore broad enough for a dedicated builder record, but the exact legal/entity chronology and country attribution should be refined in a future primary-source pass.

## Shigemori
The Shigemori chronology needs careful handling because Effects Database first indexed the brand in April 2010, yet product videos for Ruby Stone and Ruby Stone Premium predate that index and go back to 2008. The April 2010 weekly batch identifies G.O.T. Bass Drive, Mighty Stone, Pretone, Ruby Stone, Ruby Stone Premium, Stone Drive Custom and Stone Drive Deluxe. Stone Drive Custom has documented Version 3 control changes. Later Muse belongs in a chronology bridge rather than the 2008-2012 core count. The archive keeps the pedal-maker identity separate from unrelated companies carrying the Shigemori name.

## Celestial Effects
Celestial is valuable as an example of a pedal division developing inside an existing company. Domenic Mancini's timeline says the idea was proposed in July 2008 as a second division of Thermalogic, followed by two years of design, tooling and field testing. The first four designs were Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn and Scorpio. A 2009 trademark/application phase formalized the Celestial name and a twelve-zodiac concept. The catalog subsequently includes Aries, Aquarius, Cancer and Sagittarius among the dirt-relevant products. Thermalogic and Celestial remain related but distinct archive entities.

## Neo-Classic Effects
Neo-Classic is a useful microbuilder specimen because the surviving timeline distinguishes research, prototype, revised prototype and later public indexing. Steve Mavronis began researching pedal construction in January 2010, completed the first self-branded 741 Overdrive in May 2010, built only two copies in that first project, and made a slightly revised spare in May 2011. The archive therefore treats the 741 as a prototype/specimen-sensitive product rather than evidence of an immediately large commercial production run.

## Scope boundaries and evidence rules
The pass deliberately excludes kits, ordinary modifications, non-dirt effects and generic marketplace brands from the central dirt count unless they materially illuminate the builder's product lineage. Product-index publication dates are treated as evidence of documentation, not automatically as first-sale dates. Prototype, collaboration, signature and later reissue states remain separately searchable where the evidence supports that distinction.
