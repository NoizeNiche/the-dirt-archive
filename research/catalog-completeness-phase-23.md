# Catalog Completeness Phase 23

## Scope

This phase expands the archive's complete-lineup discovery layer across five major boutique builders/labels and adds two current-product records found outside the current Effects Database catalog snapshot.

- EarthQuaker Devices — 88 catalog entries in Effects Database.
- Death By Audio — 45 catalog entries in Effects Database, plus current Amp Crash discovery.
- Electronic Audio Experiments — 21 catalog entries in Effects Database, plus current Scattering discovery.
- Black Arts Toneworks — 16 catalog entries in Effects Database.
- Fulltone — 49 catalog entries in Effects Database.

Total new discovery records: 221.

These are discovery records, not verified public archive records. Product-level chronology, edition/generation distinctions, enclosure changes, builder relationships, production periods and image-rights status still require dedicated research.

## EarthQuaker Devices

Effects Database lists 88 products and identifies EarthQuaker Devices with Jamie Stillman in Akron, Ohio. Stillman's own timeline is unusually valuable for the archive: he began experimenting in 2005, spent 2005–2007 building pedals on perfboard, built the Big Muff-derived prototype that became the Hoof for Dan Auerbach around January 2007, sold the first production Hoof on March 18, 2007, found Tone Factor as an early dealer in November 2007, released Disaster Transport in December 2007, hired the first full-time employee in 2009 and went full-time in 2010.

The EQD catalog contains an unusually rich mixture of dirt, modulation, delay, reverb, pitch, filter, utility and experimental products. The archive should preserve the complete lineup rather than reducing EQD to its fuzz/drive reputation.

Important product-level research branches include:
- Hoof family and production/graphic changes.
- Tone Reaper / Hoof / Hoof Reaper relationship.
- Cloven Hoof / Cloven Hoof Reaper.
- Erupter, Hizumitas, Terminal, Fuzz Master General and Stealth Fuzz.
- White Light / White Light Legacy and related historical lineage.
- Gray Channel, Special Cranker, Plumes, Westwood, Palisades, Dunes and other drive families.
- Limited editions, EarthQuaker Day releases and artist collaborations.
- Time Shadows relationship with Death By Audio.
- Gary, which Effects Database describes as a fuzz-into-overdrive design developed with Lee Kiernan of Idles.

## Death By Audio

Death By Audio's official history states that Oliver Ackermann founded the company in 2002. It began as a web-based pedal customization service, moved through warehouse spaces and became associated with the Williamsburg Death By Audio effects pedal factory/venue/studio before relocating the pedal workshop to Queens after the venue closed in 2014. The company continues to handmake effects in New York City.

Effects Database lists 45 products. The catalog includes Fuzz War, Soundwave Breakdown, Supersonic Fuzz Gun, Octave Clang, Evil Fuzz, Interstellar Overdriver, Waveformer Destroyer and many experimental modulation, filter, delay and reverb products. This makes DBA particularly important to the archive's preservation mission because many products are strongly tied to experimental/noise-rock history and limited or unusual production runs.

Current-product discovery: Death By Audio's official site currently documents Amp Crash, a collaboration with Rainger FX built around a microphone/speaker concept expanded into a multi-effects engine with reverb, echo and chorus. It is added as a discovery record even though it is not present in the current Effects Database brand list.

Important relationship branches:
- Time Shadows with EarthQuaker Devices.
- Phosphene Scream and Speed Tripper with Levitation.
- Thee Ffuzz Warr Overload with John Dwyer/Thee Oh Sees.
- Destroyer Series products as a distinct modern production family.
- Early Soundwave Breakdown and later revisions/editions.

## Electronic Audio Experiments

EAE was founded in 2015 by guitarist and electrical engineer John Snyder. The company's own history describes a small Boston workshop and a product philosophy combining original designs with homages to forgotten circuits. Snyder's 2025 retrospective identifies June 17, 2015 as the date he began taking orders for the first batch of Longswords, making the Longsword an especially useful anchor for the archive's chronology.

Effects Database currently lists 21 EAE products. Product research should treat version changes as first-class archival data rather than assuming a model name represents one immutable circuit or enclosure. Examples include Halberd V1/V2 and Sending V1/V2.

EAE's official site also currently documents Scattering, a stereo reverb released August 27, 2026. It is added as a current discovery record beyond the Effects Database snapshot.

Important research branches:
- Longsword chronology from the 2015 first run through later revisions.
- Halberd V1/V2 and the change away from hard-to-source NOS germanium devices.
- Sending V1/V2 and firmware history.
- Dagger, Surveyor, Citadel, Limelight and other drive families.
- Dude Incredible and its relationship to the Intersound IVP/Percolator tradition.
- 0xEAE line with Obstructures.
- Collaborations with Benson, Mask Audio Electronics, Chase Bliss and Pile.

## Black Arts Toneworks

Effects Database identifies Black Arts Toneworks as a Chattanooga, Tennessee operation run by Mark Wentz and lists 16 products. The builder's own FAQ states that BAT has been building pedals since 2010 and emphasizes small-company service and high-gain dirt.

The official BAT site also preserves an archive section that is useful for historical research. It documents the Priestess, Raw Heart OD, Skyboost and other products that may require reconciliation against the Effects Database catalog. This is exactly the kind of builder/archive mismatch the Dirt Archive should preserve rather than silently normalize.

Priority product branches:

## Fulltone

Effects Database lists 49 Fulltone products spanning fuzz, overdrive, distortion, boost, wah, modulation, delay and utility products.

Fulltone's own current history states that the company was founded in 1991. Operations in California were suspended in 2022. In 2023 Brad Jackson of Jackson Audio/Jackson Ampworks licensed the Fulltone Musical Products designs, and Fulltone USA launched at Winter NAMM 2024, taking over design, manufacturing, support and distribution while continuing to work with Mike Fuller.

This creates a critical archival relationship: the archive should distinguish original Fulltone Musical Products production from Fulltone USA production rather than treating a model name such as OCD, Soul-Bender or Full-Drive as a single uninterrupted manufacturing record.

Priority branches:
- OCD chronology and version/production distinctions.
- Full-Drive family chronology.
- '69 / '70 fuzz families and germanium/silicon distinctions.
- Soul-Bender and SB-2.
- Octafuzz OF-1/OF-2 and Ultimate Octave.
- PlimSoul and PlimSoul MkII.
- Supa-Trem and Deja' Vibe production changes.
- 2024+ Fulltone USA relaunch and which legacy models returned under the new production relationship.

## Editorial / data implications

### 1. Complete catalog discovery is not verification

The 221 records in this phase are discovery records. They establish that a builder/model belongs in the research universe. They do not assert a final production date, generation count or exact manufacturing relationship.

### 2. Current builder sources should supplement Effects Database

Effects Database remains an efficient catalog census source, but current official builder pages can reveal products that have not yet entered the database. Amp Crash and Scattering demonstrate why the archive needs a living discovery pass.

### 3. Collaborations need explicit relationship records

A pedal can have multiple meaningful entities: brand, designer, collaborator, OEM/manufacturer, retailer-exclusive partner and licensing successor. The archive should not flatten those relationships into a single builder string.

### 4. Revived brands need production-period separation

Fulltone is the clearest example in this phase. Original California production and Fulltone USA production should be modeled as distinct production relationships under the same customer-facing brand lineage.

### 5. Historical preservation includes discontinued products

DBA, BAT and EQD all have product histories that extend beyond their current retail catalogs. Archived/discontinued products should remain discoverable rather than disappearing when a builder removes a product page.
