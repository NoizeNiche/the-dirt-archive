# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 13 — Major Lineage Audit

## Scope

This batch reconciles high-value BOSS/Roland, DOD/DigiTech, Ibanez/Maxon/Nisshin Onpa, and Sola Sound/Colorsound relationships without collapsing distinct retail, manufacturer, ownership, or production-state identities.

## Editorial findings

### BOSS / Roland
- Preserve BOSS as the retail/effects brand and Roland as the parent corporate entity.
- OD-1 is the clean historical anchor for the BOSS compact-series dirt chronology: official BOSS history dates the compact launch to 1977 and identifies OD-1 as the first compact unit.
- DS-1 remains a distinct model and later anniversary/reissue states must be separate records rather than new builders.
- FZ-2, FZ-3, and FZ-5 are separate searchable model identities. A later product must not be treated as simply a generation of the earlier one.

### DOD / DigiTech
- DOD and DigiTech belong in one corporate/brand relationship graph, but DOD model history cannot be reconstructed from the current DigiTech catalog alone.
- FX model numbers are identifiers, not a reliable chronological sequence.
- A 1992 Electronic Musician guide provides a useful contemporaneous corridor for FX50B, FX52, FX53, FX55B, FX56B, FX57, FX58, and FX59.
- Current DigiTech manuals are useful for confirming later model existence, but not sufficient by themselves to date every original DOD production state.

### Ibanez / Maxon / Nisshin Onpa
- Preserve Ibanez as the marketed instrument/effects brand and Maxon/Nisshin Onpa as the manufacturer lineage where documentation supports it.
- OD-808 and TS808 are linked retail/product identities, not duplicate names for a single database object.
- Soundtank is treated as a manufacturing exception until model/period-specific factory evidence is established; the Maxon relationship from classic Tube Screamer products must not be inherited automatically.

### Sola Sound / Colorsound
- Colorsound is retained as Sola Sound's branded line while Sola Sound remains the historical manufacturing entity.
- Power Boost and Overdriver are separate searchable names with a documented naming/production transition.
- Early-1970s Tone Bender Fuzz is not collapsed into the earlier MKI/MK1.5/MKII/MKIII/MKIV family merely because the name repeats.
- Supa Tone Bender and Jumbo Tone Bender are kept as distinct retail identities with continuity/rename evidence recorded.
- Vox-marketed Power Boost is preserved as a separate relationship state rather than merged into standard Colorsound retail records.

## Public-site rule

These relationships are ready for a future public-safe lineage edge dataset because they describe identity, ownership, marketing, naming, and product-family relationships rather than circuit reconstruction.

Do not publish schematics, BOMs, PCB layouts, component recipes, or gutshot reconstruction material.
