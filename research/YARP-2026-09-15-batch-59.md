# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 59: Pearl CF-1 and Japanese Wedge/OEM Caution

## Scope
Catalog and attribution sweep for the Pearl CF-1 Cry Fuzz and the early Japanese wedge-fuzz corridor, with special attention to separating documented maker identity from later collector/OEM hypotheses.

## Pearl Musical Instrument Co.

Pearl Musical Instrument Co. is a Japanese musical-instrument manufacturer. A surviving 1970s CF-1 Cry Fuzz specimen is explicitly marked MADE IN JAPAN and is documented by a Japanese specialist dealer as a Pearl Musical Instrument Co. product from the 1970s. The unit is a fuzz-wah pedal.

The CF-1 should therefore enter the canonical Pearl catalog as an evidence-qualified historical dirt product, while its physical pedal manufacturer remains Pearl unless stronger model-specific OEM evidence emerges. Pearl's modern corporate history independently confirms the company's Japanese manufacturing base and export expansion, but it does not by itself establish the factory or subcontractor for the CF-1.

## CF-1 Cry Fuzz

- Model: CF-1 Cry Fuzz.
- Era: 1970s.
- Category: fuzz-wah.
- Brand/builder attribution: Pearl Musical Instrument Co., evidence-qualified.
- Country marking: Japan.
- Known documentation: surviving specialist-retailer specimen with model identity, date range, and Made in Japan marking.
- Exact introduction year: unresolved.
- Exact factory/subcontractor: unresolved.

Tone Machines Blog's 2026 research notes that the CF-1 appears to use a four-transistor fuzz section with paired clipping-diode sets and shows family resemblance to some Japanese Big Muff-era products. That observation is useful for future OEM comparison but is not sufficient to establish that Pearl was made by Elk, Shin-Ei, Teisco, or any other particular factory. The archive therefore records the resemblance as a research lead only.

## Japanese wedge-fuzz corridor

A separate documentary thread concerns the Sekova-style wedge fuzzes and the Greco No. 35 Fuzz Booster. Tone Machines Blog found a November 1967 Japan Music Trades advertisement showing the Greco No. 35 Fuzz Booster in a wedge enclosure visually corresponding to the Sekova family. The same research connects Nisshin Onpa to Greco guitar pickups and later Maxon effects, but explicitly leaves open whether Nisshin Onpa manufactured the wedge fuzz that early.

This produces two useful conclusions for the archive:

1. Greco No. 35 is an early Japanese fuzz product with a documentary 1967 trade-press appearance.
2. Nisshin Onpa/Maxon should not be promoted from plausible to confirmed manufacturer of the entire early wedge family without model-specific evidence.

The Sekova FA-II Fuzz has separate historical documentation placing its major wedge revision around 1969, while the broader wedge family appears to have undergone several versions. The archive should keep version chronology open rather than forcing a single 1960s factory attribution.

## OEM/rebrand leads worth preserving

The wider Japanese Standard Fuzz/Fuzz-Wah ecosystem contains numerous marketed names including Greco, Sekova, Ibanez, Mica, Antoria, Aria Diamond, Bruno, Marlboro and others. Existing archive work already separates the Shin-Ei/Teisco Standard Fuzz families from the unresolved early wedge family. Batch 59 reinforces that separation.

## Evidence state

**Evidence-qualified:** Pearl CF-1 as a 1970s Japanese Pearl-branded fuzz-wah; Greco No. 35 Fuzz Booster documented in November 1967 Japan Music Trades advertising; Sekova FA-II documented as a late-1960s wedge fuzz revision.

**Unresolved:** Pearl CF-1 physical OEM factory; earliest wedge-fuzz factory; exact relationship between Greco No. 35, Sekova versions and Nisshin Onpa; exact production order of all wedge generations.
