# The Dirt Archive Research Protocols

## Purpose

The Research Protocols divide archive work into two distinct modes so breadth is completed before relationship archaeology and deep individual-pedal history.

The active default is **Protocol A**.

---

## Protocol A — Builder & Catalog Discovery

**Goal:** Find as many relevant builders and dirt products as practical during a light-to-medium research pass, then put that information into the research catalog without getting pulled into deep lineage work.

### A1. Search breadth first

Search broadly across regions, eras, catalogs, magazines, advertisements, specialist databases, collector references, manufacturer histories, forums, archives, and other useful documentary sources.

Prioritize discovering:

- pedal builders and manufacturers
- amplifier companies that made dirt pedals
- historical micro-builders
- obscure regional manufacturers
- marketed pedal brands when the maker is not yet known
- private-label or OEM-looking products as **discovery records only**
- revival and later builders where they contribute to the 1960-2026 product universe

Protocol A is not trying to solve who manufactured every product. That belongs primarily to Protocol B.

### A2. Enumerate the builder's dirt line

When a builder or marketed brand is found, search for as much of its dirt catalog as can be established in the same light-to-medium pass.

Record, where available:

- builder / marketed brand name
- model name
- aliases or alternate names only when readily apparent
- broad category: fuzz, distortion, overdrive, dirt hybrid, bass dirt, etc.
- approximate era or known date corridor
- country / region
- source trail
- evidence status
- unresolved notes

Do **not** stop after finding the famous model. Search for the rest of the builder's dirt line.

### A3. Flag multi-product builders for later page population

If a builder appears to have multiple dirt products, explicitly flag that builder as a **catalog-completion / product-page queue item**.

The flag means:

1. the builder needs a complete or substantially complete dirt catalog;
2. each discovered pedal will eventually need its own place in the archive;
3. later work should populate those individual pedal pages with a representative photo and concise historical information.

Protocol A may collect a useful photo lead or obvious basic description when convenient, but it should not turn into a deep individual-pedal investigation.

### A4. What Protocol A should NOT spend time solving

Do not allow the following to hijack a breadth run:

- exact OEM factory attribution
- rebrand genealogy
- successor-company relationships
- ownership changes
- designer credit archaeology
- prototype versus first-sale distinctions
- generation-by-generation enclosure analysis
- serial-number systems
- detailed production chronology
- circuit lineage
- speculative claims about shared factories

Those questions belong in Protocol B.

### A5. Evidence discipline

A Protocol A record can be:

- discovery
- evidence-qualified
- verified
- unresolved

A builder or pedal should not be promoted to stronger certainty merely because its appearance resembles another product.

When evidence is thin, keep the record and flag it rather than deleting the discovery.

### A6. Completion signal

A Protocol A run is successful when it has materially expanded the builder/product universe and identified the builders or products that deserve deeper work.

Protocol A is breadth work, not final historical adjudication.

---

## Protocol B — Deep Builder, OEM & Pedal Archaeology

**Goal:** Resolve relationships and perform deep historical research after Protocol A has built a useful builder/product map.

Protocol B may investigate:

- physical manufacturer versus marketed brand
- OEM and subcontract manufacturing
- rebrands and private labels
- distributor relationships
- designer relationships
- corporate ownership and successor companies
- collaborations and licensing
- product-family lineage
- first public appearance
- prototype / beta / first sale / first shipment / first production distinctions
- generation boundaries
- production periods
- enclosure, artwork, controls and labeling changes
- serial and date conventions
- packaging changes
- reissues, revivals and limited editions
- artist/signature relationships
- production quantities
- surviving-specimen evidence
- competing historical claims and source reconciliation

Protocol B is where the archive answers questions such as **who actually made this, what changed, and how are these products related?**

---

## Protocol relationship

**Protocol A discovers the map. Protocol B explains the map.**

Do not reverse the order unless a specific research question requires it.

A strange rebrand discovered during Protocol A should normally be logged and flagged for Protocol B, not allowed to derail the current breadth pass.

Likewise, an interesting individual pedal discovered during Protocol A should normally be cataloged and queued rather than deeply excavated immediately.

---

## Individual pedal-page queue

Once Protocol A identifies a builder with multiple dirt products, create or maintain a queue for the individual pedal pages.

Each eventual pedal page should be capable of holding at least:

- pedal name
- builder / marketed brand
- category
- approximate date or era
- representative photo
- concise historical description
- evidence/source trail
- confidence / unresolved notes

Protocol B can later expand those pages with deeper chronology, relationships, and manufacturing history.

---

## Scope boundaries

The archive centers on overdrive, distortion, and fuzz, including historically relevant bass dirt and unusual hybrids.

Non-dirt effects, DSP-only products, racks, preamps, kits, mods, and accessories may be retained as boundary records when they materially help the historical story, but they should not inflate the central dirt count.

Do not publish schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries, cloning instructions, or circuit-reconstruction material.

---

## Operating command

When the user says **Protocol A**, perform a continuous light-to-medium breadth search: discover builders, enumerate their dirt catalogs, add or update research records, and flag multi-product builders for later individual pedal-page work.

When the user says **Protocol B**, stop prioritizing breadth and instead perform deep relationship, manufacturing, lineage, chronology, and individual-pedal archaeology on the selected queue.

When no protocol is specified, use **Protocol A** as the default for discovery work.
