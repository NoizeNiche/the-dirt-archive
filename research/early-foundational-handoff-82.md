# Early Foundational Handoff 82

## Pass 34: MI Audio version stratification, legal/documentary anchors, and final boutique-corridor reconciliation

Pass 34 tightens the late 2000s boutique corridor around production-state evidence rather than adding a large number of unrelated models. The main focus is MI Audio's versioned dirt families, HBE's signature lane, the Devi Ever / Ooh La La manufacturing transition, and Black Arts' later family combinations.

### Newly documented

- **MI Audio Neo Fuzz compact/original family clue**: later specialist coverage distinguishes early compact V1 examples from later larger-enclosure versions, giving the archive a useful specimen-facing family marker while retaining 2006-2008 as the stronger historical production envelope.
- **MI Audio G.I. Fuzz version family**: later catalog and market records preserve a G.I. Fuzz V2, so the archive now recognizes a version family without inventing an unsupported transition date.
- **MI Audio Crunch Box V3**: a 2010-labeled surviving specimen documents the V3 control architecture, including internal Presence and the compact red enclosure.
- **MI Audio Crunch Box version ladder**: later documentation states that the Crunch Box reached at least five major versions and that versions 4 and 5 were renamed Super Crunch Box, allowing the archive to keep the V1-V3 corridor distinct from the later MI Effects branch.
- **MI Audio Blues Pro V3**: period owner evidence confirms that the Blues Pro lineage continued into a numbered V3 state after the earlier prototype-to-product transition.
- **MI Audio Neo Fuzz legal-history anchor**: an Australian trademark journal records the September 23, 2006 filing date associated with the Neo Fuzz name for electric guitar effects pedals.
- **MI Audio Blues Pro legal-history anchor**: the same Australian trademark journal records a September 23, 2006 filing associated with the Blues Pro name.
- **Homebrew Electronics Skull Crusher production corridor**: the 2009 signature-product documentary marker is now paired with later product descriptions that preserve its boost/overdrive/distortion range without retrodating later marketplace information.
- **Ooh La La / Devi Ever Synth Mangler manufacturing bracket**: pre-Ooh La La and later Ooh La La-associated specimens are now explicitly retained as separate manufacturing states.
- **Black Arts Toneworks Coven**: a June 2012 manufacturer-authored post establishes a later dual-Pharaoh/Black Forest family product, preserved as a derivative state rather than backdated into the 2010 founding snapshot.

### Key research findings

1. **MI Audio is now one of the clearest examples of why model names are not enough.** Neo Fuzz, G.I. Fuzz, Blues Pro and Crunch Box all accumulate meaningful numbered or enclosure-based state changes, making generation recognition a central part of identification.
2. **Legal records provide a separate clock.** The September 23, 2006 MI Audio trademark filings for Neo Fuzz and Blues Pro do not prove launch dates, but they independently anchor the names in the company's documented legal history.
3. **Crunch Box V3 is now a practical specimen target.** The 2010-labeled V3 record gives the archive a concrete late-corridor object with compact enclosure, external controls and internal Presence clues.
4. **The Synth Mangler transition is now bracketed by real surviving objects.** A pre-Ooh La La specimen and a later Ooh La La-associated specimen demonstrate a genuine manufacturing transition while leaving the exact handoff date open.
5. **Black Arts' later combination products explain family evolution.** Coven is useful not because it belongs in the 2010 launch snapshot, but because it demonstrates how Pharaoh and Black Forest were later recombined into a dedicated dual enclosure.
6. **The boutique corridor is now structurally mature enough to descend.** The remaining gaps are increasingly specimen-specific or documentary-detail questions rather than missing major builder/product branches. That makes a systematic move into the 1990s / early-2000s transition appropriate.

### Evidence discipline

- Trademark filings remain legal-history anchors, never automatic launch dates.
- Later version lists are not used to infer exact earlier transition dates.
- Marketplace years remain specimen evidence unless corroborated independently.
- Manufacturer-authored later combinations remain derivative-family evidence.
- Designer, licensee, manufacturer and brand identities remain distinct.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, full BOMs, gutshot libraries or cloning instructions are added.

### Current lineage position

The archive now has a strong late-2000s boutique spine covering D*A.M., HBE, MI Audio, Devi Ever/Ooh La La and Black Arts Toneworks. The database increasingly captures not only the existence of a pedal, but the documentary clock, version state, specimen state and manufacturing relationship needed to explain why two pedals carrying the same model name may still belong to different historical states.
