# The Dirt Archive — Discovery Harvest Phase 9H

## Focus
This pass continues the discovery layer by preserving obscure builder records that expose different kinds of historical value: tiny modern workshops, unknown-brand vintage objects, regional OEM stories, and design lineage records.

## 1. 1969 Effects

Effects Database lists three products for 1969 Effects: Blonde Boost, Bone Blender and Scarlet Storm. The Bone Blender is documented as a hand-made French fuzz based on the Sola Sound Tone Bender MkII Professional, while the Scarlet Storm is another hand-built fuzz with a bias control and range control. citeturn249603search0turn249603search1turn249603search2

### Archive treatment
This is a useful boutique preservation record because the builder's identity is distinct from the historical circuit family it references. Record the builder as its own entity, preserve the models as separate records, and use a high-level `based-on / lineage` relationship to the Tone Bender MkII family. Do not publish the detailed transistor lists or construction recipe.

## 2. Orbit Fuzz Tone

Effects Database preserves `(unknown, U.S.A.) Orbit Fuzz Tone` as a separate vintage discovery record. The page has no resolved maker attribution, but the entry has survived with a dedicated record and a 2024 Tone Machines Blog demonstration. citeturn249603search4turn249603search3

### Archive treatment
Keep the maker unresolved. Do not infer a 1960s date from informal discussion alone. The record should say approximately `date unknown; period discussed as 196?` until specimen or catalog evidence establishes a real range.

## 3. Aul Instruments Fuzz

Effects Database documents an Aul Instruments Fuzz made for Mike Matthews in 1967 as part of the Guild Foxey Lady replacement story after Mosrite's bankruptcy. citeturn541739search2

### Archive significance
This belongs in the **OEM / contract production** branch of the archive because it connects Aul Instruments, Mike Matthews, and Guild without requiring them to be treated as the same entity. It is also an important bridge into the early Electro-Harmonix story.

## 4. MGI Fuzz and the Clark / Wurlitzer cluster

Effects Database relates the MGI Fuzz to Clark SS-600 Fuzz Tone, E.U. Wurlitzer “Fuzzer Buzzer,” Halifax Fuzz, Orpheum Fuzz and Mica-Tone Fuzz. citeturn541739search16

### Archive treatment
This is a good candidate for a **specimen-family research queue**, not an immediate single-manufacturer conclusion. Preserve each branded record separately and attach a provisional relationship based on the EDB family tree. The relationship itself is useful historical evidence.

## 5. Vintage Technology Banana Fuzz

Effects Database places the Vintage Technology Banana Fuzz into the later Vox/Jen branch, alongside Vox V816/V8161/V8162 and related distortion boosters. citeturn541739search3

### Archive treatment
Keep the modern builder separate from the Italian original family. The useful relation is `based-on / related-to`, not `manufactured-by`.

## 6. Small Australian and Benelux builders

Klinger Custom Pedals' Jimi Fuzz is documented as hand-made in Melbourne. citeturn541739search1

Big John Music was founded in Belgium by Jan Detremerie; its history records early Hairy Balls fuzz versions and a later transition in brand operation to the Netherlands. citeturn541739search6

Sunhaus' Djandek Geological Fuzz is a current Australian small-batch example documented by Effects Database, showing that the discovery layer should not stop at vintage pedals. citeturn541739search7

### Archive significance
These records reinforce the value of preserving **builder chronology**, not just products. A later researcher may care about the builder's first fuzz, a renamed version, a transfer of production, or a move between countries even when no large commercial catalog exists.

## 7. Pro'Traffic Fuzz Unit

Effects Database exposes Pro'Traffic Fuzz Unit in the large Tone Bender MkII relationship tree. citeturn142230search12

### Archive treatment
This is a classic discovery-layer case: preserve the model and its relationship to the MkII family, but do not treat EDB's family tree as proof of exact manufacturing origin without independent evidence.

## 8. New relationship rule: lineage vs identity

The archive should use two different concepts:

`Model -> Manufactured by -> Builder/OEM`

and

`Model -> Based on / Related to -> Historical family`

A pedal may legitimately be based on a Tone Bender design while being manufactured by a completely different company decades later. Confusing those two relationships is one of the easiest ways for a reference database to accidentally turn design influence into false OEM history.
