# Builder Completion Batch 03: Electro-Harmonix

Research date: 2026-09-14

Status: breadth-first catalog expansion. EHX is **not** declared lifetime-complete.

## Why this batch matters
The archive's breadcrumb requires large historical builders to be enumerated before deep specimen archaeology. EHX is especially valuable because first-party historical material confirms early products while the surviving catalog record spans multiple eras and later reissues.

EHX states that Mike Matthews started Electro-Harmonix in 1968 with $1,000 and identifies the LPB-1 as its first product. EHX's own timeline then dates Big Muff Pi to 1969. [EHX history]

### 1978-1980s corridor
The EHX 1970s catalog is an important primary-source artifact. EHX describes the period catalog as the black-and-white catalog from its West 23rd Street operation, when the company was producing Big Muffs, LPB-1s and a large surrounding effects line.

The archive should continue mining this catalog for dirt-adjacent products, then use later catalogs to distinguish models introduced after 1979.

### 1990s revival and Sovtek/Russian era
Keep later Russian/Sovtek Big Muff products as separate historical production entities, not as simple enclosure variants of NYC-era EHX Muffs. The EHX historical index independently identifies Russian Big Muff and Sovtek Big Muff states.

## Important taxonomy rules

### Boosters
LPB-1, LPB-2, Screaming Bird and The Mole are retained as **dirt-adjacent gain records**. They should remain searchable because they materially belong to the development of gain pedals, but they should not inflate the central OD/distortion/fuzz count without a clearly defined catalog rule.

### Big Muff family
The Big Muff is one canonical family with multiple historical production states. Do not count every reissue as another original model. Instead retain:
- family
- marketed model
- generation/era
- production state
- reissue/anniversary state
- specimen

EHX dates the Big Muff Pi's introduction to 1969. Secondary historical coverage indicates that full production ramped around 1971 and that early examples commonly associated with the triangle control layout were produced in the early 1970s. Keep the latter distinction as secondary evidence until corroborated by primary catalog/specimen evidence.

### Hot Tubes family
Treat the original 1978 Hot Tubes, later tube-era/discontinued Hot Tubes products, Nano-era derivatives and present reissue as distinct states. EHX explicitly describes the current Hot Tubes as a reissue of the 1970s original.

## Research gaps now identified
1. Mine the complete 1970s EHX catalog page-by-page for every dirt model, including products that were marketed primarily as sustainers, boosters or bass effects.
2. Locate and reconcile an early-1980s EHX catalog. EHX's own forum has a request noting the difficulty of finding a surviving 1980-84 catalog and specifically names rarer products absent from the common 1978 literature.
3. Build a separate Russian/Sovtek/NYC/U.S. Big Muff production ledger.
4. Reconcile original versus reissue identities for Triangle, Ram's Head, Op-Amp, Green Russian, Deluxe and other Muff families.
5. Search historical dealer catalogs and advertisements for short-lived EHX overdrive/distortion products that are absent from current catalogs.
6. Reconcile EHX versus Guild/Foxey Lady manufacturing and marketing relationships without duplicate builder inflation.

## Evidence quality
**Primary:** EHX corporate history, EHX historical catalog post, EHX current storefront, EHX product pages.

**Secondary:** specialist historical timelines and magazines used only to identify candidates or corroborate production corridors. Secondary claims remain qualified until reconciled against primary evidence or surviving documentary/specimen evidence.

## Disposition
EHX moves from **CENSUS_ONLY** toward **INDEXED_BASELINE_MAPPED**, but is not yet catalog-complete. The highest-value next target is the missing early-1980s catalog corridor and deeper historical enumeration of discontinued dirt products.
