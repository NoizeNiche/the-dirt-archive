# 2007-2012 Modern Boutique Dirt Audit

## Scope
This pass targets the transition from the early-2000s boutique pedal boom into the modern independent-builder ecosystem. The goal is to preserve company origins, first commercial products, production-state changes, and the distinction between dirt products and useful non-dirt chronology markers.

## EarthQuaker Devices
EarthQuaker Devices was founded by Jamie Stillman in 2004. The official company history identifies the Hoof as the first full-production-run pedal, released in 2007, and explains that the earliest pedals were built by hand in Stillman's Akron basement. The same retrospective identifies Hoof, Grand Orbiter, Disaster Transport, Hummingbird and Ghost Echo as basement-era devices. For The Dirt Archive, Hoof is therefore the key production anchor, while the broader basement period remains a distinct production context.

The Hoof Reaper is especially useful as a historical state because EQD says it began as a limited-run ToneFactor collaboration in 2010, with about 25 units, before later reintroduction. This should be modeled as a limited edition / compound product state rather than as a simple replacement for Hoof or Tone Reaper.

## Death By Audio
Death By Audio was founded by Oliver Ackermann in 2002. The company's official history says the inaugural years involved an effects-customization service before the pedal workshop evolved into a more conventional manufacturer. MusicRadar identifies Total Sonic Annihilation as the first pedal Ackermann designed that went into production. That device is preserved here as an experimental/loop boundary rather than central fuzz/OD inventory.

The historical importance of the company is its direct connection between custom/noise experimentation and standardized boutique products. Fuzz War is a core early dirt model and remains a distinct retail family. The company's later Queens workshop should not be back-projected onto early Brooklyn production.

## Malekko Heavy Industry
Effects Database dates the founding of Malekko Heavy Industry Corporation to September 2006 in Austin, Texas, by Joshua Holley and Paul Barker, with a move to Portland in 2010. Contemporary 2007 reporting describes the company as a boutique guitar-pedal manufacturer and documents the B:Assmaster as an important early product. Effects Database dates B:Assmaster publication to February 13, 2007; Tape Op reviewed it in the Jul/Aug 2007 issue.

The archive should preserve Malekko's pedal business alongside, but not merged with, its synthesizer/module work. The B:Assmaster is a clear dirt anchor. Later compact or reconfigured products such as Diabolik should be modeled as later family states, not as the same physical production state.

## Caroline Guitar Company
Caroline states that the company launched in 2010 in Columbia, South Carolina. Effects Database dates the Wave Cannon publication to November 8, 2010, while Caroline's own historical material says first sales happened in October 2010. Those dates are not treated as a contradiction: one is a publication/index date and the other is an operational first-sales date.

The first 75 Wave Cannons were entirely hand-built and numbered. This is unusually useful specimen archaeology because the early production quantity and numbering are explicit. The Wave Cannon is therefore a high-value candidate for future specimen-level work after the builder catalog is completed.

The Olympia Fuzz followed in 2011 and was launched through a Kickstarter campaign. Campaign-era launch state, later production, and future revisions should remain separate.

## Walrus Audio
The historical record requires an explicit three-date distinction. A USPTO-derived trademark record gives WALRUS AUDIO a first-use-in-commerce date of November 21, 2009. A later interview with Brady Smith says he broke from Keeley in 2011 and got Walrus Audio up and running with a business partner. Effects Database dates the Jupiter fuzz to December 22, 2011.

The archive therefore does not force one simplistic founding year. Instead: first trademark use = 2009; company operation/product development = 2011-era evidence; early documented dirt product = Jupiter, 2011. This is a useful template for other modern builders whose trademark, company, prototype and retail dates do not line up.

## Black Arts Toneworks
Black Arts Toneworks says it has been building pedals since 2010. Archived December 2010 entries document active Pharaoh production runs, while the current Pharaoh page describes a design capable of boost, overdrive and saturated fuzz behavior. Founder interviews describe the company's origin as a personally modified Russian Big Muff that attracted outside requests for copies.

For the archive, the important distinction is between the personal prototype and the commercial Pharaoh. The former belongs in origin/prototype history; the latter is the retail model. Do not infer that every early custom or one-off unit is a separate catalog model.

## Dr. Scientist Sounds
Effects Database carries Dr. Scientist product entries dated August 18, 2006, providing an early commercial-era anchor. The current official page identifies The Elements as a dual-channel overdrive/distortion with active three-band EQ and selectable clipping modes. The modern archive should distinguish the early commercial product era from later revisions and packaging states.

A full Dr. Scientist dirt-catalog audit remains pending. The Elements is sufficient for the current builder census as a verified dirt anchor, but no claim of lifetime catalog completeness is made.

## Archive interpretation
The common pattern across these builders is that the modern boutique market did not appear as a single clean launch wave. Companies often begin with modification, custom work, personal prototypes, artist requests, workshop experimentation, or small hand-built runs before becoming recognizable commercial brands.

For The Dirt Archive, the correct historical hierarchy is therefore:

origin / prototype -> first public sale or production run -> early production state -> later retail family -> revision / reissue / limited edition -> specimen

The 2007-2012 period is especially important because it creates the bridge between the 1990s boutique pioneers and the later explosion of independent pedal builders. The archive should preserve that bridge rather than treating 2010 as the beginning of the modern pedal era.
