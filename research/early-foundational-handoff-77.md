# Early Foundational Handoff 77

## Pass 29: 1998-2010 boutique corridor, manufacturing relationships and first commercial states

This pass continues the archive's breadth-first boutique corridor after Pass 28. The emphasis is on builders and products that help explain how late-1990s/2000s boutique dirt moved from one-person experiments into small-batch production, licensed manufacturing, and recognizable generation boundaries.

### Newly documented

- **Ooh La La Manufacturing / Soda Meiser**: surviving 2008 specimen documented as a Devi Ever design with Ooh La La association and Jason Myrold hand-painted enclosure work.
- **Ooh La La Manufacturing / Truly Beautiful Disaster**: Ooh La La manufacturing/management state separated from Devi Ever's original design identity.
- **Homebrew Electronics / Lorba Fuzz**: 2005 surviving specimen documented as a limited handmade U.S. fuzz run of roughly 100 examples, treated as an approximate specimen count until corroborated.
- **Homebrew Electronics / Full Metal Jacket**: 2009 period demo confirms an early FMJ production state and its exterior control/boost layout.
- **MI Audio / Tube Zone**: 2002 commercial-company anchor separated from Michael Ibrahim's 1995 design origin.
- **MI Audio / Tube Zone V1-V2**: early generation family retained as a distinct version boundary for specimen identification.
- **MI Audio / Crunch Box**: final design released in early 2006 after a late-2003 design period; initial batch of 500 sold within three weeks.
- **T-Pedals / T-Fuzz**: late-2002 prototype anchor retained separately from the builder's 2006 full-time production milestone.
- **T-Pedals / Hi-Fuzz**: 2005 Hiwatt UK commissioned fuzz relationship added as an OEM/commissioned-design branch.
- **Black Arts Toneworks / Pharaoh**: May 2010 documentary presence establishes an early production state before later 2011 database coverage.

### Key research findings

1. **Ooh La La is a manufacturing-history problem as much as a pedal-name problem.** The archive should distinguish Devi Ever as designer from Ooh La La as manufacturing/management partner and preserve surviving painted specimens as their own evidence class.
2. **Boutique product dates often have three different clocks:** design origin, first documented production, and later surviving-specimen dating. These are not interchangeable.
3. **MI Audio is an important bridge from hobby builder to commercial boutique manufacturer.** Tube Zone began as an experimental project in 1995, became the first commercial MI Audio pedal after the 2002 company foundation, and then accumulated meaningful version states.
4. **Crunch Box provides a useful production-scale marker.** The documented first 500-unit batch and its rapid sell-through give the archive a rare quantitative anchor for an early boutique distortion launch.
5. **HBE and T-Pedals show how specialist builders moved through multiple market roles.** Handmade retail products, commissioned OEM/brand work and limited runs can coexist without becoming the same lineage object.
6. **Early Black Arts Toneworks needs documentary rather than retroactive launch dating.** A May 2010 manufacturer post is a useful early-presence anchor; later 2011 database publication should not be mistaken for the product's launch date.
7. **The archive continues to prioritize specimen-facing evidence.** Control count, enclosure artwork, documented batch size, branding/manufacturing role, public demo dates and surviving labels are more useful for identification than circuit reconstruction.

### Evidence discipline

- Manufacturer statements, historical interviews, period demos, specialist databases and surviving-market specimens remain separate evidence classes.
- Approximate dates are labeled as documentary or developmental anchors rather than converted into false exact launch dates.
- Manufacturing, licensing, OEM and designer roles remain distinct entities where the evidence supports that distinction.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries, or cloning instructions are added.

### Current lineage position

The archive now has a denser bridge from the late-1990s one-person boutique ecosystem into the 2000s production boom: Z.Vex/Devi Ever-era experimentation, Ooh La La manufacturing, HBE handmade runs, MI Audio commercial expansion, T-Pedals prototype-to-production development, and the emergence of Black Arts Toneworks. The identification target remains the physical/historical state of a pedal rather than the model name alone.
