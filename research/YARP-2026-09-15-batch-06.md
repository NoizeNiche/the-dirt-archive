# YARP 2026-09-15 — Japanese OEM/export batch 06

## Purpose

Continue the breadcrumb's active Japanese OEM/export census with breadth-first catalog work. This batch is deliberately catalog-level. It does not attempt specimen archaeology, circuit reconstruction, schematics, PCB documentation, or cloning material.

## 1. Shin-Ei / Honey / Companion lineage

### Identity decision
Keep **Honey**, **Shin-Ei**, and **Companion** as historically searchable names within one related manufacturer/brand lineage rather than treating every retail label as an independent builder. The relationship is strong enough for a canonical lineage record, but individual product attribution remains model-specific where the surviving evidence is ambiguous.

### Dirt catalog baseline
- Honey Baby Crying FY-6 / Super Fuzz — 1967-era anchor.
- Honey Special Fuzz — separate dirt product; do not merge with FY-6 merely because both are Honey/Shin-Ei-era fuzzes.
- Honey Psychedelic Machine — hybrid multi-effect; retain as a boundary/hybrid record rather than inflating the central fuzz count.
- Shin-Ei FY-6 Super Fuzz — 1968 branded successor/continuation of the Honey FY-6 family.
- Shin-Ei Companion FY-2 — late-1960s/early-1970s fuzz family, including the documented early germanium and later silicon-era distinctions.
- Shin-Ei Companion FY-6 / Fuzz Master branded states — retain as related retail/production states rather than separate manufacturers.
- Shin-Ei Uni-Vibe and related non-dirt products remain outside the central dirt catalog except as lineage evidence.

### Evidence notes
Shin-ei's current official site explicitly states that the Honey FY-6 Baby Crying effect was released in 1967 and that Shin-ei branded the FY-6 in 1968. The same source describes the modern Shin-ei FY-6 as a reproduction of the 1968 branded model. Shin-ei's current product catalog also retains Companion FY-2 as a separate historical product. citeturn2search0turn2search1turn2search4

The present-day Shin-ei company should **not** be silently treated as the uninterrupted corporate entity of the original Japanese manufacturer. A 2018 interview with current owner Bob Feldman describes the modern company as a reincarnated Shin-ei operation and says the current owner acquired the Shin-ei, Honey, and Companion trademarks. This belongs in the revival/ownership relationship layer, not as proof of uninterrupted historical corporate continuity. citeturn2search7

The FY-2 chronology remains evidence-qualified. Secondary historical material distinguishes an earlier germanium version from later silicon versions, while the exact first-production date remains a research target. ## 2. JEN Elettronica

### Identity decision
Keep **JEN Elettronica** as the Italian physical-manufacturer/OEM entity. Vox, Dallas Arbiter, Sam Ash and other marketed names should be relationship nodes when supported by model-specific evidence.

### Dirt catalog baseline
- JEN Double Sound — fuzz/wah, Pescara, Italy; 1970s production state documented.
- Vox by JEN Double Sound — marketed relationship to the same Italian manufacturing lineage.
- JEN Jumbo Fuzz — early-1970s Italian dirt product; evidence supports a distinct model and multiple marketed versions.
- JEN fuzz/wah family records — retain model-specific entries where names or enclosure states differ rather than collapsing all treadle fuzz/wah units into one generic JEN record.

### Evidence notes
Effects Database identifies the Vox by JEN Double Sound as an Italian-made fuzz/wah produced by Jen Electronica in Pescara and sold under both JEN and Vox branding. Tonehome independently dates a JEN Double Sound example to 1973. citeturn2search14turn2search9

A surviving 1973 JEN Jumbo Fuzz listing provides useful production-state clues and records the Dallas Arbiter Jumbo Fuzz, Sam Ash Jumbo Fuzz and Fuzzola II relationships. These should remain model-specific and evidence-qualified rather than being treated as interchangeable names. citeturn2search12turn0search9

## 3. Coron Musical Instrument Co.

### Identity decision
Keep **Coron Musical Instrument Co.** as the working physical-manufacturer entity for the documented Japanese Coron dirt family. Grant, Storm, Asama Engineering, Memphis and other labels remain marketed/OEM relationship nodes unless stronger model-specific evidence changes attribution.

### Dirt catalog baseline
- Coron Distortion 10.
- Coron Distortion 15.
- Coron first-series Distortion family / related branded OEM states.
- Grant-branded related distortion states.
- Storm-branded related distortion states.
- Asama Engineering-branded related distortion states.
- Additional Coron-attributed marketed names from the existing archive census remain discovery/reconciliation targets rather than automatically verified products.

### Evidence notes
Effects Database describes the Coron Distortion as a Japanese-made pedal from the late 1970s/early 1980s and identifies Grant, Storm, Asama, Memphis and other OEM-branded appearances. It also distinguishes Distortion 10 and Distortion 15 as closely related models. citeturn2search6

A separate Japanese source confirms the existence of both Coron Distortion 10 and Distortion 15 and treats them as separate products. This supports retaining the two model names independently in the catalog rather than reducing the family to one generic Coron Distortion entry. citeturn2search4

## Research decisions carried forward

1. **Retail name != manufacturer.** Keep marketed/export labels searchable without creating duplicate builder entities.
2. **OEM attribution is model-specific.** A documented relationship for one pedal does not automatically transfer to every visually similar pedal.
3. **Revival != uninterrupted continuity.** Modern Shin-ei is recorded as a revival/current operation with acquired historical trademarks, not as automatic proof of uninterrupted corporate identity.
4. **Hybrid products remain boundaries.** Multi-effects such as the Honey Psychedelic Machine are historically relevant but should not inflate the core dirt count.
5. **Conflicting dates remain unresolved.** Exact FY-2 introduction/production boundaries need more primary-period evidence.
