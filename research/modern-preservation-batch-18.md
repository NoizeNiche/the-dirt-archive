# Modern Preservation Track — Batch 18

## Position
2023 census continues. This batch prioritizes products with unusually clear historical ancestry, compound identity, or manufacturer-documented functionality. Discovery indexes remain discovery aids; promotion requires stronger evidence.

## Verified / strong evidence

### JHS Pedals — Legends of Fuzz: Berkeley
- Introduced in 2023 as a JHS interpretation of the 1973 Seamoon Fresh Fuzz.
- JHS added a side-mounted JHS mode switch that changes the voicing from the vintage base interpretation.
- Controls: Gain and Bite.
- Archive identity: a modern JHS product inspired by a historical pedal, not a Fresh Fuzz generation.
- Status: CATALOGED / HISTORICAL-ANCESTRY MAPPED.

### JHS Pedals — Legends of Fuzz: Mary-K
- Introduced in 2023 as a JHS interpretation of the late-1960s Kay Fuzz Tone.
- Controls: Volume and Frequency.
- JHS mode adds a germanium clipping stage; expression output permits external Frequency control.
- Archive identity: modern tribute product with documented historical source, not a Kay generation.
- Status: CATALOGED / HISTORICAL-ANCESTRY MAPPED.

### JHS Pedals — Legends of Fuzz: Plugin
- Introduced in 2023 as a JHS interpretation of the Jordan Electronics Boss Tone plug-in fuzz.
- Controls: Volume and Attack.
- JHS mode adds additional hard clipping and a midrange change.
- Standard stompbox enclosure intentionally differs from the original direct-to-jack format.
- Status: CATALOGED / HISTORICAL-ANCESTRY MAPPED.

### Bixonic — Axentrix A1
- Bixonic's current history identifies Axentrix A1 as the first new-generation Bixonic product, released in 2022 as a U.S. export model.
- This means 2023 discovery lists should not be used to date the product's introduction to 2023.
- Current Bixonic history also separates the original 1994-2005 Expandora-era company/product history from the 2020s revival.
- Axentrix A1 is therefore cataloged as a distinct modern product family, with the Expandora relationship treated as brand/product ancestry rather than a simple generation sequence.
- Status: CATALOGED / IDENTITY MAPPED / DATE CORRECTED.

### Crazy Tube Circuits — Motherload
- 2023 product combining two distinct analog circuit lineages inspired by RAT and Big Muff designs in one enclosure.
- The two sides have separate EQ controls; a LINK EQ function makes the control sets interact across both sides.
- Archive identity: compound product containing two distinct historical product lineages. Do not collapse it into either a RAT or Big Muff family record.
- Status: CATALOGED / COMPOUND PRODUCT.

### Fairfield Circuitry — Roger That
- Modern Fairfield product described by the manufacturer as an FM modulator/demodulator with FET preamp, CV on TUNE, RFI/EMI approximation and intentionally unstable distortion/noise behavior.
- Archive category remains Distortion / experimental dirt rather than attempting to force it into a conventional RAT, Muff, or amp-style lineage.
- Status: CATALOGED / CATEGORY MAPPED.

### Bispell Audio — Gleam V2
- 2023 GPX coverage identifies Gleam V2 as a silicon multi-fuzz using four transistors and multiple classic fuzz voicings.
- Reported control/voicing system spans FZ-1, Fuzz Face and Tone Bender-style territories.
- Because the available 2023 evidence is stronger for the V2 product identity than for a complete V1-to-V2 change log, V2 is preserved as a generation candidate rather than asserting a full circuit-generation chronology.
- Status: DISCOVERED / VARIANT_MAPPED CANDIDATE.

## Method notes
- Historical tribute products are separate modern products even when their ancestry is explicit.
- Compound pedals retain contributing lineages separately.
- Bixonic demonstrates why company chronology and product chronology must be separated: the modern Axentrix A1 belongs to the 2020s revival, but Bixonic dates its release to 2022 rather than 2023.
- V2 labels are not automatically treated as proof of a circuit revision when a complete change record is unavailable.
