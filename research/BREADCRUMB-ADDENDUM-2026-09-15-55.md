## Historical findings

- A 1968 city directory independently places Applied Audio Products Inc. at 111 Market St., Kenilworth, with Jack Gentul named in connection with the company. Tone Machines separately reconstructs the same Kenilworth facility as the post-Hilgen Applied location.
- Tone Machines documents a 1968 Trans-Tek plant announcement in South Plainfield naming Applied Audio as an associate organization and explicitly mentioning effects production including the Applied Big Fuzz. This is strong shared-facility evidence, but not proof that Trans-Tek physically built every Applied pedal.
- Jack Gentul's continuity from Sano through Hilgen and then Applied is retained as historical context. Model-level factory attribution still governs individual pedal records.
- Kimberly Buzz Box production is now represented as a period-specific sequence: LIMMCO v1, an interim Applied-built state, then later UMI production. The interim Applied state is high-confidence research validation but remains a reconstruction rather than a surviving factory statement.
- UMI Buzz Tone, Apollo No. 843, and Conrad Buzz Box remain separate later UMI marketed states within the same broader Kimberly / Applied family.
- Applied Big Fuzz and later Applied Big Bass naming are kept separate until model-specific evidence permits consolidation.
- Nomad Banshee remains Applied-made and LIMMCO-distributed, but its exact manufacturing date remains unresolved because component dates can reflect surplus stock.

## Validation posture

This batch strengthens the distinction between three different kinds of historical connection: physical manufacturing, shared facilities / corporate association, and distribution / house-brand relationships. None are being substituted for one another.
