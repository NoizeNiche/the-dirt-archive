# Early Foundational Handoff 79

## Pass 31: HBE catalog anchors, MI Audio generation boundaries, Devi Ever licensing transition, and early Black Arts snapshot

Pass 31 continues the 1998-2010 boutique corridor with a tighter emphasis on period documentary anchors and version boundaries that can actually help identify surviving pedals. This pass deepens Homebrew Electronics, separates MI Audio design-origin chronology from commercial production generations, adds a strong early Devi Ever licensing-transition record, and expands the May 2010 Black Arts Toneworks manufacturer snapshot.

### Newly documented

- **Homebrew Electronics Power Screamer**: January 2005 Vintage Guitar review provides an early period catalog anchor with documented overdrive controls, diode selection and boost switching.
- **Homebrew Electronics Power Screamer database marker**: Effects Database preserves a September 9, 2005 publication marker, kept separate from launch chronology.
- **Homebrew Electronics UFO Fuzz / Octave**: August 2006 owner evidence confirms the product in active use and documents its dedicated octave footswitch.
- **Homebrew Electronics Big D**: January 2006 owner discussion establishes a period OD/distortion sales and usage state.
- **Homebrew Electronics ParaDrive**: Effects Database gives a July 7, 2008 documentary anchor and records its independent boost/overdrive architecture and controls.
- **MI Audio Tube Zone original design origin**: MI Audio historical material preserved by Effects Database states the first Tube Zone was built in 1995 after years of overdrive experimentation.
- **MI Audio Tube Zone second generation**: MI Audio's 2006-2008 account distinguishes a lower-profile enclosure, three-position mid switch, higher output and internal brightness/character adjustments.
- **MI Audio Crunch Box early commercial production**: historical documentation identifies an early-2006 release and records the first 500-unit batch selling within three weeks.
- **MI Audio Crunch Box V2**: 2008 period owner evidence identifies V2 by its internal presence control, reinforcing the specialist-documented version boundary.
- **Devi Ever Truly Beautiful Disaster**: November 2008 surviving-object evidence preserves a very early late-2003/early-2004 Devi Ever-built TBD while the designer interview documents the later Ooh La La licensing transition.
- **Black Arts Toneworks Ritual**: May 2010 manufacturer archive places the one-knob Ritual within the same early catalog snapshot that includes Pharaoh, LSTR and Black Forest.

### Key research findings

1. **HBE now has a genuine early-2000s catalog spine.** The Power Screamer is supported by period magazine evidence as early as January 2005, while the 2006-2008 owner trail shows the UFO, Big D and related HBE products operating as a coherent boutique catalog rather than isolated later references.
2. **The ParaDrive gives HBE a later 2008 documentary marker.** This is useful because it demonstrates the builder's dual-purpose OD/boost product architecture without requiring us to backdate the model.
3. **MI Audio needs multiple clocks.** The Tube Zone's 1995 design origin is not the same thing as the MI Audio company's commercial chronology, and the second-generation Tube Zone has identifiable historical changes that deserve a separate generation state.
4. **Crunch Box V1/V2 is now a specimen-facing identification problem rather than a vague model-version label.** The early commercial release anchor, first-batch quantity and 2008 V2 evidence give the archive multiple independent handles for future specimen comparison.
5. **The Devi Ever / Ooh La La transition is clearer.** A surviving 2008 record explicitly preserves an early Devi Ever-built TBD while the designer's later interview explains that the flagship design was subsequently licensed to Ooh La La. The archive therefore keeps designer, licensee/manufacturer and surviving specimen states distinct.
6. **Black Arts' May 2010 catalog snapshot is broader than Pharaoh alone.** Ritual can now join Pharaoh, LSTR and Black Forest as documented early-period catalog objects without forcing an exact release date where the manufacturer archive only proves presence.

### Evidence discipline

- Period magazines, manufacturer archives, specialist databases and community/specimen evidence remain separate evidence classes.
- Database publication dates are not silently promoted to launch dates.
- Design-origin dates remain labeled as design-origin anchors.
- Version boundaries are promoted only when period documentation records a meaningful exterior, control, or historical change.
- Licensing, manufacturing, designer and brand roles stay distinct.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, full BOMs, gutshot libraries or cloning instructions are added.

### Current lineage position

The boutique corridor now has a stronger bridge from early-2000s small-builder experimentation into the 2005-2010 commercial boutique boom. HBE contributes a documented multi-model catalog; MI Audio supplies explicit generational development; Devi Ever demonstrates the messy handoff between designer-built and licensed/manufactured states; and Black Arts Toneworks supplies a manufacturer-authored 2010 snapshot of a rapidly forming heavy-dirt identity.
