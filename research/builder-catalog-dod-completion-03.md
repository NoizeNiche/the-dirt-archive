## Important evidence notes

The current DigiTech manual index explicitly preserves a large portion of the DOD FX catalog, including FX51, FX55C, FX59, FX66, FX69B, FX70, FX76, FX86, FX91, FX92, FX101, FX102 and YJM308. [https://digitech.com/product-manuals/]

DigiTech's historical page confirms the DOD 250, FX-series expansion and the later Lamb Series, naming FX69 Grunge among the signature experimental models and identifying Punkifier, Death Metal and Carcosa among the historical DOD story. [https://digitech.com/history/]

The existence of multiple suffix states matters. FX50B, FX55B/55C, FX56B and related revisions should be preserved as separate production/model-state records where period documentation supports them, rather than being silently folded into a generic FX number.

## Boundary rules

FX10 remains a historically relevant gain product but is not automatically counted as a central overdrive/distortion/fuzz record. Envelope filters, compressors and modulation units remain outside the central dirt count even when historically adjacent in the FX catalog.

The archive should not infer circuit identity from a model name or forum claim. A catalog entry records documented existence and historical relationships first; production-state archaeology comes later.
