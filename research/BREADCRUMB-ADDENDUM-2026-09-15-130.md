# YARP Breadcrumb 130

## Purpose
Period-catalog and OEM-lineage pass for the Lafayette/LRE Fuzz Sound and the later Univox Uni-Wah/U-1095 corridor.

## New research conclusions

- `VAR-120`: The Lafayette 1968 catalog provides a firm period anchor for the imported LRE Fuzz Sound, documenting it as a guitar/bass fuzz with a two-control layout and 1.5V penlight-battery operation. The catalog does not identify the Japanese factory.
- `VAR-121`: Current historical research links the later Univox U-1095 Super Fuzz to the same Japanese manufacturer as the Uni-Wah and argues that this may be the manufacturer Unicord used for the earlier LRE Fuzz Sound. The factory name remains unresolved.
- `VAR-122`: Melos FS-1 remains a strong house-brand research lead because it is directly grouped with the LRE/Apollo/Mica/Monacor/Olson family, while newer research suggests Melos may have been a house brand of the unnamed OEM. This is not promoted to fact.
- `VAR-123`: The broader AM-44A/LRE Fuzz Sound family remains on attribution hold. No available evidence justifies assigning Shin-Ei, Nisshin Onpa or Ace Tone as the physical factory for every label in the family.

## Cross-reference / dedupe decisions

- Lafayette Radio Electronics remains a U.S. marketed/distribution identity, not a Japanese pedal manufacturer.
- Unicord remains the Univox-side corporate/marketing chain; it is not being substituted for the physical Japanese factory.
- Melos is retained as a separate marketed identity and possible house-brand lead, not merged with an unnamed manufacturer.
- The later U-1095 chronology does not retroactively prove the factory of the 1967-1968 LRE Fuzz Sound.

## Evidence notes

The 1968 Lafayette catalog is a particularly useful period source because it fixes the LRE product in contemporary U.S. retail history rather than relying on modern seller dating. Tone Machines Blog's 2026 research supplies the cross-generation manufacturing hypothesis linking LRE, Uni-Wah and U-1095, but explicitly leaves the factory unnamed. Effects Database preserves the product-family relationships among LRE, Melos and the AM-44A cluster.
