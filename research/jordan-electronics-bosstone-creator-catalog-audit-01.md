# Jordan Electronics / Jordan Electronics Co. — Dirt Catalog Audit 01

## Scope

This audit establishes a manufacturer-first baseline for Jordan Electronics, with emphasis on documented fuzz/overdrive products and known branding/licensing transitions. It intentionally separates original Jordan manufacture from later Sho-Sound/Sho-Bud/Nashville branded examples and later revival products.

## Manufacturer entity

Jordan Electronics was a California electronics company associated with Victoreen Instrument Company. Period and historical sources place its guitar-effects activity in the mid-1960s. Vintage Guitar identifies Jordan Electronics as a division of Victoreen and identifies Alhambra, California as the early location; later Boss Tone examples are commonly separated into Alhambra, Pasadena, and Nashville production/branding corridors. [Vintage Guitar; Guitar World]

## Verified core dirt catalog

### Boss Tone / Bosstone, Model 1000

- Product class: fuzz / distortion; direct guitar-output-jack plug-in effect.
- Manufacturer/brand: Jordan Electronics.
- Earliest commercial corridor: 1966 NAMM introduction, with commercial availability following in 1967 according to historical sources.
- Original locations: Alhambra, California, followed by Pasadena, California.
- Later Nashville corridor: Boss Tone was licensed/marketed for the Sho-Sound/Sho-Bud market, creating a distinct Nashville-branded production/retail layer rather than a reason to merge every example into a single undifferentiated specimen class.
- Controls: Volume and Attack.
- Archive rule: keep Alhambra, Pasadena, and Nashville/Sho-Sound examples as production/branding corridors under the same retail family unless stronger primary evidence proves a manufacturer change.

Guitar World explicitly groups vintage Boss Tone units into Alhambra, Pasadena, and Nashville categories and identifies the Nashville version as associated with Sho-Bud/Sho-Sound licensing. Effects Database likewise indexes separate location/branding generations. [Guitar World; Effects Database]

## Related Jordan dirt product

### Creator Volume Sustainer, Model 6000

- Product class: fuzz / distortion-adjacent sustained-volume effect.
- Jordan product appearing in the late-1970s period in historical secondary documentation.
- Historical account from Kit Rae places the Creator after a Jordan employee brought a Big Muff Pi into the shop and associates the model with George Cole and engineer Bob Garcia, with a late-1970s Jordan context.
- Treat as a distinct Jordan retail model, not as a Big Muff Pi rebadge.
- Evidence level: candidate-to-probable historical model. The surviving documentation is materially thinner than the Boss Tone evidence, so the archive should not assign an exact launch year or manufacturing run length without stronger primary evidence.

## Jordan effects catalog boundary

Vintage Guitar identifies other Jordan products including the Creator, Vibrasonic, volume pedal, and Gig Wah. These are retained as catalog-boundary records rather than being promoted to core dirt unless additional evidence establishes the specific dirt role of a given model.

## OEM / licensing / branding relationships

### Sho-Sound / Sho-Bud

Historical sources consistently identify a Nashville/Sho-Sound Boss Tone corridor associated with Sho-Bud. The exact division of labor between Jordan manufacture and later Sho-Sound production is not fully settled by the surviving evidence. One strong historical account describes Sho-Sound as a licensed Jordan Boss Tone, while period-user recollections disagree about whether Sho-Bud ever physically manufactured the units. The archive therefore records the relationship as **licensed / branded / manufacturer attribution unresolved**, rather than claiming an unsupported factory transfer.

### Gretsch distribution

Secondary historical material describes the Nashville Sho-Sound Boss Tone as distributed through Gretsch. This is catalog/distribution metadata, not evidence that Gretsch manufactured the pedal.

## Variant chronology note

Boss Tone specimens should not be dated solely from the presence of the Boss Tone name. The literature documents location and construction differences between Alhambra, Pasadena, and Nashville examples, and the archive should preserve those as generation/specimen evidence. Exact circuit reconstruction is intentionally outside the public archive scope.

## Research confidence

**Boss Tone:** high confidence as a Jordan core dirt model; medium-to-high confidence on Alhambra → Pasadena → Nashville chronology; high confidence that Sho-Sound branding/license should be kept distinct from the Jordan manufacturer entity.

**Creator Volume Sustainer:** medium confidence as a Jordan product; exact dates, production quantity, and full production chronology remain open.

## Open research targets

1. Locate surviving 1966–1969 Jordan Electronics advertisements/catalogs that show Boss Tone and other Jordan effects together.
2. Establish the strongest documentary boundaries between Alhambra and Pasadena Boss Tone specimens.
3. Resolve the exact legal/manufacturing relationship between Jordan Electronics and Sho-Sound/Sho-Bud in Nashville.
4. Find primary Jordan documentation for Creator Volume Sustainer Model 6000 and determine whether it was a late Jordan product or a post-Jordan continuation.
5. Audit Jordan's non-dirt products so the full builder catalog can be bounded before individual specimen archaeology.

## Public archive boundary

This record does not publish schematics, component lists, PCB layouts, gutshot libraries, or circuit reconstruction instructions. The purpose is historical identification, chronology, manufacturer attribution, product-family structure, and evidence tracking.

## Primary/secondary source notes

- Guitar World, "The Jordan Boss Tone fuzz is the original guitar plug-in" — identifies the 1966 introduction, Jordan Electronics background, and Alhambra/Pasadena/Nashville production/branding categories.
- Vintage Guitar, "Jordan Boss Tone" — identifies Jordan Electronics as a Victoreen division and lists additional Jordan effects including Creator and Gig Wah.
- Effects Database, "Jordan 1000 Boss Tone" — preserves separate Alhambra, Pasadena, and Nashville/Sho-Sound indexed variants.
- Kit Rae, "The Big Muff History / Big Muff Clones" — historical secondary account connecting Jordan's Creator Volume Sustainer Model 6000 to a late-1970s Jordan context.
- Steel Guitar Forum archival discussions — useful oral-history/specimen evidence for the Jordan/Sho-Sound relationship, but not treated as sole proof where stronger primary evidence is required.
