# 2008-2014 Microbuilder Audit 09

## Purpose
This pass expands the 2008-2014 boutique dirt census with builders whose documentary trail is strong enough to justify catalog-layer records, while keeping discovery-only and unresolved manufacturing identities separate.

## Cause & Effect Pedals (CE Pedals)
Mark Roberts and Brian Alexson document an iterative design process beginning in fall 2008 and announcement/first shipments of FET Dream in March 2009. Effects Database currently indexes one CE product, so the archive records FET Dream as the first documented retail dirt product without inventing a larger hidden catalog.

## Chellee Guitars
Odelya provides an unusually clean design-to-product trail: the builder says work began in early 2009 and the pedal is documented in July 2010. Its interchangeable clipping modules are treated as configuration/specimen states rather than separate retail products. Later Odie models are not retroactively assigned to the 2010 corridor.

## RPG Electronics
RPG Electronics appears in Effects Database's August 2, 2010 weekly overview with Blue Flame. The record explicitly says it was made for Blue Flame Music. The archive preserves that marketed/store relationship but leaves physical manufacturing identity unresolved.

## RWL Electronics
The C.A.B. Driver record states that two years of experimentation preceded the 2010 publication. This is evidence for a development corridor, not proof of commercial sales beginning in 2008. The pedal is retained as a handcrafted discrete overdrive/boost record.

## Viva Analog
Jean-Charles Maillet's history predates the boutique era by decades. The important 2012 anchor is the spring release of original pro-grade DIY effect pedal kits, including 360 Fuzz, 360+, and Octave Amp in the indexed catalog. The archive distinguishes DIY kits from completed retail pedals and preserves the 1995 Acoustic 360 pedal-format project as an antecedent rather than conflating the two.

## Castledine Electronics
Stuart Castledine's MkOne Bender and Supa Fuzz MkI are documented in 2010-2011. These are vintage-style reproductions/revival products. They remain linked to the historical Sola Sound/Marshall product families but are never merged into original manufacture. Later production/revision states should be treated separately where evidence supports them.

## Buffalo FX
Buffalo FX has unusually useful generation evidence. Fuzz Face Ge was published in March 2012; Germanium Fuzz appeared in May 2012 with a documented V1 limited to 50 units, followed by V2/V3 transistor-source changes. CVIII silicon fuzz and the 18V Power Booster were documented in August 2013, and Evolution in June 2014. The archive therefore treats Buffalo FX as a high-priority catalog-completion target with an excellent bridge into specimen archaeology.

## Enormous Door FX
ATX Dynamic Overdrive and ICBM appear in NAMM 2013 documentation. ICBM is explicitly identified as the original version later renamed SCUD Fuzz following a trademark conflict. The archive models this as a naming/retail-state transition, not as two unrelated products.

## Vertex
Dynamic Distortion is documented February 2014 as a California-handcrafted analog hybrid spanning overdrive, distortion and fuzz. The wider Vertex catalog contains later products, but this pass only promotes the early dirt anchor and leaves complete chronology for a later builder-completion queue.

## Champ Electronics
John Chambers' 2011 interview states that Champ effects were hand built and hand wired in-house, with make-on-order/deposit production. TreBoost Plus is documented in 2009 and has multiple configuration states. Because it is principally a treble booster, it is retained as dirt-adjacent scope rather than inflated into the main OD/distortion/fuzz count.

## Plugged It!
Effects Database's March 2009 weekly overview identifies Ge Fuzz, Fuzz Lab, Dual Distortion, Ge Fuzz, Green Box and related products. The surviving trail is useful for census breadth, but physical identity and geography are not yet strong enough for canonical-builder status. These remain discovery records pending reconciliation.

## Evidence boundaries
No circuit reconstructions, schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs or cloning instructions are included. The catalog layer records historical identity, chronology, relationships and evidence state only.

## Research consequence
The 2008-2014 boutique census now has stronger coverage of Canadian, British, American and Japanese-associated product trails, plus several microbuilders whose production scale or OEM identity would otherwise disappear from a famous-model-only history. The next useful move is to reconcile these discovery records against the existing master census and then complete the deepest catalog gaps for Buffalo FX, Vertex, Chellee, and any overlapping builders already present elsewhere in the archive.
