# YARP Breadcrumb 142

## Focus
Deepen the 1960s-1970s U.S. regional manufacturer census by separating physical OEM makers from the brands and distributors whose names appear on the boxes.

## Promoted
- Applied Audio Products Inc.: verified dirt maker. Specialist reconstruction identifies Applied as an actual New Jersey pedal manufacturer; a 1968 Kenilworth directory lists the company as an amplifier manufacturer. Applied-built dirt includes its own fuzz and numerous customer/OEM products such as Goya, Guild, Lou Rose and Crown.
- United Musical Industries (UMI): verified dirt maker. Effects Database identifies the UMI Buzz Tone & Volume Expander as U.S.-made by United Musical Instruments; a Gearphoria retrospective identifies founder Richard Soloway and states UMI made pedals.
- LIMMCO / Long Island Musical Merchandise Co.: verified limited manufacturing entity. Specialist research reports owner Bob Seidman's statement that the first Kimberly Buzz Box and Volume Expander units were initially made in LIMMCO's Long Island warehouse before production was outsourced. This does not imply a general LIMMCO pedal catalog.

## Production-chain clarification
- Kimberly is now mapped as a multi-stage lineage: initial in-house LIMMCO manufacture, a later Applied Audio phase, and later UMI production. Exact dates for every handoff remain unresolved.
- Apollo and Conrad Buzz Box & Volume Expander labels are preserved as marketed/product identities associated with the UMI lineage rather than duplicated as separate builders.
- Trans-Tek is not promoted as a separate pedal builder here because the evidence describes Applied effects production occurring within an associated manufacturing context, not a sufficiently distinct Trans-Tek pedal catalog.

## Held / exclusions
- Applied's earliest Kimberly involvement remains partly reconstructed, so the archive does not back-project every pre-UMI Kimberly unit onto Applied.
- Manny's / Orpheum / Clark remain unknown-OEM families despite newly established release-era evidence.
- GM Electronics Fuzz Up and Emthree Fuzz Super remain discovery leads pending stronger physical-maker attribution.

## Editorial consequence
This pass materially improves the archive's manufacturing graph without multiplying brands into fictional manufacturers. The builder layer now records not only who sold a fuzz, but who is actually documented as having built it, including narrowly defined in-house manufacturing where the historical evidence warrants it.

## Next queue
Continue screening obscure regional makers and early OEM shops in the U.S., Canada, Australia and Europe. Prioritize direct manufacturer evidence and period trade documentation; add no candidate merely because a collector database assigns the brand a “maker” field.
