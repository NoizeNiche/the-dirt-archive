# Ibanez / Nisshin Onpa / Maxon Catalog Audit 03

## Scope
Breadth-first audit of the Ibanez/Maxon dirt corridor, emphasizing manufacturer catalogs and historical catalog indexes rather than individual specimen archaeology.

## Primary catalog trail
Ibanez's official catalog archive exposes dedicated effects/catalog material across 1984-1989, including **1984 Sound Effect**, **1985 Effects Pro Spec**, **1985 Ibanez Compact Effects Master Series**, **1986 Ibanez Compact Effect Power Series**, **1987 Ibanez Power Series**, and **1989 Soundtank**. The Japanese archive preserves the same effects-specific chronology. This establishes that the decade needs to be treated as multiple retail generations, not as one continuous Ibanez pedal line.

Maxon's 1980 effects brochure documents **OD801 D&S, OD802 D&S II, OD808 Over Drive, MB10 Bass Booster, MB20 Power Booster, MB30 Treble Booster, MB40 Distortion Booster, and SD5 Double Sound**, providing a useful period-primary bridge between the late-1970s Maxon baseline and the later nine-series corridor.

## Tube Screamer / Power Series corridor
The historical Tube Screamer trail distinguishes the **1985 Master/L-series STL Super Tube**, the **1986 Power Series TS10**, and the later **Soundtank TS5**. TS5 is documented as a 1991-1998 Soundtank model and as a Taiwan-produced economy-generation retail identity. These should remain separate generations even where the product name remains Tube Screamer.

## Soundtank dirt corridor
The official Ibanez archive confirms the 1989 Soundtank catalog as a discrete catalog generation. Secondary historical sources add useful production-state evidence:

- **CR5 Crunchy Rhythm:** documented as an early Soundtank drive model, with evidence for a metal-enclosure corridor around 1989-1991.
- **TM5 Thrashmetal:** documented as an original Soundtank dirt model, with early metal-enclosure production and later plastic Soundtank production. Do not collapse these into one specimen state.
- **SF5 60's Fuzz:** documented as an early metal-enclosure Soundtank fuzz, with later **FZ5 60's Fuzz** naming/relabel states. Preserve SF5 and FZ5 as searchable production-state identities linked to one family, rather than treating them as unrelated designs.

The SF5/FZ5 relationship is especially useful to the archive because it demonstrates how a product can change enclosure, labeling, and retail designation without becoming a wholly separate historical family.

## OEM / attribution rule
Do not automatically assign the Soundtank drive and fuzz products to Nisshin Onpa/Maxon merely because earlier Ibanez dirt products in the 8-series and 9-series corridor are tied to Maxon design/manufacturing history. One source explicitly states that Soundtank pedals were not designed or manufactured by Maxon, while other historical sources document Maxon/Nisshin Onpa's earlier Ibanez relationships. The archive therefore keeps the marketed Ibanez identity and the physical manufacturer/OEM relationship unresolved until product-specific evidence supports attribution.

## Current ledger implications
The main `builder-catalog-ibanez-maxon-02.tsv` remains the working catalog. This audit adds evidence and chronology for the 1985 Master/L, 1986-1989 Power Series, and 1989+ Soundtank transitions and identifies the following next research targets:

1. Page-level extraction from the 1984 Sound Effect and 1985 Master/L catalogs.
2. Page-level enumeration of every dirt model in the 1986 Compact Effect Power Series and 1987 Power Series catalogs.
3. Page-level enumeration of the 1989 Soundtank and 1990 Sound Effects catalogs.
4. Product-specific manufacturer/OEM reconciliation for Soundtank models before assigning Maxon/Nisshin Onpa attribution.
5. Reconciliation of 7-series and 10-series dirt models against the 1990-1994 Ibanez catalog archive.
