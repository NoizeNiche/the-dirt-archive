# 2008-2013 Microbuilder Audit 08

## Scope

This pass targets boutique and microbuilder activity documented during 2008-2013. The emphasis is catalog breadth, identity chronology, and evidence-state discipline. It does not publish schematics, PCB layouts, BOMs, or reconstruction instructions.

## Noisemaker Industries

The builder's own historical interview provides one of the strongest surviving microbuilder timelines. First prototype pedals and amps are placed in June 2008, the first batch was sold in December 2008, the company name was adopted in January 2009, and a full ordering website launched in January 2010. A first dealer was added in February 2011, with the custom shop formally appearing in winter 2011/2012. Early dirt designs include Bad Mother Fuzzer and Soviet Overdrive, followed by A-OK Boost and Sludgemaker. The 2012 launch of 3.14 and Four Horsemen is explicitly stated by the builder. Effects Database indexes 21 products in the Noisemaker catalog. These dates should be treated as prototype, first-batch, brand, storefront, dealer, and custom-shop milestones rather than one generic start date.

## Infanem

Infanem's Analog Harmony Synthesizer is an important hybrid record because its fuzz stage is integral to a larger analog PLL harmony system. The builder description calls it an entirely new circuit made in Portland, with design, PCB etching, and silkscreening done in-house. Driving Notion is documented in September 2011 and uses parallel clean and heavily distorted paths. There's No Future In Fuzz enters the documentary trail in February 2012. The family should therefore remain split between conventional fuzz/OD records and hybrid synth/fuzz records, avoiding an artificially broad fuzz-only count.

## Yellowcake

FXDB's February 2012 intake establishes Yellowcake as a documented builder with three early dirt records: Black Box, Furry Burrito Fuzz, and Il Brutto. Il Brutto is unusually valuable for specimen research because the surviving record explicitly separates v1 and v2. V1 is a germanium fuzz; the later v2 changes the gain architecture and adds separate transistor/gain controls and bias control. The aluminum enclosure is described as individually etched, so enclosure finish variation should not automatically be treated as a generation boundary.

## MI Audio / MI Effects

MI Audio has a substantial earlier dirt catalog and therefore belongs in the builder completion queue, not as a single 2012 discovery. The surviving index includes Blue Boy, Blue Boy Deluxe, Blues Pro Overdrive, Classic Distortion, Crunch Box, Super Crunch Box, G.I. Fuzz, Neo Fuzz, Tube Zone Overdrive and later MI Effects products. Cross Over Drive is particularly strong evidence: it is documented as a prototype in 2011, described in January 2013 as a FET overdrive moving through boost, overdrive, distortion and fuzz ranges, and then identified in June/October 2013 as a limited run of 250 units because of discontinued FETs. Prototype, development, shipment, and limited-production states should remain distinct.

## Cosmic Sound FX

The February 2012 FXDB intake documents three dirt products in a compact cluster: The Fuzz Machine, The Ladyland Fuzz, and The MkII. The available evidence is sufficient for discovery and catalog mapping but not yet for a canonical physical-manufacturer attribution or a lifetime catalog-complete claim. The MkII should remain a distinct marketed product identity while any relationship to historical Tone Bender topology is treated as lineage/context rather than manufacturer equivalence.

## Cross-builder audit observations

1. Noisemaker provides an unusually clear example of the transition from hobbyist prototype work to named company, web storefront, retail dealer network, and custom-shop operation.
2. Infanem demonstrates why hybrid dirt products must be retained without inflating conventional fuzz totals.
3. Yellowcake demonstrates the importance of generation and version notes even when the surviving evidence is mostly product-page documentation.
4. MI Audio / MI Effects demonstrates a broader identity problem: brand nomenclature and physical manufacturer history should be kept separate until supported, while individual product states such as Cross Over Drive's prototype and 250-unit production run are explicitly separated.
5. Cosmic Sound FX remains a legitimate discovery target, but geography and physical production identity require further documentary work.
