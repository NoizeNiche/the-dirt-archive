# Sola Sound / Colorsound: 1976-1979 Transition Audit

## Scope
This audit closes another section of the Sola Sound / Colorsound dirt catalog by focusing on the late-1970s transition period. It distinguishes documented retail names, overlapping product families, enclosure changes, and OEM/rebrand relationships without treating enclosure appearance as sufficient proof of identity.

## Strong historical anchors

### Sola Sound Tone-Bender Fuzz / late Mk IV corridor
- A 1976 Sola Sound Tone-Bender Fuzz specimen is documented with a July 1976 date stamp and the familiar Volume / Tone / Fuzz control layout.
- Contemporary historical accounts describe the Tone-Bender Fuzz as the late form of the Mk IV family, with the Mk IV/Tone-Bender Fuzz lineage continuing into the mid-1970s before being discontinued around 1976.
- Surviving 1970s examples show that the family changed construction and electronic implementation over time. The archive therefore treats `Mk IV`, `Tone-Bender Fuzz`, and later collector terminology as related identifiers rather than automatically equivalent SKU names.

### Supa Tone Bender
- A dated 26 July 1976 example is documented as a Supa Tone Bender in the wide case shared with the Jumbo-format family.
- This is a separate retail family from the late Tone-Bender Fuzz even though both are Colorsound/Sola Sound products and can occupy historically similar-looking enclosures.
- Period/history sources place the Supa Tone Bender in the early-to-mid-1970s Colorsound expansion and associate its lineage with the Big Muff era.

### Jumbo Tone Bender
- The Jumbo Tone Bender is a distinct Colorsound/Sola Sound retail identity.
- Historical sources document the Jumbo as appearing around 1975-76 and note that Sola Sound supplied the same broad design corridor under multiple brand names and enclosures.
- The archive keeps `Jumbo Tone Bender` separate from `Supa Tone Bender`, while retaining a family/relationship link where evidence supports it.

## Late-1970s identification rule
A pedal should not be assigned to a generation solely from:
- case width,
- graphic color,
- knob count,
- the presence or absence of a Colorsound logo,
- or a collector nickname such as "Batman".

Those characteristics can be useful specimen evidence, but period examples demonstrate that Sola Sound reused enclosures and graphics across overlapping production states and OEM runs.

## OEM / alternate-brand corridor
The late Tone-Bender/Jumbo-era historical record includes Sola Sound manufacturing for other marketed names. Sources identify examples such as:
- Park Fuzz Sound
- Carlsbro Fuzz
- CSL Super Fuzz
- B&M / Champion Fuzz variants
- CMI Fuzz Unit
- G.B. Fuzz / G.B. Fuzz Unit
- Pro Traffic Fuzz Unit
- Eurotec Black Box Fuzz Module

These entries should be represented as marketed products/specimens with manufacturing relationships to Sola Sound where evidence is sufficient. They are not to be counted as independent factories by default.

## Colorsound Overdriver / Power Boost corridor
Historical sources support a Power Boost to Overdriver naming/development relationship:
- Colorsound Power Boost is the earlier family name.
- Colorsound Overdriver appears as the later retail identity, with a documented 1970s evolution.
- Some later examples include a master-volume control, but the original 1970s examples did not universally have that control.

The archive therefore records control-layout changes as production-state/specimen evidence rather than using the master-volume presence as a family identifier.

## Catalog / ad-era product universe
Period advertising and historical catalogs show that Colorsound's 1970s range extended well beyond the famous Tone Bender products. The late-period universe includes dirt and dirt-adjacent names such as:
- Tone-Bender Fuzz
- Supa Tone Bender
- Jumbo Tone Bender
- Overdriver
- Wah-Fuzz / Wow Fuzz
- Wah-Fuzz-Swell
- Octivider
- Fuzz Phazer
- Sustain

The archive should retain dirt-adjacent products when the period source markets them as fuzz/distortion/overdrive or when they are relevant to identifying a hybrid dirt family, but should not collapse modulation-only products into the core dirt count.

## Evidence grading
- **Verified-period anchor:** dated surviving specimen or period advertisement/catalog with identifiable model name.
- **Strong historical relationship:** multiple independent historical sources agree on product family or OEM relationship.
- **Candidate:** plausible model or production relationship that requires a primary-period source before public promotion.
- **Unresolved:** conflicting dates, branding, or manufacturing attribution.

## Research conclusions
1. The late-1970s Sola Sound catalog cannot be represented as a simple linear Tone-Bender Mk I-II-III-IV sequence.
2. Late examples show overlapping enclosures and evolving internal production states, so the archive must preserve specimen evidence independently of family identity.
3. Supa Tone Bender and Jumbo Tone Bender remain separate retail families.
4. OEM/alternate-brand products are part of the Dirt Archive's history but should inherit a manufacturer relationship only where the evidence supports it.
5. Colorsound's 1970s dirt catalog was broader than the iconic Tone Bender line; overlooked hybrid and dirt-adjacent models deserve separate records.
