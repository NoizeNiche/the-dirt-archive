# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 21: East German distortion and late British sustain lineage

## Scope
This batch audits overlooked European entities surfaced by the historical fuzz census. It includes one genuine historical distortion lineage from East Germany, plus late-1970s British sustain/compression products that belong in the effect-builder genealogy but should not inflate the core fuzz/OD/distortion count.

## VEB Musikelektronik Klingenthal / Böhm

**Identity:** East German state electronics/manufacturing lineage associated with musical equipment in Klingenthal.

**Dirt catalog signal:** Böhm Trickverzerrer, also referred to as the Trickverzerrer or Verzerrer.

**Evidence:** Current historical references describe the Trickverzerrer as an original East German distortion/fuzz product from the 1960s-1970s. Fredric Effects' historical product page independently identifies the original Böhm Trickverzerrer and describes its primitive stage-oriented design, while Guitar.com places the original in the 1970s East German ecosystem and calls it East Germany's only distortion effect. RockBoard/PedalPedia attributes the original production to VEB Musikelektronik in Klingenthal, although the surviving public documentation around the exact corporate relationship is thinner than for West German builders.

**Archive treatment:** Add Böhm Trickverzerrer as a genuine East German historical dirt product and add VEB Musikelektronik Klingenthal as a manufacturer candidate. Do not treat the modern Fredric Effects Verzerrer as a historical production continuation. Exact original production years and the precise Böhm/VEB corporate naming chain remain open.

## Orange / late British Sustain boundary

**Identity:** Orange marketed/manufacturer lineage, separate from the later modern recreation.

**Catalog signal:** Vintage Orange Sustain.

**Evidence:** ToneHome documents an original Orange Sustain from the 1970s and dates it broadly to that decade. Guitar Interactive and Orange's current site explain that the modern Vintage Series Sustain is a redesigned revival of the 1970s product, with modern electronic and power changes. This product is fundamentally a compressor/sustainer rather than a fuzz or overdrive, so it belongs in the archive as a scope boundary and historical-builder relationship rather than central dirt count.

**Archive treatment:** Preserve vintage Orange Sustain as a boundary record and distinguish the modern 2022 revival from the original 1970s product.

## Dharma Sound

**Identity:** English marketed brand, physical manufacture/design lineage unresolved.

**Catalog signal:** Dharma Sound Sustainer, 1974.

**Evidence:** ToneHome documents a Dharma Sound Sustainer made in England in 1974 and states that it is closely related to Orange Sustain and Futuristic Sounds Sustain. ToneHome's wording explicitly marks the designer relationship as uncertain, describing the engineers as potentially related to Colorsound/Sola Sound.

**Archive treatment:** Add Dharma Sound as a historical British candidate/boundary entity. Preserve the Orange/Futuristic Sounds relationship as evidence-qualified rather than asserting common manufacture without stronger documentation.

## Futuristic Sounds

**Identity:** English marketed brand.

**Catalog signal:** Futuristic Sounds Sustain, 1977.

**Evidence:** ToneHome documents a 1977 English Sustain pedal and says it is identical to the Dharma Sound and Orange variants at the product-family level, while explicitly retaining uncertainty about the engineering/manufacturing relationship.

**Archive treatment:** Keep Futuristic Sounds as a separate marketed-brand candidate/boundary. Do not merge it into Sola Sound or Orange at the builder level merely from product similarity.

## Schaller cross-check

The European evidence also reinforces an existing Schaller correction. ToneHome documents a 1972 Schaller Fuzz-Sustain specimen and separately documents the 1968-1970 Schaller Universal-Fußpedal / Double Pedal with integrated fuzz, treble/bass boost and tremolo. These should remain distinct catalog products and physical-state families even though both belong to Schaller's broader gain/effect ecosystem.

## Evidence principles reinforced by this batch

1. East Germany should not disappear from the historical builder map simply because surviving products are rare.
2. Modern recreations are useful historical evidence but are not proof of uninterrupted manufacturing continuity.
3. Sustain/compressor products should remain visible as boundary records when they materially document a builder's historical gain/effects catalog, but should not inflate fuzz/OD/distortion counts.
4. Similar product families across Orange, Dharma Sound and Futuristic Sounds are relationship evidence, not automatic proof of a shared physical factory.
5. Schaller's combined-effect products deserve separate retail model records rather than being collapsed into its standalone Fuzz.
