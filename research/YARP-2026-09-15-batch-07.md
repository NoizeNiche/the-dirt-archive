# YARP 2026-09-15 — British/European + Japanese OEM bridge batch 07

## Purpose

Continue the breadcrumb's active breadth-first work. This batch expands builders and marketed/OEM relationships adjacent to the Japanese FY-6/Super-Fuzz web while also filling historically important British dirt builders. It remains catalog-level research only.

## 1. FY-6 / Super-Fuzz export web: stronger model-specific relationship evidence

### Univox / Unicord
The Univox Super-Fuzz should remain a marketed/export identity, not a duplicate Japanese builder. Historical material places the Super-Fuzz in the Honey → Shin-Ei lineage, with Unicord responsible for the U.S. import/distribution side. Early surviving units include the grey stamped-metal form before the later die-cast enclosure.

Source trail:
- Shin-ei: https://www.shin-ei.com/
- Univox history summary: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Univox_Super-Fuzz
- Whotabs: https://www.thewho.net/whotabs/gear/guitar/univoxsuperfuzz.html

### LRE / Tempo / Crown / J.H. Experience / Apollo
The surviving historical trail supports treating these as distinct marketed/export identities around the Super-Fuzz family rather than separate physical manufacturers. Tone Machines Blog's 2026 research specifically documents early Honey OEM relationships with Univox and LRE, followed by Tempo and Crown, then Apollo Deluxe Fuzz Tone Expander and J.H. Experience in later export markets.

Source trail:

- Effects Database, Crown Super Fuzz: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/crown/superfuzz

### Shaftesbury / Rose-Morris
Shaftesbury Duo Fuzz remains a UK-marketed relationship node. The current evidence is especially useful because it distinguishes early Honey-era examples from later Shin-ei manufacture. Tone Machines Blog reports early Duo Fuzz examples with the Honey-era on/off Expander pot and small Made in Japan sticker, while later Shin-ei examples use the revised embossed/flat enclosure sequence. Rose-Morris remains the distributor/brand owner context; Shin-ei is the documented Japanese manufacturing relationship for later production.

Source trail:

### Teisco / Guyatone / Elk Wau-Fuzz OEM cluster
The Teisco Wau Wau Fuzz should remain a marketed/OEM record with evidence-qualified factory attribution. Effects Database identifies it as an early-to-mid-1970s Japanese OEM fuzz-wah likely related to the Shin-Ei line and explicitly groups it with Ibanez Wau Wau Fuzz, Guyatone FS-5, Elk Wau Fuzz and other rebranded versions. Because "likely" is not proof, the archive should not convert this into unconditional Shin-Ei manufacture.

## 2. JEN Elettronica — catalog completion bridge

The JEN layer should be expanded beyond Double Sound and Jumbo Fuzz. The surviving manufacturer story supports JEN as an Italian physical/OEM entity formed around Ennio Uncini and Joe Benaron, with later export/marketing relationships to Vox, Dallas Arbiter and Sam Ash.

Priority dirt records for the next ledger pass:
- Double Sound / Vox Double Sound
- Jumbo Fuzz / Dallas Arbiter Jumbo Fuzz / Sam Ash Fuzzola II relationship
- Stereo Fuzz Wah
- Cry Baby Super / Mister Cry Baby Super where the fuzz or distortion function materially qualifies as dirt-hybrid
- other JEN-branded fuzz/distortion states visible in period catalogs

## 3. WEM — foundational British catalog expansion

WEM deserves a dedicated canonical builder record and a dirt catalog larger than Project V alone.

### Confirmed / high-confidence dirt baseline
- Rush Pep Box — c. 1965-1967; early WEM commercial fuzz.
- Project V — late-1960s sophisticated silicon fuzz; early surviving examples have 1968 component dates, while surviving advertisements begin in 1969. Do not turn a component date into a guaranteed launch date.
- Fifth Man guitar's Project IV effect — keep as a related/boundary antecedent rather than automatically counting it as a standalone pedal.

### Project V production-state evidence
Fuzzboxes documents serial/construction sequence evidence that separates early Project V examples from later units: obsolete Pep Box-style battery hatch, circular knobs, later pointer knobs, changes in bottom plate, jack labeling, and internal construction. These are useful future specimen-identity fields, not reasons to split the builder or retail model into different products.

Source trail:
- Fuzzboxes: https://fuzzboxes.org/projectv

## 4. Rotosound — catalog + OEM relationship

Rotosound should be retained as a marketed British builder/brand with a documented Sola Sound manufacturing relationship.

### Dirt catalog baseline
- Rotosound Fuzz / Tone Bender-derived fuzz, including the later-known MKIII-associated production.
- Growler — hybrid fuzz + wah product, historically distinct from the standard Fuzz and worth a separate hybrid record.

Rotosound's own history says its fuzz pedals were built by Denmark Street neighbor Sola Sound and went through multiple circuits/enclosures. The current RFB1 reissue is explicitly described as a reproduction of the original limited 1960s Rotosound fuzz and uses modern components, so the reissue should remain a separate modern production state.

Source trail:

- Rotosound historical page: https://www.rotosound.com/blog/cool_timeline/2660/
- Premier Guitar historical review: https://www.premierguitar.com/gear/rotosound-fuzz-pedal-review

## 5. Park — Marshall-marketed / Sola Sound-built dirt

Park Fuzz Sound should be represented as a marketed brand within the Marshall/Park relationship rather than as an unrelated manufacturer. Surviving examples span approximately the late 1960s to mid-1970s, and Fuzzboxes documents a Sola Sound build relationship. The surviving MKIII/MKIV-family units are especially useful for later production-state research.

Source trail:
- Fuzzboxes: https://fuzzboxes.org/parkfuzzsound
- Guitar Center Tone Bender history: https://www.guitarcenter.com/riffs/gear-tips/amplifiers--effects/history-tone-bender

## 6. Selmer (UK) — two-stage fuzz-wah catalog

Selmer's Fuzz-Wah is important because the company itself provides evidence of a transition from a UK-designed/produced first version to later Far-East-imported production.

Catalog records:
- Selmer Fuzz-Wah first version, c. 1968-1971.
- Later Fuzz-Wah production state from c. 1971 onward, with factory attribution requiring model-specific evidence.

This is a strong example of why the archive must keep one retail model linked across manufacturing transitions instead of creating a false second builder.

Source trail:
- Vintage Hofner historical page: https://www.vintagehofner.co.uk/gallery/gallery3/wah.html

## 7. John Hornby Skewes / Zonk — foundational Leeds fuzz

John Hornby Skewes should remain a canonical UK builder/marketing entity with a dedicated fuzz catalog. The Zonk Machine MKI is a primary dirt anchor for the 1965-1966 period. It is historically adjacent to the Tone Bender story but should not be merged into Sola Sound merely because the designs share ancestry.

Source trail:
- British Pedal Company historical manual: https://www.britishpedalcompany.com/downloads/BPC-Product-Manual-2021-V1.pdf

## Decisions carried forward

1. FY-6 export labels remain searchable marketed/OEM identities, not duplicate manufacturers.
2. Honey-era and Shin-ei-era production are distinct historical states within related product lineages.
3. "Likely OEM" remains an evidence-qualified relationship, not canonical factory attribution.
4. Reissues and revivals are separate production states from original historical products.
5. Hybrid fuzz/wah products remain cataloged but clearly marked as hybrids so they do not inflate the core fuzz-only count.
6. Component date codes are supporting dating evidence, not standalone proof of first commercial release.
