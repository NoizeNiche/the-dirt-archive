# Early Foundational Handoff 93

## Pass 45: 2007-2010 artisan scaling, internet-born fuzz builders, and digital-era scope boundaries

Pass 45 pushes the archive across the 2008-2010 threshold, where tiny basement shops begin to scale, online communities become part of product development, and digital modules appear alongside analog boutique dirt.

### Newly documented or clarified

- **EarthQuaker Devices Hoof exact first-sale anchor**: Effects Database reproduces Jamie Stillman's statement that the first production Hoof was sold on March 18, 2007 via eBay. This is a stronger specimen-facing anchor than a generic 2007 date.
- **EarthQuaker Devices Hoof basement production state**: manufacturer history states that the earliest Hoof and companion pedals were built entirely by hand in Stillman's West Akron basement before the workshop scaled.
- **Death By Audio Fuzz War**: March 2008 forum evidence is retained as public-production/preorder evidence, not as an invented prototype date.
- **Black Arts Toneworks Pharaoh**: builder-history and importer material now preserve the progression from one repaired Black Russian Muff, to a few online-requested units, to progressively larger small batches under the Pharaoh name.
- **Blackout Effectors Musket Fuzz**: June 23, 2008 publication provides a clean public-era anchor; early Red Rifles graphics are retained as specimen evidence rather than treated as a circuit revision.
- **Spaceman Effects**: 2009 founding is retained as an artisan-builder origin boundary; no first dirt product date is invented without product-level evidence.
- **Red Panda Bitcrusher ToneCore**: 2009 first product is retained as a digital-distortion scope boundary. The 2009-2011 module generation is separately documented from later standalone pedals.
- **Caroline Guitar Company Wave Cannon**: October 2010 first sales and first 75 hand-built numbered units provide one of the cleanest tiny-batch public-launch clocks in the early 2010s bridge.
- **Fuzzrocious Grey Stache**: Ryan Ratajski's timeline connects the first 2008 kit-built experiments to roughly 50 friend/band builds and then to the Grey Stache after permission to use a BMP-derived building block.

### Key research findings

1. **2007-2010 is the point where production method becomes catalog history.** EQD, Fuzzrocious and Black Arts show tiny-batch construction, hand-finished graphics and online demand shaping recognizable products.
2. **Online evidence has different strengths.** Forums and period databases can establish public circulation or publication dates, while manufacturer histories remain stronger for first-sale and production clocks.
3. **A graphic can be an identification clue without being an electrical-generation marker.** Musket Red Rifles artwork and early hand-painted Fuzzrocious examples help identify families but do not, by themselves, prove circuit changes.
4. **Digital products require a clear scope boundary.** Red Panda's ToneCore Bitcrusher belongs in the builder chronology because it is historically relevant to distortion culture, but it should not inflate the central analog fuzz/overdrive/distortion count.
5. **The boutique ecosystem is now visibly international and online-native.** Ohio, Brooklyn, Detroit, New Jersey and Portland sit beside the Canadian, Japanese, Scandinavian and European nodes already mapped.
6. **Exact first-sale events are disproportionately valuable.** The March 18, 2007 Hoof sale and October 2010 Wave Cannon first-sales period provide documentary anchors that are stronger than marketplace guesses.

### Evidence discipline

- Manufacturer and builder histories remain preferred for launch and production clocks.
- Period forum and database records are explicitly labeled as public-circulation or publication evidence where appropriate.
- Graphics, enclosure format, numbering and hand-finish are used as identification clues only when they are documented.
- Company founding, first product, first sale and later scaling events remain separate historical layers.
- Scope-bound digital modules are excluded from the central dirt count when their primary identity is outside analog fuzz/overdrive/distortion.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries or cloning instructions are added.

### Current lineage position

The archive now has a continuous bridge from the 1990s boutique pioneers through 2010: the first boutique generation gave way to internet-shaped micro-manufacturers, artisan workshops and specialized digital builders. EarthQuaker and Fuzzrocious demonstrate basement/DIY scaling; Black Arts and Blackout Effectors show online-requested fuzz becoming product families; Spaceman represents the artisan-limited model; Red Panda establishes a parallel digital path; and Caroline closes the decade with a precisely documented first-production batch.
