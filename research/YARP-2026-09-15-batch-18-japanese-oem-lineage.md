# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 18 — Japanese OEM lineage and public edge stabilization

## Purpose
Continue the builder-first archive while stress-testing the Japanese OEM graph before runtime integration.

## Findings

### Honey / Shin-Ei / Univox
Shin-Ei's own historical material supports treating the Honey FY-6 and later Shin-Ei FY-6 as sequential historical identities rather than unrelated products. The Univox Super-Fuzz remains a separate marketed/import identity inside the same documented lineage.

### JEN / Vox / Elka / Nova / Unicord
Specialist historical indexing consistently groups the Italian JEN Fuzz, JEN Tone Bender, Vox V828/V8281, Elka Fuzz, Nova Tone Bender and Unicord Fuzz within one Italian JEN lineage. The archive therefore promotes family-level OEM/marketing edges while retaining each retail identity as a separate searchable object.

### Teisco / Ibanez Standard Fuzz / Antoria / Bruno
The Fuzz Machine family is strongly linked across multiple marketed names, including Ibanez Standard Fuzz No.59 and Antoria/Bruno variants. However, current specialist records disagree on the exact allocation of factory responsibility between Teisco, Shin-Ei and OEM affiliates. These remain HOLD_FOR_REVIEW and are not promoted as universal manufacturer facts.

### SoundTank / Maxon
Ibanez's official catalog archive preserves the 1989 and 1991 SoundTank catalogs. Maxon's official corporate history states that Maxon designed and manufactured many Ibanez products, including SoundTank, while specialist historical sources dispute blanket attribution. The archive keeps SoundTank manufacturer attribution model-specific until the historical exceptions can be reconciled.
