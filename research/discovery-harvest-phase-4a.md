# The Dirt Archive — Discovery Harvest Phase 4A

This pass expands the discovery universe using current Effects Database builder pages and related model pages. The purpose is preservation and catalog growth, not blind publication. EDB is treated as a discovery backbone; individual records should later be normalized and, where practical, corroborated.

## 1. High-value small / independent builders

### BMF Effects
Effects Database lists 17 products and identifies BMF Effects as run by Scott Kiraly in Hermosa Beach, California. Kiraly's timeline gives September 17, 2005 as the first commercial pedal sold, April 2006 as the first distributor, and 2007–08 artist exposure milestones. Dirt-relevant discovery records include:
- Aries Fuzz
- Fat Bastard
- Ge Spot F2
- Ge Spot MkI
- Holy Balls
- Purple Nurple
- Rocket 88 Fuzz
- Sisyphuzz

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/bmf
Status: builder chronology = documented; individual model histories = discovery-stage.

### G.S. Wyllie / Glenn S. Wyllie
Effects Database documents G.S. Wyllie as a one-person North Carolina workshop and records ten products. Particularly valuable historical records include Fuzzmite, Moonrock, Rising Sun, Wylo, Wylo Delux, X-Fuzz and Xylo. EDB's biography says production was small-scale and often made-to-order, and that Wyllie both fabricated/finished enclosures and built the pedals. The Rising Sun is identified as Shin-ei Superfuzz-derived; Wylo as Tone Bender-related; Moonrock emerged from an enclosure-casting accident and became a named model.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/gswyllie
Status: strong small-builder preservation candidate.

### Heavy Electronics
Effects Database identifies Sayer Payne as the builder and records a chronology beginning with pedal making around 2003/04, company formation in July 2007, and later expansion. Dirt-relevant records include Grind Fuzz and El Oso bass distortion, plus gain/drive products such as Hwy-77, Push and Sonic Oppressor that may fit the broader dirt archive after normalization.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/heavy
Status: builder chronology documented; model-level research pending.

### VFE Pedals
Effects Database identifies Peter Rutter as the builder in Puyallup, Washington. Timeline: BYOC discovery in March 2008, heavily modified pedals sold in summer 2008, first circuit board designed May 2009, company founded January 2010, full-time June 2010, relay-switching transition October 2010, retail/silkscreen transition April 2011. Dirt-relevant catalog includes 5:00 Fuzz, Cocktail Fuzz, Fuzz Duo, Killer Rabbit, Killer Rabbit Stereo, Vomit Hound, Woodchipper, Alpha Dog, Dark Horse, Fiery Red Horse, Pale Horse, Red Wolf, The Scream, The Triplet, Triumvirate, White Horse and Yellow Submarine.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/vfe
Status: excellent multi-generation boutique archive target.

### Spiral Electric FX
Effects Database documents a builder history around Tom Cram and lists 14 products. Dirt records include Allora High-Gain Darlington Fuzz, Black Spiral Fuzz, Brute, Demhe Fuzz, Ge Fuzz, Private Reserve Premium Fuzz and Starbird Fuzzstortion. The page also links Cram's prior DOD/DigiTech work, giving a useful builder lineage record.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/spiral
Status: strong modern boutique preservation candidate.

### Electronic Audio Experiments
Effects Database lists 21 products and identifies the Boston company around John Snyder. Dirt-relevant records include Dude Incredible, Eldritch Blast collaboration, 0xEAE Fuzz collaboration, Dagger, Glaive, Halberd, Longsword and related gain products. The Surveyor page documents a V2/V2.5 evolution in 2021–24, showing why production/version history should be modeled independently from model names.

Sources:
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/electronicaudioexperiments
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/electronicaudioexperiments/surveyor
Status: strong builder + variant-history candidate.

## 2. Major boutique catalogs worth harvesting deeply

### Effector 13 / Devi Ever
Effects Database lists 45 products, including Beautiful Disaster, Circuit Breaker, Devine Hammer, Disaster Fuzz, Krackle Fuzz, Moldy Peach, NoFi, Shoe Gazer, Soda Meiser, Stone, Synth Mangler, Tri-Fuzz, Truly Beautiful Disaster, Vintage Fuzz Maker and Vintage Fuzz Master. The category links both Effector 13 (2006) and OohLaLa Manufacturing, so manufacturer/brand transition should be preserved rather than flattened.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/effector13

### Fuzzrocious
Effects Database lists 43 products and describes the company as a small operation created/run by Ryan Ratajski and Shannon, with Ryan designing/building and Shannon doing hand-painted graphics. Dirt-relevant records include 420 Fuzz, Blast Furnace, Cicada Fuzz, Demon King, Feed Me, Green Stache, Grey Stache, Grey Stache Plus and collaborations such as Maggotor and Typhon. EDB chronology states Ryan made his first pedal with his father around 2008 and later built about 50 pedals for friends/bands before formalizing the operation.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/fuzzrocious

### BJF Electronics (BJFE)
Effects Database lists 30 products and identifies Björn Juhl in Stockholm, with company founding in July 1999 and first Baby Blue Overdrive deliveries in December 2000. Dirt-relevant records include Arctic White Fuzz, Bone Bender MkI, Bone Bender MkII, Candy Apple Fuzz, Face 109 Fuzz, Fuzz 68, Pink Purple Fuzz, Snow White Fuzz, Sparkle Face, Dyna Red Distortion and Emerald Green Distortion Machine.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/bjfe

### Empress Effects
Effects Database lists 31 products and states the company began in Ottawa in 2005 with Steve Bragg operating alone for a few years. Dirt records include Fuzz, Germ Drive, Heavy, Heavy Menace, Drive, Distortion and Multidrive. This is a useful example of a one-person origin that later became a larger production operation.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/empress

### Holowon Effects
Effects Database lists 33 products. Dirt-relevant products include Black Distorter, Faygun Fuzz, Furr Fuzz, Fuzzrite Clone, Golden God, Pink Rubber Mad Man, Rubber Magnet, Short Fuse, Static Egg, Sterkte!, Thermal Wave, Tin Man and Velcro Fuzz. Even when some are derivatives or clones, the builder's own product names deserve preservation as distinct catalog records.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/holowon

## 4. Current-product / 2025–2026 discovery caution

Current Effects Database pages are useful for identifying modern products, but current status can change. The archive should snapshot the discovery date and source URL rather than assuming an active listing means permanent availability.

Examples found in this pass:
- Jersey Devil Pedal Company: EDB currently says the company was founded July 2025 and is a small made-to-order operation. The page currently shows three named models in its biography even though its category count is 0, demonstrating that page indexing can lag the narrative text. This is an important reason to preserve discovery strings separately from normalized catalog counts.
- EDB's 2026 NAMM page currently lists new dirt products such as Alameda Guitars Fuzzoo, Collision Devices Snack Fuzz and Crazy Tube Circuits Triptychon.
- EDB's current fuzz and overdrive indexes are large enough that broader discovery import should continue before deep verification.

Sources:
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/jerseydevil
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/events/namm/2026
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/type/fuzz
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/type/overdrive

## 6. High-value reclassification rules from this harvest

- A builder page can contain multiple dirt records plus non-dirt products; don't force every product into the public dirt catalog.
- Designer, builder, brand and manufacturer can be distinct entities.
- Collaborations should be their own relationship type rather than overwriting the primary builder.
- Reissues, replicas and historical originals should remain separate records even when the marketed name is identical or nearly identical.
- A single-person operation is historically valuable even with a tiny catalog.
- A page's stated catalog count can disagree with its narrative text or indexing; discovery snapshots should preserve both without silently reconciling them.
- Modern products can still matter to the historical record, particularly when they document the revival/reinterpretation of older fuzz families.
