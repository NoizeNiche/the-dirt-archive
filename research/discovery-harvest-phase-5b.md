# The Dirt Archive — Discovery Harvest Phase 5B

## Catalog-scale modern and small-builder targets

This phase continues the broad discovery strategy. Product names are retained as archival leads and should not be mistaken for fully researched public records.

Effects Database currently lists 98 products. Its builder history records Josh Scott beginning pedal modification/building as a hobby in 2006, selling pedals and modifications by late 2007, and formally founding JHS Pedals in December 2009. The catalog includes vintage reinterpretations, original designs, collaborations and artist products.

### Catalinbread
Effects Database currently lists 124 products and identifies Nicholas Harris as founder/president, with the company based in Portland, Oregon. Its large catalog should be harvested in a dedicated builder batch, especially the many fuzz, distortion and vintage-reinterpretation records and the Fuzz Flowers special creations.

**Source:** https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/catalinbread

### T-Rex Engineering
Effects Database currently lists 77 products. The company was founded by Lars Dahl and Sebastian Jensen in Denmark, with a timeline beginning in 1996. The catalog should be harvested broadly, but dirt records should be separated from delay, modulation and utility effects.

**Source:** https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/trex

### One Control
Effects Database currently lists 53 products and identifies Tomokaz Kawamura as operator in Iwate, Japan. Timeline details include a March 2010 start, first pedal sales in September 2010, and early development of the Mosquite Blender. The builder deserves a dedicated Japanese modern catalog pass, including BJF collaborations.

**Source:** https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/onecontrol

### Jim Dunlop / Dunlop family
Effects Database currently lists 28 products, grouped across Jim Dunlop, Fuzz Face, Cry Baby, MXR, Way Huge, Hendrix and other branded series. This should be treated as a corporate/brand family rather than a simple 28-pedal builder page because the underlying catalog spans acquired and distributed brands.

**Source:** https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/dunlop

### Royal Co., Ltd.
Effects Database lists 8 products and identifies Royal Co., LTD. as Tokyo-based. Dirt leads include:
- Royal RF-1 Fuzz Box
- Royal RP-2 Fuzz Wah Machine
- Royal RC-1 Effect Machine

The RF-1 page contains a potentially important naming conflict: an RF-1 with TF-1 model number also mentions Thunder Electronic Co., Ltd. Preserve both Royal and possible Thunder relationships without asserting equivalence until corroborated.

**Source:** https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/royal

### Gammatronics
Effects Database lists 2 products and preserves a valuable small-builder history. Angelo Marruzzi reportedly began cloning classic pedals in the early 1980s and released a commercially available Rangemaster clone in 1995, which ran through 2003. The page says he later stopped producing Gammatronics pedals to focus on music. This is a strong one-person historical-preservation target.

Known catalog/history leads include:
- G-Spot Overdrive Preamp
- Octavia
- Fuzzface clones
- Rangemaster clone

Because these are historical clones/recreations, the archive should distinguish them from original vintage designs rather than treating them as new historical circuit families.

**Source:** https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/gammatronics

### Aclam
The Mocker is a high-value modern preservation record because Aclam explicitly documents it as a recreation of the VOX UL730 fuzz circuit, using new-old-stock OA200 diodes and adding bias/tone controls. This should be categorized as a modern historical reinterpretation, not as an original Vox production model.

**Source:** https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/aclam/mocker

### Blackout Effectors

Dirt records include Fix'D Fuzz, Fix'D Fuzz Deluxe, Musket Fuzz, Blunderbuss, Fuzz Bear, FUBÄR, Crystal Dagger, Twosome Dual Fuzz and special Twosome variants. ## OEM and obscure-vintage leads

### Sangil / Iron Butterfly Fuzz Wah
Preserve the Iron Butterfly Fuzz Wah as a rare specimen/provenance record connected to former Mosrite personnel Ed Sanner and Ken Gilstrap. The surviving record indicates a failed broader deal and only a small number of units made/sold. This is precisely the kind of short-run history that a preservation archive should retain.

**Source:** https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/unknown/usa/ironbutterfly

### Guyatone / Japanese Wau-Wau family
The Guyatone FS-5 page provides a dense relationship cluster involving Bruno, Aria Diamond, Electra, Elk, Excetro, Guyatone, Ibanez, Lord, Luxor, Mica, Pax, Selmer and Teisco. Preserve these as distinct branded entries plus a shared family relationship. This is a prime OEM-network research target.

**Source:** https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/guyatone/fs/5

## Research principle reinforced

Large current companies deserve catalog harvesting, but **small/defunct builders remain disproportionately important**. A two-product builder such as Gammatronics or a tiny historical run such as Sangil can have greater preservation value than another mass-market product page. Discovery should therefore measure not only product count but also risk of historical disappearance.

## Next batch priorities

1. Harvest the full dirt scope of JHS, Catalinbread, T-Rex, One Control and other large catalog builders.
2. Continue alphabetic Effects Database country indexes to discover builders missed by fuzz-category browsing.
3. Build a dedicated short-run/defunct-builder queue.
4. Continue Japanese OEM networks and unknown-manufacturer products in parallel.
5. Keep all discovery records separate from publication-ready historical records until names, relationships and dates are normalized.
