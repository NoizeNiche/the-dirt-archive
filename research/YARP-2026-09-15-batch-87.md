# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 87: LIMMCO Wholesale / Private-Label Relationship Node

## Entity identity
Long Island Musical Merchandise Company (LIMMCO) is added as a distribution/wholesale and house-brand entity, not as a physical pedal factory. Specialist historical research identifies owner Bob Seidman as a major connection between multiple 1960s U.S. effects and Japanese import relationships.

## Dirt relationships
- Kimberly Fuzz Box and Volume Expander: LIMMCO house brand. Earliest documented production began in-house at LIMMCO's Long Island warehouse before a third-party manufacturing arrangement was adopted.
- Applied Audio Products relationship: later Kimberly production is attributed to Applied, preserving a manufacturer transition rather than one continuous LIMMCO factory state.
- UMI relationship: later Kimberly/related Buzz Tone production is attributed to United Musical Industries, again retained as a separate manufacturing state.
- Nomad: another LIMMCO house-brand/private-label identity with Applied-made dirt products including Rogue/Banshee states.
- Broader LIMMCO network: historical research connects LIMMCO with Applied, Nomad, Kimberly, UMI, Guild, Goya and early Japanese import activity. These remain separate entities with relationship edges rather than one canonical builder.

## Historical significance
LIMMCO is a crucial structural node for understanding how early U.S. fuzz products moved from small local manufacture to outsourced production and imported/OEM distribution. It also provides a better explanation for why identical or related dirt designs appear under multiple retail names.

## Evidence posture
Distribution and house-brand status are evidence-qualified. Exact dates for every LIMMCO relationship remain a deeper catalog and advertisement audit. No physical manufacturing capability is attributed to LIMMCO beyond the documented initial in-house Kimberly assembly period.
