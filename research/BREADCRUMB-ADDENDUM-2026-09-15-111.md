# YARP Breadcrumb 111

## Purpose
Australian early-effect research pass focused on the boundary between commercial builders, published designs, and kit ecosystems, with a secondary audit of British OEM labels.

## Cross-reference / dedupe decisions

- **Anthony Leo / Electronics Australia / Playtone Fuzzmaster** is preserved as a published-design and kit ecosystem, not promoted to a conventional commercial pedal manufacturer. The 1967 Electronics Australia article and later historical references are strong evidence for the design and kit identity, but not for a factory-built product line.
- **CSL, Park and Carlsbro** remain marketed/OEM identities associated with Sola Sound. No duplicate manufacturers were created.
- **WEM Rush Pep Box** is retained as a licensed/marketed relationship involving Pepe Rush and WEM; roles remain separate from any Sola Sound manufacturing relationship until product-specific evidence is stronger.
- **Tone Bender / Colorsound naming changes** are treated as product-family and generation relationships, not duplicate builders.

## Evidence notes

The Australian Radiotechnical/Electronics Australia index records Anthony Leo's "Fuzz box for electric guitars" article in August 1967; the article was later republished in Popular Electronics in February 1968. Kit Rae's historical timeline identifies a Playtone Fuzzmaster sold in kit form in Australia and ties it to that published design. This makes the Leo/Playtone corridor historically significant while still leaving the exact commercial/retail manufacturing arrangement unresolved.

The same historical timeline documents CSL Super Fuzz, Park Fuzz Sound, and Carlsbro Fuzz as Sola Sound OEM-labeled products, and describes the WEM Rush Pep Box as a licensed WEM product connected to Pepe Rush. These relationships strengthen the archive's manufacturer-versus-marketed-brand model without creating duplicate builder records.
