## PRP research standard

PRP research is expected to match the depth and persistence of the Builder A-Z census. Each pedal gets broad, repeated source coverage and identity reconciliation rather than a single-source lookup.

Research should check, where available:
- manufacturer product pages and current/retired catalogs
- manufacturer manuals, brochures, archived pages, and official statements
- reputable pedal databases and historical catalogs
- interviews and builder statements
- reputable reviews and demos for documented sound/function evidence
- schematics, service documents, PCB/trace evidence, or other technical documentation when pedal-specific evidence exists

Identity handling:
- A distinct pedal gets its own parent record.
- Documented V1/V2/etc. revisions belong inside the relevant parent pedal record unless the builder treats the later product as a separate pedal.
- Builder-made/factory modifications may be documented when verified.
- Post-sale DIY, forum-only, user mods, and aftermarket modifications are excluded from the archive's version/modification history.
- Enclosure colors, knob colors, graphics, limited cosmetic editions, and similar appearance-only differences do not become versions unless the builder documents a meaningful functional or historical distinction.
- A V1 and V2 may share the same enclosure/colorway; version identity is based on documented changes, not appearance.
- Do not infer component types from related models. Component fields require evidence specific to the pedal being researched.

PRP should continue asking project-level questions when an unresolved taxonomy or evidence rule could materially affect the archive, rather than silently making a structural assumption.
