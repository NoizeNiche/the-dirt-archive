# Protocol C: Broad-Spectrum Pedal Discovery and Modern Coverage

## Purpose

Protocol C is the archive's breadth engine. It exists to prevent research from drifting toward only the famous historical pedals or the founding-era canon.

The archive's central dirt scope remains overdrive, distortion, fuzz, bass dirt, and meaningful hybrid gain products. Protocol C treats the full **1960-2026 builder and product universe** as active research, with special emphasis on products introduced, revised, revived, discontinued, or documented from the 1990s through 2026.

The goal is not to replace historical archaeology. The goal is to ensure that historical archaeology never consumes the research queue while thousands of newer and less-famous products remain invisible.

## 1. Breadth has priority over fame

Do not use cultural importance, collector value, price, artist association, or historical fame as the default discovery filter.

A small one-person builder with three documented pedals can be just as important to the census as a major manufacturer with hundreds of products.

The queue must actively seek:
- boutique builders
- micro-builders
- regional builders
- discontinued builders
- short-lived brands
- artist/signature builders
- experimental builders
- small Japanese, European, Australian, Latin American, Canadian and other regional makers
- OEM/private-label brands
- rebrand/importer identities
- limited-run and convention-only dirt products
- modern digital/hybrid dirt products when they materially belong to the dirt story
- current products introduced in 2025-2026

## 2. Modern-era sweep is a dedicated research track

Run a standing modern sweep across:
- 1990-1999
- 2000-2004
- 2005-2009
- 2010-2014
- 2015-2019
- 2020-2022
- 2023-2024
- 2025-2026

Do not wait for the vintage queue to finish before advancing these corridors.

Each corridor should be searched by region and by builder size. In particular, repeatedly search for companies that appeared only on retailer pages, forums, eBay, Reverb, NAMM/Musikmesse coverage, YouTube demos, or social channels before establishing an independent storefront.

## 3. Discovery-source ladder

Use the following source ladder for breadth discovery.

### Tier A: broad indexes and first-party catalogs
1. Effects Database
2. manufacturer/builder official sites
3. official catalogs, manuals, dealer sheets and archived product pages
4. Internet Archive / Wayback captures of builder sites and storefronts
5. manufacturer social accounts when they document releases or discontinuations

Effects Database is a principal discovery index because its stated project goal expanded from small effect lists to an all-pedal overview and specifically emphasizes small builders and historical brands.

### Tier B: structured modern gear databases
Use these as discovery and cross-check sources, not automatic historical truth:
- Equipboard
- Reverb product/category pages
- retailer catalog archives
- dealer databases
- used-gear aggregators

Modern database pages can expose product aliases, discontinued models, product-family relationships, photographs, release-era clues, and builder names that are otherwise difficult to discover.

### Tier C: specialist journalism and historical coverage
Search:
- Premier Guitar
- Guitar World
- Guitar Player
- MusicRadar
- Tone Report and successor archives
- Vintage Guitar
- Guitar.com
- Music instrument trade coverage
- NAMM and Musikmesse reporting
- builder interviews
- specialist pedal websites and collector archives

### Tier D: community and collector evidence
Search broadly through:
- The Gear Page
- TalkBass
- TDPRI
- Seymour Duncan forums
- Freestompboxes
- Reddit communities
- Facebook groups and public Facebook posts where searchable
- archived forums
- collector databases
- YouTube comments and demo descriptions
- Reverb seller/collector listings
- eBay listings and completed-sale references when they preserve photographs or dated documentation

Community sources are primarily **discovery evidence** unless the source itself preserves a first-party document or directly identifies a documented specimen with provenance.

Do not assume that an unindexed Facebook group does not exist merely because search engines cannot surface it. Record the search limitation rather than asserting absence.

## 4. Search strategy for every builder

For each builder encountered, perform a catalog sweep before deep archaeology.

Search combinations should include:
- builder name
- builder aliases
- builder + overdrive
- builder + distortion
- builder + fuzz
- builder + discontinued
- builder + old models
- builder + catalog
- builder + manual
- builder + archive
- builder + Reverb
- builder + Effects Database
- builder + forum
- builder + NAMM
- builder + signature
- builder + V1 / V2 / V3 / V4
- builder + limited edition
- builder + collaboration
- builder + OEM / made for / distributed by

For modern builders, also search the builder's own domain for old product paths, PDFs, manuals and press releases.

## 5. Product capture standard

Every newly discovered product should initially record, at minimum:
- builder/brand identity
- marketed model name
- aliases
- effect category
- family relationship if known
- earliest known date or date corridor
- latest known date or current/discontinued state
- source trail
- discovery status
- photo availability
- manufacturing/OEM uncertainty

Do not require complete historical certainty before recording a candidate.

Use explicit states such as:
- DISCOVERY
- CANDIDATE
- INDEXED
- VERIFIED
- CURRENT_SNAPSHOT
- DISCONTINUED_SNAPSHOT
- UNRESOLVED

The purpose is to prevent omission without turning weak evidence into fact.

## 6. Modern product variants matter

For newer products, collect variant photography and documentation at the same time as the initial model record.

Capture meaningful differences such as:
- V1/V2/V3 revisions
- enclosure changes
- control-layout changes
- typography/artwork changes
- alternate footswitch arrangements
- standard versus deluxe versions
- standard versus custom-shop versions
- artist/signature editions
- limited editions with meaningful hardware or artwork changes
- manufacturer relocation or OEM change
- discontinued design followed by a materially different revival

Do not create a separate historical model merely for an arbitrary color finish unless the finish is itself a documented edition or meaningful production state.

## 7. Photo-first breadth support

Photo collection is part of discovery, not a later cosmetic task.

For every modern or obscure product found, attempt to capture multiple external photo references when legally and technically practical:
- first-party product photography
- archived first-party photography
- Effects Database image sets
- dealer photography
- Reverb/used-market specimen photography
- collector photography
- forum specimen photography
- historical catalog scans that show the product

Record the original source page, source type, date when known, and model/variant association.

Do not publish images merely because they appeared in search results. Preserve source attribution and use source pages rather than pretending the archive owns third-party photography.

## 8. Breadth queue balancing rule

As a default continuous-run target:
- 50% modern and broad-spectrum builder/product discovery
- 25% catalog-completion and duplicate/OEM reconciliation
- 15% regional and micro-builder discovery
- 10% individual pedal archaeology and variant deep dives

The ratios are guidance, not a rigid accounting requirement. The essential rule is that the research queue must not collapse back into a handful of famous vintage pedals.

## 9. Anti-drift rule

When a historically fascinating pedal appears during a broad sweep, record the lead and its sources, then return to the breadth queue unless its builder/product family is already substantially mapped.

A deep dive is allowed to interrupt breadth only when:
- the source is unusually ephemeral and likely to disappear
- the product is otherwise almost impossible to document
- the finding resolves a major OEM/alias duplication problem
- the evidence materially changes the archive's hierarchy

Otherwise, breadth wins.

## 10. Regional coverage rule

Do not equate the English-language web with the pedal universe.

Modern discovery must periodically sweep:
- United States
- Canada
- United Kingdom
- Ireland
- Japan
- South Korea
- China/Hong Kong/Taiwan
- Australia/New Zealand
- Germany/Austria/Switzerland
- France/Belgium/Netherlands
- Italy
- Spain/Portugal
- Scandinavia
- Poland/Czechia and Central Europe
- Greece/Balkans
- Brazil/Argentina/Chile and Latin America
- Indonesia/Malaysia/Singapore/Philippines
- other regions surfaced by source discovery

Where language barriers exist, record original-language product names and search terms before normalizing them into English catalog fields.

## 11. Modern current-state rule

Current products are moving targets. For products sold in 2025-2026:
- timestamp the source snapshot
- preserve the current official product page
- record whether the product is in production, discontinued, sold out, or unclear
- record announced successor/replacement relationships when documented
- rerun current-product sweeps periodically

A current storefront should never overwrite a historical production state.

## 12. Boundary handling

The Dirt Archive remains centered on dirt.

Boosts, preamps, amp-in-a-box products, EQ/drive hybrids, octave fuzzes, synth/fuzz hybrids, distortion/synth products, and bass dirt can enter the central archive when their dirt role is meaningful.

Purely non-dirt effects can be retained as boundary or relationship records when useful for builder/catalog reconstruction, but they should not inflate the central dirt count.

DSP and digital products are not excluded merely because they are digital. Exclude them only when they fall outside the archive's defined dirt role.

## 13. Source weighting

Use the following working evidence hierarchy:

1. Original manufacturer documents
2. Original catalogs, manuals and dated official releases
3. Archived manufacturer pages
4. Contemporary trade publications and reputable specialist historical sources
5. Effects Database and structured gear databases for indexing/corroboration
6. Dealer and used-market documentation
7. Collector/forum/social documentation
8. Search-result snippets without accessible source context

A lower-tier source can discover a product that a higher-tier source later verifies. Discovery status should not be discarded merely because the first source was weak.

## 14. What Protocol C changes

Protocol C changes the queue from:

**founding pedals -> famous pedals -> historical variants -> modern products if time remains**

to:

**broad 1960-2026 builder/product discovery -> modern-era sweep -> regional/micro-builder sweep -> catalog completion -> reconciliation -> targeted deep archaeology**

The modern archive should therefore grow outward continuously while the vintage layer grows downward in detail.

## 15. Immediate operating target

The next continuous research batches should prioritize builders and products from the 1990s-2026 period that are absent from, or only weakly represented in, `builder-master-census-01.tsv` and the builder completion queues.

The research objective is not a short list of famous boutique brands. It is a large, evidence-qualified universe of identifiable builders and products, including the obscure products that make the archive useful as a reference rather than a hall of famous hits.
