# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 31

## Focus
U.S. distributor/private-label Big Muff OEM records and separation of distributor identity from physical manufacture.

## Findings

### L.D. Heater Music Company / Lyle L600
Kit Rae's Big Muff historical archive identifies the Lyle L600 Distortion Sustainer as an Electro-Harmonix OEM version of the V2 Big Muff, made for L.D. Heater Music Company of Portland, Oregon and distributed under the Lyle brand around 1973-1974. The archive therefore records L.D. Heater as distributor/private-label customer, Lyle as marketed brand, and Electro-Harmonix as physical manufacturer.

### Marveltone / Targ & Dinner Music Company
The same historical source documents a mid-1970s Marveltone Distortion Sustainer as an Electro-Harmonix OEM V2 Big Muff. Marveltone was associated with Targ & Dinner Music Company, a Chicago wholesaler/importer. The archive keeps the wholesaler and marketed brand distinct from Electro-Harmonix manufacturing.

### Wabash Distortion Sustainer / David Wexler & Co.
Kit Rae documents the Wabash Distortion Sustainer as another Electro-Harmonix OEM V2 Big Muff for David Wexler & Co. of Chicago. The archive treats Wabash as a private-label/retail identity, not a manufacturer.

### Census consequence
These records materially expand the searchable 1970s OEM universe without creating false builder entities. They also reinforce the archive's rule that regional distributors, music wholesalers and private labels deserve catalog records when they produced historically distinctive marketed products, but their physical-manufacturer role must remain separate.
