# MJM Guitar FX — Phase One Catalog Audit 01

## Purpose

Deepen the Phase One builder/product inventory without moving into circuit reconstruction. The current MJM baseline contained the major dirt families, but several important version boundaries and historical states were still unresolved.

## Findings

### London Fuzz
The London Fuzz is the clearest anchor product. Current MJM material confirms Germanium and Silicon configurations and a dedicated bias version. Effects Database preserves a useful historical image/index trail with **early V1, V1, V2 and V3: bias knob** states and dates its indexed maker page to the 2003-2008 corridor. Older demo material also identifies the London Fuzz as MJM's flagship since 2000 and documents a 2017 update involving custom colours, an aluminum faceplate, new graphics and aluminum knobs.

Archive treatment: maintain one London Fuzz family with meaningful variant/state records. Do not use enclosure color alone as a generation boundary. Bias capability is a genuine identification feature.

### London Fuzz Classic
Surviving Reverb listings identify a **London Fuzz Classic** as a re-reissue of earlier hand-painted/stickered London Fuzzes. One listing says the Classic returns to the old visual concept with a high-quality paint finish, retains the older transistor family, and adds an internal bias trimpot. This deserves a separate reissue/product-state record rather than being folded into an original London Fuzz generation.

### London II
Audiofanzine's surviving 2013 owner report establishes a two-control London Fuzz II state using a silicon Fuzz Face-type design. The same report notes that a newer version existed with an additional tone control. Exact start/end dates and the relationship to London Fuzz remain unresolved.

Archive treatment: keep London II separate from London Fuzz until primary chronology proves it was simply a revision. Record the 2-control state as historical specimen evidence, not as an assumed universal specification.

### Brit Bender
Effects Database independently preserves the original two-knob Brit Bender and the three-knob version, both published on 2005-09-19. The original is described as using three selected germanium devices; the later three-knob state has a tone control and a changed voicing. The control-count difference is strong identification evidence.

Archive treatment: retain **2-knob original** and **3-knob/V2** as distinct historical states. Do not assign an exact switch-over date without dated catalog or specimen evidence.

### Foxey Fuzz
Audiofanzine has a 2007 owner record describing a three-knob analog fuzz and stating that the enclosure was designed and made in Canada by MJM. Multiple period/community sources identify it as a Big Muff-derived design. The physical exterior is therefore useful to the archive, especially because color/graphic examples survive.

Archive treatment: model remains distinct. Variant chronology is still incomplete.

### China Fuzz
A 2009 dealer review documents two voicings called **Leeds** and **Loose**. A 2010 Audiofanzine owner report documents a two-control/toggle version and explicitly says the newer version did not match the pictured older pedal. The same evidence identifies the design as a Univox Superfuzz reproduction.

Archive treatment: add a visual/version-change note. The Leeds/Loose control behavior is a useful identification clue, but exact production boundaries are not yet established.

### Roctavios
The product is firmly present in catalog indexes, but spelling varies between **Roctavios** and **Roctavious**. This is best handled as a search alias until primary MJM material establishes the preferred historical spelling.

### King Buzz
Reverb's product record places the King Buzz in the 2010s and identifies it as a three-transistor fuzz based on the Burns Buzzaround. Older dealer material describes NOS germanium and positive-ground operation. The model should stay distinct from MJM's other fuzz families.

### Overdrive family: Blues Devil → Phantom → Luxora
The overdrive layer is more complicated than the initial inventory suggested. Audiofanzine contains an owner report for a Blues Devil example dated to 2002 and identifies it as handmade in Canada. Zikinf describes Phantom as discontinued and explicitly groups **Blues Devil, Phantom and Luxora** as three MJM overdrive variants/states. Equipboard independently labels Luxora as the newer option to Phantom.

Archive treatment: preserve these as three searchable retail identities linked by a **documented family/continuation relationship**, while avoiding an exact schematic-level or year-by-year lineage claim. The evidence supports the relationship more strongly than it supports precise generation dates.

## What remains unresolved

1. Exact production corridors for London Fuzz V1/V2/V3 and Classic.
2. Whether London Fuzz Silicon and London II are parallel products or successor/revision states.
3. Exact Brit Bender 2-knob → 3-knob transition date.
4. Foxey Fuzz production span and any documented enclosure/artwork revisions.
5. China Fuzz version chronology, including when the newer enclosure replaced the older pictured form.
6. First/last documented dates for Roctavios, King Buzz and Luxora.
7. Whether additional MJM dirt models exist outside the currently indexed 10 core dirt identities and the Dallas Boost boundary record.

## Evidence hierarchy for this batch

Primary MJM FAQ/product statements are treated as strongest for variant/function facts. Archived or dated maker/demo material is next. Historical database indexes and specialist databases are used for chronology and discovery, with retailer/resale pages used primarily for surviving physical specimen evidence. Community comments are retained as leads, not as automatic canonical facts.

## Editorial boundary

No schematics, PCB layouts, gutshot libraries, complete BOMs, cloning instructions or circuit-reconstruction material were collected or promoted.
