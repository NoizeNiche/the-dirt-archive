# The Dirt Archive — Japanese & OEM Fuzz Research, Phase 1

This research pass maps Japanese fuzz families and OEM/retail relationships that are difficult to identify. It is a research document, not a claim that every model/date is fully verified.

## FY-6 / Super Fuzz family

Shin-ei states that the Honey Co. FY-6 “Baby Crying” effect was released in 1967 and was the first incarnation of the Super Fuzz. Shin-ei says its 1968 FY-6 Super Fuzz followed it. This gives us a strong two-stage family origin: **Honey → Shin-ei**. The archive should preserve that lineage without treating later reproductions as proof of every original-production detail. [Shin-ei]

Effects Database and secondary research show the FY-6 family sold under many labels, including Companion, Univox, Teisco, Mica, Jax, Rands, Aria, Hohner, Kent, Mayfair, Royal, Tempo, Zenta and others. These should be modeled as branded/OEM relationships where evidence supports them, not as unrelated designs. [Effects Database; Coda Effects]

## Honey

### Honey Baby Crying FY-6
- Earliest identified Super-Fuzz-family product.
- Shin-ei dates it to 1967.
- Candidate identification clues: Honey branding, FY-6 control layout and enclosure family.
- Exact production quantity and detailed enclosure sub-generations remain research questions.

**Status:** 1967 origin = Confirmed by current Shin-ei historical statement. Exact production chronology = incomplete.

## Shin-ei / Companion

### Shin-ei FY-6 Super Fuzz
- Shin-ei dates the branded FY-6 to 1968.
- Follows the Honey Baby Crying product.
- Important distinction: FY-6 and FY-2 are separate product families and should not be collapsed.

### Companion FY-6
- Separate branded FY-6-family record.
- Effects Database lists it as a vintage Companion product.

### Companion FY-2
- Separate Shin-ei fuzz family.
- Effects Database links it to numerous later reproductions and boutique descendants.
- Keep FY-2 genealogy separate from FY-6 genealogy.

## Univox / Unicord

### Univox U-1095 Super-Fuzz
Effects Database places U-1095 in the FY-6 family. Secondary research identifies early grey stamped-metal examples and later die-cast Super-Fuzz forms. The archive should capture enclosure changes, branding, importer/retail context and relationship to the Shin-ei/Honey family.

### Univox U-250 Uni-Fuzz
A separate Univox fuzz model in the broader Shin-ei ecosystem. Do not merge it with U-1095 simply because both share a Shin-ei lineage.

## Teisco

### Teisco Fuzz Machine
Effects Database documents an early-1970s Japanese product distributed under multiple names, including:
- Ibanez Standard Fuzz, model 59
- Bruno Fuzz Machine
- Mica Fuzz
- Marlboro Wailer Fuzz
- Antoria Fuzz Machine
- Aria Diamond Fuzz
- other labels

The distinctive dual-slider/twin-switch enclosure is a useful external identifier. This should become a family record with branded aliases rather than many unrelated duplicate records.

### Teisco TF-1 Fuzz
Effects Database identifies TF-1 as a Teisco-branded FY-6 Super Fuzz family product. It should remain a separate model record from the Teisco Fuzz Machine.

### Teisco Wau Wau Fuzz
Effects Database documents a Japanese OEM fuzz-wah family related to Ibanez Wau Wau Fuzz, Guyatone FS-5, Elk Wau Fuzz and other branded forms. This is another example of **retail brand → OEM family → specific model**.

## Ace Tone

### FM-1 Fuzz Master
Effects Database documents a two-control fuzz product. Secondary research places it before FM-2.

### FM-2 Fuzz Master
A 2025 Tone Machines Blog investigation used period ads and catalogs rather than repeating the common 1969–71 range. It reports a first ad appearance in **March 1968** and finds FM-2 present in Ace catalogs through at least **1979**. The researcher reports no meaningful exterior cosmetic change across hundreds of examples, so exact dating may require specimen-level evidence.

**Archive status:** Strong research lead, but production endpoint remains approximate until corroborated.

### FM-3 Fuzz Master
Effects Database documents a later multi-function fuzz/booster product. Treat FM-1, FM-2 and FM-3 as separate models.

## Guyatone

Effects Database's Guyatone index contains multiple dirt products worth research:
- FS-1 Bazz Box
- FS-1 Buzz Box
- FS-2 Buzz Box
- FS-3 Fuzz
- FS-5 Wah-Fuzz
- FS-6 Sustainer
- FZ-2000 Fuzz
- TZ2 The Fuzz
- TZm5 Torrid Fuzz

### FS-3 Fuzz
Effects Database dates FS-3 to roughly 1972–73 and places it between FS-2 and FS-6. It reports a Honey-labeled PCB relationship to Super-Fuzz-family products.

**Archive caution:** useful lead, but OEM/circuit attribution should remain **Probable** until independently corroborated.

### TZ2 The Fuzz
A later Guyatone fuzz based on the Super-Fuzz concept. It is not the same historical product as the 1960s/70s FY-6 OEM family.

### TZm5 Torrid Fuzz
Effects Database credits Japanese engineer Toshi Torii and describes a later three-transistor fuzz with extensive controls. Treat as a later Japanese design influenced by vintage fuzz rather than an FY-6 rebrand.

## JEN / Italian Tone Bender branch

### Jen Fuzz
Effects Database documents a late-1960s Italian Jen Fuzz closely related to the Vox V828/V8281 production family. The same general product was later sold under names including Elka, Luxor, Nova and Unicord.

This belongs under the **Italian/OEM Tone Bender branch**, not the Japanese FY-6 family.

### Vox V828
Secondary Italian historical research suggests early production may have involved Eko and later JEN. That Eko-to-JEN transition should be marked **disputed** until stronger primary evidence is found.

## Archive rules reinforced by this research

1. Brand is not necessarily builder.
2. Model is not necessarily family.
3. Rebrands should be represented relationally where possible.
4. Exact dates may be approximate and should be labeled that way.
5. A meaningful technical difference may be recorded when it helps identification, but the archive does not publish complete circuit recipes, BOMs, PCB diagrams, schematics or gutshot galleries.
6. Modern recreations should be clearly separated from historical OEM products.
