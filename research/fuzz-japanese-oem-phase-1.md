# The Dirt Archive — Japanese & OEM Fuzz Research, Phase 1

This pass maps Japanese fuzz families and OEM/retail relationships that are difficult to identify. It is a research document, not a claim that every model/date is fully verified.

## FY-6 / Super Fuzz family

Shin-ei states that the Honey Co. FY-6 “Baby Crying” effect was released in 1967 and was the first incarnation of the Super Fuzz. Shin-ei says its 1968 FY-6 Super Fuzz followed it. This establishes a strong Honey → Shin-ei family origin, while exact original-production details remain subject to further research.

Effects Database and secondary research show the FY-6 family sold under many labels, including Companion, Univox, Teisco, Mica, Jax, Rands, Aria, Hohner, Kent, Mayfair, Royal, Tempo and Zenta. The archive should model these as branded/OEM relationships where supported, not as unrelated inventions.

## Honey

### Honey Baby Crying FY-6
- Earliest identified Super-Fuzz-family product.
- Shin-ei dates it to 1967.
- Identification clues include Honey branding, FY-6 control layout and enclosure family.
- Exact production quantity and enclosure sub-generations remain open research questions.

**Status:** 1967 origin = Confirmed by Shin-ei's current historical statement. Exact chronology = incomplete.

## Shin-ei / Companion

### Shin-ei FY-6 Super Fuzz
- Shin-ei dates the branded FY-6 to 1968.
- Follows the Honey Baby Crying product.
- FY-6 and FY-2 are separate product families and should not be collapsed.

### Companion FY-6
- Separate branded FY-6-family record.

### Companion FY-2
- Separate Shin-ei fuzz family.
- Effects Database links it to numerous later reproductions and boutique descendants.

## Univox / Unicord

### Univox U-1095 Super-Fuzz
Effects Database places U-1095 in the FY-6 family. Secondary research identifies early grey stamped-metal examples and later die-cast Super-Fuzz forms. The archive should capture enclosure changes, branding, importer/retail context and family relationships.

### Univox U-250 Uni-Fuzz
A separate Univox fuzz model in the broader Shin-ei ecosystem. Keep it separate from U-1095 while recording the family relationship.

## Teisco

### Teisco Fuzz Machine
Effects Database documents an early-1970s Japanese product distributed under multiple names, including Ibanez Standard Fuzz model 59, Bruno Fuzz Machine, Mica Fuzz, Marlboro Wailer Fuzz, Antoria Fuzz Machine and Aria Diamond Fuzz. The distinctive dual-slider/twin-switch enclosure is a useful external identifier.

### Teisco TF-1 Fuzz
Effects Database identifies TF-1 as a Teisco-branded FY-6 Super Fuzz family product. It should remain a separate model record from the Teisco Fuzz Machine.

### Teisco Wau Wau Fuzz
Effects Database documents a Japanese OEM fuzz-wah family related to Ibanez Wau Wau Fuzz, Guyatone FS-5, Elk Wau Fuzz and other branded forms. This is a strong example of the archive's retail brand → OEM family → specific model structure.

## Ace Tone

### FM-1 Fuzz Master
Effects Database documents the FM-1 as a two-control fuzz product. Secondary research places it before FM-2.

### FM-2 Fuzz Master
A 2025 Tone Machines Blog investigation used period ads and catalogs instead of repeating the commonly cited 1969–71 range. It reports a first ad appearance in March 1968 and finds FM-2 present in Ace catalogs through at least 1979. The researcher reports no meaningful exterior cosmetic change across hundreds of examples, meaning exact dating may require specimen-level evidence.

**Archive status:** Strong research lead; production endpoint remains approximate pending corroboration.

### FM-3 Fuzz Master
Effects Database documents a later multi-function fuzz/booster. FM-1, FM-2 and FM-3 should be separate models.

## Guyatone

Important dirt candidates in the Effects Database index include FS-1 Bazz Box, FS-1 Buzz Box, FS-2 Buzz Box, FS-3 Fuzz, FS-5 Wah-Fuzz, FS-6 Sustainer, FZ-2000 Fuzz, TZ2 The Fuzz and TZm5 Torrid Fuzz.

### FS-3 Fuzz
Effects Database dates FS-3 to roughly 1972–73 and reports a Honey-labeled PCB relationship to Super-Fuzz-family products. Treat the OEM attribution as **Probable** until independently corroborated.

### TZ2 The Fuzz
A later Guyatone fuzz based on the Super-Fuzz concept. It is not the same historical product as the 1960s/70s FY-6 OEM family.

### TZm5 Torrid Fuzz
Effects Database credits Japanese engineer Toshi Torii and describes a later three-transistor fuzz with extensive controls. Treat it as a later Japanese design influenced by vintage fuzz rather than an FY-6 rebrand.

## JEN / Italian Tone Bender branch

### Jen Fuzz
Effects Database documents a late-1960s Italian Jen Fuzz closely related to the Vox V828/V8281 production family, with later retail appearances under names including Elka, Luxor, Nova and Unicord.

This belongs under the Italian/OEM Tone Bender branch, not the Japanese FY-6 family.

### Vox V828
Secondary Italian research suggests early production may have involved Eko and later JEN. Mark the Eko-to-JEN transition as **disputed** until stronger primary evidence is found.

## FY-2 family discoveries

Effects Database also links the FY-2 family to later interpretations such as Fredric Effects Unpleasant Companion, General Guitar Gadgets Shin-ei Companion Fuzz Replica, Ghost Effects Shin-Ei Companion Fuzz FY-2, Pigeon FX Companion, SolidGoldFX Sasori, Wattson Classic Electronics Fuzz FY-2 and other modern products. These should be clearly separated from the historical OEM products.

## Database rules reinforced by this phase

1. Brand is not necessarily builder.
2. Model is not necessarily family.
3. Rebrands should be relational when the evidence supports it.
4. Exact dates may be approximate and should be labeled that way.
5. Meaningful component changes may be recorded when useful for identification, but the archive does not publish complete circuit recipes, BOMs, schematics, PCB diagrams or gutshot galleries.
6. Modern reinterpretations and clones should not inherit the historical manufacturer identity merely because a design lineage is related.
