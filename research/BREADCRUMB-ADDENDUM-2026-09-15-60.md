## Historical findings

- New Jersey business-directory data records Clark Music Industries with a January 26, 1968 registration date, giving the company a concrete legal/business anchor during the East Coast Fuzz window.
- A late-1960s trade advertisement places Clark Music Industries, Inc. at 61 Walnut Avenue, Clark, New Jersey 07066 and shows the company advertising its own audio products.
- A 1969 NAMM exhibitor list includes Clark Music Industries, Inc., confirming that the company was active in the U.S. musical-instrument trade by that year.
- A 1968 used-market advertisement establishes that the Clark Fuzz was already in circulation by October 5, 1968.
- Former Clark employee Rich Excellente's testimony remains strong evidence that Clark's operation wired fuzz boxes, but it does not prove Clark designed or populated every circuit sold under every East Coast label.
- The Clark instruction label reading `Manufactured and Distributed by Clark Music Industries` is retained as supporting evidence for the Clark-branded production state, not as proof that Clark built all related Manny's, Orpheum, Andre, Wurlitzer, MGI, AAT, or other variants.
- The anecdotal claim that Clark built cabinets for Ampeg remains HOLD_FOR_REVIEW because no independent period confirmation has yet been found.

## Validation posture

Clark is now supported by multiple independent evidence classes: business existence, period trade presence, period location advertising, product circulation, firsthand employee testimony, and maker/distributor labeling. The archive still keeps the broader East Coast Fuzz family unresolved at the common-factory level.
