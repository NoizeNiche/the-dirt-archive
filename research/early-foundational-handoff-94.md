# Early Foundational Handoff 94

## Pass 46: 2008-2012 small-batch boom, digital-pedal origins, and early internet-era production evidence

Pass 46 pushes the archive across the 2008-2012 threshold where boutique builders increasingly combine hand-built production, direct web sales, forum/community discovery, and new digital approaches. The pass deliberately keeps non-dirt digital products as scope-bound chronology when they explain a builder's identity, while preserving physical production clues for core dirt pedals.

### Newly documented or clarified

- **Black Arts Toneworks Pharaoh**: dated 2010 production posts document chassis work, screen printing, turretboard assembly, and successive batches, giving period evidence for the early production corridor.
- **Black Arts Toneworks Pharaoh 2011 batch marker**: a January 11, 2011 production notice records a sold-out batch and another run entering preparation, showing sustained small-batch production.
- **Spaceman Effects**: manufacturer history dates the company's founding to 2009 and describes the original operation as strictly limited, artisan-level, entirely hand-built production. Exact first dirt-model dating remains open.
- **Spaceman Saturn V**: retained as an early overdrive/boost family record, but deliberately left without a false launch year because the current product page does not establish its original debut date.
- **Red Panda ToneCore Bitcrusher**: Curt Malouin's timeline dates the first Red Panda product to October 2009 on the Line 6 ToneCore platform.
- **Red Panda ToneCore Subdivision**: June 2010 becomes a second documented ToneCore-era product point before the standalone Particle.
- **Red Panda Particle prototype**: first pedal prototype is documented in January 2011, providing a clean prototype-to-retail chain.
- **Red Panda Particle early beta cohort**: five beta units were built by the end of July 2011, and early batches used a Lexan label, creating a useful specimen marker.
- **Red Panda Particle first retail sale**: November 28, 2011 is documented by the builder as the date the Particle was added to the website for sale.
- **Walrus Audio Voyager**: company history states Walrus began in 2011 and the Voyager was its first pedal, creating a strong early-2010s overdrive anchor.
- **Caroline Wave Cannon**: original 2010 history confirms the October launch and the first 75 hand-built, individually numbered units; later naming changes remain separate identity layers.
- **Malekko Heavy Industry B:ASSMASTER**: 2008 period evidence confirms active production and discussion of the standard and germanium variants within the young builder's first years.

### Key research findings

1. **The digital boutique era has a different evidence trail.** Red Panda preserves dated development logs, prototype dates, small-batch counts, labels, and first retail dates, creating a model for future digital-pedal historical work.
2. **Small-batch production itself becomes observable history.** Black Arts production posts and Red Panda's beta batches show that dated workshop evidence can be as useful as catalogs for identifying early specimens.
3. **Founding dates and first products remain separate.** Spaceman provides a clear case where the 2009 company origin is certain but the exact first dirt-model date should remain unresolved until stronger evidence is found.
4. **Early internet-era builders often emerged through community feedback.** Black Arts, Caroline, Red Panda, and Walrus all show web-enabled or community-enabled pathways from experimentation into recognizable product lines.
5. **Non-dirt products can still anchor a builder's identity.** Red Panda's ToneCore modules are preserved as scope-bound chronology because they explain the transition into the standalone Particle.
6. **The archive should preserve naming changes as historical layers.** Caroline explicitly describes Wave Cannon 1/Cannonball as the product that started the company in 2010, making later names unsuitable as substitutes for the original state.

### Evidence discipline

- Manufacturer-authored histories and dated builder work logs are preferred for chronology.
- Period community posts are used as contemporaneous evidence and labeled accordingly.
- Prototype, beta, first sale, and later revision dates remain distinct.
- Current product pages are not used to infer historical launch dates unless they provide explicit chronology.
- Scope-bound digital and modulation products stay outside the central dirt count when appropriate.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries, or cloning instructions are added.

### Current lineage position

The archive now crosses the 2009-2012 boundary with a clear mix of physical and digital boutique production. Black Arts and Caroline demonstrate small-run hand production; Spaceman preserves the artisan model; Red Panda documents the transition from plug-in-like digital modules to standalone DSP pedals; Walrus establishes the 2011 overdrive wave; and Malekko shows the 2008 Portland micro-builder environment.
