# YARP Breadcrumb 128

## Purpose
Japanese pre-Maxon attribution pass focused on converting the Greco / Aria / Ibanez evidence chain into model-specific factory evidence without back-projecting later Nisshin Onpa manufacture onto earlier wedge fuzzes.

## New research conclusions

- `VAR-117`: The Greco No. 35 Haztone Fuzz Booster is directly tied to the Japanese wedge-fuzz family by a November 1967 Japan Music Trades advertisement. The evidence makes Nisshin Onpa a strong factory lead, but does not prove Nisshin was already building pedals in 1967.
- `VAR-118`: Pre-Maxon Nisshin Onpa is strongly documented as an OEM source for Aria and Greco fuzz/fuzz-wah products, including the No.57-59 and No.83 families. This is a confirmed relationship for the supported later/pre-Maxon product family.
- `VAR-119`: Greco, Sekova and Shaftesbury wedge fuzzes remain a common product-family hypothesis with multiple physical/power variants and an earliest likely date around 1967, but the ultimate factory remains unresolved.

## Cross-reference / dedupe decisions

- Do not upgrade Nisshin Onpa to confirmed manufacturer status for the Greco No.35 or every 1960s wedge fuzz solely from family resemblance.
- Preserve Aria and Greco as marketed identities and Nisshin Onpa as the physical manufacturer where ToneHome provides explicit pre-Maxon OEM evidence.
- Keep the 1967 Greco No.35 evidence separate from the 1970s Ibanez/Maxon evidence because the chronology is the key unresolved link.

## Evidence notes

Tone Machines Blog's June 2025 investigation identifies the November 1967 Japan Music Trades advertisement as showing a Greco No.35 Fuzz Booster that is clearly the same wedge form as the Sekova Wedge Fuzz. The article explicitly asks whether Nisshin Onpa was already manufacturing pedals in 1967 and does not claim proof. ToneHome's current first-series documentation separately states that Nisshin Onpa supplied pre-Maxon unbranded fuzz and fuzz-wah units to Aria and Greco, including No.57-59 and No.83 descendants.
