# The Dirt Archive — Effects Database Fuzz Discovery Batch 01

## Purpose

This is a **discovery import**, not a publication-ready census. The source is the Effects Database fuzz taxonomy, which currently reports 4,212 products. The archive can use this kind of broad index to identify obscure builders and model names, then normalize duplicates, separate editions/reissues, and research historical relationships before promoting records to the public catalog.

The first source page exposes the catalog alphabetically, including unknown/obscure brands as well as established manufacturers. That is valuable because The Dirt Archive specifically wants to preserve small builders, short-run products, defunct companies, and otherwise poorly documented pedals.

**Source:** Effects Database — fuzz index: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/type/fuzz

## Import policy

- Keep maker/brand text as a discovery string until builder identity is verified.
- Do not infer a circuit family merely from a product name.
- Preserve model names exactly enough to distinguish products, while allowing later normalization.
- Treat variants such as MkI/MkII, v2, silicon/germanium, limited editions, artist editions, and collaboration editions as potentially separate records when the evidence shows they were distinct products.
- Do not publish resale price, scarcity hype, or collector-value claims as part of the historical record.
- No schematics, gutshots, PCB layouts, cloning instructions, or complete component recipes are needed for this discovery layer.

## Immediate research targets generated from this batch

1. Build individual builder pages for obscure names where multiple products are documented.
2. Separate historical originals from modern recreations that reuse names such as Super Fuzz, Tone Bender, Buzzaround, Fuzz Face, Big Muff and Zonk.
3. Investigate unknown Japanese products: Big Mag Sustainar, Crazy Face, Crazy Tone and Fuzzie.
4. Investigate the Kurosawa? The Fuzz lead and preserve the question mark until independently verified.
5. Trace Antoria Fuzz Machine back into the Japanese OEM family already documented elsewhere in the archive.
6. Add Amtron UK-854/855/857 as a distinct vintage product family rather than a single generic Amtron entry.
7. Investigate Allsound SC-30/SC-31 together as a possible model sequence.
8. Research A.Y.A. BM-Fuzz 70/80 as a Japanese builder/product-family lead.
9. Verify Apollo 843/Fuzz King relationships and distinguish the fuzz/volume product from the fuzz-only products.
10. Determine whether the various Ananashead Fender/Tone Bender/Super Fuzz references represent separate historical interpretations, editions, or multiple builders under one brand.

## Important note on source handling

Effects Database is exceptionally valuable here because its taxonomy exposes names that ordinary “best vintage fuzz” histories omit. However, the Dirt Archive should **use it as a discovery backbone, not reproduce its prose wholesale**. Individual records should be rewritten into our own historical schema and corroborated where practical, with source links retained for provenance.
