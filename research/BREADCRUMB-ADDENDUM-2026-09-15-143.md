# YARP Breadcrumb 143

## Focus
Continue regional early-fuzz research with special attention to Australia, while promoting only makers whose dirt construction is actually documented.

## Promoted
- Claybridge Sound Systems / Ralph Norman Bridges: verified Australian dirt maker. Specialist research places the first Claybridge Fuzzmaster in 1966 and traces later Fuzzmaster variants; the 1971 Commonwealth Gazette independently confirms Ralph Bridges trading as Claybridge Sound.

## Cross-pass manufacturing findings
- Applied Audio Products Inc. is now a verified U.S. physical manufacturer, not merely an OEM attribution. Its New Jersey manufacturing identity explains a large family of late-1960s customer-branded dirt devices.
- United Musical Industries (UMI) is now a verified U.S. dirt maker through the Buzz Tone & Volume Expander and documented pedal production under Richard Soloway's company.
- LIMMCO is recorded only as a limited manufacturing entity for the initial Kimberly Buzz Box batch, because the owner testimony places those first units in LIMMCO's Long Island warehouse before outsourcing.
- Kimberly is therefore treated as a multi-stage product lineage rather than a single undifferentiated manufacturer.

## Held / exclusions
- Heathkit TA-28 remains excluded from the commercial builder layer because the surviving documentation describes it as a do-it-yourself kit. Its manufacturer is of course Heath Company, but this pass is keeping kit production separate from assembled stompbox manufacture.
- GM Electronics Fuzz Up FD3-A and Emthree Fuzz Super remain discovery leads because current evidence shows the pedals but does not yet establish the physical builder strongly enough.
- Orpheum / Clark / Manny's East Coast Fuzz remain unknown-OEM labels despite improved dating evidence.

## Editorial consequence
The Australian branch of the census is now materially stronger, and the U.S. late-1960s OEM ecosystem is becoming much more legible. The archive should preserve these manufacturing handoffs rather than collapsing them into brand names.

## Next queue
Continue screening Australian and Canadian builders, then return to obscure continental European manufacturers and the remaining 1960s U.S. regional labels. Maintain the hard dirt-maker gate.
