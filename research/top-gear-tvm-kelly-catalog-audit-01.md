# Top Gear / TVM Manchester / Kelly Amplification Catalog Audit 01

Research date: 2026-09-14

## Scope
This batch expands the early British builder universe with three small or obscure manufacturers/marketed entities that have concrete dirt evidence. The archive keeps product identity, manufacturer, marketer and uncertain relationships separate. This is catalog enumeration, not circuit reconstruction.

## Top Gear

### Builder identity
ToneHome and Effects Database identify Top Gear effects as designed and manufactured by Len Hawkes, owner of Hawkes Electronics and a physics lecturer at Brighton Technical College, at Top Gear's Sussex premises. Early pedals used gray or black panels; the Series-70 line introduced in 1975 moved to fiberglass housings with red face plates.

### Dirt catalog signal
Effects Database indexes 18 Top Gear products overall. The dirt-relevant set is:
- Top Gear Fuzz
- Top Gear Fuzz Sustain
- Top Gear Sustain
- Top Gear Treble Boost
- Top Gear Bass Boost

The central fuzz products should remain distinct records. Fuzz Sustain is a hybrid fuzz/boost/sustain product, not simply a renamed Fuzz. Top Gear Sustain and Treble Boost are adjacent gain products that should remain searchable without inflating the central fuzz count.

### Product relationships and external marketed names
Specialist sources document a relationship between the Top Gear Fuzz and the Guild Grizzly Fuzz at the product/circuit lineage level. The archive should record this as an evidence-linked relationship rather than automatically treating Guild as the manufacturer of the Top Gear product or vice versa.

The Fuzz Sustain appears in the 1975-1977 corridor in specialist surviving-pedal discussions, while Top Gear's Series-70 launch is anchored to 1975. Exact first-sale and discontinuation dates remain open.

### Evidence disposition
Top Gear moves from census-only toward indexed baseline mapping. The five dirt/gain records above are sufficiently grounded to enter the builder catalog, but exact lifetime production chronology and every regional/private-label relationship remain unresolved.

## TVM Manchester Ltd / TVM Sound

### Builder identity
Fuzzboxes documents TVM Manchester Ltd, also known as TVM Sound, as a Salford-based company incorporated on 26 September 1957 that manufactured amplifiers and PA equipment during the 1960s and 1970s.

### Dirt catalog signal
One surviving TVM fuzz box is documented. Fuzzboxes describes it as the only known surviving TVM pedal at the time of publication and places its visible construction features broadly in the 1960s. The exact production date, production volume and full pedal catalog remain unknown.

Catalog record:
- TVM Fuzz Box — Fuzz — date unresolved, likely 1960s based on surviving specimen characteristics.

The archive should not infer a larger TVM dirt catalog from amplifier/PA activity. Keep this as a one-model historical builder record until documentary evidence supports more products.

### Evidence disposition
TVM becomes a canonical candidate / indexed discovery record with a deliberately unresolved production date. The surviving-specimen status is important because the entire known pedal universe currently appears to hinge on a single surviving example.

Source:
- https://fuzzboxes.org/tvmfuzz

## Kelly Amplification

### Builder identity
Fuzzboxes documents Kelly Amplification as an early British amplifier maker associated with John Kelly Sr. and John Kelly Jr. The company operated during the 1960s and 1970s and is documented as having supplied an early fuzz box to the British market.

### Dirt catalog signal
- Kelly Amplification Fuzz Box — Fuzz — advertised May 1966 at £10.

No surviving exemplar is currently known, and the total production quantity is unknown.

### Attribution warning
This record is especially important for the archive's OEM/marketing rule. John Kelly Jr. recalled that early Kelly amplifiers were boxed and branded by Macari's Musical Exchange, and he believed a similar arrangement may have applied to the fuzz box. The surviving evidence therefore supports multiple possibilities: Kelly may have designed/built the fuzz and Macari's marketed it, or it may have been a Sola Sound/Macari design rebranded for Kelly. The archive must preserve this as an unresolved relationship rather than selecting one attribution.

### Evidence disposition
Kelly moves into the historical builder census as a documented candidate with one confirmed advertised dirt product but unresolved physical-manufacturer attribution.

Sources:
- https://fuzzboxes.org/kellyfuzzbox
- https://fuzzboxes.org/timeline

## Cross-builder findings

These three records reinforce the archive's early-British chronology:
- Kelly Fuzz Box: advertised May 1966.
- WEM Rush Pep Box: active by the August 1965 trade fair, with later WEM-marketed examples documented through the mid-1960s.
- TVM Fuzz Box: surviving specimen with broad 1960s characteristics but no firm production date.
- Top Gear: later 1970s Sussex builder with a multi-product effects catalog.

The evidence also reinforces a recurring rule: the existence of a marketed product does not by itself prove who physically manufactured it. Kelly/Macari/Sola Sound and Top Gear/Guild relationships must remain evidence-qualified.

## Public archive boundary
No schematics, PCB layouts, BOMs, gutshot libraries, cloning instructions or circuit-reconstruction material are included. Product identities, dates, relationships and evidence status only.
