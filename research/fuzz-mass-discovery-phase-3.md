# The Dirt Archive — Fuzz Mass Discovery, Phase 3

This is a high-volume discovery layer for expanding the archive beyond the founding collection. It is intentionally broader than the verified public archive. Names here are research targets, not automatic endorsements of dates, OEM relationships, or technical claims.

## Discovery backbone

Effects Database currently reports **4,212 fuzz products** in its fuzz category. It also maintains country and builder indexes and explicitly emphasizes inclusion of small builders and older/discontinued brands. This makes it useful as a discovery backbone, but The Dirt Archive should independently normalize names, split generations/editions, and verify historical relationships before promoting records publicly.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/type/fuzz

## Identification rules for mass expansion

1. Do not equate every rebrand with a separate design.
2. Do not equate every visually different edition with a new generation.
3. Preserve original model names even when later collectors use different nicknames.
4. Keep research taxonomies such as “MK1.5” explicitly labeled as research terminology when not manufacturer terminology.
5. Treat dates as ranges or approximate dates when evidence does not support an exact year.
6. Technical distinctions may be documented when they separate production periods, but no complete BOMs, schematics, PCB layouts, gutshot galleries, or cloning instructions belong in the public archive.
7. Images require ownership, builder permission, public-domain status, or an appropriate license.
8. Price, scarcity, and resale value are not archival fields of primary importance.
9. Historical OEM relationships should be relational, not duplicated as invented builder identities.
10. Every bulk-imported discovery record needs a later normalization pass before becoming a public canonical record.

## Strategic note

The target of “thousands of pedals” is achievable only by separating **discovery scale** from **editorial verification scale**. A useful archive can hold thousands of normalized product records while only a subset initially has full historical dossiers. The correct architecture is therefore:

**Discovery record → normalization → family/model relationship → evidence pass → public canonical record**

That lets the database grow rapidly without manufacturing certainty.
