## Roger Mayer Effects

### Identity / role
Roger Mayer should remain a canonical builder/designer lineage rather than being reduced to the Hendrix-associated Octavia name. Mayer's own historical account distinguishes an early period of custom studio/artist-built effects from the later commercial Rocket-series production. The record should therefore separate historical design/development, limited artist builds, and later commercial manufacture.

### Dirt catalog baseline
- Early 1964 germanium fuzz design, later revived commercially as Classic Fuzz. Mayer's current history says he designed his first fuzz guitar effects pedal in 1964.
- Octavia / Octavio development beginning in 1967. Mayer's current account states that the original 1967 recording-era units were not the same circuit, transformer, or enclosure as the later wedge-shaped 1968-69 examples.
- Wedge-shaped Octavia/Octavio, approximately 1968-1969, with only a small surviving run according to Mayer's own historical account.
- Axis Fuzz, developed in early 1967 for Jimi Hendrix, later appearing as part of the commercial Rocket-series family.
- Commercial Rocket-series Octavia, Classic Fuzz and Axis Fuzz, with commercial manufacture first occurring in the United States before Mayer returned to England around 1980.
- Metal Fuzz and Mongoose, identified by GuitarPlayer as part of Mayer's early-1980s Rocket-enclosure commercial lineup.
- Later Voodoo-series fuzz/gain products are separate modern production states and should not be back-projected into the 1960s custom-build lineage.

### Key chronology corrections
Mayer's current historical account explicitly says the Rocket-shaped Octavia enclosure did not exist before the late 1970s commercial revival period. This is important because surviving wedge Octavias from the 1968-69 period are a different production/state family. The archive should therefore never use the common later Rocket enclosure as a generic identifier for all 1960s Octavia units.

### Evidence state
VERIFIED / EVIDENCE-QUALIFIED at builder and family level. Exact surviving-unit chronology still requires specimen-level work later.

## Dan Armstrong / Sound Modifiers / Musitronics / Beigel Sound Lab

### Identity / role
Dan Armstrong is best modeled as a designer/brand/manufacturer lineage with multiple physical production partners over time. Contemporary documentation and later historical sources distinguish the original early-1970s London work, the Musitronics production period in New Jersey, and the 1981 Beigel Sound Lab return to production.

### Core dirt catalog
- Green Ringer: octave/ring-modulation effect, cataloged as one of the foundational Sound Modifier products.
- Blue Clipper: fuzz/distortion effect with sustain, part of the original Sound Modifier family.
- Purple Peaker: filter/peaking effect with gain/boost function, useful as a dirt-boundary record because it was explicitly positioned as a guitar signal shaper/booster.
- Yellow Humper: bass-oriented filter/booster, also a dirt-boundary record rather than a central fuzz count.
- Red Ranger: booster/tone-modifier, boundary record.
- Orange Squeezer: compressor/sustain effect, scope-boundary, not central dirt.

### Manufacturing chronology
A 1976 Beat Instrumental advertisement documents the Sound Modifiers family as a coordinated product line distributed by Sound Projects in London, and identifies the Blue Clipper as a fuzz-tone with sustain. A 1981 Polyphony notice reports that Beigel Sound Lab in Warwick, New York had resumed manufacturing the Dan Armstrong line, naming Blue Clipper and the other Sound Modifiers and giving a contemporary $39.50 list price for each product.

Specialist historical work also records an early UK-made Blue Clipper period before the later Musitronics production states. This should remain evidence-qualified until the primary advertisements and surviving specimens are reconciled model-by-model.

### Evidence state
INDEXED_RELATIONSHIP at builder/family level. Musitronics and Beigel Sound Lab are production-state relationships, not duplicate builders.

## Earth Sound Research

### Graphic Fuzz
The Earth Sound Research Graphic Fuzz remains a standalone American historical builder product rather than an anonymous generic op-amp fuzz. Effects Database and Amplified Parts place the pedal in the 1970s, with distinctive Filter and Boost controls. A boxed surviving example is documented as a 1970s unit. Current evidence supports the mid-1970s corridor but not an exact first-release year.

### Scope note
Earth Sound Research is primarily an amplifier/electronics company in the current documentary record, so the Graphic Fuzz should remain a focused dirt product record rather than implying a large pedal catalog until additional period evidence is found.

## Firstman / Hillwood follow-through

The current documentary trail strongly supports treating Firstman and Hillwood as a linked Japanese manufacturing lineage. A 2022 Japanese historical account reports Firstman founded in 1967, a 1970 change to the Hillwood Musical Instruments name, and renewed use of Firstman as a brand from 1977. Separate English-language product records identify the late-1970s Multivox Big Jam SE-8 Distortion as Japanese-made by Firstman for Nihon-Hammond and distributed under Multivox.

For the Dirt Archive this establishes one cataloged physical-manufacturer anchor:
- Multivox Big Jam SE-8 Distortion, circa 1978-1979 and possibly continuing briefly into the early 1980s, OEM Firstman for Nihon-Hammond / Multivox distribution.

The wider Firstman fuzz/distortion catalog remains incompletely enumerated. Do not infer FUZZrite manufacture merely because Firstman and Honey/Teisco employees have adjacent historical associations.
