# DOD Modern Dirt Catalog Audit 04

## Scope
DOD dirt products from the late-1990s transition through the Cortek-era revival and current 2026 catalog. The purpose is to separate original FX/Lamb-series history, later DOD-branded designs, revivals, and current-generation products without back-projecting modern models into the vintage catalog.

## Earlier modern products that must remain linked to the vintage catalog but not merged

### FX100 / FX101 / FX102
The late-1990s/around-2000 FX100 Integrated Tube Even Harmonic Overdrive, FX101 Grind, and FX102 Mystic Blues Overdrive belong to the final original-DOD-era expansion and should remain separate from the later Cortek revival products. The historical DOD/DigiTech account identifies the 1990s as the period in which Jason Lamb and other DOD engineers pushed the FX family into unusually experimental territory.

### FX33 Buzz Box
The FX33 Buzz Box is a vintage FX/Lamb-series dirt product combining distortion with sub-octave behavior. It belongs in the original FX catalog rather than the modern revival layer. Current catalog indexes still identify it as a historical DOD product.

### FX51 Juice Box and FX55 Supra Distortion
These remain original FX-series records. They should not be confused with later DOD branded products simply because modern catalog aggregators group all DOD products together.

## Ownership / revival chronology

DOD's official history describes the original company path through the 1970s-1990s and the expansion of the FX family during the 1980s and 1990s. The present DOD revival is a different corporate production state: in January 2023, Cor-Tek officially announced its acquisition of the DigiTech/DOD brand and the planned relaunch of DOD products. The modern 250 reissues and current products therefore receive distinct production/ownership-era treatment.

The 50th Anniversary 250 is especially useful for the archive because DigiTech/DOD explicitly states that only **1,974 units** were to be produced and that the model was hand-built in the USA. It should be indexed as a limited edition, not as ordinary continuous 250 production.

The 250-X is described by DigiTech as the **10th iteration** of the 250 family. That statement establishes a family-generation relationship without requiring the archive to publish circuit reconstruction details.

## Negative-evidence / boundary conclusion

The modern DOD catalog is broad, but not every current DOD-branded product should be retroactively assigned to the historical DOD factory lineage. The archive therefore separates:

1. original DOD/FX production;
2. late original-company experimental/Lamb-era products;
3. post-original-company DOD-branded designs and revivals;
4. current Cor-Tek-era products;
5. anniversary and limited editions.

This audit does not claim that every DOD dirt model from every regional catalog has been found. Historical paper catalogs from the 1990s and early 2000s remain a useful future source for short-lived models.

## Public-archive boundary

No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries, cloning instructions, or circuit reconstruction material are included. The archive records product identity, chronology, manufacturing/ownership relationships, categories, editions, and evidence status only.
