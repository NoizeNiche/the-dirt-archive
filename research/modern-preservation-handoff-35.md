## Major research notes
- GPX's 2023 annual surveys provide the discovery spine: 24 fuzz selections, 18 distortion selections and 29 boost/overdrive selections, with a much larger longlist. These are census/discovery evidence, not automatic introduction dates.
- Kernom Moho is a major modern preservation record. GPX selected it as 2023 Pedal of the Year. Its analog-audio/programmable-control architecture, presets and MIDI make the control layer part of the product identity.
- Great Eastern FX Co. Focus Fuzz has unusually useful launch evidence. Premier Guitar describes it as an original design using NOS germanium and modern silicon transistors, with a Focus control and a 250-unit worldwide limitation tied to transistor availability.
- Palmer Solarist V2, PedalPalFX PAL800 V4, ThorpyFX The Dane MKII and Walrus 385 MKII demonstrate the continuing importance of explicit generation labels. The archive must preserve the generation even before every technical difference is known.
- The 2023 distortion layer contains unusual architectures such as Fairfield Roger That's FM-demodulating approach, Empress Heavy Menace's integrated noise gate, and preamp/distortion products such as Bardic Rival and DSM & Humboldt Black Clouds. These should not be flattened into generic distortion categories.
- The 2023 overdrive layer shows the continuing convergence of lineage and modern control: dual Klone/TS voices, MOSFET gain stages, tube preamps, Waza Craft production identities and multi-function drive/boost products.

## Data rule reinforced
The archive separates: discovery/census year, introduction year, revision/generation year, and current availability. A roundup appearance is not enough to assign an introduction date. Explicit V2/MKII/V4 labels are preserved, while unverified technical differences remain marked as research rather than invented.
