## Important 2025 archival principles
- GPX reports 36 boost/overdrive finalists from a 46-pedal longlist, 20 distortion finalists, and 31 fuzz finalists. The Archive treats these as discovery/census evidence, not automatic proof of release chronology. 
- Explicit MKII, V2, anniversary, deluxe, special-edition and signature labels remain searchable production boundaries.
- Collaboration records such as Brothers AM should preserve both builder identities and the collaboration relationship.
- Digital/IR products such as ENGL EP635 Fireball IR remain inside the dirt archive when their primary identity is high-gain preamp/distortion behavior; digital architecture and IR functionality become identification fields.
- Dynamic or unusual control systems such as Hangman DAGG'R's gain-ramping behavior should be cataloged as product identity rather than reduced to a generic effect category.
- Amp-family lineage remains useful for identification, especially where a product is explicitly built around a named amplifier family.

## Audit method
Continue checking every year against: (1) annual GPX finalists, (2) GPX longlists and launch roundups, and (3) builder/manufacturer/contemporary specialist evidence. Existing products should be enriched, not duplicated. Explicit generation and edition labels remain independent where evidence supports a production boundary. Longlist-only products can enter the research layer when reconciliation supports them.

## Next target
