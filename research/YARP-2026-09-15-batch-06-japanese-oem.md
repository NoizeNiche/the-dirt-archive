## Major findings

### JEN / Vox / Italian Tone Bender family
The Italian Vox V828/V8281 corridor remains distinct from the UK Sola Sound Tone Bender line. Current evidence supports Jen as the Italian manufacturer for major production states. Vox Showroom documents Thomas Organ's relationship with JEN, while Fuzzboxes and specialist historical references show model-specific production boundaries. A historical EME attribution appears in specialist material for an early V828 state; this remains an unresolved attribution question and is not promoted to canonical fact.

Jen-branded Fuzz and Tone Bender records are retained as separate searchable product identities tied to the Italian die-cast family. Elka, Luxor, Nova and Unicord remain relationship candidates where the evidence supports the same family, but are not automatically merged into one retail model.

### Shin-Ei / Honey / Univox
The Japanese Super-Fuzz corridor requires a lineage model rather than a single maker label. Honey's late-1960s Psychedelic Machine / Super-Fuzz lineage precedes or overlaps later Shin-Ei production, and Univox represents an important U.S.-marketed/distribution identity. Companion FY-2, FY-6, WF-8 and WF-24 are cataloged under Shin-Ei/Companion with marketed-name relationships preserved.

### Teisco
Teisco's Wau Wau Fuzz and Fuzz Machine are treated as different catalog families. The Wau Wau Fuzz remains a fuzz-wah OEM/discovery record with reported Shin-Ei association. The Fuzz Machine is a separate fuzz product in the wider Japanese multi-brand enclosure family. Do not infer one factory relationship from the existence of the other.

### Greco / Nisshin Onpa
The Greco No. 35 Haztone Fuzz Booster remains a marketed-brand discovery record. Nisshin Onpa attribution is plausible and repeatedly reported, but the archive continues to treat it as evidence-qualified rather than universal proof for every similarly shaped Japanese wedge fuzz.

### Coron
The Coron catalog now explicitly distinguishes Coron Distortion 10, Distortion 15, Coron Over Drive and Laser Overdrive. Related Grant, Storm, Asama, Seiwa, Bias and other names remain private-label/marketed relationships where evidence supports them. Product existence is separated from exact internal production state.

### Sobbat
DB-1 Drive Breaker is retained as a core Sobbat anchor while the full Drive Breaker/Fuzz Breaker catalog remains incomplete and queued for dedicated enumeration.

## Editorial corrections

1. Similar enclosure or control layout is not sufficient to prove common manufacture.
2. A specialist source can establish discovery evidence without automatically establishing canonical manufacturer identity.
3. A single retail model may legitimately have multiple historical manufacturers or suppliers, and these should be represented as dated production states.
4. Japanese OEM chains should be modeled at the product/period level whenever factory attribution is disputed.
