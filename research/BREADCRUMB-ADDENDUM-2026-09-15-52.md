## Historical findings

- Kimberly Buzz Box and Volume Expander is now explicitly modeled as a multi-stage production history: an initial LIMMCO in-house state, a later Applied Audio OEM state, and a subsequent UMI branch.
- The Applied stage is treated as a production transition, not as the original manufacturer of the entire Kimberly run.
- Apollo No. 843 and Conrad Buzz Box & Volume Expander are retained as later UMI marketed states, separate from Applied's Kimberly production.
- Audio Designs Fuzzound remains a medium-confidence Applied family candidate because the current evidence is relational rather than a direct factory statement.
- Nomad Banshee Fuzz remains medium-confidence on exact dating even though its Applied manufacturing relationship is well supported. Tone Machines explicitly notes that the manufacturing dates are unknown.
- Nomad Rogue Fuzz remains high-confidence Applied production with LIMMCO treated as the house-brand/distribution connection, not the physical factory.
- Nomad Super Fuzz remains a separate unresolved 1966-1967 candidate potentially related to Mosrite/Fuzzrite. It is not allowed to inherit the later Applied Nomad attribution.

## Validation posture

The Kimberly/LIMMCO/Applied/UMI chain is now modeled as period-specific manufacturing transitions. This prevents a common archive error where a marketed family name is treated as if one manufacturer built every example from first run through final production.
