# The Dirt Archive — Catalog Completeness Protocol v1

## Objective

The archive is intended to document **every identifiable builder/manufacturer/brand we can reasonably discover**, and for each builder to enumerate the full documented product lineup rather than only famous or dirt-related examples. Individual products are then candidates for deeper historical research.

The target is not merely a large list. The target is a historically useful catalog in which each product can eventually carry:

- product/model name and alternate names
- brand, builder, manufacturer, OEM and distributor relationships
- country/region and approximate production window
- generations and production periods
- enclosure, artwork, control and labeling changes
- edition and reissue distinctions
- meaningful component changes when they materially distinguish versions
- documented users, endorsements, interviews and historical anecdotes
- recurring collector/repair observations, clearly separated from established fact
- source records and source URLs
- image/photography status and rights information
- unresolved questions and contradictory claims

## Builder completeness rule

Once a builder is selected for a catalog pass, capture the **whole documented lineup** available from reliable or useful discovery sources, including products outside the current Dirt scope. This prevents a fuzz-first archive from accidentally misrepresenting a builder whose actual catalog contains overdrives, distortions, boosts, modulation, delay, utility products, collaborations, limited editions or discontinued items.

Examples of currently visible dense catalogs illustrate the scale:

- JHS Pedals: 98 products in Effects Database.
- EarthQuaker Devices: 88 products in Effects Database.
- Keeley Electronics: 125 products in Effects Database.
- Mr. Black: 94 products in Effects Database.
- Electro-Harmonix: 133 products in Effects Database.
- BSM: 35 products in Effects Database.
- VFE Pedals: 42 products in Effects Database.
- Blackout Effectors: 17 products in Effects Database.
- G.S. Wyllie: 10 products in Effects Database.

These counts are **discovery snapshots**, not permanent totals. They can change as sites add, merge or split records.

## Product enumeration sources

Primary discovery backbone:
1. Effects Database builder pages and category indexes.
2. Builder/manufacturer websites and archived catalogs.
3. Interviews with builders/designers.
4. Period advertisements, catalogs and distributor literature.
5. Specialist historical sites and collector archives.
6. Reputable forum/collector material for leads and specimen observations.

Effects Database is particularly important because its stated mission includes preserving small builders and early brands that often existed mainly through eBay, forums and short-lived web presences. It therefore serves as a high-value **discovery backbone**, not an automatic statement of historical fact.

## Product-level research packet

Every significant product should eventually receive a product research packet containing these sections when evidence exists:

### Identity
- canonical model name
- alternate/market names
- badges and rebrands
- builder/manufacturer attribution
- designer attribution
- OEM relationship

### Dates
- earliest known appearance
- latest known appearance
- estimated production range
- known specimen date evidence
- catalog/ad dates
- date-quality notes

### Physical identification
- enclosure type
- dimensions when useful
- control layout
- switch/jack layout
- graphics and typography
- logo/badge changes
- knobs and hardware
- labeling details
- serial-number conventions
- footswitch/indicator changes

### Production variation
Record each meaningful change as a separate production period/generation when evidence supports it. Examples:
- germanium → silicon
- one transistor family → another
- component family change that materially affects identification or period
- capacitor/resistor technology change when historically diagnostic
- PCB revision
- hand-wired → PCB production
- enclosure tooling change
- artwork revision
- control-layout revision
- bypass/switching revision

The public archive should describe **historically meaningful component differences at a high level**. It should not expose complete BOMs, schematics, PCB layouts, cloning instructions or enough detail to reconstruct a protected commercial design.

### Historical context
- builder story
- origin of the model
- notable artists/users
- collaborations
- endorsements
- catalogs and advertisements
- company changes
- discontinuation/revival
- reissues and modern descendants

### Community record
Capture useful online material such as:
- recurring identification observations
- collector terminology
- common myths
- disputed dates
- repair observations
- owner reports
- forum claims
- builder comments
- unusual anecdotes

Every such statement should be tagged by evidence quality and phrased so that a claim is not silently upgraded into fact.

### Photography
For every product, track:
- front image
- rear image when available
- side/top/bottom images when identification requires them
- packaging/catalog image when historically valuable
- specimen photo vs generic product photo
- source URL
- original creator/photographer when known
- license or permission status
- credit line
- whether the image may be redistributed on The Dirt Archive
- whether the archive should instead use a linked external image
- capture/research date
- notes about cropping, modification or replacement risk

**Image workflow rule:** image discovery happens alongside product discovery, but **discovery is not permission**. A picture found on a manufacturer site, forum, marketplace, social account or search result should be recorded as a reference lead. It should not automatically be copied into the public archive.

Priority order for public-use imagery:
1. The Dirt Archive-owned photographs.
2. Builder/manufacturer images where permission to reproduce is granted or the terms clearly allow reuse.
3. Public-domain images.
4. Creative Commons or similarly licensed images whose exact license has been verified and whose attribution terms are satisfied.
5. User/collector photographs with explicit permission and an agreed credit line.
6. Other images retained only as **external reference links** until rights are established.

Credit is editorially required where the source or license calls for it, but **credit is not a substitute for permission or a reuse license**. The archive should preserve the original source URL even when an image is not redistributed.

### Image evidence tiers

`Reference-only`

Image located and useful for identification, but no public reuse permission established. Store source URL and creator information when available; do not copy the image into the site's public assets.

`Licensed`

Reuse rights are explicitly granted by a license such as Creative Commons, subject to its terms. Record the exact license and attribution requirements.

`Permission granted`

Copyright holder has explicitly permitted use. Record who granted permission, date, scope and requested credit.

`Owned`

Photograph is owned by The Dirt Archive or provided by a contributor under terms that transfer/grant the necessary reuse rights.

`Public domain`

Copyright status is established as public domain for the relevant jurisdiction/use.

`Withdrawn`

Previously used or proposed image should no longer be displayed. Preserve the provenance record for historical accountability.

## Research status ladder

`Discovery`

Name/model existence preserved with minimal source trail.

`Enumerated`

Product verified as part of a builder catalog, but product history is not yet fully researched.

`Researched`

Dates, identity, major variations and useful historical context have been assembled.

`Deep Research`

Multiple independent sources compared; production variations and disputed claims documented.

`Canonical`

Stable public record with source-backed history, relationships, image rights and editorial review.

## Completeness audits

A builder is not considered complete merely because its headline product count has been reached. Each catalog pass should check for:

- discontinued products
- limited runs
- artist signatures
- collaborations
- regional versions
- rebrands
- series/sub-series
- custom shop items that were commercially listed
- products that Effects Database categorizes under another effect type
- products documented only in old catalogs or archived builder pages
- products with spelling/name variants
- products whose database entry was merged into another record

## Research priority

For dense catalogs, use a two-pass system:

### Pass A — enumeration + media discovery
Capture every product name and builder relationship, and at the same time capture the best available image lead(s), source URL(s), creator/photographer when known, and rights status.

### Pass B — deep research
Research each product individually, beginning with the products where variation, rarity, historical importance or identification value is highest. Promote the best image from reference-only to licensed/permission-granted/owned/public-domain when possible.

Do not allow deep research on five famous pedals to block enumeration of the other ninety-five.

## Media manifest rule

Each catalog expansion phase should maintain a companion media manifest rather than burying image information inside prose notes. At minimum, a media record should identify:

- pedal/product ID
- image role (`front`, `rear`, `side`, `top`, `bottom`, `packaging`, `catalog`, `specimen`)
- source page URL
- direct image URL when stable and appropriate
- creator/photographer
- source organization/site
- rights status
- license or permission reference
- required credit
- public-use decision (`display`, `reference-only`, `pending`, `withdrawn`)
- research date
- notes

This lets the archive build a visual library without confusing **“we found it”** with **“we are entitled to republish it.”**

## Important editorial distinction

The archive should be capable of saying:

> “This builder is known to have made 42 products, and 31 have been individually documented so far.”

rather than implying that 31 is the builder's complete output.

Likewise, where the historical evidence is incomplete:

> “No surviving catalog has yet been located; the current lineup is reconstructed from Effects Database, advertisements and surviving specimens.”

That distinction is part of the archive's historical value.
