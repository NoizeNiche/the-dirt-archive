# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 13

## Focus

This batch expands the Japanese export/OEM census beyond the central Honey/Shin-Ei FY-2/FY-6 network. The new evidence separates at least three additional historical clusters:

1. JEN-linked Italian OEM/product states already visible in European export catalogs.
2. A Japanese-made **Maestro FZ-1A-derived** fuzz cluster around LRE, Apollo, Melos, Mica, Monacor and Olson.
3. Rare Japanese products under Royal and Mirano labels, including a Royal dual-effect fuzz/wah and a Mirano plug-in fuzz.

The archive treats similarity and shared ancestry as distinct from proven common manufacture.

## Maestro FZ-1A-derived Japanese export cluster

### LRE Fuzz Sound
Effects Database's related-model index places LRE Fuzz Sound alongside Apollo no. 843, Herald AM-44A, Melos FS-1, Mica FT-12, Monacor FS-1 and Olson X-78. This cluster is useful because it is separate from the FY-6 Super Fuzz identity crisis: these products are described as Japanese FZ-1A-style fuzzes rather than Super Fuzz variants.

### Herald Electronics AM-44A Fuzz Master
Effects Database identifies the AM-44A as a late-1960s Japanese solid-state fuzz distributed under multiple names, including Apollo, LRE, Melos, Mica, Monacor and Olson. This is a strong relationship anchor for the FZ-1A-derived cluster, although the accessible page does not provide a definitive physical factory name beyond Japanese manufacture.

Archive status: **MARKETED/JAPANESE OEM CLUSTER, INDEXED_RELATIONSHIP**. Physical builder remains unresolved rather than being retroactively assigned to Shin-Ei, LRE or any other named manufacturer.

### Monacor FS-1 Fuzzder
The model has a dedicated Effects Database page. Surviving period-style listings describe it as a Japanese-made early-1970s Fuzzder and identify it as a Japanese copy of the Maestro FZ-1A. The same listings cross-reference the LRE Fuzz Sound, Apollo 843, Melos FS-1, Mica FT-12 and Olson X-78.

Archive status: **INDEXED_RELATIONSHIP** for the Japanese/FZ-1A-derived cluster, with exact physical factory unresolved.

### Mica FT-12 Fuzzder
Effects Database places the Mica FT-12 directly in the same LRE Fuzz Sound / Apollo / Herald / Melos / Monacor / Olson relationship group. A second Mica model, **FT-12 Wailer**, is also indexed in the same family and should remain a separate retail record until direct model evidence proves otherwise.

Archive status: **DISCOVERY / INDEXED FAMILY**, with physical maker unresolved.

### Melos FS-1 Fuzzder
Effects Database directly indexes the Melos FS-1 and places it in the same Japanese FZ-1A-style group. The model should not be merged with the Melos FY-6/Super Fuzz-adjacent records that may emerge from other export corridors.

Archive status: **INDEXED_RELATIONSHIP**, physical factory unresolved.

### Olson X-78 Fuzz Master
A surviving 1960s specimen is explicitly described as a rebadged LRE Fuzz Sound and Made in Japan. This supplies model-level evidence that Olson belongs in the same marketed/rebrand cluster as LRE rather than being treated as an unrelated physical builder.

Archive status: **INDEXED_RELATIONSHIP / marketed rebrand**.

## Royal Japanese fuzz family

### Royal RFC-1 Double Effect Machine
Effects Database identifies the RFC-1 as a late-1960s/early-1970s Japanese-made dual-function fuzz/wah, probably produced by Shin-Ei and distributed under the Royal brand. The uncertainty word **probably** is preserved in the archive: this is an evidence-qualified Shin-Ei candidate, not a canonical physical-manufacturer attribution.

This model is important because Royal therefore cannot be treated as a single guaranteed Shin-Ei export identity. The Royal brand also appears in other Japanese fuzz products that require model-specific attribution.

Archive status: **DISCOVERY, Shin-Ei relationship candidate**.

### Royal Fuzz Box RF-1
A Japanese collector source documents a Royal Fuzz Box RF-1 as a large-format Japanese upper-octave fuzz, sounding closer to the early Univox Super Fuzz/Ace Tone FM-2 family than to the Maestro FZ-1A cluster. The exact manufacturer remains unresolved.

Archive status: **DISCOVERY / regional Japanese fuzz candidate**.

## Mirano Japanese fuzz expansion

### Mirano FP-1 Fuzz Pet
Effects Database identifies the FP-1 as a compact plug-in silicon fuzz produced by Mirano, a Japanese manufacturer associated with late-1960s studio equipment. The record expands Mirano beyond the previously known **Exciting Fuzz EF-1** and establishes a second dirt product in the archive.

The existence of FP-1 matters for catalog completeness because Mirano can no longer be represented only by its more famous Exciting Fuzz. The exact relationship between FP-1 and the 1970s Japanese Big Muff-clone corridor remains separate and unresolved.

Archive status: **INDEXED_RELATIONSHIP / Mirano product**.

### Mirano Exciting Fuzz EF-1
Tone Machines Blog's March 2026 research materially revises the commonly repeated early dating. A 1976 Japanese buyer's guide is reported to list the Exciting Fuzz, contradicting claims that it was already a 1968-1970 product. The specialist researcher argues for a mid-1970s release corridor and notes that repeated unchanged construction across multiple specimens is compatible with a comparatively short production run, though the exact release date remains unproven.

Archive status: **INDEXED_RELATIONSHIP / historical-date correction queued**. Do not publish the older 1968-70 dates as fact.

## Catalog normalization consequences

1. The **LRE/Herald/Apollo/Melos/Mica/Monacor/Olson** cluster is a distinct Japanese FZ-1A-derived export network and should not be merged with the FY-6 Super Fuzz cluster simply because both contain similar two- or three-knob vintage fuzz boxes.
2. A physical-factory field may remain unresolved even when a model-level relationship is strong. The archive should prefer a correct unresolved state over false factory certainty.
3. Royal is now explicitly a multi-lineage marketed brand candidate: RFC-1 is probably Shin-Ei, while RF-1 remains a separate unresolved Japanese fuzz product.
4. Mirano's catalog breadth is larger than the Exciting Fuzz alone. FP-1 and EF-1 belong to the same manufacturer-level census but not necessarily the same product family.
5. The 1976 catalog evidence for EF-1 is a useful example of **date correction through period publication**, exactly the kind of evidence the archive should prioritize before specimen archaeology.
