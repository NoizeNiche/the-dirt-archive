# YARP Breadcrumb 125

## Purpose
Japanese early-fuzz OEM archaeology pass focused on the Herald / Olson / Apollo / LRE / Melos / Mica / Monacor product corridor and the unresolved physical factory behind it.

## New research conclusions

- `VAR-110`: Secondary historical evidence ties Herald Electronics to Olson Electronics as a Chicago sourcing/wholesale operation, while Herald products were frequently Japanese-made. This is useful corporate-chain evidence but does not identify the Japanese fuzz factory.
- `VAR-111`: The Herald AM-44A Fuzz Master and Apollo No.843, LRE Fuzz Sound, Melos FS-1, Mica FT-12, Monacor FS-1 and Olson X-78 are preserved as a closely related Japanese-made product family with multiple marketed identities.
- `VAR-112`: Olson X-87 has collector evidence placing it in the Ace Tone FM-1 design lineage, but similarity is not accepted as proof of Ace Tone manufacture. Keep this as a factory-attribution research lead only.

## Cross-reference / dedupe decisions

- Do not create separate manufacturer records for Apollo, LRE, Melos, Mica, Monacor or Olson solely from the shared family evidence. Their marketed identities remain separate from the unresolved Japanese OEM factory.
- Do not merge Herald Electronics into the physical Japanese manufacturer. Herald is currently best modeled as a U.S. sourcing/marketed entity with Japanese OEM supply.
- Do not merge Olson Electronics and Ace Tone. The Olson X-87 comparison is a design-lineage clue, not a factory assignment.
- Preserve X-78 and X-87 as distinct Olson model identities until model chronology and production relationships are better documented.

## Evidence notes

Effects Database describes the AM-44A as a Japanese-made late-1960s fuzz distributed under Apollo, LRE, Melos, Mica, Monacor and Olson names. A surviving Olson X-78 listing likewise describes the product as Japanese OEM production and names Apollo and Melos equivalents. Vintage Guitar describes the Monacor FS-1 as likely another Shin-Ei product, showing that Shin-Ei is a plausible but unproven candidate within the wider Japanese export-fuzz ecosystem. Secondary historical discussion places Herald Electronics in Olson's sourcing structure. None of these sources is sufficient to name the exact factory for the AM-44A family.
