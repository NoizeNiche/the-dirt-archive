# YARP 2026-09-15 — Batch 11 Reconciliation

## Purpose
Advance the breadth-first archive without collapsing distinct retail names, production states, or OEM relationships.

## Japanese OEM findings

### Shin-Ei / Honey / FY-6 corridor
Shin-Ei's current historical material explicitly states that the Honey Co. Ltd. FY-6 Baby Crying Fuzz was released in 1967 and that Shin-Ei branded the FY-6 in 1968. Historical coverage also connects the Univox Super-Fuzz to the Shin-Ei source lineage. The archive therefore treats Honey FY-6, Shin-Ei FY-6, Companion FY-6, and Univox Super-Fuzz as linked but separately searchable retail/product records.

A broad historical list of FY-6 private-label names exists, but this batch does not promote every name to a proven factory attribution. Each marketed label requires its own evidence trail.

### JEN / Italian fuzz-wah corridor
Effects Database identifies JEN Double Sound and JEN Super Double Sound, with related retail names including Vox 9-3700, Thomas Organ 9-3701, Luxor Double Sound, Unicord Wah Fuzz, Dallas-Arbiter Fuzz Wah Face, Electra 1245 and others. Vox Showroom demonstrates why attribution must remain model-specific: at least one Vox 9-3700-era fuzz-wah is explicitly identified by its base-plate as Thomas Organ California-made, despite the broader Italian JEN production history.

The archive therefore keeps JEN as a canonical physical manufacturer/OEM entity while refusing blanket attribution for every Vox/Thomas Organ fuzz-wah bearing a related name.

### Teisco / Japanese OEM aliases
Teisco Fuzz Machine and Teisco Wau Wau Fuzz remain separate. Specialist sources report wider private-label families around the Fuzz Machine, but this batch preserves those links as candidates unless model-specific factory evidence is recovered.

## DOD reconciliation

A period 1992 Electronic Musician guide confirms a substantial FX-series dirt group: FX50B, FX52, FX53, FX54, FX55B, FX56B, FX57, FX58 and FX59. Effects Database additionally preserves FX50, FX55, FX55C, FX56, FX66, FX69/69B and FX70 among the FX-series dirt universe. The archive now explicitly treats letter-suffix changes as separate production/product states rather than silently merging them.

The FX52 Classic Fuzz is especially useful as a chronology anchor: specialist historical coverage places its catalog introduction around 1990, while period documentation confirms the product existed by 1992.

## Brazil / Latin America

NIG's official site identifies the FZD-AT as an Andy Timmons limited edition of its Multi Fuzz and Vintage Distortion; its product documentation calls out limited-series collector status, and a surviving product catalog specifies 300 units. This is retained as a signature/limited state rather than a separate core effect family.

Maza FX's archived site provides strong dated launch evidence for Broken Head Fuzz and Versatile Preamp in August 2011, Drago Distortion in September 2011, and Feel Overdrive in October 2011. The archive preserves these dated launch events independently from the current storefront lineup.

Sabbadius' official discontinued-products page preserves multiple historical fuzz/drive identities including 1966 Germanium Fuzz, 1988 Austin Texas Fuzz, 1989 Hybrid Fuzz, Black Gold Distortion, JIMI Fuzz and Toro Argentino Overdrive. These are product-existence anchors; exact first-release dates are not inferred from the names alone.
