# Early Foundational Handoff 81

## Pass 33: MI Audio 2005-2008 fuzz family, HBE signature expansion, and the Devi Ever / Ooh La La transition

Pass 33 continues the 1998-2010 boutique corridor by concentrating on records that expose family relationships, specimen states, signature-product boundaries, and manufacturing handoffs. The emphasis remains on documentary anchors that can help identify real surviving pedals without inventing launch dates from later database entries.

### Newly documented

- **MI Audio Neo Fuzz**: September 19, 2005 specialist publication marker within the MI Audio 2006-2008 family, with detailed builder-authored control and specimen clues.
- **MI Audio G.I. Fuzz**: September 19, 2005 specialist publication marker within the same 2006-2008 family, documented as a high-gain silicon companion to Neo Fuzz.
- **MI Audio Neo/G.I. Fuzz family relationship**: Michael Ibrahim's historical text explicitly describes G.I. Fuzz as the logical next step after Neo Fuzz, preserving a sibling-development relationship.
- **Homebrew Electronics Skull Crusher**: January 30, 2009 Effects Database publication marker for the Gary Hoey signature overdrive.
- **Devi Ever / Effector 13 Synth Mangler pre-Ooh La La state**: a November 2009 surviving listing explicitly identifies a Synth Mangler built before Ooh La La started building them; a May 2008 listing independently documents the Effector 13 design.
- **Ooh La La Manufacturing Synth Mangler transition state**: January 2011 specimen evidence is now paired with the pre-Ooh La La state to preserve a manufacturing-transition lineage without inventing a precise handoff date.
- **Black Arts Toneworks LSTR early-publicity corridor**: the May 2010 manufacturer snapshot is now corroborated by a June 2011 manufacturer-linked/dealer demonstration trail.
- **Black Arts Toneworks Ritual continuity marker**: the 2010 manufacturer snapshot is paired with the June 2012 first-run Black Chrome announcement as a later edition/state rather than retroactive launch proof.

### Key research findings

1. **MI Audio's 2005 fuzz catalog is a coherent family, not two isolated products.** Neo Fuzz and G.I. Fuzz share an explicit design lineage, but remain separate retail models with different transistor families and distinct specimen-facing identities.
2. **The MI Audio family has a useful 2006-2008 historical envelope.** The specialist pages preserve the models inside the same commercial-period family, while the publication date stays separate from any claimed retail launch date.
3. **HBE's signature catalog deserves its own lane.** The Skull Crusher demonstrates that HBE's 2000s catalog was not limited to its general-purpose models; signature relationships should remain visible instead of being flattened into a generic HBE bucket.
4. **The Synth Mangler is an unusually clean manufacturing-transition example.** A 2009 surviving listing explicitly says the specimen predates Ooh La La construction, while later Ooh La La specimens prove the subsequent state. The archive now has a before/after relationship without forcing an exact legal or manufacturing handoff date.
5. **Black Arts' documentary chain is strengthening.** LSTR has a 2010 manufacturer-presence anchor plus 2011 public-demo evidence, while Ritual now has a later limited-edition production marker that can be used for edition recognition without rewriting its earlier chronology.
6. **Specimen evidence and catalog evidence are complementary.** The archive increasingly treats dated listings, manufacturer pages, specialist databases and videos as separate clocks that can corroborate one another without becoming interchangeable.

### Evidence discipline

- Database publication dates remain documentary markers unless independent evidence establishes earlier or concurrent production presence.
- Surviving specimens are not assumed to represent the entire production run.
- Designer, brand, licensee and manufacturer roles remain distinct.
- Family relationships are recorded when directly described by the builder or supported by strong documentary evidence.
- Later editions are never used to manufacture earlier release dates.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, full BOMs, gutshot libraries or cloning instructions are added.

### Current lineage position

The boutique corridor now has a particularly strong 2005-2010 spine: HBE contributes broad catalog and signature-product evidence; MI Audio supplies a documented multi-model fuzz family and multiple generation clocks; D*A.M. contributes small-run and named-version states; Devi Ever/Ooh La La provides a rare designer-to-licensee manufacturing transition; and Black Arts Toneworks contributes manufacturer-authored early catalog snapshots with later edition corroboration.
