# Modern Preservation Track — Newest to Oldest

## Purpose

This track captures recent and modern dirt-pedal variation while primary evidence is still abundant. It runs alongside the long-term builder census and historical archaeology.

## Calendar order

Work backward by product era:

- 2025–2026
- 2020–2024
- 2015–2019
- 2010–2014
- 2000–2009
- 1990–1999
- 1980–1989
- 1970–1979
- 1960–1969

## Why modern first?

Modern products often have unusually rich evidence that can disappear quickly: current manufacturer pages, manuals, revision notes, firmware downloads, archived product pages, retailer listings, dated photographs, serial/date examples, interviews and firsthand owner reports. Capture these while the evidence is still close to the object.

## Modern product-family capture checklist

For every modern dirt family, ask:

1. What is the exact marketed model name?
2. Is the marketed brand the physical manufacturer?
3. When was the product introduced, revised, discontinued or reissued?
4. What are all official generations or hardware revisions?
5. What visual changes distinguish each version?
6. What control, switching or enclosure changes occurred?
7. What documented electronics or component-family changes occurred?
8. Did firmware/software change the product identity?
9. Are there artist, retailer, anniversary, regional or limited editions?
10. Did manufacturing move factories or countries?
11. Did the same platform appear under another brand?
12. Which differences are proven, which are reported, and which are only suspected?
13. What photographs are especially valuable for future visual identification?
14. What evidence could disappear first?
15. What would a person holding the pedal need to know to identify the exact version?

## Evidence priority

Prefer, in roughly this order:

1. Manufacturer documentation and official announcements.
2. Official manuals and revision-specific manuals.
3. Archived manufacturer pages.
4. Dated distributor / retailer catalog material.
5. Interviews or direct designer/manufacturer statements.
6. Dated high-quality product photographs.
7. Serial/date-code evidence from documented specimens.
8. Specialist historical references.
9. Community reports, only when clearly labeled as reports and not promoted to fact without corroboration.

## Variant taxonomy

Never force every difference into one "version" field. Keep these axes separate:

- Appearance
- Production
- Electronics
- Firmware / software
- Market / region
- Edition / special run
- Reissue / successor
- OEM / private label

A product may therefore have the same electronics but different appearance, or the same appearance but a different manufacturing period.

## Identification output

The final objective is not merely a chronology. A user should eventually be able to answer:

> I have this exact-looking pedal. Which generation is it, when was it made, where was it made, and what distinguishes it from the other versions?

## Editorial boundary

Document the object, its history, identifying features and evidence. Do not publish gutshots, schematics, PCB layouts, complete bills of materials or cloning instructions.
