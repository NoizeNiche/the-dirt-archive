# YARP Batch 12 — Major Lineage Audit

## Purpose
Reconcile high-impact relationships across BOSS/Roland, Nisshin Onpa/Maxon/Ibanez, and Sola Sound/Colorsound without collapsing distinct retail or production states.

## Findings

### BOSS / Roland
BOSS's own compact-series history anchors the family at OD-1 in 1977, followed by DS-1 in 1978. Anniversary and one-time reissue products such as the 2017 OD-1/PH-1/SP-1 box set and DS-1-4A are separate retail states rather than original-production continuations. The archive should treat BOSS as the canonical builder/brand entity with Roland relationship metadata, not merge anniversary states into the lifetime model count.

### Nisshin Onpa / Maxon / Ibanez
Maxon's current distributor documentation explicitly states that Nisshin Onpa/Maxon designed and manufactured many Ibanez products through early 2002, including TS808, TS-9, Flying Pan and SoundTank products. The OD808/TS808 pairing is therefore a manufacturer/retail cross-brand relationship, not two independent physical lineages. Product-specific exceptions still need their own evidence review, particularly within the SoundTank family.

### Sola Sound / Colorsound
The Tone Bender family is a network of canonical model states plus OEM-marketed states. The archive now separates MkI, collector-named Mk1.5, Professional MkII, MkIII, MkIV/Tone-Bender Fuzz, and the later Supa Tone Bender/Jumbo Tone Bender family. Vox, Marshall, Rotosound, Park and Carlsbro remain searchable marketed/OEM nodes. Later BOSS TB-2W is a modern Sola Sound collaboration/revival state, not an original production generation.

## Editorial rule reinforced
A shared design family does not mean shared manufacturer attribution at every date, and a shared brand name does not mean the same physical product. The archive must preserve manufacturer, marketed brand, design lineage, and production state as separate fields.
