# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 54: Universal Amplifier Corp. / Astro Amp

## Scope
Builder-first catalog completion for the obscure New York manufacturer behind the Astrotone and Sam Ash Fuzzz Boxx family.

## Builder identity
Universal Amplifier Corp., also associated with Astro Amp, is best treated as a distinct New York City physical manufacturer. Fuzzboxes documents company literature carrying an address at 615 West 131st Street, New York City, and identifies Universal Amplifier as the maker of the Astrotone and the Sam Ash Fuzzz Boxx family.

The company remains historically obscure and its full corporate chronology is unresolved. The archive should therefore preserve the manufacturer identity without inventing ownership or successor relationships.

## Enumerated dirt catalog

### 1. Astrotone
The core Universal Amplifier dirt product. Fuzzboxes documents an earliest known surviving example stamped February 1967, followed by March 1967 production states and later 1969 date-coded examples. This supports a late-1960s production corridor rather than a single fixed launch date.

The Astrotone was a three-control fuzz/distortion device sold under the Astro Amp identity. Fuzzboxes' study of approximately forty surviving Astrotone/Fuzzz Boxx examples shows multiple production states over time.

### 2. Sam Ash Fuzzz Boxx / Fuzz Box
This is a private-label/OEM state of the same Universal Amplifier product family. Sam Ash is the retail/house-brand identity; Universal Amplifier is the physical manufacturer.

Fuzzboxes documents 1967 Sam Ash examples sharing the same boards and core parts as contemporary Astrotones, including dated May 1967 production examples. The U.S. trademark record for SAM ASH FUZZZ BOXX states first use in commerce on December 31, 1967. The differing evidence types should remain separate rather than forcing one exact introduction date.

### 3. Sam Ash Ashtrotone enclosure state
Fuzzboxes documents Sam Ash-branded units housed in the same basic enclosure style as black Astrotones during the late-1960s production run. This should be represented as a production/enclosure state, not counted as a separate underlying pedal model.

## Production corridor

The strongest present evidence is:

**February 1967:** earliest known surviving Astrotone production stamp.

**March 1967:** subsequent Astrotone production state.

**May 1967:** dated Sam Ash Fuzzz Boxx production examples.

**1969:** surviving Astrotones/Fuzzz Boxxes with 1969 component date codes demonstrate later production.

**Late 1960s or early 1970s:** Fuzzboxes reports that production eventually ceased, but the precise endpoint remains unresolved.

## Relationship discipline

Do not merge Universal Amplifier Corp. with Sam Ash. Sam Ash was a retail/house-brand customer, while Universal Amplifier is identified as the manufacturer.

Do not merge Universal Amplifier with Applied Audio Products or UMI merely because all three operated in the New York/New Jersey regional fuzz economy. Their manufacturing identities remain separate.

Do not count the 2013 Sam Ash Fuzzz Boxx reissue as a vintage Universal Amplifier production state. Modern reissue manufacture is a separate historical layer.

## Historical significance

The Astrotone/Fuzzz Boxx family is important to the archive because it represents an unusually early U.S. fuzz/distortion product with a clearly documented New York manufacturing address and a long-enough production run to expose multiple factory states. It also demonstrates why the archive needs separate builder and marketed-brand nodes: the exact same underlying product family appeared under Astro Amp and Sam Ash identities.

## Evidence state

**Verified/evidence-qualified:** Universal Amplifier Corp. as a New York manufacturer; Astrotone existence and late-1960s production; Sam Ash Fuzzz Boxx relationship; multiple dated production states; Universal Amplifier service/manufacturer address.

**Unresolved:** exact company formation date; complete amplifier/effects catalog beyond the documented Astrotone family; exact final production year; corporate endpoint/successor.
