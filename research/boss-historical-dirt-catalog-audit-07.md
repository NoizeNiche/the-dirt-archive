# BOSS 1990s Dirt Chronology Audit 07

## Scope

This pass audits the BOSS compact-series dirt corridor from 1990 through 1999 using BOSS's official 40th Anniversary Compact Pedals chronology and current historical publications. It is intentionally catalog-level, not circuit-level.

## Verified official chronology

| Model | Official BOSS corridor | Archive treatment |
|---|---|---|
| OS-2 OverDrive/Distortion | 1990-present | Separate retail model; long-running lineage |
| MT-2 Metal Zone | 1991-present | Separate retail model; major high-gain lineage |

BOSS also explicitly identifies DF-2 as a 1984-era historical distortion model and HM-2 as a 1983-1992 model. The newer BOSS PX-1 article gives DF-2 a 1984-1994 corridor, while the older official 40th-anniversary chronology gives 1984-1993. The archive retains that endpoint conflict for later reconciliation.

## Important findings

### 1. 1990 was not a simple continuation of the 1980s
The OS-2 begins a distinct combined OverDrive/Distortion branch in the official chronology. It should not be represented merely as another OD-1/SD-1 descendant.

### 2. BOSS's 1993-1999 catalog contains three separate high-interest dirt branches
FZ-2, SD-2 and HM-3 appear in the same compact-era window, but they represent three distinct retail products. The archive should preserve each model independently and connect them only through chronology/family metadata.

### 3. FZ-2 to FZ-3 is an unusually clean fuzz succession
BOSS officially places FZ-2 at 1993-1997 and FZ-3 at 1997-1999. This is strong evidence for a consecutive compact fuzz lineage rather than a vague 1990s fuzz category.

### 4. The 1996-1998 short-run cluster deserves specimen-level work later
PW-2 and XT-2 are unusually short-lived products. BOSS's 2026 PX-1 historical articles revisit both, confirming their historical status. Their short production windows make them useful targets for future enclosure, labeling, regional catalog and surviving-specimen research, but that work should wait until the builder catalog is breadth-complete.

### 5. Modern historical reissues must stay distinct
The current BOSS PX-1 Plugout FX initiative in 2026 is explicitly built around recreating historic BOSS models. These modern digital products are historical-model representations, not evidence that the original physical production dates should be extended.

## Source conflict ledger

- **OD-1:** BOSS's official 40th-anniversary chronology says 1977-1988. Other historical references may give shorter original-production corridors. Do not overwrite the official chronology without reconciliation.
- **DF-2:** official 40th-anniversary chronology says 1984-1993; BOSS's 2026 PX-1 article says 1984-1994. Preserve both until primary service/catalog records resolve the endpoint.
- **PW-2:** official 40th-anniversary chronology says 1996-1997. Some secondary references circulate 1998 as an endpoint; the archive should prefer the official chronology unless stronger primary evidence emerges.

## Catalog status recommendation

BOSS is now **substantially enumerated at the retail-model level through the late 1990s**, but not specimen-complete. The remaining research priority is reconciliation of period catalogs, service-history dating, regional production states, and exact generation/enclosure transitions. No circuit reconstruction is necessary for the public archive.
