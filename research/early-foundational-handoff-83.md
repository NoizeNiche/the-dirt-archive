# Early Foundational Handoff 83

## Pass 35: 1990s boutique transition, first-generation Lovetone, Way Huge, Z.Vex, Analog Man and Klon anchors

Pass 35 begins the planned systematic descent from the late-2000s boutique corridor into the 1990s transition that helped form the modern boutique pedal ecosystem. The research emphasis shifts from catalog expansion toward builder-origin stories, first-production states, tiny runs, and explicit distinctions between prototype/design-origin history and regular retail production.

### Newly documented

- **Lovetone founding state**: Dan Coggins and Vlad Naslas met in early 1994; Lovetone was founded in 1995.
- **Lovetone Big Cheese**: 1995 original production family, with Dan Coggins tracing its design history to an earlier BC109 fuzz experiment.
- **Lovetone Brown Source**: 1995 original overdrive family, later combined with Big Cheese in the Cheese Source.
- **Lovetone Ring Stinger**: 1998-1999 ring-modulator/octave-fuzz family state.
- **Lovetone Cheese Source**: 1999 combination model combining the Big Cheese and Brown Source identities in one enclosure.
- **Z.Vex Octane**: June 1995 first shipped Z.Vex effect, establishing the company's first-product clock.
- **Z.Vex Fuzz Factory**: November 1995 original design-origin state, preserving the earliest hand-painted lineage before later Vexter production.
- **Way Huge original Fuzz Box**: February 1992 three-unit silicon fuzz run, retained as a pre-catalog specimen state.
- **Way Huge Red Llama**: 1992 early production overdrive and first major catalog model.
- **Way Huge Pro Overdrive II**: 1994 tiny-run Red Llama-derived overdrive state with estimated single-digit production.
- **Way Huge Swollen Pickle**: late-1990s original family, with a surviving May 31, 1998 specimen providing a concrete California-era production anchor.
- **Analog Man early modification/dealer state**: early-1990s vintage-effects dealing and TS9/808 modification work precede the later branded pedal manufacturing identity.
- **Klon Centaur**: 1994 early boutique overdrive production anchor associated with Bill Finnegan.

### Key research findings

1. **The 1990s boutique boom has multiple origin clocks.** Lovetone, Z.Vex, Way Huge and Analog Man demonstrate different paths into the market: partnership and design experimentation, accidental retail demand, tiny independent runs, and restoration/modification work evolving into branded manufacture.
2. **Lovetone is an unusually strong bridge between vintage revival and boutique invention.** The company began with recognizably vintage-rooted ideas but turned them into large, highly controllable products with distinctive identities and later family combinations.
3. **Way Huge shows how tiny runs can become historical objects.** The February 1992 Fuzz Box and 1994 Pro Overdrive II were extremely small runs, so specimen-level evidence is more meaningful than assuming a normal production lifecycle.
4. **Z.Vex established a different kind of boutique identity.** Octane provides the first shipped-product clock, while Fuzz Factory preserves a November 1995 design-origin moment and a later hand-painted-versus-Vexter production distinction.
5. **Analog Man needs to be modeled as a business evolution, not simply a pedal brand.** Vintage dealing and modification work are historically prior to later full-time branded pedal production, and the archive should retain that progression.
6. **Klon gives the transition a landmark overdrive state.** The 1994 Centaur belongs to the same small-builder surge but represents a different philosophy from the fuzz-heavy builders around it.

### Evidence discipline

- Builder interviews and manufacturer-authored histories are preferred for origin and chronology claims.
- Surviving specimens are used as concrete production anchors but are not assumed to represent complete runs.
- Tiny-run and prototype objects are kept separate from ordinary retail families.
- Design-origin, company-founding, first-shipped-product, first-documented-production and later revision clocks remain distinct.
- Later reissues and current product pages do not rewrite original 1990s states.
- The archive continues to avoid schematics, PCB layouts, full BOMs, gutshot libraries and cloning instructions.

### Current lineage position

The archive now has a documented bridge from the 1990s boutique emergence into the 2000s expansion. The key transition builders represented here show several distinct models of independent pedal making: Lovetone's collaborative experimental workshop, Way Huge's tiny-run garage operation, Z.Vex's rapid original-design production, Analog Man's vintage-effects/modification path, and Klon's focused overdrive niche.
