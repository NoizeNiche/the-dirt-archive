# Electro-Harmonix historical dirt catalog audit 06

## Scope
Breadth-first audit of early Electro-Harmonix overdrive, distortion, fuzz, booster, and dirt-adjacent products, with priority on period catalog and advertising evidence. No circuit-reconstruction material is included.

## Primary anchors

Electro-Harmonix's own historical catalog page identifies the 1970s black-and-white catalog as a contemporary product document and specifically notes Big Muff and LPB-1 production during the period. The surviving 1970 catalog page provides dated product numbers and prices for several early dirt products.

The 1970 catalog page documents:
- EH-2001 LPB-1 Plug into Amp.
- EH-2002 LPB-1 Plug into Inst.
- EH-2003 Mole
- EH-2005 Screaming Bird Plug into Amp.
- EH-2006 Screaming Bird Plug into Inst.
- EH-2008 Muff Fuzz Plug into Amp. or Inst.
- EH-3003 Big Muff Pi

This is important because the archive can now distinguish the early plug-in/inline product states from later stompbox catalog identities instead of treating all LPB/Muff products as one undifferentiated model.

## Early dirt catalog states

### LPB-1 Linear Power Booster
The 1970 catalog establishes separate amp-input and instrument-input product numbers. Later EHX literature retains the LPB-1 as a foundational product. Treat the two early catalog SKUs as retail/application states under one LPB-1 family, not as unrelated designs.

### Screaming Bird
The 1970 catalog establishes separate Plug into Amp and Plug into Inst. catalog entries. Preserve those application-specific retail states in the archive and avoid collapsing them into a single specimen state.

### Mole
The 1970 catalog establishes Mole as an early EHX bass-oriented boost product. Keep it in the dirt-adjacent catalog because EHX later continues to classify the Mole within its bass/gain history.

### Muff Fuzz
The 1970 catalog establishes Muff Fuzz as EH-2008 and confirms it as an early product alongside the LPB-1, Mole and Screaming Bird. Later historical sources document multiple Muff Fuzz physical/marketing states. These should be modeled as variants of the Muff Fuzz family rather than automatically split into unrelated products.

### Big Muff Pi
The 1970 catalog establishes Big Muff Pi as EH-3003 with a contemporary list price. Secondary historical chronology places later 1970s Big Muff visual/product generations across Triangle, Ram's Head, red/black, and Op-Amp-era states. The archive should treat those as separate generations under one Big Muff family, with exact first/last production dates remaining evidence-qualified where sources disagree.

### Little Muff / Little Big Muff
Secondary historical evidence identifies Little Muff Pi and Little Big Muff as distinct smaller-format members of the Muff family, including multiple visual states. A specialist Big Muff historical source reports February 1977 catalog pricing for Little Big Muff Pi and distinguishes Little Muff, Little Big Muff, and Muff Fuzz model states. These are retained as historical catalog records pending direct page-level primary catalog confirmation.

### Hot Tubes
EHX's own historical page states that the Hot Tubes was introduced in 1978. Preserve the original 1978 pedal as a distinct historical production state from the later reissue. The current EHX product page explicitly describes the modern Hot Tubes as a reissue of the 1970s original.

## 1978 period advertising confirmation

A February 23, 1978 Down Beat advertisement reproduces EHX product copy and identifies Muff Fuzz as a combined fuzz/wah/volume product in a large-format pedal form, while also advertising other contemporary EHX effects. This establishes that the Muff Fuzz name could appear in more than one physical product format and that the archive must use SKU/form-factor/date as part of identity resolution.

## 1970s product-family caution

The early EHX dirt catalog is not reducible to Big Muff alone. At minimum, the period evidence supports a layered family structure containing:
- LPB-1
- Screaming Bird
- Mole
- Muff Fuzz
- Big Muff Pi
- Little Muff Pi
- Little Big Muff Pi
- Hot Tubes

Additional fuzz/boost products should be added only after period literature or strong historical evidence is located. The archive should not use later reissues as evidence that an identically named early product existed in the same form or period.

## Historical gap confirmed

An EHX forum discussion specifically notes that researchers were unable to locate an early-1980s catalog covering the 1980-84 period and identifies rare products that appear to have been released around that gap. This is useful negative evidence: absence of an easily accessible catalog is itself a research-state note, not permission to infer a complete 1980-84 catalog from later sources.

## Current research state

This pass strengthens the early EHX catalog anchors but does not declare the builder complete. Next breadth work should target:
1. 1971-1977 catalog pages for every dirt-named product and smaller-format Muff family member.
2. The missing 1980-1984 literature corridor.
3. EHX/Guild/Foxey Lady manufacturing and marketing relationships.
4. Russian/Sovtek/NYC/U.S. Big Muff production-era reconciliation.
5. Short-lived early-1980s gain products and regional/distributor catalog entries.
