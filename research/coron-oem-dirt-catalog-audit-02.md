# Coron OEM dirt catalog audit 02

## Scope
Historical Coron / Coron-associated Japanese OEM dirt products, focusing on evidence that connects multiple marketed brands to a common manufacturing lineage. No circuit reconstruction is included.

## Core finding
Effects Database documents a Japanese-made Coron distortion family from the late 1970s and early 1980s. The same family appears under Coron, Grant, Storm, Asama, Memphis and other marketed names. Preserve these as separate marketed-brand/product nodes linked to a common Coron manufacturing relationship rather than treating each name as an independent builder.

## Dirt products / named states
The surviving indexed evidence includes:
- Coron Distortion
- Coron Distortion 10
- Coron Distortion 15
- Asama Engineering Over Drive
- Asama-branded Coron-related distortion/overdrive states
- HMI Distortion 10
- KMD Distortion
- Grant / Storm / Memphis branded equivalents documented through the same OEM family

The exact product naming conventions vary between sources. Where a numerical suffix such as 10 or 15 is explicitly used, keep the retail model distinction even when manufacturing lineage is shared.

## Chronology
The strongest public dating places the family in the late 1970s to early 1980s. Avoid assigning one production span to every OEM badge without specimen or catalog evidence.

## Evidence hierarchy
- VERIFIED_RELATIONSHIP: Coron identified as Japanese manufacturer for the shared distortion family.
- MARKETED_PRODUCT: Coron, Asama, KMD, HMI, Grant, Storm, Memphis and related branded examples where independently documented.
- UNRESOLVED: precise chronology by badge; whether every surviving badge belongs to exactly the same production run or multiple Coron sub-series.

## Why this matters to the archive
This is a model example of why the Dirt Archive must separate manufacturer, marketed brand, and OEM customer. A single Japanese factory can create a surprisingly large apparent catalog once export names are counted. The archive should count the products without multiplying the factories.
