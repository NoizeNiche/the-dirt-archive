# Early Foundational Handoff 86

## Pass 38: early-2000s boutique expansion, Japanese independent builders, and the modification-to-product bridge

Pass 38 continues the descent through the 1990s into the first years of the 2000s, emphasizing builders whose histories preserve a visible transition from small-shop experimentation, modification work, or specialist Japanese manufacturing into recognizable branded product families.

### Newly documented

- **Fulltone '69 Fuzz early state**: period research identifies 1994 examples with handwritten branding, useful as an early specimen-facing production marker within the larger '69 family.
- **Fulltone Full-Drive 2**: Fulltone's own documentation preserves the 1993 raw-aluminium Fulldrive origin and the later transition into the orange-steel Fulldrive2 with a boost footswitch; early FD2 behavior is kept distinct from later revisions.
- **Prescription Electronics RX Overdriver**: the 1990s original family is preserved as the company's Colorsound Overdriver-inspired drive/fuzz continuation after the Yardbox.
- **Prescription Electronics C.O.B.**: the Clean Octave Blend is documented as an original 1990s octave-fuzz family distinct from its later reissue state.
- **Frantone Hep Cat**: Fran Blanche's firsthand account gives a late-1995 production anchor.
- **Frantone Peachfuzz**: the 1997 product-era anchor is preserved from Blanche's firsthand history.
- **Menatone Red**: Menatone's own history places its first branded pedal in 1996 and identifies it as the lineage that became Red Snapper.
- **Menatone Blue Collar**: Japanese retail history records 1997 development in response to requests for more gain and compression than the Red.
- **Menatone Red Snapper**: Menatone explicitly separates the 1996 Red launch from the Red Snapper's 1998 NAMM debut.
- **Providence Stampede OD SOV-1**: builder interview material gives a 1997 development corridor and 1998 first-product release, described elsewhere as the brand's first mass-produced pedal.
- **Sobbat DB-01 Drive Breaker**: Sobbat's own history establishes the Kyoto builder in 1995, while specialist documentation identifies DB-01 as an original 1995 dirt pedal.
- **Keeley Compressor**: Robert Keeley's firsthand account dates the first production example to September 2001, establishing an important modification-to-branded-product boundary even though compression is outside the central dirt count.
- **Barber Direct Drive V1**: surviving period catalog evidence places V1 in 2003-2004, providing a clearly documented early-2000s boutique bridge after the 1990s foundation.

### Key research findings

1. **The boutique transition becomes an ecosystem.** Fulltone, Menatone, Providence and Sobbat show different paths into small-scale pedal manufacturing: vintage reconstruction, individual point-to-point work, Japanese high-quality manufacturing, and specialist effects building all coexist.
2. **Brand identity and model identity are separate layers.** Menatone's 1996 Red and 1998 Red Snapper should not be flattened into a single date, just as Fulltone's 1993 Fulldrive origin should not be merged blindly with later Full-Drive 2 versions.
3. **Japan deserves first-class archival treatment.** Providence and Sobbat demonstrate that the independent-effects story of the 1990s was not only an American phenomenon. Their documented 1995-1998 clocks are strong enough to become builder-first anchors.
4. **Modification culture remains a key feeder system.** Keeley's September 2001 Compressor production date follows earlier mod work and shows how a service/modification reputation could become a branded production identity.
5. **Specimen evidence is most useful when attached to a dated production corridor.** The early Fulltone '69 and Menatone/Providence/Sobbat anchors give future identification work concrete periods rather than timeless model names.
6. **Early-2000s records can be used as bridge records.** Barber's 2003-2004 Direct Drive is beyond the core 1990s target but belongs in the chronology because it shows how the boutique-drive field expanded immediately after the foundational decade.

### Evidence discipline

- Builder-authored histories are preferred for launch timing and product-family relationships.
- Period retailer or database evidence is used to support specimen windows when primary launch documents are absent.
- Exact dates are not inferred from modern resale pages unless the source itself documents an explicit historical dating method.
- Modification history is kept distinct from branded retail product chronology.
- Non-dirt products such as compressors remain scope-boundary records and do not inflate the central dirt count.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries or cloning instructions are added.

### Current lineage position

The archive now has a continuous bridge from first-wave 1990s boutique builders into the first years of the 2000s. The next useful step is not to sprint forward, but to deepen the production branches already uncovered: Fulltone's early fuzz and Fulldrive revisions, Menatone's early families, Prescription Electronics' remaining original catalog, Providence's early SOV/SDT line, Sobbat's Drive Breaker variants, and the early-generation catalog of builders who emerged immediately around the 2000 threshold.
