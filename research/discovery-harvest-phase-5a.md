# The Dirt Archive — Discovery Harvest Phase 5A

This pass expands the discovery universe using current Effects Database catalog pages. The purpose is preservation and later normalization, not publication-ready historical claims. Small builders, discontinued products, variants, and collaboration records are intentionally retained as discovery leads.

## Vintage oddities worth dedicated research

### Sangil / Iron Butterfly Fuzz Wah
Effects Database documents an unusual Iron Butterfly Fuzz Wah from old Ed Sanner stock. The page says Sangil was a short-lived company started around 1970 by former Mosrite personnel Ed Sanner and Ken Gilstrap, with Sanner handling electronics and Gilstrap sales. The page also records that only a handful of Iron Butterfly pedals were reportedly made and sold before the proposed deal failed. This should become a specimen/provenance research target, not a generic production model assumption.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/unknown/usa/ironbutterfly

## Catalog-growth implications

This phase reinforces a key strategy: Effects Database is extremely useful for identifying builders and model names that deserve preservation, including builders with only 9–20 recorded products. The public Dirt Archive can later promote these records after normalization without requiring every product to have a complete production history on day one.

## Immediate next targets

1. Harvest the next blocks of Effects Database builder catalogs, prioritizing small/defunct builders.
2. Capture product names first; research chronology second.
3. Preserve collaborations, artist editions, and variant names as separate discovery records when the source distinguishes them.
4. Build a dedicated builder alias/OEM relationship table before attempting large-scale public promotion.
5. Continue expanding the discovery layer toward thousands before doing deep evidence cleanup.
