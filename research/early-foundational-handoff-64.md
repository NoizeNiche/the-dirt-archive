# Early Foundational Handoff 64

## Pass 14: Japan/Taiwan BOSS split, Power Series dirt, and programmable fuzz/digital chronology

This pass adds ten reconciliation records focused on the late-1980s through mid-1990s moment when regional BOSS production states, specialist Ibanez Power Series models, programmable digital distortion, and short-run fuzz/metal products occupied the same market.

### Newly documented

- **BOSS HM-2 Heavy Metal**: Japan and Taiwan production states separated, with historical evidence placing the Japan period through 1988 and Taiwan production through 1991.
- **BOSS DS-2 TURBO Distortion**: Japan and Taiwan states separated around the 1987 release and 1989 production transition.
- **Ibanez DS10 Distortion Charger**: 1986-1990 Power Series window strengthened with a dated Japan example.
- **Ibanez MT10 Mostortion**: 1990-1993 short production run anchored by multiple specialist references.
- **DigiTech PDS1550 Programmable Distortion**: contemporary June 1987 advertising establishes a strong documentary date and identifies the unit as a programmable dual-distortion/overdrive product with delay.
- **BOSS FZ-2 HYPER Fuzz**: official BOSS chronology places production in 1993-1997.
- **BOSS HM-3 HYPER Metal**: Roland support documentation places production in 1993-1999.
- **BOSS OD-2R TURBO OverDrive**: official BOSS chronology places production in 1994-1999.

### Key research findings

1. **BOSS manufacturing migration must be modeled by product.** HM-2 and DS-2 both cross the Japan/Taiwan boundary at different moments, so a universal company-wide switch date is too crude for object identification.
2. **Official chronology is becoming a backbone source.** BOSS and Roland documentation supplies production windows that can anchor later specimen-level country and hardware observations.
3. **Ibanez's dirt history broadens beyond Tube Screamer.** DS10 and MT10 demonstrate a parallel Power Series lineage that deserves the same generation-oriented treatment as TS9 and TS10.
4. **Programmable distortion is a legitimate historical branch.** The PDS1550 belongs in the archive because it documents a 1987 transition toward programmable guitar dirt rather than a fixed single-effect stompbox.
5. **The 1990s compact market produced short-lived specialist dirt boxes.** FZ-2, HM-3, and MT10 are useful examples of narrow production windows that later became highly identifiable collector states.
6. **External identification is sufficient.** Country marking, series name, enclosure, control layout, dated ads/catalogs, product format, and model-specific chronology provide useful generation clues without exposing circuit-reconstruction material.

### Evidence discipline

- **VERIFIED** is reserved for manufacturer or contemporary documentary support.
- **RESEARCH** remains appropriate for specialist chronology, regional transitions, and collector/specimen observations.
- Country of origin is treated as a specimen attribute unless a dated factory transition is explicitly documented.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries, or cloning instructions are added.
