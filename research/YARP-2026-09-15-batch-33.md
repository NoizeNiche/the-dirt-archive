# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 33

## Focus
Japanese Standard Fuzz / Excetro retail-name reconciliation and manufacturer-attribution caution.

## Findings

### Standard Fuzz family
Japanese specialist retail evidence identifies a 1970s Ibanez Standard Fuzz as a Japanese-made product associated with Nisshin Onpa / Maxon manufacturing, while historical effects references document the same broad product form under names including Mica, Bruno, Aria Diamond and other export labels.

### Excetro / Teisco complication
Effects Database separately catalogs the Excetro Fuzz Machine and Teisco Fuzz Machine with overlapping marketed names and OEM-family claims. These sources are useful for discovery, but they conflict on whether Shin-Ei, Teisco or a mixed OEM chain should be regarded as the sole physical manufacturer. The archive therefore does **not** collapse every Standard Fuzz, Excetro, Teisco, Mica, Bruno or Aria Diamond unit into one canonical factory record.

### Evidence-qualified relationship
The safest current model is to preserve a Japanese Standard Fuzz / dual-mode octave-fuzz product family with model-specific manufacturer fields. Nisshin Onpa is strongly supported for the Ibanez export-branded Standard Fuzz by Japanese specialist evidence. Teisco/Excetro relationships remain model-specific and evidence-qualified.

### Why this matters
This family is a good example of why enclosure similarity and shared controls are not enough to assign one factory across an entire export ecosystem. The archive should index every marketed identity for search while preserving manufacturer uncertainty at the individual product-state level.

## Evidence boundary
No schematics, PCB layouts, BOMs, gutshots, cloning instructions or circuit reconstruction material added.
