# Early Foundational Handoff 99

## Pass 51: 2018-2020 boutique expansion, pandemic-era production, and the normalization of multi-function dirt

Pass 51 extends the archive through the 2019-2020 acceleration and backfills useful 2016-2018 production boundaries. This pass emphasizes series launches, documented small-builder beginnings, crossover between fuzz/overdrive/distortion roles, and the way physical production and market positioning became part of a pedal's identity.

### Newly documented or clarified

- **JHS Bonsai**: JHS history dates the Bonsai debut to the January 2018 NAMM Show. It follows the Muffuletta pattern of consolidating multiple historical drive voices into a single modern production pedal.
- **JHS Legends of Fuzz**: JHS dates the series release to May 15, 2020, explicitly framing it around obscure and hard-to-find vintage fuzz designs. This is treated as a series-level chronology boundary rather than a substitute for individual model records.
- **JHS 3 Series**: JHS dates the 3 Series launch to October 2, 2020. The line establishes a new lower-price tier and a major catalog/manufacturing-position transition during the pandemic year.
- **Walrus 385 Overdrive**: Walrus states that the original 385 was released in 2016. The first generation is kept separate from the later 385 MKII with preset-style A/B controls and 385+ switching.
- **Walrus Ages**: current Walrus product history says the company had not released another overdrive since 2016 before developing Ages; contemporary coverage places Ages in the 2019 period. The archive keeps 2019 as a release-period anchor pending stronger exact launch-day evidence.
- **Audio Disruption Devices Failed Transmission**: May 2020 specialist coverage documents this small-quantity Indiana builder's fuzz/overdrive with a blendable oscillator as a public-production state.
- **Tsakalis AudioWorks Six B.O.D.**: January 2019 NAMM coverage establishes the product announcement period for a multi-mode boutique drive combining several classic overdrive/distortion roles in one enclosure.
- **McGregor Crunch Transparent Overdrive**: McGregor's own later press material states that the original low-gain Crunch Transparent Overdrive was released in December 2019, establishing the company's first documented product.
- **McGregor Crunch Plus Overdrive**: the same builder statement dates the medium-gain follow-up to December 2020, preserving a clean two-model launch sequence.
- **Sitek Guitar Electronics**: a 2019 builder profile states that Andrzej Sitek began experimenting in 2005 and launched Sitek in autumn 2016. The documented catalog includes handmade fuzz, fuzz-distortion and overdrive models, strengthening the Polish independent-builder network.
- **Tone Electronix Animalizzer**: Guitar World reported on December 15, 2020 that Portuguese boutique builder Tone Electronix had unveiled the handcrafted Animalizzer, an all-in-one fuzz/overdrive/distortion pedal.
- **Danelectro 3699 Fuzz / Roebuck Distortion**: January 2020 NAMM coverage shows a larger manufacturer adopting boutique-style vintage-replication presentation. These remain scope-boundary records rather than boutique-builder entries.

### Key research findings

1. **The multi-model-in-one-box trend is now a historical pattern.** Muffuletta, Bonsai, Six B.O.D. and other contemporary pedals show a market increasingly comfortable with compact historical catalogs and multiple gain identities inside one enclosure.
2. **The boutique label increasingly overlaps with formal production.** By 2019-2020, independent builders were still working in tiny quantities while also presenting polished product families, preorder systems, and consistent retail catalogs.
3. **Pandemic-era catalog changes deserve their own chronology.** JHS's May and October 2020 releases demonstrate that 2020 was not simply a production interruption; it was also a major product-strategy year.
4. **The fuzz/overdrive/distortion boundary keeps getting blurrier.** Failed Transmission and Animalizzer make the category itself part of the identification problem, so the archive should record the maker's own category language alongside its dirt classification.
5. **Physical generations continue to matter even when the circuit's role is stable.** Walrus 385's transition from the 2016 original to the later MKII illustrates why enclosure controls and switching architecture can define separate production generations.
6. **Small international builders should be cataloged before they disappear from view.** Sitek, Tone Electronix and McGregor are useful examples of regional builders whose early public documentation may be less durable than major-brand archives.
7. **Scope boundaries provide industry context.** Danelectro's 2020 vintage-replication releases belong in the broader dirt-history narrative without distorting the central boutique-builder count.

### Evidence discipline

- Manufacturer-authored histories remain preferred for first-release and product-series clocks.
- Contemporary trade coverage is retained as announcement or public-availability evidence, not automatically as exact factory chronology.
- Later retrospective pages are not silently converted into earlier undocumented dates.
- Builder category terminology and archive classification are kept distinct when a product straddles fuzz, overdrive and distortion.
- Series-level events remain separate from individual model-level first-sale records.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries or cloning instructions are published.

### Current lineage position

The archive now crosses from the 2010s boutique boom into the 2020 pandemic-era market without losing the physical-object focus. JHS demonstrates how an established boutique builder could launch entire historical fuzz series and a budget tier in the same year; Walrus provides clear original-versus-MKII boundaries; small builders in Indiana, Poland, Portugal and Canada broaden the international network; and larger manufacturers show how boutique presentation had become an industry-wide language.
