# The Dirt Archive — Obscure Vintage Fuzz Research, Phase 2A

This pass expands the historical map beyond the obvious Fuzz Face / Tone Bender / Big Muff / Tube Screamer / RAT anchors. The goal is preservation and identification, especially for obscure builders, OEM relationships, production transitions, and products whose surviving examples are easy to misidentify.

This is a research file, not a final public catalog. Dates and relationships are marked conservatively where the evidence is incomplete or contradictory.

## 1. Mosrite / Fuzzrite lineage

### Mosrite Fuzzrite
**Archive priority: Critical**

The Mosrite Fuzzrite is one of the key American early-fuzz branches and deserves its own generational record rather than one undifferentiated pedal page.

Effects Database records three broad construction generations:

- **v1:** early germanium production.
- **v2:** later silicon production using discrete parts on a phenolic board.
- **v3:** silicon production using an orange Sprague module.

The same source preserves first-person testimony from Eddie Sanner, the designer associated with the Fuzzrite, describing the cold-floor instability of the early germanium units and the subsequent switch to silicon plus the Sprague modular network. Effects Database also records a long-running disputed quantity for the earliest germanium examples and a much larger v3 production total.

**Important archive rule:** do not publish a single simplistic statement such as “the first 250 were germanium” as settled fact. The source itself indicates that the low-number estimate has been questioned by the surviving-example evidence. Record the quantity as disputed until corroborated by additional primary documentation.

### Sierra Electronics / Rosac Nu-Fuzz

Effects Database provides unusually valuable corporate continuity evidence here. Mosrite entered bankruptcy in February 1969, after which a new company called Sierra Electronics was formed using a Sierra Bag Company facility. One of the first products was the Sierra Electronics Nu-Fuzz, described as a new Ed Sanner design. Because another business already used the Sierra Electronics name, the company changed its name to **Rosac** about a month later.

The Rosac catalog later included:

- Nu-Fuzz
- Nu-Fuzz Distortion Blender
- Nu-Wa
- Nu-Wa-Fuzz
- Tremolo Unit

The Rosac history page says the company closed around 1978–79 and credits Warren Justice and Ed Sanner for oral-history information.

**Archive implication:** Sierra Electronics Nu-Fuzz and Rosac Nu-Fuzz should be linked by a company/name-transition relationship, not treated as unrelated builders. The Eddie Sanner connection also provides an explicit Mosrite → Sierra/Rosac lineage worth preserving.

### Sanner / FuzzRite reissues

Modern Sanner FuzzRite products should be modeled as later reissues/authorized or designer-linked descendants, not merged into the historical Mosrite production record. The archive should preserve the designer lineage while keeping later manufacturing periods separate.

## 2. Guild Foxey Lady: an unusually important OEM chain

### Guild Foxey Lady
**Archive priority: Critical**

Effects Database describes the Foxey Lady as a late-1960s to mid-1970s Guild-branded fuzz/distortion line built by multiple outside manufacturers. This makes it exactly the kind of product The Dirt Archive needs to document carefully.

Current research identifies at least five historical stages:

1. **Mosrite-built Foxey Lady** — an early version based on the Mosrite Fuzzrite family.
2. **AUL Instruments production** — a transitional grey enclosure associated with Mike Matthews and AUL in New York after Mosrite's bankruptcy.
3. **Early Electro-Harmonix / Axis-related Foxey Lady** — a 2-control generation tied to the emerging Electro-Harmonix production ecosystem.
4. **Guild Foxey Lady / Triangle Big Muff family** — a three-control version associated with the Electro-Harmonix V1 Big Muff Pi.
5. **Guild Foxey Lady / Ram's Head family** — later three-control production tied to the Electro-Harmonix V2 Ram's Head Big Muff period.

This is a major example of why **model name ≠ one circuit ≠ one builder**.

**Public archive treatment:** one model family with clearly separated generations and manufacturer/OEM relationships. Photographs of external identifiers are more important than detailed internal construction.

## 3. Maestro Fuzz-Tone family

### Maestro FZ-1
**Archive priority: Critical**

Effects Database identifies the Maestro FZ-1 as introduced in 1962 and associated with Gibson's Maestro brand. It describes the FZ-1 as the first commercially available fuzz pedal and records a three-stage germanium architecture, two 1.5 V batteries, hardwired input cable, and volume/power plus footswitch operation.

Production/location clues in the source include **Kalamazoo, Michigan** for the FZ-1. The source also preserves serial-number examples and annual shipment figures. These figures should be treated as archival leads rather than automatically promoted to exact audited production totals.

### FZ-1A

The FZ-1A is a distinct production generation, beginning in the mid-1960s after renewed demand for the Fuzz-Tone. Effects Database records a move to a single 1.5 V battery and Motorola germanium devices, with production moving from Kalamazoo to Lincolnwood.

**Archive priority: Critical** because location, battery configuration, serial range and exterior markings can help distinguish specimens without exposing a schematic.

### FZ-1B

The FZ-1B represents a more substantial redesign, including silicon devices and 9 V operation. It should not be treated as merely a cosmetic FZ-1A variant.

### FZ-2 / other Maestro fuzz products

Effects Database's Maestro index also includes FZ-1S and FZ-2 plus combined products such as the FP-1/2/3 Fuzz Phazzer. A future Maestro-specific research pass should map these one by one rather than assuming every “FZ” pedal is part of a single circuit lineage.

## 4. Schaller and West German OEM fuzz network

### Schaller Fuzz
**Archive priority: High**

Effects Database describes the Schaller Fuzz as a West German design produced from the mid-1960s through the 1970s and distributed both under Schaller branding and through OEM/rebrand relationships.

Known linked retail identities include:

- Schaller
- Höfner
- Blackfield
- Kent
- Van Hall

The source distinguishes earlier germanium examples from later silicon examples, making **device family as a generation-level distinguisher** appropriate when supported by an observed specimen.

### Höfner No. 539 Fuzz/Distortion

Effects Database identifies the Höfner No. 539 as a 1960s OEM pedal built by Schaller in West Germany and sharing the Schaller Fuzz family. German labels and enclosure markings are especially useful identification evidence.

### Blackfield Fuzz

Effects Database identifies the Blackfield Fuzz as a Schaller OEM version from the late 1960s / early 1970s.

### Van Hall Fuzz

Effects Database identifies the Van Hall Fuzz as another Schaller OEM family member and specifically describes silicon BC109 examples. This is useful because it demonstrates that two pedals with superficially similar German enclosure forms may have different documented branding and production-device histories.

**Archive implication:** create a **Schaller Fuzz family** entity and connect these retail products to it. Do not create unrelated standalone “Schaller-style” entries when the evidence supports actual OEM continuity.

## 5. Elka / Italian fuzz branch

### Elka Dizzy Tone
**Archive priority: High**

Effects Database documents the Elka Dizzy Tone as a historically important Italian fuzz associated with the Tone Bender MkIII branch. It is closely tied in the historical ecosystem to Baldwin-Burns Buzzaround variants and the Italian Vox/JEN network.

A dedicated Elka pass should establish:

- enclosure variants
- badge/label changes
- relationship to JEN/Elka retail channels
- chronology relative to Buzzaround/Burns products
- whether “Dizzy Tone” naming was consistently applied across all surviving examples

Modern reissues such as Jext Telez should be separately modeled as descendants/interpretations, not historical Elka production.

## 6. Baldwin-Burns Buzzaround

### Baldwin-Burns Buzzaround
**Archive priority: High**

Effects Database preserves a November 1966 Beat Instrumental advertisement stating that a new Baldwin-Burns fuzz box had been introduced as a fresh version of the Buzz-Around with a special sustain effect and a listed price of 10 guineas.

This is valuable primary-period evidence because it fixes the product to a dated publication rather than relying only on modern recollection.

**Archive implication:** the 1966 advertisement should be stored as a source record attached to the relevant claim. The exact internal differences between earlier/later Buzzaround-branded examples should be researched separately.

## 7. Vox / JEN / Italian production transition

### Vox V828 / V8281 Tone Bender family

Effects Database identifies the V8281 as a late-1960s/early-1970s Italian-made Vox fuzz produced by JEN. It explicitly distinguishes V8281 from V828 and describes the V8281 as a two-transistor positive-ground germanium design with Italian SFT-family devices in many surviving examples.

Other research suggests the early V828 production history may involve Eko before JEN. Because the transition is not yet adequately supported by primary documentation in this pass, **Eko → JEN should remain Disputed/Reported rather than Confirmed**.

### Jen Fuzz and later labels

Effects Database documents a late-1960s Jen Fuzz connected to the Vox Tone Bender production family. Later retail names include Elka, Luxor, Nova and Unicord.

**Archive implication:** these should become linked retail/OEM models under a shared Italian production family where the evidence supports equivalence.

## 8. Kay F-1 Fuzz Tone

### Kay F-1
**Archive priority: Medium-High**

Effects Database identifies the Kay F-1 as a rare Japanese fuzz from the late 1960s with a bright orange plastic enclosure, black rubber base and a treadle-based control scheme rather than conventional fuzz/tone/level knobs.

The unusual enclosure is an excellent public identification clue. The archive should prioritize:

- external enclosure photography
- treadle configuration
- label variants
- country-of-origin marks
- documented rebrands, if any

The source contains musical-association claims that should be independently corroborated before publicizing them as historical facts. Player associations are lower priority than product identity.

## 9. Hohner and Japanese OEM connection

### Hohner MF-50 Master Fuzz Control

Effects Database identifies the MF-50 as a Hohner-branded Super Fuzz-family pedal made by Shin-Ei and sold in an OEM network with other brand names.

Useful external identifiers include:

- Expander control/power function
- Balance control
- two-position tone switch
- overall enclosure family

This should be linked to the Japanese FY-6/Super Fuzz OEM network already mapped in `fuzz-japanese-oem-phase-1.md`, but the **Hohner retail model remains its own record**.

## 10. Teisco second-generation research notes

### Teisco TF-1

Effects Database explicitly identifies the TF-1 as a late-1960s/early-1970s Teisco-branded FY-6 Super Fuzz-family product.

### Teisco Fuzz Machine

Effects Database identifies a separate early-1970s product using a distinctive dual-slider / twin-foot-switch enclosure and distributed under several retail identities, including Ibanez Standard Fuzz Model 59, Bruno, Mica, Marlboro Wailer, Antoria and Aria Diamond.

**Critical rule:** TF-1 and Fuzz Machine must remain **separate model entities**. Their relationship should not be reduced to “both are Teisco fuzz.”

## 11. Guyatone vintage expansion

The Guyatone FS series contains several distinct dirt products, including:

- FS-1 Bazz Box
- FS-1 Buzz Box
- FS-2 Buzz Box
- FS-3 Fuzz
- FS-5 Wah-Fuzz
- FS-6 Sustainer

Effects Database also documents later products such as FZ-2000, TZ2 and TZm5. These should not be treated as one continuous family merely because they share the Guyatone brand.

### FS-3 Fuzz

Current research places the FS-3 around 1972–73 and reports a possible Honey-labeled PCB relationship. Keep that OEM relationship **Probable** until corroborated through another source or multiple specimens.

### FZ-2000

Effects Database describes the FZ-2000 as a later design inspired by historic Fuzz Face-style circuits but using FETs rather than germanium transistors. This makes it a useful example of a **historically inspired later design**, not a vintage Fuzz Face OEM rebrand.

## 12. Rosac as a preservation priority

Rosac deserves a dedicated builder/company entity because its history intersects with:

- Mosrite
- Eddie Sanner
- Sierra Electronics
- Nu-Fuzz
- Nu-Wa
- Nu-Wa-Fuzz
- Montgomery Ward distribution/contract work
- a late-1970s company closure

This is exactly the kind of builder history that can disappear when an archive only catalogs famous pedal models.

## 13. Boutique descendants worth preserving separately

The archive's research should also record modern builders when they preserve otherwise obscure historical designs, but under **modern builder → interpretation/reissue** relationships.

Examples surfaced in this pass include:

- Jext Telez Dizzy Tone — modern Elka Dizzy Tone interpretation
- Jerms FZ-1 / FZ-1A — Maestro recreations
- Ashbass Fuzzbrite — Mosrite Fuzzrite reproduction
- Jordan (by Mahoney) FUZZtite / Acid Tone — Fuzzrite-derived designs
- Burns/JMI Buzzaround reissues

These are valuable historical documentation targets because they show which obsolete circuits/designs were revived and when, but they must not be presented as original vintage production.

## 14. Reliability and evidence rules from this pass

### Confirmed / strong evidence

- Period advertisements or manufacturer statements that directly establish a date or product identity.
- First-party historical pages.
- Multiple independent surviving examples agreeing on an identifier.

### Probable

- OEM attribution supported by a strong secondary database but not yet corroborated by primary documents.
- Model-family relationships that fit enclosure, controls, branding and historical distribution patterns but still lack a definitive factory document.

### Reported / Unverified

- Exact quantities inferred from survivor counts.
- Player associations without period documentation.
- Precise date ranges based on seller lore or modern recollection alone.
