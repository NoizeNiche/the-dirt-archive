# Early Foundational Handoff 95

## Pass 47: 2009-2012 numbered micro-runs, digital-pedal boundaries, and the second boutique wave

Pass 47 advances the archive from the 2009-2012 small-batch boom into a clearer second-generation boutique landscape. The emphasis is on production clocks, numbered runs, control-layout boundaries, and the distinction between core dirt products and adjacent digital/modulation builders.

### Newly documented or clarified

- **Spaceman Rumblefuzz**: manufacturer product documentation preserves a 50-unit numbered run, with serials 00001-00005 finished in blue powdercoat with blue LED eyes and numbers 6-50 unfinished with red LEDs.
- **Spaceman Rumblefuzz first sub-group**: the first five numbered units are retained as a specimen sub-group rather than a claimed circuit revision.
- **Spaceman Mercury III**: specialist historical evidence places the boost family in 2010 with an approximately 120-unit run; production-count confidence remains secondary until stronger primary documentation appears.
- **Spaceman Gemini III**: specialist historical evidence places the fuzz in 2011 with an approximately 220-unit run.
- **Strymon blueSky**: official June 10, 2010 launch documentation establishes a clean first-generation digital-pedal boundary outside core dirt.
- **Strymon El Capistan**: 2010 development and shipping posts establish a first-generation product corridor without forcing a single launch day.
- **Walrus Audio Voyager**: builder history identifies the company start in 2011 and Voyager as the first Walrus pedal.
- **Barber Gain Changer**: December 2012 specialist publication establishes an early product-era anchor for the compact model.
- **Barber Gain Changer compact state**: the two-toggle, approximately 2.3-inch-wide format is preserved as the defining original compact specimen state, distinct from later SR-family products.
- **Hudson Broadcast Standard**: manufacturer version history explicitly identifies the single-switch dark-blue model as the first Broadcast version.
- **Hudson Broadcast Standard Dual**: later two-switch version adds independent level controls and internal gain adjustment, creating a clear external-layout generation boundary.
- **REDESIGN Percolator**: January 23, 2012 publication establishes a three-knob production state and explicitly distinguishes the added control from the underlying Harmonic Percolator interpretation.
- **REDESIGN Black Fuzz**: December 4, 2012 publication establishes a late-2012 fuzz catalog anchor.
- **6 Degrees FX Sally Drive**: September 2012 web evidence begins the public corridor, with 2013 reviews documenting a small Canadian hand-wired production environment.
- **6 Degrees FX Sally Drive early specimen**: the three-knob red race-car enclosure and front 9V jack are preserved as physical identification clues, while the internally adjustable Clean Boost remains specific to the Custom version.

### Key research findings

1. **Numbered micro-runs are now a major identification class.** Spaceman's Rumblefuzz demonstrates that serial intervals and finish/LED differences can identify early sub-groups without claiming an electronic redesign.
2. **Digital boutique builders require a distinct chronology model.** Strymon's dated press releases and development posts provide exact public anchors even when the products sit outside the archive's dirt categories.
3. **Compact enclosure transitions become increasingly visible.** Barber's Gain Changer provides a clean bridge between large-format boutique manufacturing and the smaller pedalboard-focused generation that followed.
4. **Manufacturer version pages can resolve otherwise fuzzy histories.** Hudson's Standard versus Standard Dual distinction is more useful for physical identification than an undated “Broadcast” entry.
5. **Publication evidence remains valuable when labeled correctly.** REDESIGN and 6 Degrees show how specialist publication dates can establish public catalog presence without being silently rewritten as exact factory launch dates.
6. **The second boutique generation is becoming multinational.** U.S., Canadian, UK and Pacific Northwest builders now interlock with the earlier Japanese, Scandinavian and European network.

### Evidence discipline

- Manufacturer-authored product and version histories remain preferred for production windows and generation boundaries.
- Specialist databases are used as dated public-circulation evidence or secondary production-count evidence, not silently promoted to primary sources.
- Numbering, finish, LED color, enclosure dimensions and control layouts are recorded as specimen clues when supported.
- Adjacent digital and modulation products remain scope-bound chronology where they illuminate a builder's path but do not count as core dirt.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries or cloning instructions are added.

### Current lineage position

The archive now has a strong 2009-2012 second-generation map. Spaceman represents numbered artisan micro-runs; Strymon demonstrates the digital boutique path; Walrus and Barber show early-2010s pedalboard expansion; Hudson documents an evolving single-to-dual overdrive enclosure family; and REDESIGN/6 Degrees strengthen the obscure-builder field.
