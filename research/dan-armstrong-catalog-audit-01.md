# Dan Armstrong Catalog Audit 01

## Scope
Builder/product-level audit for Dan Armstrong's plug-in effects modules, with emphasis on dirt and gain products. Preserve Dan Armstrong's design/brand role separately from manufacturing entities such as Musitronics/Mu-Tron and later reissue makers.

## Core historical line
Dan Armstrong's own recollections and later historical sources identify a family of small color-coded effects modules developed in the 1970s. A 1976 Modern Recording advertisement provides a strong period catalog anchor and names six products: Green Ringer, Yellow Humper, Purple Peeker, Red Ranger, Blue Clipper and Orange Squeezer. The advertisement states that Musitronics Corporation manufactured the Dan Armstrong Sound Modifiers.

## Manufacturing and chronology
Dan Armstrong recalled beginning the effects work in London in 1973 with electronics designer George Merriman, with Green Ringer first and Blue Clipper following. Later product-history sources place the family in both UK-made pre-Mu-Tron examples and US Musitronics production. The archive should therefore model production states by geography/manufacturer rather than treating every Armstrong module as a single manufacturing run.

Source: https://sites.google.com/site/davidmorrinoldsite/home/trouble/troubleeffects/dan-armstrong-blue-clipper

A surviving listing documents a UK-made Yellow Humper from 1975-1977, reinforcing the need for separate UK and US production-state records where specimen evidence supports them.

Source: https://reverb.com/item/87007005-vintage-dan-armstrong-yellow-humper-bass-boost-preamp-fx-unit-pedal-1970-s

## 1976 period catalog anchor
Modern Recording's February/March 1976 ad lists all six Sound Modifiers together at $29.95 each and explicitly identifies Musitronics Corporation in New Jersey as manufacturer. A separate April/May 1976 issue repeats the same six-product family. This is strong period evidence that the complete six-module family was commercially marketed by 1976.

## Dirt relevance
Blue Clipper is the central Armstrong dirt model, while Green Ringer crosses into octave fuzz/ring-mod territory. Red Ranger is a gain/boost boundary rather than a distortion model. The archive should not inflate the dirt catalog by counting Purple Peeker, Yellow Humper, or Orange Squeezer as distortion devices.

## Reissue / revival separation
Later Armstrong-branded and Dan Armstrong Enterprises products should be recorded as revival/reissue entities. Effects Database and later retailer material document reissues of the classic modules, but the original 1970s Musitronics production state must remain distinct.
