# The Dirt Archive — Boutique / One-Person Builder Discovery, Phase 3B

This research pass deliberately preserves small, discontinued, one-person, garage, and micro-run pedal builders. These are easy to erase from conventional histories even when they produced identifiable commercial models. Effects Database is being used as a discovery index; individual records should be promoted only after editorial normalization and source review.

## Why these builders matter

The archive should not equate historical importance with production volume. A builder who made 12, 30, 50, or 100 pedals under a named product line is still historically useful. A discontinued micro-builder may be especially valuable because the public record can disappear when the builder's old site goes offline.

Record:
- builder / brand name
- designer or principal builder when known
- geography when documented
- approximate operating period
- every commercially named dirt model we can identify
- limited-run and prototype names when they were actually offered or sold
- builder-to-builder relationships
- later reissues or licensed versions
- evidence state and unresolved uncertainty

Do not record a price or scarcity score merely because a collector market exists.

## High-priority one-person / micro-builder captures

### GoosoniqueWorx — Singapore
Effects Database identifies GoosoniqueWorx as a one-man operation run by Ravi Goose. The builder's own timeline says he began making gear in 2002, sold early local products, and launched the Fudge 73 in 2007, described as a Triangle Muff clone. The Pixie is described as a hybrid fuzz. The archive should preserve both even though the company is primarily remembered by many players for later high-gain pedals.

Known catalog names from the Effects Database page:
- MangoJuice — FET distortion
- Pixie — hybrid fuzz
- Rapture Overdrive
- Fudge 73 — Triangle Muff clone
- Seventheaven / 7thvn
- Kult
- Noizetherapy
- Boosty
- Blitzkrieg
- Citrus
- Tremor

Particularly valuable historical note: Ravi's interview says the early fuzz work was difficult because NOS parts were hard to acquire, and all pedals were hand-made by him in-house. The 7thvn and Kult later used US-made PCBs while remaining hand-built.

Status: strong builder-history source; individual model chronology still needs deeper capture.

This is exactly the kind of builder where "model" and "specimen / prototype" need to stay separate.

### Go deeper: 73 Effects
An archived Effects Database interview with Bryan Bell describes 73 Effects as a one-man operation working at maximum capacity and refusing to lower quality simply to increase output. Bell specifically discusses a straight-up vintage-spec Super-Fuzz-type pedal and an octave fuzz project.

This should become a builder record even if only a handful of dirt models can be fully reconstructed.

Status: strong interview lead; model list requires separate catalog capture.

### Main Ace FX — James Granuzzo
Effects Database describes Main.Ace.FX as a small garage operation in Colonia, New Jersey, co-founded with Justin. The historical interview explicitly describes cloning well-known fuzz circuits as an early learning stage before developing original modifications and circuits.

Archive value: preserve as a micro-builder / DIY-to-commercial transition, but do not publish circuit reconstruction details.

Status: strong builder-history lead; catalog extraction required.

### Oxfuzz — Ken Baluke
Effects Database interview records Oxfuzz as a one-man operation. Pedals were built and tested at the builder's home/studio, with hand wiring, vintage-style parts, and particular attention to germanium selection.

Archive value: this is precisely the sort of tiny builder likely to vanish from search results without deliberate preservation.

Status: strong builder-history evidence; model catalog needs separate extraction.

### BSM — Bernd C. Meiser
Effects Database currently lists 35 BSM products and identifies the company as a specialized German boutique builder founded by Bernd C. Meiser and his wife Steffi. It also records numerous fuzz and booster models and notes the small-scale, hand-built nature of production.

Dirt/fuzz/high-priority names include:
- FassBender Fuzz Machine
- FuzzBender Fuzz Machine
- J-Fuzz
- Spectrum Fuzz Booster
- Albuquerque
- HS Hornby-Skewes variants
- RM series
- RPA / RPA Major
- VX series
- CM 71/73
- Fireball
- Studio '75

Important preservation note: the builder is now deceased and the business is described as having ceased operations. This makes BSM a preservation priority, but the archive should separate confirmed historical facts from any later collector claims.

Status: high priority.

### SviSound — Mark Svirkov
Effects Database's archived interview says Mark Svirkov ran SviSound and moved from Russia to Varna, Bulgaria. He said he had built his first pedal in 1979 and later made pedals for friends before selling them.

Archive value: a valuable bridge into Eastern European independent pedal history, especially because the personal-builder origin predates the boutique wave commonly documented online.

Status: high priority. Catalog still needs reconstruction.

### G.S. Wyllie — Glenn S. Wyllie
Effects Database describes Wyllie as an American one-person builder and artist in North Carolina who built guitars, stringed instruments, and analog effects. It documents work beginning in the early 1960s and identifies a later pedal catalog.

Archive value: unusually early independent-builder history. Because the record crosses from homemade experiments into named effects, it deserves a dedicated historical entity rather than being treated only as a modern boutique brand.

Known public catalog count on Effects Database: 10 products.

Status: very high priority historical preservation target. Avoid overclaiming exact early dates until primary documentation is found.

### DB Design
Effects Database documents a DB Design Lemon Fuzz as a completely hand-built pedal, with an early listing dated 2007 and vintage-germanium positioning.

Archive value: an example of a micro-builder whose brand footprint may survive primarily in old database pages and dealer/collector references.

Status: candidate. Need builder identity and complete catalog.

### Purdy
Effects Database documents Purdy Fuzz as the first pedal built by Purdy and describes it as hand-built and modeled after a famous vintage fuzz.

Status: candidate micro-builder. Need builder identity, operating dates and additional models.

### Pedal Pawn
Effects Database currently documents the Silicon Fuzz as a hand-built UK fuzz designed after extensive testing of vintage fuzz faces. The entry identifies hand-selected silicon transistors and a custom voice.

Archive value: useful modern builder record, especially if version history appears later.

Status: candidate modern builder.

### SM Fuzz — Scott McKeon
Effects Database documents the SM Fuzz as a hand-built UK germanium fuzz designed and produced by guitarist Scott McKeon.

Archive value: a musician-builder crossover and a clear named product that could otherwise disappear into social-media history.

Status: candidate; capture production years and editions.

## Data-model recommendation from this pass

A tiny builder should NOT be dropped merely because Effects Database reports only 1–3 products. Instead:

Builder → Brand → Model → Edition/Run → Specimen

Use an optional `production_scale` field with values such as:
- One-off
- Micro run (<25 known/sold)
- Small run (25–100)
- Boutique run (101–1000)
- Established production (>1000 or unknown but clearly larger)
- Unknown

This is descriptive, not a rarity score.

Also add `builder_operating_status`:
- Active
- Inactive
- Discontinued
- Unknown

This would let the public archive preserve tiny builders without turning the interface into a popularity contest.

## Editorial policy

Effects Database is extremely valuable for discovery because it preserves model names, interviews, historical timelines, and builder pages that may no longer be easy to find elsewhere. It should not be treated as automatically authoritative for every date, circuit attribution, production quantity, or OEM claim. Preserve the information, but normalize the confidence later.

The archive can therefore ingest a broad candidate universe now and perform evidence cleanup as a separate editorial pass.
