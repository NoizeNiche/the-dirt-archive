# Japanese OEM fuzz-wah map 01

## Purpose
Cross-builder relationship map for the distinctive Japanese vintage fuzz-wah / treadle products that appear under Teisco, Guyatone, Ibanez, Elk, Mica, Antoria, Aria Diamond, Lord, Bruno, Memphis and related export names.

## Evidence pattern
Effects Database documents Teisco Wau Wau Fuzz as an early-to-mid-1970s Japanese OEM product and identifies a broad relationship with Shin-Ei-associated manufacture, while Tone Machines Blog's ELK catalog research independently notes that the ELK WF-880/WF-1100 Wau-Fuzz family overlaps with Guyatone FS-5 as early as 1971 and with several Ibanez/Maxon/Greco products.

The archive should therefore model this as a **relationship graph**, not as a single canonical manufacturer claim:

marketed brand -> retail model -> period evidence -> likely/shared OEM family -> confidence state

## Strong relationship nodes
- Guyatone FS-5 Wah-Fuzz — documented by 1971 in the ELK research trail.
- ELK WF-880 Wau Fuzz — catalog anchor by 1973.
- ELK WF-1100 Wau-Fuzz — subsequent ELK catalog generation.
- Teisco Wau Wau Fuzz — early-to-mid-1970s Japanese OEM record.
- Ibanez Wau Wau Fuzz — related marketed version in the same family.
- Aria Diamond Wah-Fuzz — related export-market candidate.
- Elk Wau Fuzz — related ELK marketed version.

## Archive rule
Shared enclosure, control layout, or similar historical appearance is evidence of a relationship, not proof of physical manufacture by one company. Product-level attribution must remain distinct from brand ownership and distribution.

## What this map unlocks
This family is a high-value example of why The Dirt Archive needs explicit OEM records. One physical design can move through several brand channels and countries while the manufacturer remains hidden or only probabilistically identifiable. The archive will preserve the uncertainty instead of flattening the history.
