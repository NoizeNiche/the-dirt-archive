# Early Foundational Handoff 102

## Pass 54: 2022-2025 modern boutique diversification, first documented V2/V3 states, and global builder expansion

Pass 54 advances the archive through the 2022-2025 modern period while continuing the geographic expansion started in Pass 53. The central question remains which physical and historical state of a dirt pedal is being documented, with announcement, first sale, first shipment, limited edition and later revision preserved as separate clocks.

### Newly documented or clarified

- **Silktone Fuzz**: February 25, 2022 public launch evidence establishes the original Silktone Fuzz generation, including its distinctive external bias-monitor display.
- **Silktone Fuzz+**: October 2024 documentation establishes a revised generation with expanded tone controls, a second footswitch for Raw mode and a more pedalboard-oriented format.
- **Frank Marino Audio**: April 2024 marks the first documented availability of the builder's initial three pedals, Dragonfly overdrive, Juggernaut fuzz and Maxoom boost. The commercial products derive from Marino's much older custom stage and studio builds.
- **Frank Marino Juggernaut / Dragonfly**: the two dirt products are separated from Marino's private custom-build lineage and retained as 2024 commercial first states.
- **Aleks K Production Hot Ice V2**: November 2024 builder documentation gives a clean V1/V2 boundary through revised silkscreen artwork, increased output and documented voicing changes.
- **Aleks K Production Honey Moon V3**: November 2024 documentation gives a V3 boundary through lighter paint, revised silkscreen, increased output and a brighter/open voicing description.
- **29 Pedals TOKI**: late-2024 public coverage records a minimalist three-toggle fuzz/fuzzy-driver and establishes the model's modern mid-gain fuzz/drive identity.
- **Beetronics Abelha**: January 2024 NAMM documentation establishes the Tropical Fuzz as a Brazilian Tropicália-inspired product from the Dutch boutique builder.
- **Cachalote 214 Fuzz V1**: the builder's official history states that development took two years and the first version launched in 2018, creating a strong original-generation anchor.
- **Cachalote 214 Fuzz MKII**: the same history records more than 300 original units followed by another year of testing and prototypes before the MKII, preserving a later generation with documented octave, EQ, expression/CV and switching changes.
- **McGregor Classic Fuzz**: September 2024 announcement evidence adds the Canadian builder's first documented fuzz product after its Crunch overdrive family.
- **Drunk Beaver XR Series**: November 2024 documentation establishes an affordable modern series that reworks legacy Polish EXAR and PUE products, while preserving those historical source brands as separate lineages.
- **Animal Factory Amplification**: official builder history anchors the Mumbai/India company to 2013 and expands the archive's South Asian chronology.
- **Animal Factory Dirty Mirror**: May 2025 is preserved as the announcement/pre-production state, with projected late-June to mid-July availability deliberately not converted into a confirmed shipment date.

### Key research findings

1. **Version archaeology is becoming increasingly manufacturer-documentable.** Silktone, Aleks K and Cachalote provide explicit V2/V3 or first-generation boundaries without requiring internal circuit inspection.
2. **Commercial pedal identities increasingly coexist with much older private prototypes.** Frank Marino Audio demonstrates why the archive must distinguish a designer's historical custom builds from the date a branded production pedal actually became available.
3. **The tiny-builder economy is increasingly global.** Canada, India, Brazil, the Netherlands, Poland and Canada/USA collaborative contexts now sit alongside the earlier US, UK, Germany, Spain, Argentina, Mexico and Australia records.
4. **Modern fuzz increasingly overlaps with drive by design.** TOKI, Juggernaut, Sensation-era products, CROM and several newer builders demonstrate a market where category labels themselves are useful evidence rather than rigid taxonomy.
5. **Legacy-brand archaeology is feeding modern low-cost product lines.** Drunk Beaver's XR Series shows how a modern builder can act as a preservation and reinterpretation layer for older national pedal brands.
6. **Announcement and availability evidence remains essential.** Animal Factory's Dirty Mirror shows why projected availability should be preserved as projected, especially when the source itself describes a pre-production state.
7. **Regional catalog research can reveal production methods and cultural references not visible in major-brand histories.** Cachalote's Brazilian design history, Beetronics' Tropicália reference and Animal Factory's Mumbai identity broaden the archive beyond the usual Anglo-American narrative.

### Evidence discipline

- Manufacturer-authored material remains preferred for launch dates, generation boundaries, production quantities and first-run descriptions.
- Contemporary trade coverage is retained as announcement or public-circulation evidence when first-party chronology is incomplete.
- Projected future availability is not converted into a confirmed shipment date.
- Private custom prototypes, public announcements, preorders, first sales and first shipments remain separate events.
- Legacy source brands and modern reinterpretation builders are modeled separately when historical identity materially depends on both.
- Finish, artwork, enclosure, control count, footswitching and jack placement are treated as identification clues only when supported by documentary evidence.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries or cloning instructions are added.

### Current lineage position

The Dirt Archive now reaches from the first pandemic-era micro-builders into the mid-2020s, where pedal history increasingly includes designer signatures, revived vintage references, V2/V3 visual changes, regional production identities, collaborative authorship and hybrid fuzz/drive categories. The archive's physical-object approach remains especially valuable because modern pedals can change rapidly even while keeping the same model name.
