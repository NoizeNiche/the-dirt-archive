# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 121: Melos / Makoto Shokai OEM Hypothesis Audit

## Question
Can the Japanese FZ-1A-derived Melos Fuzzder family be assigned to Makoto Shokai rather than simply being left as an unnamed OEM?

## Evidence gathered
- Specialist Japanese research identifies the **Melos FS-1 Fuzzder** as part of the same general FZ-1A-derived family as L.R.E., Apollo, Mica, Monacor, and Olson variants.
- A detailed Japanese historical blog attributes production of the Melos Fuzzder to **Makoto Shokai**, a Tokyo Kanda firm that also handled an Ace Tone Tokyo sales function and was developing technical capabilities during the late 1960s.
- The same research discusses Melos equipment beyond fuzz, including echo products, and argues that Makoto Shokai's activities crossed sales/distribution and technical/production roles.
- Effects Database independently links Melos FS-1, Apollo No.843, LRE Fuzz Sound, Mica FT-12, Monacor FS-1 and Olson X-78 as closely related products, but does not itself establish Makoto Shokai as the physical factory.

## Attribution status
**MAKOTO SHOKAI = CANDIDATE OEM / CONTRACTOR, NOT VERIFIED CANONICAL FACTORY.**

The current evidence is enough to preserve Makoto Shokai as a serious research lead, but not enough to rewrite every related Fuzzder-family product as Makoto-built. The archive should maintain at least three separate layers:
1. Melos as marketed brand/product identity.
2. Makoto Shokai as a candidate production/technical partner.
3. The still-unresolved physical manufacturing chain for each individual export label.

## Related family
- Melos FS-1 Fuzzder
- Apollo No.843 Fuzz King
- L.R.E. Fuzz Sound
- Mica FT-12 Fuzzder / Wailer
- Monacor FS-1 Fuzzder
- Olson X-78 Fuzz Master
- Herald AM-44A Fuzz Master is also associated with this broader family in current specialist cataloging, but the exact model-to-model factory relationship still requires careful comparison.

## Important uncertainty
Older collector literature sometimes collapses this family into a single factory assumption. Newer Japanese research instead suggests changing OEM arrangements across Honey/Shin-Ei and other suppliers. Those historical production states must remain model-specific.
