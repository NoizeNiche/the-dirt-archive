# YARP Breadcrumb 115

## Purpose
Deepen the New York / New Jersey early-American OEM network, with special attention to United Musical Industries and its relationship to LIMMCO, Applied Audio, and branded Buzz Box products.

## New builder record

- `BUILDER-110` — **United Musical Industries (UMI)**, United Musical Industries, New York.
- Founded by **Richard Soloway** around 1967 in Farmingdale/Long Island, according to the Effects Database interview record.
- Documented UMI products include **Buzz Tone & Volume Expander, Tone Booster, Treble-Bass Booster & Mute, and Wa Pedal**.
- Related branded products such as **Apollo No. 843 Buzz Box & Volume Expander** and **Conrad Buzz Box & Volume Expander** are attributed to UMI.

## Relationship / dedupe decisions

- UMI is maintained as a **separate manufacturer** from Applied Audio Products Inc.
- **LIMMCO** remains a distributor/house-brand node, not merged into UMI or Applied.
- **Kimberly** remains a marketed identity whose production history can move across manufacturers; the archive does not treat every Kimberly version as a different builder.
- Apollo and Conrad remain branded identities attached to the UMI manufacturing relationship where evidence supports it.
- Similar enclosure concepts and related fuzz lineages are not treated as proof of common manufacture without explicit supporting evidence.

## Evidence notes

Effects Database's UMI page records Richard Soloway's first-hand account of starting United Musical Industries in the mid-1960s/around 1967, building fuzz and wah products and selling U.M.I. equipment through hundreds of music stores. The same source indexes four UMI products.

Effects Database separately associates the Apollo No. 843 and Conrad Buzz Box & Volume Expander with UMI. Contemporary historical research also describes a transition in LIMMCO's Kimberly Buzz Box production, with UMI appearing as the later Long Island manufacturer after earlier production associated with LIMMCO and Applied Audio. These are recorded as a manufacturing/brand sequence rather than as one continuous manufacturer identity.
