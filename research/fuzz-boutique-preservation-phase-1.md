# The Dirt Archive — Boutique & Defunct Fuzz Preservation, Phase 1

This phase focuses on smaller, collectible, discontinued and historically interesting builders whose information is likely to become harder to find. The archive should not try to preserve every product page from every modern company. Instead, it should prioritize builders and models where historical context, versioning, or loss of documentation creates a useful identification/preservation role.

## D*A*M (Differential Audio Manifestationz)

Effects Database describes D*A*M as David Main's company in South Yorkshire, England, specializing in fuzz pedals, often based on vintage designs, and gives October 25, 2004 as the point when D*A*M became a means of making a living. This builder should receive a dedicated archive profile because its catalog is unusually fuzz-centered and its own product notes often include production-specific distinctions.

### Important catalog targets
- Fuzz Sound family
- Fuzzrong / FR-70
- Fuzzaround
- Meathead family
- Tone Bender-related products
- Colorsound Fuzz Box reissue
- other limited-run fuzzes

### Strong variation example: Fuzz Sound MkIV
Effects Database preserves a D*A*M statement that Fuzz Sound numbers #040 onward received the FS-75 model marking, revised typography, a change from the “Professional” wording to “MKIV,” and a different LED presentation. The same source says the first 15 of this particular MkIV run used a specified Mullard transistor combination, with a few exceptions. For the Dirt Archive, the **external/model-number/production-run distinctions** belong in the public record; the detailed component recipe does not.

### Strong variation example: Fuzzrong FR-70
D*A*M describes the FR-70 as the silicon-enhanced sister to the FR-69 and as a replica of the Rosac Nu Fuzz with its own tonal changes. The archive should model FR-69 and FR-70 as related models/variants rather than merging them into one generic D*A.M. fuzz.

## Blackout Effectors

Effects Database documents Blackout Effectors as founded by Kyle Tompkins in Vancouver in 2008 and moved to Asheville in 2009. The brand's 17-product catalog contains several dirt designs, including Fix'D Fuzz, Musket Fuzz, Twosome, Special Twosome variants, Fuzz Bear, Blunderbuss, FUBÄR and Crystal Dagger.

### Fix'D Fuzz
Effects Database explicitly says the Poppy and Ant graphic versions have no differences in the pedal/circuit and are graphic variants. This is exactly the kind of distinction the archive should capture as an **edition/graphic variant**, not a new model.

### Fix'D Fuzz Deluxe
A later product that expands on the earlier Fix'D Fuzz and the version used in the Twosome. Treat as a distinct model.

### Musket Fuzz
A Big-Muff-family design with an important variant called the Blunderbuss Musket. Effects Database describes the Blunderbuss as an alternate take rather than simply Musket V3.

### Twosome
A dual-fuzz product combining the Musket Fuzz and Deluxe Fix'D Fuzz. This should be modeled as a combination/dual product relationship, not simply a revision of either pedal.

### Fuzz Bear / FUBÄR
The Fuzz Bear is described by Effects Database as Tone Bender MkII-inspired with additional controls. FUBÄR is a later, more complex development from the Fuzz Bear idea. This is a useful example of a builder evolving a family rather than merely reissuing one model.

## Prescription Electronics

Prescription Electronics is a high-preservation-value builder because several products survive mainly through secondary documentation and collector knowledge.

### Experience
The Experience is a highly unusual multi-effect involving fuzz, octave and swell. Effects Database lists it as an octave-fuzz product and records multiple footswitchable functions.

### Yardbox
The Yardbox is described as a reproduction of an early Sola Sound Tone Bender family, and the source explicitly distinguishes it from Jumbo, Supa and Vox versions.

### Face Lift
Effects Database states that two versions were available for the Face Lift using silicon or germanium transistors. This belongs in the archive as a clear **version distinction** because it materially affects identification.

Important archive rule: preserve the existence of the silicon/germanium versions, but do not publish complete component lists.

## Effector 13 / Devi Ever lineage

Effector 13 is especially important because the product history crosses company/brand transitions.

### Truly Beautiful Disaster
Effects Database lists both the original Devi Ever version and an OohLaLa Manufacturing version. This is a textbook example for the archive's **brand/manufacturer transition** model.

The product itself is a highly unusual oscillating fuzz/noise instrument with a feedback loop. The archive should preserve its editions/production relationships without exposing circuit reconstruction information.

### Devi Ever historical preservation
Devi Ever should have a dedicated builder profile because later catalog references often mix the Effector 13 period, Devi Ever branded products, and subsequent licensing/reissues. The archive should separate original-builder products from later branded interpretations.

## SkinPimp

SkinPimp is a valuable defunct-boutique preservation candidate.

### Face Plant
Effects Database describes the Face Plant as a two-germanium-transistor fuzz with overdrive behavior through guitar-volume control.

### Buzzaround
SkinPimp's Buzzaround is listed alongside a large family of later Buzzaround copies and reinterpretations. This is useful for the archive's genealogy work because it lets us distinguish the historical Baldwin-Burns Buzzaround from later boutique descendants.

### SkinBender
Effects Database lists at least SkinBender Mk I and SkinBender Mk II. These should be recorded as separate named generations/products.

## Lovetone

Lovetone was founded by Dan Coggins and Vlad Naslas in 1995. Effects Database preserves a timeline that dates the Meatball prototype to summer/autumn 1994, the company's founding and other early designs to spring 1995, RingStinger and Cheese Source to 1998, and the company winding down in the 2000s. This is exceptionally valuable primary-ish chronology for a boutique builder.

### Dirt relevance
- Big Cheese
- Brown Source
- Cheese Source
- Ring Stinger

The Cheese Source is especially interesting because Lovetone's historical timeline describes it as a repackaging of the Brown Source overdrive and Big Cheese fuzz into one product. The archive should represent the relationship among those models.

An Effects Database auction record for a Dan Coggins-owned Cheese Source documents serial 0035 and an early red-orange finish with purple silkscreen, providing a useful specimen/provenance example. This is exactly the kind of evidence we eventually want in specimen records.

## Smallsound/Bigsound

Smallsound/Bigsound is a high-value preservation candidate because modern references often point to older products after production has ended.

### Team Awesome! Fuzzmachine
Effects Database documents the product as a fuzz with a clean blend and strong low-end retention, aimed especially at bassists but useful for guitar.

### Research priority
The builder's broader catalog, production dates, revisions and Mini/Team Awesome relationships should be documented from archived builder material and surviving examples before modern secondary references disappear.

## Builder-selection rule reinforced

A small builder is not automatically included simply because Effects Database lists it. High priority should go to builders where one or more of the following apply:

- the builder is discontinued or documentation is disappearing
- the builder's catalog contains named revisions or editions
- the builder has important vintage-family reinterpretations
- the builder has meaningful OEM or licensing relationships
- collectors routinely confuse versions
- surviving historical evidence is sparse but recoverable
