# Catalog Completeness Phase 26 — Builder-First Expansion

## Direction change

This phase formalizes the archive's working order:

**Builder universe → product universe → product variations → production periods → identification characteristics → relationships / genealogy.**

The archive should first build a broad map of who made dirt pedals and what they made. Detailed study of individual boxes comes after the builder and product universe is substantially covered.

This prevents a handful of famous pedals from consuming the research budget while obscure builders, short-lived brands, regional manufacturers and small product families remain invisible.

## Source basis

Effects Database is being used here as a **builder/product census backbone**, not as the final authority on dates, manufacturing relationships or variation history. Its value is that it deliberately preserves many small and discontinued builders and exposes product names that can then be reconciled against builder sites, catalogs, interviews, advertisements and surviving specimens.

Representative Phase 26 discovery anchors include:

- Analog Man: 20 catalog records and a builder timeline beginning with Analog Man activity in 1993.
- Barber Electronics: 32 catalog records including extensive Direct Drive, Burn Unit, Gain-Changer and fuzz-related families.
- BMF Effects: 17 catalog records; Scott Kiraly dates the first commercial pedal to September 17, 2005.
- Catalinbread: 124 catalog records; Nicholas Harris dates the company's pedal beginning to 2003.
- Devi Ever FX: 75 catalog records; Devi Ever dates the business to late 2003 under the Effector 13 name.
- Fuzzrocious: 43 catalog records; Ryan Ratajski describes early building around 2008 and the subsequent launch of Grey Stache production.
- Guyatone: 26 catalog records spanning multiple Japanese and U.S. periods.
- J. Rockett: multiple product families spanning Pro, Mini, Signature, Masterbuilt and other lines.
- Keeley: 125 catalog records across original, modified, custom-shop, signature and collaboration products.
- Lovepedal: 62 catalog records across numerous short-run dirt, boost and fuzz products.
- Menatone: 36 catalog records with multiple documented versions of products such as King of the Britains.
- MI Audio: 20 catalog records; Michael Ibrahim dates Tube Zone experimentation to 1995 and company formation to 2002.
- Moody Sounds: 9 records with a timeline beginning in the mid-2000s.
- Smallsound/Bigsound: 14 catalog records centered on Brian Hamilton's Philadelphia operation.
- Systech: 11 records tied to the Kalamazoo operation and its Sound Factory circle.
- T-Pedals: 12 records; Alberto Dani dates T-Fuzz prototypes to 2002 and full-time activity to 2006.
- Wampler: 78 catalog records covering major overdrive, distortion and fuzz lines.
- Z. Vex: 69 catalog records, including hand-painted, Vexter and Vextron production families.

## Editorial treatment

All Phase 26 product records remain **Discovery** records. They establish product existence and builder association only.

Do not infer a new generation merely because a product name has a suffix such as II, Deluxe, Mini, Select or V2. Preserve the name first; determine whether the designation is a model, edition, production generation or collector shorthand during later research.

Likewise, an Effects Database product listed under a builder does not automatically prove that the named builder manufactured every unit. OEM, distributor, licensing and collaboration relationships must be resolved during the later identity pass.

## Later research order

Once builder enumeration is sufficiently broad:

1. Normalize each builder's canonical identity and aliases.
2. Reconcile complete product lineups against builder sources.
3. Separate retail brand from actual manufacturer/OEM where necessary.
4. Identify obvious product families and related models.
5. Research individual production variations.
6. Build specimen-identification characteristics.
7. Add relationship and genealogy records only when evidence supports them.

The archive should therefore become a map before it becomes a microscope.

## Note on completeness

Phase 26 is intentionally another expansion step rather than a claim that the builder universe is complete. The working goal is to keep finding builders across vintage, boutique, Japanese, European, Australian, South American, OEM and one-person histories until the remaining gaps become smaller and more visible.
