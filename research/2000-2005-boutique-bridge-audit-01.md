# 2000-2005 Boutique Bridge Audit 01

## Scope
This pass documents the transition from the first 1990s boutique builders into the rapidly expanding 2000-2005 independent pedal market. The focus is builder identity, product existence, chronology anchors, and relationship boundaries. No circuit reconstruction is included.

## Diamond Pedals
Diamond's own legacy history places the beginning of the company story in 2003, when Michael Knappe and Tim Fifield began developing the Diamond Drive as a hobby. Diamond's first shipment was approximately 30 hand-built Drive pedals sent to a Halifax music store in June 2004. Effects Database records the same chronology and adds the first Diamond Compressor shipment in March 2005 and first Memory Lane shipment in July 2005. The archive therefore treats Diamond Drive as the first hard product anchor, with Compressor and Memory Lane as 2005 expansion records.

Sources:
- https://www.diamondpedals.com/pages/legacy
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/diamond
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/interviews/brands/diamond

## Xotic
Xotic's official history creates an unusually clean chronology. The company traces its roots to 1996 in the San Fernando Valley. Robotalk 1 was designed and introduced in 1998 and became the first pedal carrying the Xotic name. In August 2000 Prosound Communications Inc. was established as the parent company and Xotic Effects marketing expanded. Xotic's product history dates AC Booster to September 2002, RC Booster to November 2002, and BB Preamp to January 2005. The latter three form a particularly important early-2000s gain sequence, while the original bass/preamp business should remain a separate contextual branch.

Sources:
- https://xotic.us/about-us/
- https://xotic.jp/about-us/

## Hermida Audio Technology
Premier Guitar's builder profile identifies Brian Hermida's first pedal as the Mosferatu in 2003. The same account says the Zendrive came from an earlier design that Hermida refined after the Mosferatu proved too gain-heavy for the sound he was targeting, with the first Zendrive sent to Robben Ford. The archive anchors Mosferatu to 2003 and Zendrive to the 2004 release corridor. Zendrive 2 is kept later, with Effects Database indexing it in 2006. Later Lovepedal manufacturing is a separate production relationship and does not replace the original Hermida identity.

Sources:
- https://www.premierguitar.com/builder-profile-hermida-audio-technology
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/hermida/zendrive2

## Pigtronix
Pigtronix states that the original Disnortion appeared in 2005 and that Howard Davis and David Koltai were responsible for the original design. Pigtronix's FAQ identifies the Disnortion as the source of the later Octava concept, but the archive should keep the original Disnortion and later Octava products separate. The company identity is anchored to 2004, making Disnortion one of the clearest dirt products in the first Pigtronix commercial corridor.

Sources:
- https://www.pigtronix.com/faq/
- https://www.pigtronix.com/pedals/disnortion-micro/

## Catalinbread
Catalinbread's official history says Nicholas Harris began the company in June 2003 after ToneFactor asked to sell the Super Chili Picoso. Effects Database independently reproduces the June 2003 founding anchor and records a rapid early expansion. The archive therefore treats Super Chili Picoso as the first Catalinbread-branded product anchor, but does not assume that every later catalog pedal existed by 2005 merely because the company was founded in 2003.

Sources:
- https://catalinbread.com/pages/about
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/catalinbread

## Cusack Music
Cusack Music says it was founded in 2002 as a garage side project and later expanded into pedal manufacturing. Effects Database dates the original Cusack Screamer and Screamer Fuzz entries to September 2005, giving us strong first-wave dirt anchors. Current Cusack materials show multiple later revisions, including Screamer V3 and Screamer Fuzz V3, so those generations must remain separate from the 2005 originals.

Sources:
- https://cusackmusic.com/about/
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/cusack/screamer
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/cusack/screamer/fuzz

## MI Audio
Effects Database currently indexes 20 MI Audio products, including Blues Pro Overdrive, Crunch Box, Tube Zone Overdrive, Classic Distortion, Blue Boy, G.I. Fuzz, Neo Fuzz and later Super Crunch Box. The Crunch Box record documents its relationship to the Blues Pro and Tube Zone families, making those three particularly useful as a product-family cluster for later historical work. Exact founding and first-sale dates remain less securely anchored than the Diamond/Xotic/Catalinbread records, so this builder stays at historical-baseline status pending period catalogs or dated maker material.

Sources:
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/miaudio
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/miaudio/crunchbox
- https://miaudio.com/rd-lab/

## Menatone bridge
Menatone's current site gives unusually strong retrospective anchors: Red Snapper is identified as the first Menatone pedal and debuted in 1996; Blue Collar debuted in 1997 as the second pedal. These records connect the 1990s boutique founding layer to the 2000s independent market and should be retained separately from later PTP, Deluxe, and Mini production states.

Sources:
- https://www.menatone.com/red-snapper-4knob-ptp
- https://www.menatone.com/blue-collar-ptp

## Archive interpretation
This batch adds a useful chronological bridge without flattening the boutique era into a single "2000s" bucket. The strongest evidence now shows several different paths into the decade: Xotic emerges from bass/preamp work; Diamond grows from an engineering hobby; Catalinbread begins through a retailer-driven first pedal; Hermida starts with a designer's individual builds; Pigtronix grows from a founder/designer collaboration; Cusack begins as an engineering-led family shop; and MI Audio and Menatone develop broader builder catalogs.

The archive should use these different origins as identity metadata, not as assumptions about circuit lineage or manufacturing equivalence.
