# Honey / Shin-ei Companion catalog audit 01

## Scope
This audit establishes the historical Honey / Shin-ei / Companion fuzz family as a relationship map. It deliberately avoids circuit reconstruction and does not assume every FY-2/FY-6-branded pedal was made by the same physical factory without evidence.

## Core chronology

### Honey Baby Crying FY-6, 1967
Current Shin-ei historical material identifies the Honey Co. Ltd. Baby Crying FY-6 as the first incarnation of the Super Fuzz family, released in 1967. This is useful as a brand/product chronology anchor, although the modern Shin-ei site is a retrospective source and should not replace period documentation when an original catalog or advertisement is found.

### Shin-ei / Companion FY-2
Vintage Guitar's historical account says engineer Fumio Mieda extracted the fuzz concept associated with the Honey Psychedelic Machine and packaged it as the Shin-ei Companion FY-2 in 1967. Effects Database preserves a dedicated Companion FY-2 entry and a large related-brand tree including Avora, Jax, Kimbara, Suzuki, Tele-Star, Tempo, Thomas and Zenta examples.

The archive should not flatten all of these marketed labels into a single product record. They remain separate marketed-model nodes linked through an OEM / family relationship field.

### FY-6 Super Fuzz / Fuzz Master, 1968 onward
Shin-ei's current historical page identifies the Shin-ei FY-6 Super Fuzz as released in 1968 and describes it as the next incarnation of the Honey Baby Crying fuzz. Effects Database separately catalogs Companion FY-6 Super Fuzz / Fuzz Master and the related product universe.

### Companion wah-fuzz branches
Effects Database's Companion category contains WF-24 8-Tr Fuzz Wah and WF-8 8-Tr Fuzz Wah alongside FY-2 and FY-6. These need to remain their own models because wah-fuzz and standalone fuzz are separate retail products even when they share a manufacturer/family ancestry.

## High-value related-brand census
Effects Database's FY-6 family lists many marketed versions, including Apollo 846 Deluxe Fuzz Tone Expander, Avora FY-6, Clear Sound Super Fuzz, Companion FY-6, Crown Super Fuzz, Factone Fuzz Machine, Hagstrom Super Fuzz, Hohner MF-50 Master Fuzz Control, J.H. Experience FY-6, Jax FY-6, LRE Super-Fuzz, Mana FY-6, Mayfair 9540, Mica-Tone Fuzz Master, Oscar FY-6, Rands FY-6, Zenta FY-6, Shaftesbury Duo Fuzz and Teisco TF-1, among others.

This list is a discovery index, not a statement that all products are physically identical or share one production period. Brand, OEM and generation relationships must be resolved product by product.

## Teisco branch
Effects Database documents the Teisco TF-1 Fuzz as a late-1960s/early-1970s marketed version of the FY-6 Super Fuzz family. A separate Teisco Fuzz Machine entry documents another Japanese fuzz family with numerous marketed names. The archive should keep TF-1 and the Fuzz Machine separate until stronger documentary evidence ties their production to one factory or design line.

## Modern revival layer
The current Shin-ei site explicitly sells reproductions of the 1968 Shin-ei FY-6, the Companion FY-2 and the Honey Baby Crying FY-6. It also lists an Astron-branded FY-6 described as a private-label revival of the current Shin-ei FY-6. These are modern revival / promotional entities and must not be mixed into the vintage production chronology.

## Catalog disposition
The Honey -> Shin-ei -> Companion chronology is now strong enough to justify a dedicated family node in the archive. The next layer should concentrate on period Japanese catalogs, OEM customers, enclosure states, branding changes and specimen-level evidence. Avoid using modern replica pages as proof of exact vintage production claims.
