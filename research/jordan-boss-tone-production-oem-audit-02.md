# Jordan Electronics / Boss Tone: Production, Branding & OEM Audit 02

Status: research / verified with uncertainty preserved
Scope: original Jordan Boss Tone and Sho-Sound/Sho-Bud relationship; no circuit reconstruction.

## Primary archive conclusion
The Boss Tone should be modeled as a product family with distinct production corridors rather than as one undifferentiated pedal.

### Corridor A: Jordan Electronics, California
- Jordan Electronics entered guitar effects in the mid-1960s under the Jordan Juniors line.
- The Boss Tone was demonstrated at the July 1966 NAMM show and appears to have reached commercial availability in early 1967.
- Period and later historical sources consistently distinguish California production from the later Nashville/Sho-Sound corridor.
- Modern secondary references commonly divide surviving examples into Alhambra and Pasadena factory/location groups. These are useful specimen identifiers, but the archive does not treat enclosure/location alone as proof of an exact internal production generation.

### Corridor B: Sho-Bud / Sho-Sound, Nashville
- The Boss Tone became especially popular with pedal-steel players in Nashville.
- Historical sources identify a licensed Sho-Bud/Sho-Sound version, sold under the Sho-Sound name.
- Vintage Guitar describes the Sho-Sound Boss Tone as a Jordan-licensed version and says surviving examples were likely manufactured by Jordan and badged for Sho-Bud, sometimes appearing in red as well as the more common black plastic housing.
- Other historical accounts assert that Sho-Bud/Music City Manufacturing later produced units in Nashville. This manufacturing claim is not resolved strongly enough for the archive to collapse the two interpretations. It is therefore recorded as an unresolved OEM/manufacturing question.
- Gretsch distribution is associated with the later Sho-Sound/Boss Tone commercial corridor in secondary historical accounts.

## Versioning / specimen rule
Aion FX's historical reconstruction identifies five Boss Tone versions, dividing them into three California variants and two Nashville variants. This is useful as a research lead, but the Dirt Archive treats those labels as secondary research terminology rather than period factory nomenclature unless a period Jordan document independently confirms the exact numbering.

The archive therefore records:
- California / Alhambra
- California / Pasadena
- Nashville / Sho-Sound
as production-location or branding corridors, with finer V1-V5 classifications reserved for specimen evidence and cross-source reconciliation.

## Named products / related Jordan Juniors
Verified historical product names associated with Jordan's compact Juniors line include:
- Boss Tone
- Boss Boost
- Vico Vibe

The Dirt Archive does not automatically classify every Jordan Juniors effect as a core dirt pedal. Boss Tone is the clear core fuzz/dirt anchor. Boss Boost is a boost boundary record; Vico Vibe is outside the core dirt catalog but useful for complete builder/product context.

## Brand / manufacturer graph
Jordan Electronics = original marketed/manufacturing entity in California corridor.

Sho-Bud / Sho-Sound = separate Nashville commercial/branding entity associated with a licensed Boss Tone version.

Music City Manufacturing = historically reported Sho-Bud-associated manufacturing name; exact responsibility for Boss Tone production remains unresolved.

Gretsch = later distribution/brand-channel relationship reported for Sho-Sound products; not treated as the pedal manufacturer.

Baldwin = later corporate ownership/distribution trail connected to Sho-Bud, but not evidence by itself that Baldwin manufactured the original Boss Tone.

## Evidence notes
- Guitar World (2025) states the Boss Tone appeared at NAMM in July 1966 and became commercially available in early 1967, and distinguishes Alhambra, Pasadena and Nashville examples.
- Vintage Guitar (2014) states the Boss Tone was used by many players, describes the Sho-Sound version as licensed with Jordan, and says those versions were likely built by Jordan and badged for Sho-Bud. The wording "likely" is important and is preserved as uncertainty.
- Steel Guitar Forum archival discussions from 2000-2002 contain firsthand owner recollections and competing manufacturing claims. These are useful specimen and oral-history leads, but they are not treated as definitive factory records.
- Aion FX (2024-era project documentation) provides a five-version historical classification and notes a California-to-Nashville transition. Because this is a modern secondary reconstruction, the archive uses it as a cross-check rather than as sole authority for period nomenclature.

## Open research questions
1. Locate surviving Jordan Electronics period catalogs, dealer sheets or ads that explicitly date Alhambra/Pasadena production.
2. Determine when the Pasadena move occurred and whether all Pasadena examples can be assigned to one product generation.
3. Resolve whether Sho-Bud/Music City Manufacturing physically built any Boss Tone units or whether all Sho-Sound units were Jordan-made and badge/distribution-only.
4. Identify period documentation for red Sho-Sound Boss Tone examples and determine whether color correlates with a specific run.
5. Trace the Sho-Sound → Baldwin → Gretsch rights/distribution chain without conflating ownership with manufacturing.
6. Build a specimen taxonomy using enclosure, label, location, serial/marking and documented provenance only where each feature is evidence-backed.

## Archive boundary
No schematics, PCB layouts, BOMs, circuit reconstruction, cloning instructions, or gutshot library are published here. This file is strictly historical/catalog and relationship research.
