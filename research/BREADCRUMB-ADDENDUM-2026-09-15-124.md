# YARP Breadcrumb 124

## Purpose
Japanese wedge-fuzz and New Jersey manufacturing pass focused on distinguishing genuine factories from importers and marketed labels.

## New research record

- `BUILDER-121`: Trans-Tek Manufacturing Company, preserved as a research-only manufacturing/production organization associated with Applied Audio at the 1968 South Plainfield factory.

## Cross-reference / dedupe decisions

- Do not promote Walco-Linck into a Japanese factory. Walco Electronics remains a U.S. company/marketed node with Japanese-made effects and an unidentified physical OEM.
- Do not promote Herald Electronics into a Japanese manufacturer. Herald appears to be a Chicago-based import/brand identity; the AM-44A family is tied to multiple labels including Apollo, LRE, Melos, Mica, Monacor and Olson.
- Do not split Shaftesbury Duo Fuzz into a separate builder. It remains a Rose-Morris marketed/distributed identity for Japanese Honey/Shin-Ei manufacture, with Honey-era and Shin-Ei-era physical generations preserved separately.
- Do not assign the Japanese wedge-fuzz family uniformly to Nisshin Onpa. Current evidence supports the possibility for some Greco/Ibanez-era products, but model-by-model factory attribution remains incomplete.

## Evidence notes

Tone Machines Blog's recent Applied Audio research identifies a June 1968 Trans-Tek factory in South Plainfield, New Jersey, with Applied Audio described as an associate organization and the planned production explicitly including amplifiers and effects such as the Applied Big Fuzz. This makes Trans-Tek historically relevant as a production organization even though no independently marketed Trans-Tek pedal line has been established.

Effects Database describes the Herald AM-44A Fuzz Master as a Japanese-made late-1960s fuzz distributed under several brand names and linked to the LRE/Melos/Mica/Monacor/Olson family. This strengthens the marketed-label/OEM model without identifying the physical Japanese factory.

Current research also continues to distinguish the Honey-era from Shin-Ei-era Shaftesbury Duo Fuzz examples. Rose-Morris distributed the Shaftesbury line in the UK, while the underlying Japanese manufacturing lineage passed from Honey to Shin-Ei after Honey's bankruptcy.
