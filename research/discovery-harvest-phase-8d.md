# The Dirt Archive — Discovery Harvest Phase 8D

## Purpose

This phase continues the long-tail preservation pass. The goal is to capture small builders, one-person shops, limited-run products, and early catalog footprints before normalization and public promotion.

Effects Database is being used as a **discovery backbone**, not as a source to copy wholesale. Product names and builder histories should be retained as leads, then normalized and independently checked where practical.

## Small-builder records worth preserving

### Moody Sounds — Sweden
Moody Sounds is described by Effects Database as mainly a one-man company run by Albin Roslund, with additional help from a small crew. The timeline gives unusually concrete early history: in October 2005 Baby Box Noise Machine serial 002 and Moody Fuzz were sold to Tambourine Studios; in 2006 the company began listing fuzz and tremolo kits; in 2009 the Moody Mushroom pedal was developed; assembled pedals gained international retailers; and by 2011 9volt Japan was a seller.

Products worth retaining include:
- Baby Box / Noise Box
- Moody Fuzz
- Hjärt Müller Bass Drive
- Zeygot Fuzz
- Moody Tremolo
- Moody Vibes
- Mushroom Echo
- Carlin Compressor
- Carlin Phaser
- Carlin Ring Modulator

**Archive value:** Strong. This is a clear early-2000s European small-builder timeline with both finished-pedal and DIY-kit history.

### Orion Effekte — Germany
Orion Effekte is identified by Effects Database as a one-man company from Osnabrück, run by Jan van Triest. The timeline records summer 2004 first pedals made for friends as Fuzz Face clones, an autumn 2005 Big Muff modification phase, official start in winter 2005, and a 2011 signature Lemming Fuzz.

**Archive value:** Strong. Capture the transition from hobby builds/mods into a named commercial builder and preserve the 2005–06 legacy web presence as a historical source lead.

### Montgomery Appliances — USA
David Gill's one-man operation in Montgomery County, Maryland is particularly valuable for production-history research. His own timeline records first home-built gear in summer 2007, an initial small run of roughly seven FSH MKII pedals, subsequent one-offs, about 50 A/DA Flanger replicas, and then a deliberate shift toward fuzz. The Effects Database catalog lists 15 products.

Dirt products include:

**Archive value:** Very strong. This is a model example of why the archive should preserve one-person production histories and named small runs.

### Leila Vintage Electronics — USA
Leila Vintage Electronics was run by Morgan Hargrave as a one-person operation in Truckee, California. The timeline records vintage amp repair beginning in 2002, a first scratch amp build in 2003, first pedal build in 2004, and the start of selling amps and pedals under the Leila Vintage Electronics name in 2005.

Known products:
- KoaDrive
- Mark II Fuzz
- Pulse Trem
- Soft Delay
- SparkleComp

**Archive value:** Strong despite the tiny five-product footprint. This is exactly the class of builder that can disappear from general histories.

### Jamés Pedals — Sweden
James Bennett is documented as building his first pedal on a kitchen table in 2004, followed by a guitar-parts/repair/modification business in 2005. By 2007 the pedal side had largely taken over; a website was registered in 2010; and permanent premises were established in 2012.

Products listed include:
- Ass Kickin' Overdrive
- Blender
- Fuzzy Pie — Bass Fuzz
- Hey B-ABY!
- Treble Booster MkII
- Treble Booster MkIII
- Trebletastic Overdrive

**Archive value:** Moderate to strong. Useful as a snapshot of the mid-2000s Swedish DIY-to-commercial transition.

### T. Jauernig Electronics — USA
T. Jauernig is documented as a one-man operation from Wausau, Wisconsin. The timeline begins in early 2004 with the Luxury Drive, initially made for personal use. A local dealer asked for ten units, creating the first commercial order; the builder then sent a Luxury Drive to Greg Koch and the business developed from there.

Effects Database lists 22 products and the brand should be researched model-by-model, with special attention to early production and artist relationships.

**Archive value:** Strong. This is a useful record of how a single pedal can turn a home build into a boutique company.

## Additional preservation leads

### M.A.S.F. — Japan
Effects Database currently lists 17 products, including Biloma, Bloody Emily, Epilepsy, Halothan, Homicide Noodle, Kidnapper (Octave Fuzz), Lavender Head, Live Convulsion, Paranoid, PJ Fuzz, SCM, Starry Wisdom (Bass Fuzz), Swan Song, Thornoscillator and several Ma.S noise machines.

**Archive value:** High. The catalog mixes fuzz, octave fuzz and noise/special-effect devices and should not be reduced to conventional guitar-pedal categories.

### [sfx] sound
Effects Database lists the Micro Fuzz, a handmade compact fuzz using a dedicated low-noise switching system and a three-control layout (fuzz, volume, tone).

**Archive value:** Moderate. Small-builder record useful for tracking the mini-pedal and handmade-production wave around 2010.

### Loophole Pedals — USA
Effects Database identifies Loophole as a one-man operation founded in May 2016 by Mike Copeland in Waxahachie, Texas. The business evolved from repair/modification services into finished pedals, with the first named drive, Grey Matter, launched in 2019.

**Archive value:** Strong for the later boutique transition from mod/rehouse work into original products, even though the current EDB catalog entry is small.

### Oxfuzz
Effects Database's interview describes Oxfuzz as a one-man operation, with pedals built at home and tested at the builder's studio. The builder explicitly discusses hand-wiring, germanium selection and a preference for straightforward graphics.

**Archive value:** High. The interview is particularly useful because it records manufacturing philosophy in the builder's own words.

### KR Musical Products
Effects Database preserves both The Fuzz and The Gypsy Fuzz. The Gypsy Fuzz is described as a vintage-style fuzz using both PNP and NPN silicon transistors and positioned between a fuzz and overdrive in use.

**Archive value:** Moderate to strong. Preserve the two products separately rather than grouping them under generic "Fuzz." 

### Pierce Custom Electronics
The Ennio is a documented Fuzzrite-oriented product. The archive should preserve the product as a modern historical reinterpretation, with the builder's own stated dissatisfaction with existing Fuzzrite clones and the decision to build a version with conventional enclosure, LED and power arrangements.

**Archive value:** Moderate. Useful as evidence of the 2010-era boutique clone/reinterpretation wave.

## Editorial handling rules reinforced

1. A tiny catalog is not a reason to omit a builder.
2. One-person workshop history is itself archive-worthy information.
3. Preserve first-person chronology whenever available, but do not reproduce long interview text wholesale.
4. Keep original product names even when names reference vintage circuits or brands.
5. Distinguish a modern reinterpretation from a historical OEM or original manufacturer.
6. Treat exact production counts as claims requiring source provenance.
7. Capture discontinued products as historical records even when current manufacturer sites no longer show them.
8. Do not confuse EDB's category count with a verified production census.
