# Sola Sound / Colorsound: 1970s Catalog & OEM Audit 03

## Scope
This pass expands the Sola Sound / Colorsound 1970s product universe while preserving the archive's manufacturer/brand/OEM distinctions. It does not publish schematics, PCB layouts, BOMs, or circuit-reconstruction instructions.

## Major historical correction: Supa vs Jumbo

The archive must keep **Supa Tone Bender** and **Jumbo Tone Bender** as separate retail names while recording their close relationship. Premier Guitar's historical account places the Supa Tone Bender in 1973 and describes the later Jumbo as a renamed/closely related continuation. Kitrae's Big Muff history adds an important nuance: three-transistor Jumbo examples exist and the two-transistor-count families should not be assumed identical from the badge alone. This means enclosure-only identification is unsafe.

## Power Boost / Overdriver corridor

Period history indicates that the Colorsound **Power Boost** was one of the first Colorsound-branded pedals, appearing in 1970 advertising. Sources describe an orange 18V-era version followed by a darker 9V Overdriver naming/repackaging around the early 1970s. Later Power Boost-marked pedals can occupy the same broad physical family while carrying later production characteristics. The Dirt Archive should therefore treat:

- original Power Boost;
- early Overdriver;
- later Power Boost-branded units;
- later wide-case/white-label examples

as related but separately evidenced production states, not one undifferentiated product record.

## OEM / alternate-brand corridor

Sola Sound continued manufacturing effects for other British companies while developing Colorsound. The Tone Bender archive should therefore preserve marketed-brand identities separately from the physical-builder node. Historical sources identify Barnes & Mullins and Carlsbro among the British companies using Sola Sound manufacturing, and later histories document multiple names surrounding the 1970s Jumbo/Supa design corridor, including B&M/Champion, CMI, G.B. and Pro'Traffic examples.

These records should be represented as **brand/OEM relationship candidates or verified relationships where documentation is strong**, rather than counting each badge as a new Sola Sound factory catalog entry.

## Evidence notes

1. Analogman's historical Colorsound page supports a 1973-era thin-case Tone Bender, a 1974-era Supa Tone Bender and a 1974-era Overdriver for the American market.
2. Kitrae's Colorsound history places the original 18V Power Boost in the 1969-70 corridor, the Overdriver rename/repackage around 1971, and later enclosure/label transitions through the 1970s.
3. Premier Guitar's Tone Bender history identifies the 1970 Tone Bender Fuzz, Power Boost, Overdriver, Supa Tone Bender and later Jumbo/OEM corridor.
4. Guitar Center's current historical overview warns that the 1973 Supa and 1975 Jumbo should not be confused with earlier three-transistor Tone Benders.
5. Big Muff Pages documents the possibility of overlapping Supa/Jumbo production and warns against assuming every Jumbo enclosure contains the same internal generation.

## Public-archive boundary

This audit deliberately records product identity, chronology, relationships and evidence status without publishing circuit diagrams, PCB layouts, complete component lists, gutshot collections or cloning instructions.
