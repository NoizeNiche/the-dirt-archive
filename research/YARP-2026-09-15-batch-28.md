# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 28

## Focus
Soviet/Eastern Bloc historical fuzz and the Kazan factory corridor.

## Findings

### Kazan Musical Instruments Factory / Soviet Booster
Surviving period documentation and collector references identify a 1977 Soviet pedal commonly called the Kazan Booster. A surviving manual gives the formal description as an "Amplifier with level limitation, Booster PR 2.940 019 RE" and indicates intended use with several electrified instruments. Contemporary surviving examples use Soviet-standard five-pin DIN connections. Secondary collector documentation identifies the manufacturing site as the Kazan Musical Instruments factory in Kazan, USSR.

### Archive treatment
The archive adds Kazan Musical Instruments factory as a **canonical historical manufacturing candidate / facility-level entity**, but does not invent a more specific corporate successor chain than the evidence supports. The retail nickname "Kazan Booster" is retained as the searchable product identity. Because the surviving sources disagree on whether the device should be classed as booster, limiter, or fuzz by modern terminology, the archive preserves the period description while tagging it as a dirt-adjacent fuzzy gain product rather than forcing it into a modern category.

### Anta
An additional Soviet fuzz identity, Anta, is retained as a **discovery-only candidate**. Surviving collector descriptions place it in the Soviet planned-economy instrument/effects context and distinguish it from the Kazan Booster, but current accessible evidence does not establish a factory, legal manufacturer, or reliable production chronology. It therefore does not become a canonical builder and is not counted toward verified builder totals.

## Evidence boundary
The Kazan record is intentionally facility-level and evidence-qualified. Anta remains unresolved. No schematics, PCB layouts, BOMs, gutshots, cloning instructions or circuit reconstruction material added.
