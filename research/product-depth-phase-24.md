# Product Depth Phase 24 — Major Boutique Dirt Families

## Status

Focused product-level historical research following Catalog Completeness Phase 23. These records are research-grade notes for later promotion into verified archive entities. They deliberately separate model names from versions, production periods and manufacturing relationships.

## 1. Fulltone OCD

### Historical anchor
Fulltone states that the OCD began in 2004. The company explicitly rejects the common collector shorthand that treats every later change as a new numbered version: its current documentation describes a single OCD lineage with small value changes designated V1.0 through V1.8. Fulltone says units serial 205811 and higher represent the less-compressed revision commonly called "v2.01" by users.

### Archive model
Treat OCD as one retail family with production-period records rather than as an undifferentiated V1/V2 split.

Recommended chronology fields:
- V1.0 through V1.8: documented internal production variations.
- Serial 205811+: Fulltone's less-compressed revision, commonly called V2.01 by collectors/users.
- Custom Shop OCD v1.4: separate modern reproduction/limited production event, not original 2000s production.
- Fulltone USA OCD production: separate manufacturing era from original Fulltone Musical Products production.

### Important current evidence
Fulltone USA currently offers OCDv2, OCD-Germanium and a limited Custom Shop OCD v1.4. The Custom Shop page states that only 500 v1.4 units were produced for the current run and describes the run as a part-for-part recreation of the original v1.4 specification.

Sources:

- https://www.fulltoneusa.com/collections/ocd/products/custom-shop-ocd-v1-4
- https://www.fulltoneusa.com/products/ocd-germanium

## 2. Fulltone Full-Drive

### Archive model
The Full-Drive family should be treated as a long-lived retail family with multiple distinct production generations and circuit/feature revisions.

The current Fulltone USA catalog distinguishes Full-Drive1, Full-Drive2 v2 and Full-Drive3. Fulltone's Full-Drive1 documentation explicitly positions the pedal as a recreation of the late-1990s/early-2000s Full-Drive sound and identifies the earliest Full-Drive 2 Comp-Cut behavior as distinct from later Flat-Mids and MOSFET-era versions.

### Research distinctions to preserve
- early Full-Drive 2 / pre-Flat-Mids period
- Flat-Mids era
- MOSFET-era Full-Drive 2
- Full-Drive 2 MOS / MOSFET variants
- later Full-Drive 2 v2
- Full-Drive 3
- modern Full-Drive1 revival

Do not collapse "Full-Drive 2" into one record merely because the enclosure name remained stable.

Sources:
- https://www.fulltoneusa.com/products/full-drive1
- https://www.fulltoneusa.com/products/full-drive2-v2
- https://www.fulltoneusa.com/products/full-drive3

## 3. EarthQuaker Devices Hoof family

### Historical anchor
EQD states that Jamie Stillman sold the first production Hoof on March 18, 2007. The Hoof was one of EQD's earliest production pedals and was built during the company's basement period. EQD describes the design as a germanium/silicon hybrid Muff-style fuzz inspired by the Russian Muff family.

### Hoof Reaper relationship
EQD states that Hoof Reaper was originally released as a limited run in 2010 and later reintroduced because demand was high. It combines the Hoof, Tone Reaper and an analog octave-up circuit in one enclosure, with seven switching combinations.

The archive should therefore model:
- Hoof as the primary family/model.
- Tone Reaper as a related but independent product family.
- Hoof Reaper as a combination product/generation rather than a "Hoof variant."
- Tentacle as the later standalone octave-up lineage.

### Cloven Hoof
Cloven Hoof should be researched as its own Hoof/Muff-derived family rather than treated as a revision of the original Hoof. The archive should record its relationship to the Hoof concept without implying direct production continuity.

Sources:
- https://www.earthquakerdevices.com/about
- https://www.earthquakerdevices.com/hoof-reaper
- https://www.earthquakerdevices.com/blog-posts/fuzzcyclopedia

## 4. Death By Audio Fuzz War

### Historical importance
Oliver Ackermann describes the Fuzz War as the result of a long search for an "ultimate sustain distortion." MusicRadar's interview records this design goal and notes that the pedal can move from overdrive into extreme wall-of-fuzz territory.

DBA's current product documentation describes a three-control architecture with an active multi-curve tone control and a very large output range. The archive should treat the Fuzz War as a flagship DBA family with production/graphic history, rather than simply as one modern pedal SKU.

### Related DBA families requiring separate lineage
- Soundwave Breakdown
- Supersonic Fuzz Gun
- Octave Clang
- Interstellar Overdriver
- Apocalypse
- Destroyer-series products

DBA's current Apocalypse page documents five selectable post-amplifier paths, demonstrating that later DBA products can contain multiple internally meaningful effect modes and should not be reduced to one generic "fuzz" category.

Sources:
- https://deathbyaudio.com/collections/1/products/fuzz-war
- https://www.musicradar.com/news/tone-makers-death-by-audios-oliver-ackermann
- https://deathbyaudio.com/collections/fuzz/products/apocalypse

## 5. EAE Longsword

### Historical anchor
EAE's 2025 retrospective states that John Snyder began taking orders for the first batch of Longswords on June 17, 2015. EAE calls the Longsword a flagship product whose history tracks the company's evolution from a small workshop to a larger production operation.

### Version chronology
EAE's current Longsword documentation identifies:
- V1 — initial release
- V2 — added diode clipping and Gain 1/2 features
- V2.1 — removed Range toggle and fixed it at the optimal intermediate setting
- V3 — added 9–18V operation and replaced Gain 1/2 with Boost mode
- V4 — changed to a six-knob layout, surface-mount PCB and external Boost control
- V4.5 — January 2021 revision
- V4.6 — June 2026 revision beginning at serial #3253, adding intelligent momentary/latching footswitch behavior and reducing Boost switching pop

This is an unusually strong example of why the archive needs explicit version records. The customer-facing model name remains Longsword while documented engineering and physical changes accumulate across production.

### Finish variants
EAE documents Standard, Acid Etch, Rose Gold, Camouflage, Purple, Teal and the 2025 10 Year Edition finishes. These should normally be represented as editions/finish variants, not separate models, unless evidence indicates substantive production differences.

Sources:
- https://www.electronicaudioexperiments.com/pedals/longsword
- https://www.electronicaudioexperiments.com/blog/2025/6/17/a-decade-of-studying-the-blade

## 6. EAE Halberd

### Version chronology
EAE's current documentation identifies:
- V1 — original release in Fall 2019, BB enclosure, NOS germanium transistor in one gain stage.
- V2 — August 2021, smaller 125B enclosure, removal of NOS germanium, lower current draw, electronic bypass, Voice switch and protection circuitry.
- V2.5 — January 2025 beginning at serial #1684, revised Pre and Depth control tapers and +3 dB maximum gain.

The archive should not treat V2 as merely a cosmetic enclosure revision. EAE explicitly documents component sourcing, power-consumption and switching changes.

The current manual also states that Halberd began as a standalone iteration of the preamp from Sending, which was originally released in summer 2018. This is a key relationship in the EAE genealogy.

Sources:

## 7. EAE Sending

### V1 → V2 relationship
Sending V1 was a tap-tempo analog delay with a discrete preamplifier. EAE describes V1 as a pivotal product from the bedroom DIY period and says the difficulty of fulfilling its preorders helped catalyze a redesign of manufacturing around surface-mount assembly.

Sending V2, announced for July 2023, is a near-total redesign. EAE retained the concept of a discrete preamp overdriving the delay but changed the preamp itself, added two MN3005 BBDs, a 32-bit processor, presets, MIDI control, LFO modulation and an insert loop.

Archive rule: V1 and V2 are separate production generations under one Sending family. V2 is not a minor revision.

Sources:
- https://www.electronicaudioexperiments.com/blog/2023/6/20/sending-v2-coming-july-2023
- https://www.electronicaudioexperiments.com/pedals/sending
- https://www.electronicaudioexperiments.com/legacy

## 8. Black Arts Toneworks Pharaoh / LSTR

### Pharaoh
BAT describes Pharaoh as a fuzz-rooted dirt pedal capable of boost, overdrive and saturated fuzz. Its dual tone controls, input-headroom switch and diode-selection system create several historically meaningful operating modes.

### LSTR
BAT describes LSTR as the "unruly brother" of the Pharaoh. It uses the same broad Big Muff family territory but is voiced differently, with two tone controls and a wider, more aggressive rock/fuzz response. BAT currently marks LSTR as out of production.

### Pharaoh Supreme
BAT's archived pages identify Pharaoh Supreme as a 2015 product combining the Standard and Primitive Pharaoh capabilities with an adjustable pre-input saturation control.

Archive model:
- Pharaoh: core family
- Pharaoh Supreme: distinct expanded edition/generation
- LSTR: related but independent family
- Sarcophagus: combination product containing Pharaoh and LSTR with switchable order

Sources:

- https://www.blackartstoneworks.com/pedal/lstr/

## Editorial rule

Where builder documentation supplies an exact serial threshold, date, version, or production statement, preserve it as a source-backed claim. Where the community uses shorthand such as "V2.01" or collector generation numbers, label the term as user/collector terminology unless the builder itself uses it.
