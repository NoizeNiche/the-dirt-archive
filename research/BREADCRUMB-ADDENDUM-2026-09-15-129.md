# YARP Breadcrumb 129

## Purpose
Japanese early-fuzz OEM archaeology pass focused on separating the 1967-68 LRE Fuzz Sound manufacturer from the later Shin-Ei era and tracking the unknown Univox OEM behind Uni-Wah and U-1095.

## New research conclusions

- `VAR-120`: The 1968 LRE Fuzz Sound is preserved as a Japanese-made Lafayette/LRE marketed product with the physical manufacturer unresolved. Current evidence treats it as distinct from the earlier 1967 Amplifier Corp. of America / Unicord Fuzz King.
- `VAR-121`: A 2026 synthesis links the same unknown manufacturer to later Univox Uni-Wah and U-1095 Super Fuzz production. This is a high-value shared-manufacturing lead but not a named-factory identification.
- `VAR-122`: Melos is preserved as a possible house-brand clue within this OEM corridor, not as a confirmed manufacturer.

## Cross-reference / dedupe decisions

- Do not assign the LRE Fuzz Sound to Shin-Ei solely because it is Japanese-made. Current research specifically argues the LRE unit was made by a different, still-unidentified factory.
- Do not merge the 1967 ACA/Unicord Fuzz King with the later Japanese LRE Fuzz Sound despite their design relationship and reused naming.
- Do not create a new builder record for the unknown Univox OEM until period documentation names the company or factory.
- Keep Melos as a marketed identity/house-brand hypothesis rather than upgrading it to a manufacturer.

## Evidence notes

Tone Machines Blog's 2023 Fuzz King research separates the summer 1967 ACA/Unicord Fuzz King from the 1968 Japanese LRE Fuzz Sound. Its 2025 Univox Uni-Wah investigation and 2026 Super Fuzz synthesis argue that the later Uni-Wah and U-1095 share a manufacturer and that the same factory probably made the earlier LRE unit, while explicitly noting that the factory name is not yet known.

## Next queue

Continue the hunt through Unicord/LRE catalogs, importer paperwork, Japanese trade advertisements and surviving model-specific documentation for the unknown factory behind LRE Fuzz Sound, Uni-Wah and U-1095. In parallel, audit other Melos-branded effects for evidence that could turn the house-brand hypothesis into a documented corporate identity.
