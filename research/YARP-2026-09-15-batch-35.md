# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 35

## Focus
Kazan Soviet booster/fuzz manufacturing context, including the 1977 product anchor and the newly documented relationship to the local effect family.

## Findings

### Kazan Booster / Бустер
A surviving 1977 manual identifies the product as an amplifier/booster with level limitation, intended for electrified instruments including guitar, organ, and theremin. Modern collector records commonly call it the Kazan Booster, while academic treatment notes that the Soviet name describes distortion boosting and that the unit functions in practice as a fuzz-like effect.

### Manufacturing location
Multiple independent sources place manufacture in Kazan in the Tatar ASSR/Republic of Tatarstan. The accessible period evidence supports a Kazan manufacturing node but does not name a definitive factory legal entity. The archive therefore keeps the builder identity at the facility/region level and leaves the exact corporate manufacturer unresolved.

### Relationship to other Kazan effects
Current collector documentation claims the same factory produced the Kazan Kvaker and Vibrato units. Because that assertion is not backed here by a period factory document, the relationship remains a discovery lead rather than canonicalized manufacturer genealogy.

### Category treatment
The product is retained as fuzz/booster boundary dirt. Its historical terminology matters because Soviet product names do not map neatly onto modern effect categories.

## Evidence boundary
The academic source is particularly useful because it explains the product's historical terminology without turning a functional interpretation into a manufacturer claim. No schematics, PCB layouts, BOMs, gutshots, cloning instructions or circuit-reconstruction material added.
