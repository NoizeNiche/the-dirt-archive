### Findings

- Confirmed the existence of a 1966 LIMMCO Musical Instruments & Accessories catalog, but the catalog itself was not retrieved in this pass. It is therefore logged as a primary-source target rather than mined for unverified model claims.
- Kimberly fuzz chronology is now separated into three production states: initial LIMMCO warehouse production, a later Applied Audio state, and a later UMI-linked state.
- The 1966 launch terminology is split carefully: the fuzz was initially labeled Kimberly Fuzz Box, while later advertising/references use Kimberly Buzz Box and Volume Expander. The exact naming-change date remains unresolved.
- Bob Seidman testimony remains the strongest evidence for the initial LIMMCO-built Kimberly state. This should not be generalized to later Kimberly versions.
- Nomad remains a LIMMCO house-brand/distribution identity rather than a single factory. Its known supplier states continue to include unresolved early Super Fuzz, Applied Audio Banshee/Rogue, and a UMI-linked Tone Booster.
- LIMMCO's early U.S.-Japan business role is useful historical context, but cross-border relationships still require model-specific manufacturing evidence.
