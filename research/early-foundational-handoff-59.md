## Major findings
- Companion FY-2 now has an explicit rare early-germanium research state separate from later silicon-heavy production.
- Jax FY-6 examples documented by Tone Machines span 1971-1976, demonstrating that the Super Fuzz OEM network remained active well beyond the first 1968 Shin-ei-branded state.
- Mica-Tone, Hohner MF-50, Rands and Mayfair are preserved as distinct retail/OEM objects instead of being collapsed into a generic Super Fuzz record.
- The March 1974 Music Trades announcement found for the Hohner MF-50 provides a stronger chronology anchor than undated collector listings.
- Teisco Fuzz Machine is now explicitly separated from Teisco TF-1. Its retail network includes Ibanez Standard Fuzz No. 59 and several other branded forms documented by Effects Database.
- Teisco Wau Wau Fuzz and Guyatone FS-5 are preserved in a separate Japanese fuzz-wah OEM branch; manufacturer attribution remains cautious.
- Kent 6400 is retained as a West German Schaller/OEM branch rather than incorrectly merging it with the Japanese network.
- Univox U-250 is reaffirmed as a separate Univox fuzz model, distinct from U-1093/U-1095 Super-Fuzz production.
- Shin-ei's late-1970s catalog presence is recorded as an upper-bound research lead, supporting continued OEM production into the late 1970s without inventing a universal discontinuation date.

## Evidence rule
The archive now distinguishes at least four kinds of historical relationships: original branded product, later branded OEM product, importer/retail presentation, and probable family resemblance. These relationships must not be silently converted into manufacturer claims. Collector-era terminology is useful for identification, but it is labeled as such.
