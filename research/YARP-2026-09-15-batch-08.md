# YARP 2026-09-15 — FY-6 export-brand reconciliation batch 08

## Purpose

Deepen the Japanese Super-Fuzz export web at the **catalog/identity layer**, following the breadcrumb rule that marketed names must be reconciled before they are promoted to independent builder entities. No specimen archaeology or circuit-reconstruction material is included.

## FY-6 / Super-Fuzz brand cluster

Effects Database's Jax FY-6 entry provides a useful consolidated relationship list for the Japanese OEM family. It includes Apollo No. 846, Avora FY-6, Clear Sound Super Fuzz, Companion FY-6 Fuzz Master, Crown Super Fuzz, Factone Fuzz Machine, Hagstrom Super Fuzz, Hohner MF-50 Master Fuzz Control, J.H. Experience FY-6, Jax FY-6 Fuzz Master, LRE Super-Fuzz, Mana FY-6 Fuzz Master, Mayfair 9540 Fuzz Master, Mica-Tone Fuzz Master, Oscar FY-6 Fuzz Master, Rands FY-6, Zenta FY-6, Shaftesbury Duo Fuzz, Teisco TF-1 Fuzz, Tempo TR-6 Fuzz and Univox U-1095/U-1093 states. Treat this as a discovery/reconciliation index, not blanket proof that every entry was manufactured by exactly the same factory at every date.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/jax/fy6

### 1. LRE Super-Fuzz

LRE (Lafayette Radio Electronics) is now a high-confidence marketed/export identity within the FY-6 family. Tone Machines Blog's model-specific research identifies the 1968 gray LRE Super Fuzz as one of the earliest OEM versions and distinguishes later black and orange/blue catalog states. Importantly, the author reports that the 1970-1971 versions have different enclosure/board histories and that some exact production questions remain unresolved.

Archive treatment:
- marketed/export entity, not a Japanese physical manufacturer;
- separate product-state records for gray 1968 and later catalog states when documentary evidence supports them;
- do not assume every LRE Super Fuzz was identical internally merely because the commercial name remained constant.

Source: https://www.tonemachinesblog.com/2023/03/lre-super-fuzz-and-super-drive.html

### 2. Rands FY-6

Rands is now supported as a model-specific rebrand rather than an unverified name floating in the census. Effects Database identifies a Rands FY-6 Fuzz as another Japanese OEM Super Fuzz rebrand, and Tonehome documents a Rands Super Fuzz FY-6 specimen marked made in Japan by Shin-ei, dated 1977. Tone Machines also records a Rands FY-6 specimen dated October 1976 and uses it as evidence that FY-6-family production continued much later than the common "1960s pedal" shorthand suggests.

Archive treatment:
- marketed/export identity tied to the Shin-ei FY-6 family;
- preserve specimen date evidence separately from model introduction date;
- do not use one dated Rands specimen to date all Rands production.

Archive treatment:
- marketed/export identity;
- factory attribution remains model-specific unless a period primary source states it;
- use the relationship cluster as discovery evidence, not as automatic attribution for unrelated Jax pedals.

### 5. Hohner MF-50 Master Fuzz Control

Hohner belongs in the FY-6 reconciliation queue because Effects Database lists the MF-50 in the same Japanese Super-Fuzz relationship family. It should remain a Hohner marketed-brand record pending model-specific evidence establishing the exact Japanese supplier and production corridor.

### 8. Crown Super Fuzz

The Crown Super Fuzz has its own model-level record in Effects Database, which identifies it as a rebranded Japanese OEM version of the Super-Fuzz circuit. Crown should therefore be retained as a marketed brand node rather than promoted to a separate Japanese builder.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/crown/superfuzz

### 9. J.H. Experience FY-2

The J.H. Experience FY-2 is documented as a 1970s OEM edition of the Shin-Ei FY-2. This is valuable because it proves the **FY-2 export network is not identical to the FY-6 export network**, even when the enclosure families and brand lists overlap. The archive should maintain separate family relationships for FY-2 and FY-6 rather than treating all Japanese fuzz labels as one "Super Fuzz" product.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/jhexperience/fy2

## Catalog implication: do not collapse the network

The archive now has enough evidence to treat the Japanese FY-6 ecosystem as a **family of retail/export manifestations** rather than one pedal sold under a few names. At minimum, the research graph needs distinct nodes for:

Honey → Shin-Ei manufacturer lineage
Univox / Unicord → U.S. marketed/export state
LRE → Lafayette Radio Electronics marketed/export state
Tempo → Unicord-related export state
Crown → marketed/export state
Apollo → marketed/export state
J.H. Experience → FY-2 marketed/export state
Rands → marketed/export state with dated Shin-ei-attributed specimens
Shaftesbury / Rose-Morris → UK marketed/distribution state
Teisco → marketed/OEM state with factory attribution unresolved by product
Hohner → marketed brand with model-specific supplier research required
JAX / Mana / Zenta / Avora / Mayfair / Mica / Oscar / Clear Sound → evidence-qualified reconciliation queue

This is exactly the kind of historical web that the builder-first architecture is designed to preserve: one physical production ecosystem can generate many retail identities without those identities becoming duplicate builders.
