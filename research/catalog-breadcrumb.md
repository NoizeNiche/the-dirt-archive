# The Dirt Archive — Persistent Research Breadcrumb

## Purpose
This file is the handoff point for future research sessions. Read it before beginning a new catalog phase.

## Current research strategy
1. Builder enumeration is broad enough to pause for a product-depth pass.
2. Work product-by-product across the catalog.
3. For each product capture chronology, generations/versions, identification clues, relationships/OEM lineage, source records and image leads where evidence exists.
4. Keep discovery records separate from verified/canonical product histories.
5. Keep image discovery separate from image permission/license and public-republish status.
6. Process the catalog in repeatable batches until the current catalog has research coverage.

## Media rule
An image source is not automatically a redistribution right. Track discovery/reference status separately from permission/license and public-republish status. Preferred public-image sources: manufacturer-provided images with permission, direct permission, Wikimedia/public domain, compatible Creative Commons licenses, and Archive-owned photographs. Collector/marketplace images remain identification references until rights are established.

## Batch 05 notes
- **Deluxe Big Muff Pi:** introduced May 16, 2014 as a feature-expanded modern NYC Big Muff branch. EHX distinguishes it from the later Sovtek Deluxe Big Muff.
- **Double Muff:** introduced 2010 and discontinued 2020. EHX describes it as two Muff Fuzz stages in one compact enclosure, with Single and Double modes.
- **Graphic Fuzz:** introduced 2008 and discontinued 2023. Six-band EQ and switchable fuzz make it a distinct EHX product rather than a Big Muff generation.
- **Little Big Muff Pi:** introduced 2006 as a compact die-cast Big Muff branch. EHX describes it as the classic Big Muff circuit in a smaller enclosure.
- **Hot Tubes:** original product introduced in 1978. Modern EHX version is explicitly presented as reproducing the 1970s original, so the archive separates original and reissue eras.

## Research rule for the 2,000-ish catalog
Do not write 2,000 bespoke essays. Use structured records with concise history, identification notes, lineage/relationship notes, generation records when evidence supports them, source links and one useful image lead. Deep dives are reserved for products where version history or historical importance warrants them.
