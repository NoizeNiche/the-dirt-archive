## Batch 07–11 research notes
- **Marshall SupaFuzz:** 1966 origin as a Sola Sound-supplied Marshall product. A short-lived early MKI-derived/filter form is separated from the conventional 1966–1968 Professional MKII-era SupaFuzz. Marshall assumed production around 1968 and continued into the early 1970s.
- **Sola Sound Tone Bender Professional MKII:** three-transistor Sola Sound model introduced in 1966 and used as the OEM basis for major Vox, Marshall and Rotosound branded products. The canonical Sola Sound record remains separate from those branded variants.
- **Tone Bender MKIII:** major 1968 redesign with a substantially different circuit family and expanded controls. Sola Sound, Vox, Rotosound and Park forms are retained as related but distinct catalog objects.
- **Vox Tone Bender Professional MKII:** British Sola Sound OEM branch supplied to Vox approximately 1967–1968. It is explicitly separated from the later Italian-made Vox V828/V8281 lineage.
- **Rotosound Fuzz Box:** Sola Sound-supplied branded family spanning MKII-related and later MKIII-era production. The archive avoids treating all Rotosound fuzz examples as one unchanging specification.
- **Maestro FZ-1S:** later Super-Fuzz branch, distinct from FZ-1/FZ-1A/FZ-1B and best identified through its later enclosure and expanded control arrangement. Exact factory dates remain incompletely documented.
- **Maestro FZ-2:** extremely uncommon Canadian/Turner-associated Fuzz-Tone branch. Regional provenance is an important identification clue; exact dates remain uncertain.
- **Maestro MFZ-1:** 1976–1978 Total Foot Control fuzz with the unusual full-surface footswitch wedge enclosure and foot-accessible controls.
- **Vox V828:** Italian JEN-built Vox/Thomas Organ branch. It is a separate Italian production lineage, not the same object as the British Sola Sound Professional MKII or MKIII.
- **Vox V8281:** separate later Italian JEN variant with multiple enclosure/panel combinations. Exact chronological sub-division remains incomplete.
- **Honey Baby Crying:** earliest documented object in the Super-Fuzz family, released in 1967 and forming the starting point for the later Shin-Ei/FY-6/OEM network.
- **Shin-Ei FY-6:** 1968 Shin-Ei-branded continuation of Honey’s Baby Crying design and a central reference object for the later OEM network.
- **Companion FY-6 Fuzz Master:** later Companion-branded FY-6 branch illustrating the 1970s private-label network.
- **Shin-Ei Companion WF-24:** integrated 8-Tr fuzz/wah using an FY-6-derived fuzz section, demonstrating the Super-Fuzz architecture being reused in combination effects.
- **Univox U-1095:** North American/Unicord branch with early stamped-metal and later die-cast production forms. U-1093/U-1095 chronology remains an explicit uncertainty rather than a forced conclusion.
- **Colorsound catalog:** Effects Database currently groups 82 products under Colorsound / Sola Sound. The Archive has now begun capturing the dirt and combination-effects branch separately instead of treating Colorsound as a single generic Tone Bender continuation.
- **Colorsound Supa Tonebender:** early-1970s four-transistor Big Muff-derived fuzz branch, distinct from the germanium MKIII/MKIV family.
- **Colorsound Tone Bender Jumbo:** roughly mid-1970s to 1980 three-transistor Big Muff-derived branch with related Vox MKIII and OEM manifestations.
- **Colorsound Power Boost / Overdriver:** major early Colorsound boost/overdrive lineage. Early orange Power Boost examples are associated with 18V operation; the grey 9V Overdriver represents the later naming/enclosure branch. Exact end dates remain intentionally cautious.
- **Colorsound Fuzz Box:** 1990s revival-era one-knob fuzz associated with Dick Denney design material; the Archive does not equate the claimed 1961 design origin with 1961 commercial pedal production.
- **Colorsound Fuzz 4:** modern multi-voicing four-transistor Colorsound fuzz, clearly separated from vintage Sola Sound production.
- **Colorsound Tone Bender Distortion:** later Colorsound-branded product whose name must not be used as evidence of direct continuity with the vintage germanium Tone Bender generations.
- **Colorsound Wow/Wah Fuzz family:** multiple early-1970s combination-effects names are retained separately because exact model lettering, switch arrangements and enclosure details can distinguish otherwise similar objects. Supa-format Wah-Fuzz and Wah-Fuzz-Swell are treated as later configurations, not mere aliases.

## Identity architecture
Model names are not always unique across builders or OEM branches. The public catalog now routes pedal detail pages by unique `pedal_id` while preserving name-based compatibility, and research layers prefer builder-plus-model matching. This prevents identically named products from receiving the wrong historical dossier.

## Research philosophy
The batch system is not a limit on depth. For each builder or family under active research, capture obscure, short-lived, regional, OEM, private-label, transitional and commonly confused models when evidence exists. Favor specific identification clues and high knowledge density over generic prose. Tier A families may receive extensive generation maps, terminology notes, lineage mapping and disputed-history notes; straightforward Tier C records should remain efficient.
