# The Dirt Archive — Persistent Research Breadcrumb

## Current strategy
Builder enumeration is paused. Work product-by-product through the current catalog. Each research record captures as much useful historical and identification information as the evidence supports, including chronology, generation/version notes, external identification clues, OEM/lineage relationships, alternate terminology, source records and an image lead when useful. Discovery records remain separate from canonical history. Image discovery remains separate from republication rights.

## Batch 07 notes
- **Marshall SupaFuzz:** 1966 origin as a Sola Sound-supplied Marshall product. A short-lived early MKI-derived/filter form is separated from the conventional 1966–1968 Professional MKII-era SupaFuzz. Marshall assumed production around 1968 and continued into the early 1970s.
- **Sola Sound Tone Bender Professional MKII:** three-transistor Sola Sound model introduced in 1966 and used as the OEM basis for major Vox, Marshall and Rotosound branded products. The canonical Sola Sound record remains separate from those branded variants.
- **Tone Bender MKIII:** major 1968 redesign with a substantially different circuit family and expanded controls. Sola Sound, Vox, Rotosound and Park forms are retained as related but distinct catalog objects.
- **Vox Tone Bender Professional MKII:** British Sola Sound OEM branch supplied to Vox approximately 1967–1968. It is explicitly separated from the later Italian-made Vox V828/V8281 lineage.
- **Rotosound Fuzz Box:** Sola Sound-supplied branded family spanning MKII-related and later MKIII-era production. The archive avoids treating all Rotosound fuzz examples as one unchanging specification.

## Batch 08 notes
- **Maestro FZ-1S:** later 1970s-era Super-Fuzz branch, distinct from FZ-1/FZ-1A/FZ-1B and best identified through its later enclosure and expanded control arrangement. Exact factory dates remain incompletely documented.
- **Maestro FZ-2:** extremely uncommon Canadian/Turner-associated Fuzz-Tone branch. Its regional provenance is an important identification clue, and the archive preserves its uncertainty instead of inventing precise dates.
- **Maestro MFZ-1:** 1976–1978 Total Foot Control fuzz with the unusual full-surface footswitch wedge enclosure and foot-accessible controls.
- **Vox V828:** Italian JEN-built Vox/Thomas Organ branch. It is not the same object as the British Sola Sound Professional MKII or later British MKIII and must be identified as an Italian production lineage.
- **Vox V8281:** separate later Italian JEN variant with multiple documented enclosure/panel combinations. Exact chronological sub-division remains incomplete, so the archive records the uncertainty.

## Identity architecture
Model names are not always unique across builders or OEM branches. The public catalog now routes pedal detail pages by unique `pedal_id` while preserving name-based compatibility, and research layers prefer builder-plus-model matching. This is specifically intended to prevent identically named products from receiving the wrong historical dossier.

## Editorial rule
Document the object, not the recipe. Do not publish gutshots, schematics, PCB diagrams or complete circuit recipes. Research depth is encouraged; unsupported certainty is not.
