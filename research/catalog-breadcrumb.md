# The Dirt Archive — Persistent Research Breadcrumb

## Current strategy
Builder enumeration is paused. Work product-by-product through the current catalog. Each research record captures as much useful historical and identification information as the evidence supports, including chronology, generation/version notes, external identification clues, OEM/lineage relationships, alternate terminology, source records and an image lead when useful. Discovery records remain separate from canonical history. Image discovery remains separate from republication rights.

## Specimen identity rule
The Archive now treats **appearance**, **production identity**, and **electronics identity** as separate axes. A different enclosure color, graphic or finish is not automatically a new circuit. Conversely, two visually similar specimens may represent different electrical variants, production generations, OEM branches or reissues. When evidence supports a distinction, record it at the specimen/generation level rather than flattening it into the model name.

## Catalinbread specimen rule
Catalinbread is being used as a live test case for the general architecture. Standard models, mini formats, retailer exclusives, limited finishes and confirmed electronics variants stay linked to the parent family without being flattened together. DLS Red is treated as a genuine product/electronics variant. Fuzzrite Germanium is treated as an electronics variant from silicon Fuzzrite. Karma Suture GE and SI are explicit family-level electronics distinctions based on the manufacturer’s own manual index. Cosmetic anniversary, Blackout, Fireworks and retailer-exclusive examples remain appearance specimens unless evidence establishes a circuit change.

## Tone Bender specimen findings
- Fuzzboxes.org explicitly treats the MKI, MK1.5, Professional MKII, British Vox Professional MKII, Italian Vox V828 grey, and Italian Vox V828 black as distinct identification cases. The MK1.5 is a retrospective name for a two-transistor Sola Sound transition, while the V828 is an Italian-made cousin rather than a missing link in the British sequence.
- The MK1.5 has a documented rare gold-painted “Goldie” specimen and early examples with a 500k level pot, demonstrating why color and component history should be recorded independently.
- Professional MKII examples include early short-board MK1.5 conversions and conventional large-board production. Surviving examples can therefore share model naming and appearance while differing in construction history.
- Fuzzboxes.org notes an unusual Sola Sound MKII anomaly whose internal circuit does not match the enclosure expectation. The identification lesson is fundamental: exterior cosmetics can guide a search, but the exact electronics identity cannot always be proven from the shell alone.
- The British Vox Professional MKII, Marshall Supa Fuzz, and Rotosound Fuzz Box were Sola Sound OEM products related to the Professional MKII family. The Italian Vox V828 is a separate PCB-built branch related to the MK1.5 topology, with documented two- and later three-transistor variants.
- MKIII production was also supplied under Vox, Rotosound and Park branding, while later 1970s production moved from germanium toward more consistent silicon devices. These should be represented as related OEM/generation records rather than one universal specification.
- The research source itself emphasizes that exact specimen identification can require inspection of the internal circuitry. The public Archive will document this historical fact without publishing gutshots, schematics or complete circuit recipes.

## RAT specimen findings
- JHS’s historical RAT timeline divides the family into multiple revisions such as 1979 Fringe Logo, 1984 White Face, 1986 Black Face and 1988 RAT2 Flat Top. The Archive treats these as specimen/production identities rather than interchangeable colorways.
- The White Face to Black Face change is documented as a graphics/manufacturing correction rather than a new circuit, demonstrating why visual change and electrical change must remain separate.
- Later sloped RAT2 production is documented as a manufacturing transition. JHS also records an op-amp-era change from LM308 to OP07DP in later production, which belongs in electronics identity rather than appearance metadata.
- The 2003 silver 25th Anniversary RAT is retained as a limited-production specimen because its finish and surviving PCB provenance are part of its historical identity.

## Catalinbread current findings
- The current manufacturer lineup separates a Foundation Overdrive series from a Boost/Fuzz/Distortion series. Current dirt records include DLS, SFT, RAH, Sabbra Cadabra, Formula 55, Formula 5F6, Galileo, Galileo 4K, DLS Deluxe, Clean Little Secret, Little Secret, Fuzzrite, Fuzzrite Germanium, Katzenkönig, Giygas 2K, Fuzzrite Mini and related variants.
- SFT currently appears in standard, Blackout and Fireworks presentations. Manufacturer copy for the current SFT explicitly describes the same insides with new artwork, so these should not be promoted to new circuit records without additional evidence.
- Galileo Red Special is a documented commemorative colorway patterned after the Red Special guitar. It is stored as an appearance variant under Galileo unless independent evidence establishes a circuit revision.
- Katzenkönig has a documented Fireworks Collection edition. It remains linked to the standard Tone Bender MKII + Rat hybrid product identity unless separate evidence establishes electrical changes.
- Dirty Little Secret Deluxe is retained as a distinct product rather than a large enclosure of the standard DLS because the manufacturer documents a substantially expanded design with phase inverter, power-amp simulation, output-transformer section, external controls and a built-in boost.
- Giygas 2K is retained as a distinct current product rather than merged into older Giygas history because the manufacturer describes a new slimmer package and revised gain distribution.
- Fuzzrite Germanium is treated as an electronics-defined sibling of the silicon Fuzzrite. Catalinbread documents two NOS PNP germanium semiconductors plus a polarity-inverter IC and a modern/vintage low-end switching function.
- The Catalinbread manual index explicitly lists Karma Suture GE and Karma Suture SI. These are preserved as distinct electronics variants rather than colorways.

## Identity architecture
Model names are not always unique across builders or OEM branches. The public catalog now routes pedal detail pages by unique `pedal_id` while preserving name-based compatibility, and research layers prefer builder-plus-model matching. This prevents identically named products from receiving the wrong historical dossier.

## Research philosophy
The batch system is not a limit on depth. For each builder or family under active research, capture obscure, short-lived, regional, OEM, private-label, transitional and commonly confused models when evidence exists. Favor specific identification clues and high knowledge density over generic prose. Tier A families may receive extensive generation maps, terminology notes, lineage mapping and disputed-history notes; straightforward Tier C records should remain efficient.

## Public research UI
Pedal cards now expose a research-status badge when a dossier is attached. Pedal detail pages also receive a compact research-status row with linked source and generation counts where the dossier supplies them. The visual specimen layer supplements that research presentation with a dedicated specimen gallery and visible appearance/variant/electronics metadata.
