# 100-Builder Advisory Council

## Important framing

This is a **synthetic builder advisory panel grounded in real builder practices and public documentation**. It is not a record of private interviews, emails, votes, or endorsements. The 100 names below are canonical builder identities already represented in The Dirt Archive's builder census. The panel asks: *Given how this builder publicly documents, supports, designs, discontinues, collaborates on, or historically presents effects, what would plausibly be useful on an independent archive?*

No named builder is being quoted or represented as personally endorsing a feature.

## Research basis

- Wampler maintains manuals for current pedals and a dedicated discontinued-products area, illustrating the value of preserving documentation after a product leaves the current lineup. https://www.wamplerpedals.com/downloads/ and https://www.wamplerpedals.com/products/c/discontinued/
- Walrus Audio exposes manuals, technical support resources, artist material, and discontinued products, showing that builder-facing product context naturally spans documentation, support, and history. https://www.walrusaudio.com/pages/manuals and https://www.walrusaudio.com/pages/pedals
- EarthQuaker Devices publishes manuals, troubleshooting, repair information, and builder/collaboration stories, showing the value of linking product records to support and maker context. https://www.earthquakerdevices.com/support and https://www.earthquakerdevices.com/blog-posts/kyle-ng-of-brain-dead
- Wilson Effects explicitly maintains an archive for pedals no longer offered and discusses custom builds, demonstrating that discontinued and bespoke work can coexist without being treated as ordinary current catalog products. https://www.wilsoneffects.com/faq
- Reverb's interviews with builders repeatedly surface product evolution, collaboration, usability, and discontinuation decisions as meaningful historical context. Examples: Wampler, Fredric Effects, Bondi Effects, and Magnetic Effects.
- BOSS maintains long-form historical product-line histories, demonstrating the usefulness of model lineage and revision chronology for established brands. https://articles.boss.info/echoes-in-time-the-history-of-boss-delay-pedals/


## Council synthesis

The strongest builder-facing need is **control over context and accuracy, not promotion**.

A builder is likely to care that an archive can answer:
- What exactly is this product?
- Is this the original, a V2, a reissue, a limited edition, or a variation?
- When did this version exist?
- What documentation survives?
- Where can an owner find the official manual, support, or repair destination?
- Is an image actually the correct version?
- How can the builder flag an error or provide better evidence?
- How is the builder's catalog represented after products are discontinued?

The most valuable dedicated feature would therefore be a **Builder Profile / Builder Archive page**, not a generic "brand page." It should behave like a historical catalog index with a concise identity block, all archived dirt models, versions, discontinued status, aliases, documented collaborations, and official documentation/support links.

A second high-value feature would be a **Builder Correction & Contribution channel**. It should be private to the maintenance workflow: builders could submit exact corrections, missing products, official photographs, manuals, or historical notes for review. Nothing should auto-publish into the canonical catalog.

A third is **model lineage and revision history**. The current archive already has the beginnings of this through exact Builder + Pedal identities, Versions, Colorways, and comparison. Builder feedback strongly supports making that family structure more visible.

## Proposed builder-facing product layers

| Layer | What it does | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Builder Archive | Dedicated page for one builder's historical dirt catalog | Gives a builder a stable historical landing page without making the site a sales page |
| Catalog timeline | Orders documented models, versions, reissues, and discontinued entries chronologically where evidence exists | Makes revision history legible |
| Documentation shelf | Manuals, official support, warranty/repair destinations, and builder links | Keeps useful owner resources attached to exact records |
| Builder correction intake | Private submission path for factual fixes and source/photo contributions | Gives builders a route to improve accuracy without granting direct catalog write access |
| Exact photo contribution | Controlled way to submit official photographs for missing/revision-specific records | Helps close the exact-photo gate |
| Collaboration context | Structured collaborators, artists, designers, or licensed editions | Preserves meaningful product history without turning the page into a press-release feed |
| Custom / one-off classification | Clear treatment of documented bespoke builds | Prevents custom work from becoming false mass-market catalog data |
| Alias & lineage view | Historical names, successor brands, and product-family relationships | Prevents identity fragmentation |

## What not to build

The council synthesis does **not** point toward builder ratings, popularity rankings, marketplace scores, star systems, "best builder" awards, pay-to-rank placement, or an ad-heavy builder storefront. Those mechanisms would change the archive from reference work into a promotion layer.

Likewise, builder-facing functionality should not bypass the existing exact-identity and photo rules. A builder correction is a valuable source, but it still belongs in the same canonical review process.

## Highest-priority product ideas from the council

### 1. Builder Archive pages

Each canonical builder should eventually have a stable page containing:
- builder identity and aliases
- all archived dirt pedals
- current / discontinued status where documented
- product-family relationships
- documented years or eras where available
- official manuals/support links
- notable collaborations where documented
- exact photo coverage for the builder's catalog

This would make the existing builder filter evolve naturally into a real archival destination without changing the underlying data model.

### 2. Builder correction / contribution workflow

Create a private submission mechanism that allows a builder to provide:
- correction to a model name
- missing pedal
- version/revision distinction
- official manual
- official photograph
- historical note
- official support link

The submission should enter a review queue. It should never directly mutate `PEDAL_INDEX.json`.

### 3. Revision / lineage timeline

Turn existing `version_of`, `version_label`, `parent_pedal`, and variation relationships into a visual product-family trail. This is particularly useful for builders with long-lived designs, reissues, and materially revised products.

### 4. Documentation shelf

Add a compact "Official resources" area on exact records for:
- manual
- builder product page
- support/repair
- warranty
- firmware/software where relevant

These are links to builder-owned resources, not copies of builder support systems.

### 5. Better historical treatment of discontinued builders

A builder should not become less visible merely because its own current site no longer sells an old model. The archive's historical mission makes retired catalogs useful first-class material.

## Governance fit

These suggestions fit the existing single-source-of-truth architecture.

The builder archive should remain a **view over canonical data**, not a parallel database.

Builder submissions should become reviewed research/source inputs, not direct public writes.

The public site should remain descriptive and should not expose internal research confidence, evidence grades, or maintenance workflow details.

## Bottom line

The builder council's strongest message is:

**"Don't just list my pedal. Preserve the story of the product accurately, keep the old stuff findable, make revisions understandable, and give me a sane way to correct the record."**

That direction fits The Dirt Archive unusually well because it strengthens the archive's historical purpose instead of turning it into a sales or social platform.
