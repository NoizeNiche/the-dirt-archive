## Historical findings

- Clark Music Industries is now the strongest documented physical-builder candidate for at least part of the East Coast Fuzz family. Former Clark employee Rich Excellente reported wiring fuzz boxes there, and the Clark-labeled pedal carries a manufacturer/distributor statement. This still does not prove every Manny's, Orpheum, Gracin's, Mica-Tone, or Andre-labeled unit was Clark-built.
- The East Coast Fuzz family has documented commercial presence no later than January 18 1968 for Manny's and October 5 1968 for Clark, tightening the market window without relying on ambiguous pot-code dates.
- Manny's Fuzz is now explicitly separated into two questions: domestic distribution is established, while physical manufacture remains unresolved.
- Andre Fuzzier remains a strong research lead because of shared construction traits with other East Coast examples, but a common factory and an Andre workshop build are still competing hypotheses.
- The interior name `Gene` found in one Orpheum is retained only as a low-confidence lead and is not used to attribute the entire family to Gene Andre.
- AUL remains a separate, well-documented New York OEM shop responsible for a distinct Guild Foxey Lady transition. Its existence demonstrates the regional OEM model but does not solve the East Coast Fuzz factory question.

## Validation posture

The East Coast Fuzz family now has a stronger candidate builder hierarchy:
1. Clark Music Industries as the strongest current candidate for at least a production subset.
2. Andre Audio-Tronics as a possible related workshop or common-source relationship.
3. Applied Audio, UMI, and Universal Amplifier Corp retained only as regional peer candidates, not inferred builders.

No candidate was promoted to a public lineage edge because the family contains multiple enclosure generations and inconsistent component populations.
