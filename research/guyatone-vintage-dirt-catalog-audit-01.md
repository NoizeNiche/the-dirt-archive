# Guyatone vintage dirt catalog audit 01

## Scope
This audit isolates Guyatone's vintage fuzz / distortion / wah-fuzz evidence and preserves uncertainty around Japanese OEM relationships. It is a catalog layer, not a circuit reconstruction.

## Chronology anchors

### FS-1 Bazz/Buzz Box
Surviving Japanese collector documentation identifies a very rare Guyatone FS-1 Bazz Box, probably from around 1967. The unusual spelling is preserved as a specimen-level observation; release dating remains approximate.

### FS-2 Buzz Box
The 1969 Guyatone catalog is a hard chronology anchor for the FS-2 Buzz Box. Contemporary Japanese guitar-media coverage identifies a 1969 example and describes it as an upper-octave fuzz. Preserve 1969 as catalog evidence, not necessarily the exact first-production date.

### FS-3 Fuzz
Guyatone catalogs from 1972 and 1973 show the FS-3 Fuzz. Tone Machines' catalog research places its availability between the continued FS-2 in 1971 and its replacement by the FS-6 Fuzz Sustainer in the 1974 catalog. Effects Database places the FS-3 in the 1972-1973 corridor. The archive should therefore use 1972-1973 as the strongest documented production corridor, with exact launch date unresolved.

### FS-5 Wah-Fuzz
The 1972 and 1973 Guyatone catalogs show the FS-5 Wah-Fuzz alongside FS-3 and FS-4. A surviving FS-5 is documented as Made in Japan. It belongs in the historical Japanese wah-fuzz/OEM map, but physical manufacturer attribution should not be collapsed into Shin-ei without stronger documentation.

### FS-6 Fuzz Sustainer
The 1974 Guyatone catalog shows FS-6, replacing the FS-3 in the listed fuzz position. This is a distinct Guyatone model node and should not be treated as a mere FS-3 revision.

### 1977 PS-series gain line
The 1977 Guyatone catalog explicitly lists PS-102 Zoom Box as a distortion pedal. The same catalog lists PS-101 Phaser and PS-103 Driving Box, confirming that Guyatone's compact PS-series effects line had moved well beyond the early FS family by 1977.

## OEM and family-tree caution
Effects Database and collector research connect numerous Japanese fuzz and wah-fuzz products across Guyatone, Shin-ei, Honey, Teisco, Ibanez and other labels. These relationships are useful discovery evidence, but the archive should keep the marketed Guyatone model, physical manufacturer, and OEM relationship as separate fields until documentary evidence supports a stronger assignment.

## Source notes
- 1969 Guyatone FS-2 chronology is supported by Guitar Magazine's vintage-pedal feature on a 1969 FS-2 and Japanese collector documentation.
- Tone Machines' Guyatone FS-3 chronology cites 1971, 1972, 1973 and 1974 Guyatone catalogs and places FS-3 between FS-2 and FS-6.
- Guitar Catalog's 1974 and 1977 Guyatone catalog pages provide period catalog evidence for the FS-series and PS-series.

## Archive disposition
Guyatone moves from a single scattered Japanese-brand mention into a structured vintage dirt catalog with a clear 1969 -> 1972/73 -> 1974 -> 1977 chronology. Individual specimen generations and factory attribution remain open for later archaeology.
