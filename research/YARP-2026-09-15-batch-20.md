# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 20: obscure historical dirt makers and cross-border lineage

## Scope
This batch continues the builder-first census. It deliberately favors model-level evidence that can establish a builder or relationship node, while refusing to convert repeated internet claims into exact manufacturing chronology when the surviving evidence is weaker.

No schematics, PCB layouts, BOMs, gutshots, cloning instructions, or circuit-reconstruction material are included.

## Garen Electronic Music Instruments MFG. / Garen

**Identity:** French manufacturer, Paris.

**Dirt catalog signal:** Garen Distortion / Chambre de Distorsion; later Garen Wah-Wah with distortion function.

**Evidence:** A Garen family history maintained by Jean Guen's descendants states that the Garen transistor distortion pedal was released in 1966 and had a three-position harmonic selector. The same source states that Garen presented a wah-wah pedal capable of wah and distortion in 1969. Specialist research documents surviving Garen Distortion specimens dated 1966, 1969 and a possible 1970 example, while explicitly treating the exact end of production as unresolved.

**Archive treatment:** Promote Garen from generic historical mention to a canonical French builder candidate with an indexed dirt baseline. Record the 1966 introduction as manufacturer-family evidence, but keep 1966-1970 as a production corridor rather than a hard endpoint.

## Selmer UK

**Identity:** British Selmer entity, distinct from later or non-UK Selmer names.

**Dirt catalog signal:** Buzz Tone; Fuzz-Wah.

**Evidence:** Fuzzboxes.org documents the Buzz Tone as an early British fuzz already presented at the August 1966 British Musical Instrument Industries Trade Fair, with late-1966 British advertising. The same source places its assembly plausibly at Selmer's Holborn factory but labels that factory attribution as likely rather than proven. It also identifies the 1968 Fuzz-Wah as a later redesign of the Buzz Tone's fuzz concept. This gives us a chronological product relationship without assuming the exact production quantity or end date.

**Archive treatment:** Selmer's Buzz Tone and Fuzz-Wah remain separate retail models with a documented developmental relationship. Do not merge the two into one pedal record.

## Astro Amp / Universal Amplifier Corp.

**Identity:** New York City manufacturer.

**Dirt catalog signal:** Astro Tone Fuzz Box / Astrotone; Sam Ash Fuzz Box private-label state.

**Evidence:** Analog Man identifies the original Astrotone as made in New York City in 1966-1968 by Astro Amp, and documents the Sam Ash branded version as a rebrand of the same product family. Specialist timeline evidence likewise places Astro Tone in 1966 and describes Sam Ash as a related New York retail/private-label state.

**Archive treatment:** Canonicalize the physical manufacturer as Astro Amp / Universal Amplifier Corp., preserve Sam Ash as a marketed/private-label relationship, and keep the separate enclosure states searchable rather than counting them as unrelated builders.

## Manny's / East Coast Fuzz cluster

**Identity:** New York retail/brand cluster, physical manufacturer unresolved.

**Dirt catalog signal:** Manny's Fuzz and related Clark / Orpheum / East Coast fuzz identities.

**Evidence:** Recent historical research by Tone Machines Blog identifies multiple newspaper advertisements for Orpheum and other East Coast fuzz-branded products, upgrading the existence/distribution evidence for the cluster. The same research continues to treat the exact manufacturer and first-release corridor as unresolved, with surviving component dates and historical relationships used as supporting evidence rather than a final factory attribution.

**Archive treatment:** Keep Manny's, Clark, Orpheum and related names as marketed-brand candidates until the physical manufacturer is documented. This is a relationship/census problem, not permission to merge them into Mosrite merely because some examples resemble Fuzzrite-derived products.

## Montarbo

**Identity:** Italian manufacturer/brand.

**Dirt catalog signal:** Sinphoton / Sinfhoton; PB-2 Phase Shifter Buzzer as a multi-effect boundary containing the Sinphoton fuzz section.

**Evidence:** Surviving Sinphoton specimens and specialist research document an early physical state with white knobs and stylized Montarbo script, plus a later state with a modern Montarbo logo and altered controls. A Montarbo PB-2 listing documents a separate multi-effect pedal that incorporates the Sinphoton fuzz alongside phase shifting. The long-repeated 1968 introduction date is not treated as proven: Tone Machines Blog explicitly notes that credible primary advertising/catalog evidence for that exact first-release year has not yet been found.

**Archive treatment:** Canonical Montarbo builder target, Sinphoton indexed as the core dirt model, PB-2 retained as a hybrid boundary. Exact first-release year remains unresolved; physical-version chronology is evidence-qualified but not yet a full production ledger.

## Caldironi Musica / Italian Whau-Whau family

**Identity:** Italian manufacturer candidate centered near Milan; keep the manufacturer attribution as evidence-qualified rather than absolute.

**Dirt catalog signal:** Meazzi Whau-Whau Distortion; EUR Distortion Whau-Whau; G.I.S. Distortion Whau-Whau; Goldsound/Silversound/Cosmosound variants and related Whau-Whau/Repeat states.

**Evidence:** Effects Database identifies the Meazzi family as Italian-made and attributes it to Caldironi Musica in Milan, with Meazzi, Goldsound, G.I.S., Mac, Gun and Cosmosound appearing as brand states. ToneHome independently describes Caldironi Musica as a prolific Italian producer of effects marketed under multiple brand names, and dates the Goldsound Distortion Whau-Whau Repeat to 1972/73 while retaining the factory attribution as probable. A surviving sales description also identifies the Meazzi-branded unit as Italian-built and lists Goldsound, Cosmosound and G.I.S. equivalents.

**Archive treatment:** Build Caldironi Musica as a manufacturer candidate/relationship hub, but preserve each marketed name and each documented product state separately. Do not infer that every Italian Goldsound/Silversound/G.I.S./JEI/VOX effect was necessarily made by Caldironi without model-level evidence.

## JEN Elettronica: Ricken's bridge

**Identity:** Already-canonical Italian manufacturer.

**Dirt catalog signal:** Ricken's Fuzz FR III.

**Evidence:** ToneHome identifies the Ricken's Fuzz FR III as made by JEN in Pescara, Italy, during the late 1970s. This adds a later private-label/rebrand state to the already mapped JEN catalog and helps connect the Italian OEM trail beyond the earlier Vox/JEN product families.

**Archive treatment:** Add Ricken's Fuzz FR III as a JEN relationship/catalog row, not as a separate physical builder.

## Herald Electronics and the FZ-1A-derived Japanese cluster

**Identity:** Japanese manufacturer candidate.

**Dirt catalog signal:** Herald Electronics AM-44A Fuzz Master, with Apollo, LRE, Melos, Mica, Monacor and Olson marketed states documented in the same family.

**Evidence:** Effects Database has a dated 1970 Herald catalog entry and identifies the AM-44A as a Japanese-made late-1960s fuzz marketed under several regional names. Surviving Monacor FS-1 and Melos FS-1 listings independently document Japanese production and place these products in the same Maestro-derived retail cluster. This is a strong model-family discovery node, but it should not be used to assert that every visually similar Japanese two-knob fuzz came from Herald.

**Archive treatment:** Keep Herald as a manufacturer candidate with an indexed AM-44A baseline and a relationship map for Apollo/LRE/Melos/Mica/Monacor/Olson. Factory assignment outside documented family records remains unresolved.

## Royal RF-1 / RFC-1

**Identity:** Japanese marketed/manufacturer candidate; physical factory remains model-specific and cautious.

**Dirt catalog signal:** Royal RF-1 / TF-1 Fuzz Box; RFC-1 Double Effect Machine.

**Evidence:** Effects Database identifies the RFC-1 as Japanese-made in the late 1960s/early 1970s and probably produced by Shin-Ei. A 2026 specialist restoration article documents the Royal RF-1/TF-1 as an individual fuzz product and the RFC-1 as a combined Royal fuzz plus effects machine, while explicitly discussing the Royal-branded fuzz as a distinct retail product.

**Archive treatment:** Keep Royal as a marketed/manufacturer candidate with separate RF-1/TF-1 and RFC-1 model records. Preserve the Shin-Ei attribution as probable where cited, not universal.

## Mirano Exciting Fuzz

**Identity:** Japanese marketed brand; manufacturer unresolved.

**Dirt catalog signal:** EF-1 / Exciting Fuzz.

**Evidence:** Tone Machines Blog's March 2026 research is especially important because it uses a Japanese 1976 annual buyer's guide as a period anchor. The pedal was still being sold in 1976, which argues strongly against commonly repeated 1968-1970 release dates. The research instead favors a mid-1970s release corridor and continues to treat the exact manufacturer as unresolved, mentioning the names Mirano, Katayama and Rozz as open research leads.

**Archive treatment:** Promote the 1976 sale evidence into the product timeline, but do not promote the physical manufacturer beyond unresolved/marketed-brand status. This is a good example of replacing inherited internet chronology with period evidence.

## JBX / Melos / Monacor Japanese OEM bridge

**Identity:** Japanese OEM/marketed family.

**Dirt catalog signal:** JBX FS-100 Square Wave / Univox SQ-150 / Melos and Monacor states.

**Evidence:** Effects Database identifies the JBX FS-100 as a Japanese-made OEM version of the Univox SQ-150, with Melos and Monacor-branded examples in the same family. This adds a Swedish marketed label to the Japanese OEM network and demonstrates another case where national brand identity does not identify the physical manufacturer.

**Archive treatment:** JBX remains a marketed-brand relationship node; Univox remains the U.S. marketed/imported identity; the physical Japanese OEM should stay unresolved until model-level manufacturer evidence is found.

## Key census consequences

1. Garen should be treated as a real French builder target, not merely a one-off fuzz entry.
2. Selmer's 1966 Buzz Tone belongs in the British builder census independently of the 1968 Fuzz-Wah.
3. Astro Amp / Universal Amplifier Corp. gives the U.S. census a distinct New York manufacturer and a clean Astro Tone → Sam Ash private-label relationship.
4. Manny's/East Coast Fuzz remains a relationship puzzle, not a confirmed Mosrite subsidiary/OEM record.
5. Montarbo deserves its own Italian manufacturer target with a Sinphoton catalog baseline and a deliberately unresolved 1968 date.
6. Caldironi Musica is emerging as a meaningful Italian OEM candidate whose importance is larger than any single Meazzi pedal.
7. JEN's late-1970s Ricken's FR III relationship expands its catalog bridge.
8. Herald and the FZ-1A-derived Japanese cluster should be tracked as a manufacturer candidate plus retail-name graph, not a flat list of independent builders.
9. Royal and Mirano stay carefully separated from stronger Shin-Ei relationships until model-level evidence improves.

## Evidence quality notes
Primary or manufacturer-adjacent evidence is strongest for Garen's release history. Fuzzboxes.org is especially valuable for period British documentation. Tone Machines Blog provides high-value historical reconstruction for obscure products but explicitly marks some claims as hypotheses; those hypotheses are preserved as such. Marketplace listings are used only as specimen/date corroboration, never as sole proof of manufacturer identity.
