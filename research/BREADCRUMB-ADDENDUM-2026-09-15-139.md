# YARP Breadcrumb 139

## Focus
Continue the hard dirt-device qualification gate, with special attention to early custom and micro-builders.

## Confirmed
- Jürgen Peter: verified custom dirt maker. Historical Museum of Canadian Music material explicitly identifies Peter as the person who built the homemade fuzz box used by Al Birmingham during the 1966 recording of The Haunted's “1-2-5”. This qualifies as documented dirt-device construction, but does not establish a commercial pedal company.
- fOXX / Ridinger Associates remains one canonical builder lineage after the earlier Liverpool Fuzz-Tone evidence upgrade. No duplicate Steve Ridinger builder node was created.

## Boundary decisions
- A single well-documented custom dirt build can qualify an individual as a custom/micro-builder even when no commercial product line is established.
- The archive must not convert a one-off custom build into a fictional company, catalog, or production run.
- Retail advertisement, distribution, importation, or unresolved branding remains insufficient for verified-builder status.

## Research queue
Continue searching early Canadian, U.S., British and European custom/micro-builders. Prefer evidence that names the person or workshop as the actual maker of a dirt device. Once qualified, move the identity into the builder catalog workflow rather than spending unlimited time on unrelated OEM family trees.
