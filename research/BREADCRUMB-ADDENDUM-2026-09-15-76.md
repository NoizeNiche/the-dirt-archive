# Dirt Archive Breadcrumb Addendum 76

Date: 2026-09-15

## Batch
UMI wah and booster OEM audit.

## Work completed
- Revalidated the four UMI own-brand dirt/effect identities currently supported by the research set: Buzz Tone & Volume Expander, Tone Booster, Treble-Bass Booster & Mute, and Wa Pedal.
- Added UMI-branded Apollo No. 843 and Conrad Buzz Box & Volume Expander as marketed-label states in the UMI Buzz Tone family, while keeping Apollo and Conrad separate from the physical manufacturer identity.
- Added a separate unresolved bucket for alternate-brand Tone Booster versions because Gearphoria explicitly says the alternate manufacturers were not identified.
- Preserved a later-format 1970s UMI Wa specimen as a separate research record rather than assuming every Wa enclosure/state represents one uninterrupted production configuration.
- Kept Kimberly split from UMI: Kimberly v1 remains LIMMCO and v2 Applied in the existing transition ledger. UMI is not inferred from family resemblance alone.

## Evidence notes
- Effects Database preserves Richard Soloway's account that UMI began with a fuzz box and wah-wah pedal and later sold UMI equipment through hundreds of music stores.
- A surviving 1969 UMI Wa carries a CTS potentiometer dated to the 18th week of 1969.
- Gearphoria explicitly identifies the UMI Tone Booster and Treble-Bass Booster & Mute as pedals made by UMI, while stating that the alternate Tone Booster manufacturers were not identified.
- Effects Database relates Apollo No. 843 and Conrad Buzz Box & Volume Expander to the UMI Buzz Tone family.
- A Japanese specialist dealer documents a later 1970s UMI Wa format with wah and volume operation, but the exact production chronology remains open.

## Editorial outcome
- No new public lineage edges promoted.
- No alternate-brand Tone Booster factory attribution promoted.
- No Kimberly-to-UMI collapse made.
- UMI remains a distinct New York manufacturer branch rather than a generic label for related late-1960s U.S. fuzz products.
