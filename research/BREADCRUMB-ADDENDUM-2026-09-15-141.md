# YARP Breadcrumb 141

## Focus
Continue strict dirt-device qualification into obscure 1960s-1970s North American and Australian builders, prioritizing evidence that names the physical maker.

## Promoted
- Astro Amp / Universal Amplifier Corporation: verified dirt maker via the New York City Astro Tone fuzz family, documented as made in 1966-68. Sam Ash remains a marketed/rebrand relationship, not a second builder.
- Fi-Sonic / Philip Dreoni: verified dirt maker via the Fi-Sonic Fuzz Blender family and surviving hardware. Phil Dreoni is independently identified as the manufacturer behind Fi-Sonic amplifiers; period Australian advertising places Fi-Sonic fuzz products in 1965-67. Exact revision chronology remains incomplete.
- Verlage Amplifiers / Ken Verlage: verified dirt maker via the Verlage Buzz-A-Tone/Buzz Tone and an early Verlage Model 65 documented with a built-in buzz/fuzz circuit. The 1967 advertising window is stronger than any inferred exact pedal release date.

## Held / exclusions
- Manny's / Orpheum / Clark / other East Coast Fuzz labels remain outside the maker layer. Specialist research still identifies the physical OEM as unknown, despite multiple branded examples.
- ZB Custom remains outside the verified maker layer because it is not established as the builder of the Mosrite-derived fuzz it sold.
- Fi-Sonic standalone pedal assembly details remain partly unresolved. Qualification is based on the documented Fi-Sonic fuzz product family plus the maker identity behind the brand, not on guessed factory records.

## Editorial consequence
The census now includes another Australian dirt-maker lead and a rare Canadian builder, while preserving uncertainty where production chronology or OEM identity is still incomplete. The archive should continue to prefer a defensible smaller census over speculative completeness.

## Next queue
Screen remaining obscure 1960s-1970s Canadian, Australian, U.S. regional, and continental European builders. Prioritize period ads, manufacturer catalogs, surviving-object attribution, and direct maker testimony. Keep unknown OEM families separated from named brands until the physical maker is established.
