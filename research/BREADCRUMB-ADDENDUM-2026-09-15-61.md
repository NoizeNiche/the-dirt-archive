## Historical findings

- Manny's Fuzz has a firm commercial anchor by Jan 18 1968 through a new-goods DiFiore's Music House advertisement in Cleveland, plus a collector account placing an owned example at that retailer in 1967-1968. This establishes distribution and availability, not physical manufacture.
- Orpheum Fuzz is documented in a Sep 13 1968 St. Louis Post-Dispatch used-gear classified, establishing broad circulation outside New York; the evidence still does not identify its factory.
- Clark SS-600 / Fuzz Tone remains the strongest builder-attributed member of the family, with Clark business identity and firsthand wiring testimony supporting production or assembly involvement. This is not generalized to all East Coast variants.
- Gracin's Fubar, AAT Fuzzier, MGI Fuzz, Mica-Tone Fuzz, Halifax Fuzz, and E.U. Wurlitzer Fuzzer Buzzer remain unresolved marketed states. They share family-level relationships and, in some cases, associated reverb-product evidence, but none receives a factory assignment without model-specific support.
- Andre Fuzzier remains a live physical-builder candidate because Gene Andre's independent New Jersey shop and surviving effects show unusually strong construction similarities. A common upstream OEM remains a competing explanation.
- Gilsonite Music Industries is confirmed as an established New Jersey music business, but its corporate/legal record does not establish that Gilsonite physically manufactured the East Coast Fuzz variant. Business existence and pedal manufacture remain separate facts.
- The East Coast family itself is retained as a high-confidence family classification, but its common physical builder remains unresolved. Specialist sources continue to describe multiple enclosure generations and substantial part variation across the late-1960s production window.

## Validation posture

Do not infer a common factory from location, knobs, fasteners, component sourcing, enclosure appearance, or shared reverb-product traits alone. Clark, Andre, LIMMCO, Applied, UMI, Universal Amplifier Corp., and other regional firms remain separate candidate actors until model-specific production evidence connects them.
