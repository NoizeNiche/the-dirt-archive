# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 14

## Scope

This batch continues the builder-first census after the Japanese OEM pass. The target is not individual specimen archaeology, but completion of previously discovered historical builders and separation of marketed brands, OEM suppliers, and unresolved manufacturers.

## 1. Herald Electronics, Japan

Herald Electronics now receives a dedicated catalog anchor through the AM-44A Fuzz Master. Effects Database identifies the AM-44A as a Japanese-made late-1960s fuzz distributed under Apollo, LRE, Melos, Mica, Monacor, and Olson names. The relationship is useful because it gives the archive a concrete model-level bridge between the early Japanese FZ-1A-derived export family and the individual retail labels.

The archive should not treat every visually related Japanese FZ-1A-style pedal as Herald-made. AM-44A is the model-specific anchor; the associated labels remain relationship records until their own specimens or catalog pages provide stronger attribution.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/herald/fuzzmaster

## 2. Earth Sound Research

Earth Sound Research's Graphic Fuzz is now upgraded from a simple census mention to a cataloged historical dirt product. Tone Machines Blog dates a surviving 1976 example, while Effects Database identifies Earth Sound Research as a New York 1970s amplifier/effects maker with the Graphic Fuzz as a distinctive mid-1970s product.

The archive records the Graphic Fuzz as a standalone ESR design rather than inheriting the MXR/DOD op-amp distortion lineage merely because the period overlaps. Exact company chronology and the complete ESR effects catalog remain incomplete.

Source trail:
- https://www.tonemachinesblog.com/2011/10/earth-sound-research-graphic-fuzz.html
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/earthsound/graphicfuzz

## 3. Bell Electrolabs

Bell Electrolabs gains stronger designer-level evidence. Black Guitars reproduces an interview with Tony Koorlander, who worked with Bell from February 1975 to February 1979. Koorlander identifies Stan Wilson as the Bell Fuzz designer, and describes the project as an improvement on the Big Muff Pi rather than a direct factory copy.

The archive therefore keeps Bell Electrolabs as a distinct British builder and the Fuzz as its own model family. Designer attribution is recorded separately from circuit ancestry. Bell's broader module system still requires additional catalog enumeration.

Source: https://www.blackguitars.com/bell-electrolabs.html

## 4. Gretsch Expandafuzz / Controfuzz

Gretsch remains a marketed-brand target with unresolved manufacturing history. Tone Machines Blog documents the 1973 Expandafuzz and identifies the Controfuzz as its fuzz section, alongside other early-1970s Gretsch effects such as the Tremofect and Tone Divider. The same source notes Baldwin ownership of Gretsch during this period and discusses, but does not prove, possible JEN and Maestro relationships.

The archive therefore preserves Gretsch as the marketed entity and does not assign JEN or Maestro as the physical manufacturer without model-specific evidence. Expandafuzz and Controfuzz remain separate searchable product states within the Gretsch effects family.

Source: https://www.tonemachinesblog.com/2012/06/gretsch-expandafuzz-1973.html

## 5. Kay / Uecks

The Kay Fuzz Tone receives a deliberately cautious Japanese OEM record. Tone Machines Blog's 2026 research reports that the Kay pedal contains predominantly Japanese-origin components and that the same or related pedal was sold in Japan under the Uecks name. Uecks is associated with Ueki Gakki, and a June 1976 buyer's guide confirms Uecks pedals were commercially available by that date.

The physical manufacturer remains unresolved. The archive must not convert the Uecks connection into proof that Ueki Gakki built the Kay Fuzz Tone. This is a strong example of the marketed-name-to-OEM distinction the archive is designed to preserve.

Source: https://www.tonemachinesblog.com/2026/07/kay-fuzz-tone-what-we-know.html

## 6. Jen FZ-III and the Italian Tone Bender corridor

The JEN catalog is expanded with the FZ-III as a distinct mid-1970s dirt family. Tonehome places the JEN FZ III in Pescara in the mid-1970s and identifies FBT, JHS, and Ricken's as other branding states. Effects Database separately catalogs the JEN FZ-III and its JHS/Ricken's/FBT relationships.

The earlier JEN Fuzz / Vox V828/V8281 lineage remains separate. The archive should not treat FZ-III as merely another badge on the earlier germanium V828 family: the surviving evidence points to a later silicon design and a distinct product state.

Source trail:
- https://www.tonehome.de/jen/fuzz-fz-iii/
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/jen/fz3
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/vox/thomas/tonebender
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/vox/jen/tonebender

## 7. JEN Double Sound family

The JEN catalog now has a stronger family-level bridge around Double Sound and Super Double Sound. Effects Database associates JEN Double Sound with Italian manufacture and catalogs later marketed states under Vox, Dallas Arbiter, Elka, Unicord, Luxor and other export names. Luxor Double Sound is explicitly identified as JEN-built in Pescara.

For the archive, the key distinction is between the physical JEN family and the various branded/export states. Some related fuzz-wah products remain model-specific or uncertain, so broad family grouping must not overwrite the evidence grade of an individual marketed model.

Source trail:
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/jen/doublesound
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/jen/doublesound/super
- https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/luxor/doublesound

## 8. Firstman / Hillwood

Firstman / Hillwood remains a P0 Japanese builder target, but the current evidence supports only cautious catalog expansion. The archive retains Multivox Big Jam Distortion as an OEM-marketed relationship rather than assuming that every Big Jam product belongs to Firstman. Effects Database's historical entry attributes the Big Jam Distortion to Japanese manufacture around the late 1970s and identifies Firstman as the OEM for Nihon-Hammond, distributed by Multivox.

The builder ledger therefore keeps Firstman/Hillwood canonical at the lineage level but marks the complete dirt catalog unresolved. A future period-catalog pass should look for Firstman-branded fuzz/distortion products outside the Big Jam relationship.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/multivox/bigjam/distortion

## 9. Evidence rule reinforced by Batch 14

Several targets in this batch demonstrate why the archive cannot use visual similarity, component origin, or shared circuit ancestry as a substitute for factory documentation. Kay/Uecks is unresolved despite the Japanese component trail; Gretsch/JEN remains only a reported relationship; Bell's Big Muff ancestry does not make Bell an EHX product; and Herald's multi-brand family is model-specific rather than a license to assign every nearby Japanese fuzz to Herald.

## Status

This is a catalog-completion batch. No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries, cloning instructions, or circuit-reconstruction material are included. Deep specimen archaeology remains deferred until the relevant builder catalogs are substantially mapped.
