# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 10

## Focus

This batch follows the FY-2 export trail outside Japan and the commonly repeated Shin-Ei rebrand map. The key result is a **negative attribution** that is just as important as a positive OEM link: at least one Mexican FY-2-style family appears to be locally built rather than Shin-Ei-made, despite close visual similarity.

## Peak Bell / Mexico corridor

### Peak Bell-2 Fuzz Tone Box
Tone Machines Blog's September 2024 research identifies the Peak Bell-2 as a late-1970s Mexican pedal that closely reproduces the Shin-Ei FY-2 in appearance and construction. The author describes the attribution to a Mexican local builder as likely but not proven, and explicitly rejects the rumor that Shin-Ei necessarily built it for Mexico: the examined components, switches, pots and construction did not match Shin-Ei FY-2 examples. The article instead places Peak within a local Mexican OEM/business corridor and notes that additional visually related Mexican pedals appeared afterward.

Archive status: **MARKETED/REGIONAL CANDIDATE, DISCOVERY**. Do not attach Peak Bell-2 to the Shin-Ei physical-builder node. It belongs in the FY-2-inspired regional copy/derivative family until a named Mexican manufacturer is recovered.

### SonaTone Fuzz / Distorsion
Tone Machines Blog identifies Sona Tone as one of the later Mexican FY-2 copies that appeared near the Peak Bell-2 and Suzuki Fuzz in the same regional corridor. Effects Database independently added SonaTone as a tracked brand/effect in 2025. Exact builder and date remain unresolved.

Archive status: **MARKETED/REGIONAL CANDIDATE, DISCOVERY**.

### Takeuchi Fuzz-Tone / Distorsion
Tone Machines Blog places Takeuchi in the Mexican FY-2-copy sequence, apparently appearing later, around the 1980s, and looking closely related to the same regional builder as Peak Bell and the updated Suzuki. The builder attribution is a specialist inference, not a primary-source identification.

Archive status: **MARKETED/REGIONAL CANDIDATE, DISCOVERY**.

### Suzuki Fuzz-Tone / Distorsion
This is an important same-brand/different-family warning. Effects Database records a Suzuki-branded pedal sold in Mexico in the 1970s that resembles and sounds like a Maestro FZ-1 rather than an FY-2 or Super Fuzz. The page explicitly says it is not an FY-series Shin-Ei fuzz, despite the Suzuki name and Japanese parts. This means **Suzuki cannot be represented as one single product lineage**. The FY-2 Suzuki record and the Maestro-style Suzuki Fuzz-Tone must remain separate records under the marketed brand.

Archive status: **MARKETED REGIONAL ENTITY, DISCOVERY**, with Shin-Ei physical-builder attribution rejected for this specific model.

## Why this matters to the master lineage model

The Peak/SonaTone/Takeuchi sequence demonstrates that visual similarity to a famous OEM pedal is not enough to promote a regional pedal into that OEM factory's catalog. Conversely, the Suzuki case demonstrates that even a brand that appears in genuine Shin-Ei export lists can also carry a separate locally produced or locally sourced pedal based on an unrelated historical circuit family.

The archive therefore needs two parallel relationship types:

1. **OEM/rebrand relationship**: the same physical manufacturer's product sold under another name.
2. **derivative/copy relationship**: a later or regional manufacturer intentionally reproduces the visual/product concept without evidence of being the original OEM.

These should never be collapsed in the public lineage graph.
