# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 116: East Coast Fuzz Release-Date Reconciliation

## Cluster
**Manny's / Orpheum / Clark / related East Coast Fuzz labels**

## Builder status
**Physical manufacturer unresolved.** These are treated as a single documented OEM/house-label family for relationship mapping, not as a confirmed single-factory attribution.

## Release evidence
Newspaper classifieds provide direct 1968 evidence for the marketed products:
- An **Orpheum Fuzz** was advertised as used in the September 13, 1968 St. Louis Post-Dispatch, implying commercial availability before that date.
- A **Clark Fuzz** was advertised as used in the October 5, 1968 Syracuse Herald-Journal, establishing that the Clark-branded version was already in circulation by then.
- A separate 1968 retail advertisement from DiFiore's Music House lists a **Manny's Fuzz** alongside Maestro Fuzz-Tone, Kent Distorter, Mosrite Fuzzrite, Fuzz Face and an Ace Tone Fuzz Master, confirming the product's place in the commercial market by 1968.

A surviving Manny's example is documented in a secondary-market record as having been purchased April 19, 1968, providing additional specimen-level corroboration, but that single purchase date should remain specimen evidence rather than a universal first-sale date.

## Relationship discipline
The 2024-25 historical investigation identifies roughly eleven known East Coast Fuzz brandings. Manny's, Orpheum and Clark are the most visible. The family is associated with a modified Mosrite Fuzzrite-style design, but similarity does not identify the physical factory. Clark may have been more deeply involved in enclosure supply or contracting, but that remains a hypothesis rather than a proven manufacturer relationship.

## Catalog implication
The archive should record:
1. A canonical unresolved **East Coast Fuzz OEM family** node.
2. Individual marketed/retail states for Manny's, Orpheum, Clark and additional labels as evidence supports.
3. 1968 as a firm commercial-availability corridor, with earlier availability plausible but not universally established.
4. Separate germanium and silicon-stated variants where documented, without inferring a global chronological sequence from isolated specimens.
