# Maestro vintage fuzz catalog audit 01

## Scope
This audit establishes Maestro's early fuzz catalog and its production-site lineage. Maestro is retained as a Gibson-marketed brand/entity, with production location and product generation kept explicit rather than treated as one homogeneous factory run.

## Early fuzz chronology

### FZ-1 Fuzz-Tone, 1962
Maestro introduced the FZ-1 Fuzz-Tone in 1962. It is widely documented as the first commercially available fuzz pedal. Effects Database preserves many individual FZ-1 specimens and serial-number observations, providing a useful specimen layer for future archaeology.

The archive records the historical claim and the 1962 introduction without copying circuit-level details.

### FZ-1A Fuzz-Tone, 1965-1968
The FZ-1A followed in 1965 and continued through the late 1960s. Documentary and specimen evidence shows production associated with both Kalamazoo, Michigan and later Lincolnwood, Illinois. This makes the FZ-1A an especially useful example of why production-site changes belong in the generation layer rather than being collapsed into a single undifferentiated model record.

### FZ-1B, 1968
The FZ-1B is a distinct 1968 generation and should not be treated as an FZ-1A variant. Period and retrospective histories describe it as a redesigned silicon-era Maestro fuzz.

### FZ-1S and 1970s Maestro dirt
The broader Maestro 1970s catalog includes FZ-1S Fuzz-Tone, MFZ-1 Fuzz and BB-1 Bass Brassmaster. These belong in the dirt census even though the BB-1 is aimed at bass. Effects Database's documentation index confirms the existence of FZ-1S and BB-1 alongside other Maestro effects.

## Other Maestro gain/fuzz candidates
The historical Maestro universe includes Octave Box products and the FP-2 Fuzz Phazzer, but these should remain separate from the core fuzz line because their effect class differs. The archive should preserve them as adjacent gain/fuzz boundary records rather than inflate the fuzz catalog.

## Production-site distinction
Effects Database documents different Maestro Fuzz-Tone production locations by generation: FZ-1 in Kalamazoo; FZ-1A in Kalamazoo and later Lincolnwood; FZ-1B in Lincolnwood; and later FZ-2 production in Toronto. These location differences should be visible in future model/generation pages where evidence permits.

## Relationship caution
Maestro history is often discussed alongside Gibson, Moog, Guild and later corporate ownership. The archive should preserve those entities as brand, designer, manufacturer or corporate relationship roles rather than merging them into one builder identity. In particular, Glenn Snoddy and Revis V. Hobbs belong in the designer/development relationship layer for the early FZ-1 history.

## Archive disposition
Maestro graduates from a single foundational-brand mention into a structured historical family with at least four major early Fuzz-Tone generations and additional 1970s dirt products. The next useful pass is specimen chronology and period catalog reconstruction rather than more generic historical summaries.
