# Builder → Pedals Research Block 109

## Builder
**ThorpyFX — Adrian Thorpe / ThorpyFX team**

## Verification basis
ThorpyFX's official About page identifies Adrian Thorpe as the founder and says the company launched in 2014, with its first public pedal, Gunshot, released in April 2015. The same page says ThorpyFX hand-builds its pedals in-house in Britain. The current official catalog and individual product pages were used as the primary evidence for current dirt products. Official ThorpyFX blog posts, manuals, and the builder's own product/video pages were used for older products where those identities remain directly documented.

The archive follows the builder's named product identity rather than treating every gain setting as a new category. A product is placed in multiple target categories only where ThorpyFX explicitly gives it multiple dirt identities or distinct dirt sections.

## Product notes
- **Gunshot:** ThorpyFX explicitly identifies Gunshot as an overdrive, describing its cascading gain engine and the original goal of making an overdrive that responds like a real valve amplifier. The current MKIIB keeps the same core sonic identity, so the lineage is consolidated rather than split into version duplicates.
- **Peacekeeper:** ThorpyFX explicitly calls Peacekeeper a low-gain overdrive. Its broad range from near-clean edge-of-breakup through low/mid gain remains part of the overdrive identity.
- **Kudu:** ThorpyFX explicitly calls Kudu an overdrive and describes it as a mid-gain drive and gain-stacking pedal. It remains Overdrive only.
- **The Dane:** ThorpyFX explicitly describes both the MKI and MKII as a dual mid-gain overdrive and boost pedal. The boost side is outside the archive's target categories, so The Dane remains Overdrive only.
- **Electric Lightning:** ThorpyFX describes this as a valve overdrive and boost. The boost side is outside scope; the named drive identity is Overdrive.
- **Bunker:** ThorpyFX presents Bunker as a Plexi Overdrive. It remains Overdrive only because no separate named distortion or fuzz identity is established in the product description.
- **Warthog:** ThorpyFX names Warthog a distortion and separately explains that its gain controls can move from clean boost through overdrive into distortion and, at extreme settings, fuzz. An official ThorpyFX guidance article explicitly says it can provide boost, overdrive, distortion, or fuzz. Because the builder itself assigns all three target dirt identities to the pedal, Warthog is retained in Overdrive, Distortion, and Fuzz.
- **Cogmeister:** ThorpyFX explicitly describes Cogmeister as a single pedal containing booster, overdrive, distortion, and fuzz sections in series. It therefore receives all three target dirt categories while the boost function itself remains outside the archive.
- **Fallout Cloud:** ThorpyFX explicitly identifies Fallout Cloud as a fuzz and describes it as a Triangle-era-inspired four-transistor fuzz. It remains Fuzz only.
- **Field Marshal:** ThorpyFX explicitly identifies Field Marshal as a fuzz, specifically a modern version of the Lovetone Big Cheese. Its tone-stack bypass and boostable balance control remain functions of the fuzz circuit, not extra dirt categories.
- **WOPR:** ThorpyFX explicitly identifies WOPR as a combination built from the Fallout Cloud concept plus a silicon Rangemaster-inspired circuit. The current product title calls it Fuzz and Boost, so it remains Fuzz only.
- **Shaftesbury Duo Fuzz:** ThorpyFX presents the pedal as the Shaftesbury Duo Fuzz, a recreation of a vintage Duo Fuzz with two fuzz voicings. The standard and Heritage editions are consolidated because they are the same named product with different enclosure formats/features.
- **Six Zero:** ThorpyFX describes Six Zero as a vintage-style fuzz combined with a tremolo. The fuzz is the target dirt identity; the tremolo is outside scope.
- **Hanami:** ThorpyFX explicitly identifies Hanami as a germanium fuzz and describes it as the successor to the earlier Boneyard concept. It remains Fuzz only.
- **Boneyard:** ThorpyFX and reputable coverage identify Boneyard as a germanium fuzz covering multiple Tone Bender-era voices. It remains Fuzz only; different transistor-mode and bias settings are behaviors inside the named fuzz product.
- **Veteran:** ThorpyFX's official 2016 product video describes Veteran as a combined Germanium Fuzz and Rangemaster-style treble booster, with the fuzz side first in the signal path. The archive retains Veteran historically as Fuzz only because the other section is a boost rather than Overdrive or Distortion.
- **Old Timer:** ThorpyFX explicitly describes Old Timer as a three-in-one pedal containing an Octavia-type octave fuzz, a Fuzz Face-style section called Tacit Blue, and a Uni-Vibe. It therefore receives Fuzz only; the Uni-Vibe section is outside scope.

## Explicit exclusions / restraint
- **Scarlet Tunic:** excluded because ThorpyFX presents it primarily as an amp emulator rather than a named Overdrive, Distortion, or Fuzz pedal.
- **The J:** excluded because ThorpyFX explicitly presents it as an analog amp emulator based on the Lazy J20. Its ability to distort is part of the amp-emulation behavior, not a separately named dirt identity.
- **Heavy Water:** excluded because it is a dual boost product. ThorpyFX discusses overdrive-like results when stacking its two boost sides, but the named identity remains Boost.
- **Team Medic:** excluded because ThorpyFX presents it as an EQ/boost/buffer utility rather than a target dirt product.
- **Chain Home:** excluded because it is a tremolo plus boost product.
- **Deep Oggin, Camoflange, Pulse Doppler, ER-2:** excluded as modulation effects.
- **For Teddy:** excluded because the current product listing does not establish a sufficiently clear target dirt identity for the archive.
- **Fuzz Remains DIY kit:** excluded because the archive is cataloging finished builder products, not DIY kits.
- **WOPR boost section:** not promoted to Overdrive because ThorpyFX identifies that side as a Rangemaster-inspired boost while the target dirt identity of the complete pedal is Fuzz.
- **Six Zero tremolo section:** not counted separately because modulation is outside scope.
- **Boneyard transistor modes:** not counted as separate pedals or categories because they are alternate operating modes inside one named fuzz product.
- **Normal revisions and enclosure versions:** consolidated where ThorpyFX presents them as the same named product lineage. This includes Gunshot versions, The Dane MKI/MKII, Field Marshal versions, Shaftesbury standard/Heritage formats, and similar normal revisions.
- **2000 AD collaboration editions:** not added as separate product records where ThorpyFX explicitly describes them as special editions of Gunshot, Warthog, and Fallout Cloud. The underlying ThorpyFX pedal identities are already captured above.
