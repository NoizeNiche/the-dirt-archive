# Builder → Pedals Research Block 110

## Builder
**Mr. Black — Jack Deville / Jack Deville LTD.**

## Verification basis
Mr. Black's official About page says the product line was founded in early 2012 as a special division of Jack Deville LTD., and that Jack Deville designs the circuits and the pedals are handmade in Portland, Oregon. The current Mr. Black catalog still lists dedicated overdrive, distortion, and fuzz products, while Mr. Black's historical product pages and established pedal references document additional discontinued dirt products.

The archive follows named product identity rather than assuming that every high-gain setting creates a second category. Boost-first products and modulation/reverb products remain outside the census.

## Product notes
- **Deuce Coupe:** Mr. Black's current page describes the revived Deuce Coupe as a naturally responsive overdrive with a separate Ultra Boost function. The boost remains outside scope, while the named drive identity is retained as Overdrive.
- **Bass OD Mk. II:** Mr. Black explicitly describes this as a bass overdrive/preamp with touch-sensitive grind. It remains Overdrive only.
- **BB-74x:** historical references identify BB-74x as an overdrive built around the response of an 18-watt EL84 combo. It remains Overdrive.
- **Vintage Overdrive:** Mr. Black explicitly identifies this as an analog overdrive and boost. The boost function is outside scope, so it remains Overdrive.
- **Black LTD. Overdriver / Overdriver Mk. II:** Mr. Black's own historical pages explicitly call both products overdrive. They remain historical Overdrive entries, with the Mk. II kept separate as a distinct named limited product.
- **Classic 88:** established historical listings identify Classic 88 as a re-engineered tube-style overdrive. It is retained historically under Overdrive.
- **Gold Drive "One Hundred":** established historical listings identify this as an EL34-style drive designed around hot British amplifier roar. It is retained as Overdrive; it is not promoted into Distortion solely because it can reach aggressive gain.
- **Jalapeño:** historical catalog references identify this as an Overdrive. It remains Overdrive.
- **JDS-50:** established product references identify JDS-50 as an Overdrive. It remains Overdrive.
- **OD-503 Mini Amplifier Overdrive:** the product name itself and historical catalog references identify it as an amplifier-style Overdrive. It remains Overdrive.
- **Orro:** established references identify Orro as a mid-gain Overdrive. It remains Overdrive only.
- **Sour Cream & Onion:** historical catalog references identify this product as an Overdrive. It remains Overdrive.
- **Super-Extra Five Thousand:** historical catalog references identify it as a clean boost plus amplifier overdrive. It is included for the Overdrive side while the boost function remains outside scope.
- **ThunderClaw:** Mr. Black's current page explicitly calls ThunderClaw a high-gain distortion pedal. It remains Distortion only despite its ability to clean up with the guitar volume.
- **White Widow:** established references identify White Widow as a dedicated Distortion. It remains Distortion only.
- **Black LTD. Distortion:** Mr. Black's historical product page explicitly identifies this as a high-gain distortion, with a limited run of 50 pieces. It remains Distortion.
- **SludgeMaster:** Mr. Black explicitly identifies SludgeMaster as a high-gain fuzz built around the late-1970s IC distortion/sustainer sound. It remains Fuzz only.
- **Black LTD. Darling Fuzz:** Mr. Black's historical page identifies this limited product as a fuzz. It remains Fuzz.
- **Darling Fuzz / Black Fuzz:** historical references identify Black Fuzz as the discontinued version/lineage of Darling Fuzz. The archive consolidates those names rather than counting a color/limited naming change as a second fuzz product.
- **Doomstick Fuzz / Doomstick II Fuzz:** specialist coverage identifies Doomstick as a one-knob, aggressively gated fuzz and later Doomstick II as its follow-up/revoiced version. These remain Fuzz entries and are treated as one Doomstick lineage unless Mr. Black presents a materially distinct named product.
- **Barbeque Fuzz:** historical product coverage identifies Barbeque as a Mr. Black Fuzz. It remains Fuzz only.

## Explicit exclusions / restraint
- **Boost Tiger, Black LTD. Treble Booster, Boost Drive One-Hundred, Black LTD. Power Drive:** excluded because their named identity is primarily Boost rather than Overdrive, Distortion, or Fuzz.
- **BloodMoon, SuperMoon, Eterna, Heaven's Gate, Dark Echo, TunnelWorm, GilaMondo, Pana-Trem, Vintage Ensemble, Mod.One, and other modulation/reverb/delay products:** excluded because they are outside the three target dirt categories.
- **High-Five:** excluded because it is an instant power-chord generator/effect rather than a dedicated dirt pedal.
- **Mini Octaves and other octave/modulation-only products:** excluded.
- **Black LTD. Darling Fuzz color/limited variants:** consolidated with the underlying Darling Fuzz identity.
- **Doomstick naming:** Doomstick and Doomstick II are treated as one product lineage for now because the later version is a revision of the same named fuzz concept; no separate circuit-family record is created from the revision alone.
- **High-gain behavior:** ThunderClaw, Vintage Overdrive, JDS-50, Orro, and other gain pedals are not automatically duplicated into adjacent categories merely because they can produce more saturated tones.
- **Historical limited products:** retained where product-level references clearly identify the dirt function; weakly documented one-offs and mystery products are excluded.
