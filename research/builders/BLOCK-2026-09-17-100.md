# Builder → Pedals Research Block 100

## Builder
**Electronic Audio Experiments (EAE) — John Snyder / EAE**

## Verification basis
Electronic Audio Experiments' official current pedal catalog and official Legacy Products pages were used as the main source for the census. EAE's own product pages and its “Which EAE drive should you buy?” guide explicitly describe many of the company's drive, distortion, and fuzz identities. Current products are separated from discontinued/hiatus products where the company itself does so.

EAE itself notes that “drive pedal” is often used as shorthand for pedals that distort a guitar signal and that boost, overdrive, distortion, and fuzz overlap heavily. The archive therefore does not infer extra categories from tone descriptions alone. A pedal is placed in a target category when EAE explicitly identifies that product with the category or gives it a distinct dirt function.

## Product notes
- **Halberd:** EAE explicitly identifies Halberd as a transistor-based overdrive and describes it as a medium-gain overdrive circuit. It remains Overdrive only.
- **Citadel:** EAE explicitly describes Citadel as a traditional overdrive as well as a preamp. The preamp function is outside the target categories, so it remains Overdrive only.
- **Model feT:** EAE explicitly describes Model feT as a smooth overdrive and an amplifier-style preamp. It remains Overdrive only.
- **Limelight:** EAE explicitly calls Limelight a dual-channel overdrive. Its separate boost is not counted as a dirt category; its ability to push the overdrive channel into heavy distortion does not create a second Distortion identity.
- **The Bard:** EAE explicitly calls The Bard a compact overdrive pedal. Higher Drive settings can distort harder, but the product identity remains Overdrive.
- **Dagger:** EAE's archived page describes Dagger as a Longsword-based drive pedal and explicitly identifies the Drive control as increasing overdrive. It remains Overdrive only.
- **0xEAE Boost:** EAE's Legacy catalog calls this a boost/overdrive with massive output. It is retained under Overdrive, while the boost function itself is outside the target scope.
- **Greathammer:** EAE explicitly calls Greathammer a bass-focused overdrive. Its very wide gain range and ability to become rowdy do not create a separate Distortion entry.
- **Longsword:** EAE's current and official technical material identify Longsword as a general-purpose distortion pedal. Its broad range includes lighter gain, but the manufacturer's own product identity is Distortion, so it is retained there rather than also being inferred as Overdrive.
- **Surveyor:** EAE explicitly describes Surveyor as a Tube Voice distortion generator and a compact reinterpretation of the IVP preamp. It remains Distortion only.
- **Dude Incredible / Tube Voice:** EAE describes the Tube Voice side as a transformer-loaded overdrive circuit with medium-gain and distorted textures. It is retained under both Overdrive and Distortion because the company explicitly describes it as an overdrive circuit that produces distinct distorted sounds as a core channel function.
- **Glaive:** EAE explicitly identifies Glaive as an octave fuzz and describes up to extreme gain, gating, and octave-up behavior. It remains Fuzz only.
- **0xEAE Fuzz:** EAE explicitly identifies this as a fuzz and calls it an octave fuzz companion. It remains Fuzz only.
- **Eldritch Blast:** EAE explicitly calls Eldritch Blast a fuzz built around a high-gain octave/intermodulation generator. It remains Fuzz only.
- **Dude Incredible / Percolate:** EAE explicitly describes this half of Dude Incredible as a Percolator-style fuzz. It is retained under Fuzz.

## Explicit exclusions / restraint
- **Beholder:** excluded because EAE identifies it primarily as a reverb pedal with a fuzz circuit, making reverb the product's principal identity rather than a standalone dirt pedal.
- **Sending / Sending V2:** excluded because these are delay products with a colorful preamplifier, not standalone dirt identities.
- **Hypersleep, Mirror House, Scattering, Prismatic Wall, and other time/modulation/reverb products:** excluded.
- **µ Blaster and similar boost/utility products:** excluded when the primary identity is boost or signal shaping rather than Overdrive, Distortion, or Fuzz.
- **Model feT / Citadel / Surveyor amplifier-emulation functions:** the amp/preamp aspect is outside the target categories; only the explicitly identified dirt function is counted.
- **0xEAE Bass Driver:** excluded because EAE presents it as an adaptation of a solid-state bass amplifier rather than as one of the three core dirt pedal identities. The successor Greathammer is retained because EAE explicitly calls it a bass-focused overdrive.
- **Glaive vs. 0xEAE Fuzz:** kept as separate named product identities because EAE presents Glaive as a new product and describes it as a reworking of the discontinued 0xEAE Fuzz for wider availability, rather than simply a version label.
- **Dude Incredible:** retained as a single product identity with its two explicit dirt channels rather than splitting the chassis into fake separate pedals. The channel-specific notes explain the Overdrive/Distortion and Fuzz functions.
- **B-stock, finish variants, normal revisions, and colorways:** consolidated with the underlying product identity.

## Status
**Verified research block.** Electronic Audio Experiments is a previously uncovered builder in the fresh census. The current and legacy catalogs establish a substantial lineup across Overdrive, Distortion, and Fuzz. The clearest category overlaps are within Dude Incredible, where EAE explicitly documents a transformer-loaded overdrive/distortion Tube Voice channel and a Percolator-style fuzz channel. Longsword and Surveyor are retained as Distortion identities, while Halberd, Citadel, Model feT, Limelight, Bard, Dagger, 0xEAE Boost, and Greathammer are retained as Overdrive identities. Glaive, 0xEAE Fuzz, Eldritch Blast, and the Percolate side of Dude Incredible are retained as Fuzz identities. Non-dirt multi-effects, delays, reverbs, boosts, and amp-oriented products without a distinct target dirt identity remain excluded.
