# Block 082 — Orange Amplification

## Builder
**Orange Amplification**

## Verification basis
Primary and authoritative sources reviewed:
- Orange official current Pedals catalog
- Orange official individual product pages for Fur Coat, Getaway Driver, Guitar Butler, Bass Butler, and Bax Bangeetar
- Orange manufacturer-authored Fur Coat manual
- Orange product/support material describing Guitar Butler's Dirty-channel overdrive behavior
- active-repository search for “Orange Amplification” before addition

The active repository contained no prior Orange Amplification match before this block was added. Orange is treated as the builder identity for Orange-branded dirt pedals documented below.

The reviewed material supports a focused Orange dirt catalog spanning current/recent Overdrive, Distortion, and Fuzz products. Preamp pedals are included where Orange explicitly documents them as delivering the target dirt function. Amplifier products are not treated as pedals, even where their manuals describe overdrive or distortion.

## Overdrive
Current / manufacturer-supported identities:
- Getaway Driver
- Guitar Butler
- Bax Bangeetar

Historical / manufacturer-supported identities:
- none identified

Verification notes:
- **Getaway Driver** is an explicitly named Orange Overdrive pedal. Orange describes it as a hot-wired 1970s Orange amp-in-a-box with bold gain and edge-of-breakup response.
- **Guitar Butler** is retained as Overdrive because Orange's product/support material describes its Dirty channel as a classic overdrive channel and the current product page identifies it as a dual-channel preamp pedal with a dedicated Dirty channel. The clean channel, compressor function, CabSim, and routing features do not create additional target identities.
- **Bax Bangeetar** is retained as Overdrive because Orange explicitly states that the pedal provides a wide range of overdriven and distorted tones and describes it as a standalone drive pedal. The product is therefore recorded in both Overdrive and Distortion below.

## Distortion
Current / manufacturer-supported identities:
- Bax Bangeetar
- Bass Butler

Historical / manufacturer-supported identities:
- none identified

Verification notes:
- **Bax Bangeetar** receives Distortion as well as Overdrive because Orange explicitly describes its gain structure as producing overdriven and distorted tones.
- **Bass Butler** receives Distortion because Orange explicitly describes its Guitar/Dirty channel as delivering gritty, guitar-style distortion and searing distortion inspired by classic Orange guitar amps. It remains Distortion only because the reviewed manufacturer material does not explicitly present it as a separate Overdrive product.

## Fuzz
Current / manufacturer-supported identities:
- Fur Coat

Historical / manufacturer-supported identities:
- none identified

Verification notes:
- **Fur Coat** is explicitly presented by Orange as a vintage-style fuzz pedal inspired by the Foxx Tone Machine. Its switchable octave-up and active EQ are secondary functions and do not create separate dirt identities.

## Category-overlap decisions
- **Bax Bangeetar**: Overdrive + Distortion. Orange's manufacturer page explicitly describes an enormous range of overdriven and distorted tones.
- **Getaway Driver**: Overdrive only. Orange explicitly names it an Overdrive pedal and describes 1970s amp-like gain. Higher saturation is treated as operating range rather than a second catalog identity.
- **Guitar Butler**: Overdrive only. Orange/support material explicitly describes the Dirty channel as a classic overdrive channel. Its preamp, compression, CabSim, and routing functions remain secondary.
- **Bass Butler**: Distortion only. Orange explicitly describes the guitar-style channel as distortion. No separate Overdrive identity is promoted from generic gain language.
- **Fur Coat**: Fuzz only. Orange explicitly identifies it as a vintage fuzz pedal. Octave-up is a secondary effect.

## Historical / variant handling
- No separate historical Orange dirt pedal identity was established from the reviewed manufacturer material. Orange's current site states that the Bax Bangeetar was its first pedal since the 1960s, so speculative or weakly sourced 1960s pedal names are not promoted into the census.
- Product revisions, regional pages, availability states, and presentation variants do not create additional dirt identities.
- Guitar Butler and Bass Butler remain separate named products despite their shared “Butler” naming.

## Exclusions
- Orange amplifiers, combo amps, heads, and speaker products are outside the pedal census even where their manuals describe overdrive or distortion behavior.
- Terror Stamp is excluded as an amp/power-amp product rather than a dedicated dirt-effect identity.
- King Comp, Two Stroke, Amp Detonator, Acoustic Pedal, and other current Orange products without an explicit target dirt identity are excluded.
- Generic saturation, gain, grit, or distortion-like descriptions are not used to manufacture extra categories where the product-level classification is unclear.
- Cloning, schematics, components, internal construction, and reverse-engineering material are outside scope.
