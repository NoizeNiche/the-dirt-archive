# Builder → Pedals Research Block 095

## Builder
**Mask Audio Electronics — Alec Breslow**

## Verification basis
Mask Audio Electronics' official About page identifies Alec Breslow as the person behind the company and describes the move from DIY projects into a line of production pedals. The official pedal collection identifies a separate production-pedal catalog, while the site also maintains a distinct DIY Projects catalog. This block limits the census to named physical production pedals and clearly documented former production/limited pedals, excluding DIY-only projects, utilities, modulation, and unresolved catalog names.

The builder's own product descriptions are unusually explicit about fuzz identity and occasionally describe usable overdrive territory. Category overlaps are retained when the manufacturer clearly presents the target function as part of the product's design, but gain behavior alone is not promoted into a second category. Normal revisions, finishes, and small-batch germanium selections are not split into separate identities.

## Product notes
- **Germanium Part Garden:** Mask Audio explicitly describes this as an overdrive that can become a fuzz and separately describes fuzz gain/compression behavior. It is therefore retained in both Overdrive and Fuzz.
- **Part Garden:** the official page describes it as two fuzzes in one pedal and explicitly says it can work as a smooth overdrive, wild fuzz, or anything in between. It is retained in both Overdrive and Fuzz. The manufacturer currently marks the product discontinued as of December 10, 2025, with remaining stock on clearance; this does not create a separate product identity.
- **LARAN (prebuilt):** the official MAE page identifies this as a fully built fuzz made in collaboration with Gorva Design and describes a range from mild, crisp overdrive to full-on fuzz. It is retained in both Overdrive and Fuzz. The DIY kit is not separately counted because the archive is tracking physical pedal identities rather than kit listings.
- **TRUST YOURSELF:** MAE's official page directly identifies the design as a Rat-based pedal. The Stompbox Sonic reference library independently classifies Trust Yourself as Distortion, and a current product reference also lists it as a RAT-style distortion. It is therefore retained under Distortion rather than forcing an Overdrive label from generic gain behavior.
- **Little Acorn:** the official product page describes it as a no-knob Big Muff-style design, and MAE's official Fuzz-tagged production collection includes Little Acorn. It is retained under Fuzz.
- **MAYBE:** the official page explicitly describes the pedal as combining the YES! and NO. circuits and says it can provide full-bodied fuzz; one side can also be pushed through an overdrive. Because the core named identity is a dual-fuzz design, it is retained under Fuzz only rather than promoting its secondary overdrive behavior into a separate category.
- **CLUBBER LANG:** MAE describes this as a Harmonic Percolator-inspired design with a dedicated fuzz stage and increased gain. It is retained under Fuzz only.
- **Hot Water:** MAE describes the pedal as using a Big Muff-style transistor count with four times the gain and explicitly says higher gain increases fuzz compression. It is retained under Fuzz.
- **BSRI x MAE | MAYBE THE REAL TREASURE...:** the official collaboration page explicitly calls this a fuzz pedal and describes its gain staging as moving toward a controllable distortion vibe. It is retained under Fuzz only because the manufacturer presents the product identity as fuzz rather than a separate distortion product.
- **CIVIL MATH:** the manufacturer describes the design as a Russian Big Muff adaptation with a modified boost and muff section. It is retained under Fuzz because the product is explicitly framed around the Russian Big Muff family. The aggressive boost/saturation behavior does not create a separate Distortion identity.
- **BLACK MATH:** the manufacturer describes the design as based on NYC Big Muff reissues and focuses on the muff gain/tone structure. It is retained under Fuzz. Stompbox Sonic's reference library also independently categorizes Black Math as Fuzz.
- **CASCADER:** MAE calls Cascader a one-knob fuzz and describes it as combining a smooth fuzz with a low-gain gated fuzz, while noting the resulting mix can cross into distortion-like saturation. It is retained under Fuzz only.
- **Screws:** the official product page explicitly identifies it as an octave fuzz with an ever-present octave. It is retained under Fuzz.
- **YES!:** MAE's DIY page identifies YES! as the DIY version of the now-discontinued YES! gated fuzz and describes the production identity in explicitly fuzz terms. It is retained as a historical Fuzz identity. The DIY version itself is not separately counted.
- **NO.:** MAE's DIY page identifies NO. as the DIY version of the discontinued NO. octave fuzz. It is retained as a historical Fuzz identity. The DIY version itself is not separately counted.
- **Ears Come To Die:** MAE's official DIY page states that this fuzz was originally released as a limited-edition Fuzz Lab pedal and is now offered as a DIY project. The historical physical pedal identity is therefore retained under Fuzz, while the DIY project listing is excluded as a separate identity.

## Explicit exclusions / restraint
- **DIY-only projects and kits:** excluded from the production-pedal census, including Dark Grime DIY Project, Small Format Drive (SFD) DIY Project, Big Clang DIY Project, Modern Revolt Octave Fuzz DIY Project, YES DIY, NO DIY, LARAN DIY KIT, Titan, Fragments, Ears Come To Die DIY, and other DIY listings. Where a DIY listing directly documents a former physical production pedal (YES!, NO., Ears Come To Die), only the former pedal identity is retained.
- **ALONE:** excluded as modulation/flanger rather than dirt.
- **NECKBRACE:** excluded as a phaser; its internal output-gain trim does not create a target dirt identity.
- **SEND-IN MODS:** excluded as a service rather than a standalone pedal identity.
- **???**: excluded because the official catalog does not provide a resolved product identity suitable for the census.
- **TRUST YOURSELF:** retained under Distortion only. Its Rat association is clear enough to support the target category, but no separate Overdrive entry is created merely from its gain control.
- **MAYBE and BSRI x MAE | MAYBE THE REAL TREASURE...:** fuzz identities retained despite descriptions of distortion/overdrive territory; those descriptions are treated as operating range rather than separate builder-level product categories.
- **Germanium Part Garden variants:** Chef's Choice germanium transistor selections are treated as configurations of one Germanium Part Garden identity, not separate products.
- **Cosmetic/finish variations:** not split into separate identities.
- **Collaborations:** BSRI x MAE | MAYBE THE REAL TREASURE... and LARAN (prebuilt) are retained as cataloged physical pedal identities because MAE presents them in its production pedal catalog; their collaborator relationships do not create duplicate builder identities in this block.

## Status
**Verified research block.** Mask Audio Electronics is a previously uncovered builder in the fresh census. The official catalog documents a strongly fuzz-oriented physical pedal lineup, with explicit historical/discontinued fuzz identities and one clear Rat-style distortion identity. Germanium Part Garden and Part Garden, plus the prebuilt LARAN collaboration, have sufficiently explicit manufacturer wording to retain both Overdrive and Fuzz identities. DIY-only listings, modulation, services, unresolved names, and category-inflating gain descriptions were deliberately excluded.
