## Univox / Unicord
**Status:** builder-confirmed / catalog-in-progress

Univox was a U.S. musical-instrument brand operated by Unicord. Historical catalog sources document multiple dirt-oriented effects under the Univox name, including fuzz and overdrive models.

**Dirt products identified from historical/current reference sources:**
- Super-Fuzz U-1095
- Square Wave Fuzz
- Uni-Drive UD-50

The Univox identity should remain separate from Shin-Ei and Honey even where historical manufacturing relationships connect the products. Product names and underlying manufacturer relationships can be preserved as later research metadata.

**Sources:**

## Electra
**Status:** builder-confirmed / catalog-in-progress

Electra was a guitar/effects brand associated with St. Louis Music in the 1970s. Historical references document multiple dirt-oriented Electra effects, including standalone pedals and the Electra MPC modular effects system.

**Dirt products identified from historical sources:**
- Distortion 500D
- Fuzz Wah
- Pedal Drive 515AC
- MPC Dynamic Fuzz
- MPC Overdrive / Power Overdrive
- MPC Treble/Bass Boost

The first-pass catalog should preserve named Electra dirt products without splitting individual MPC module revisions. The Electra brand should remain distinct from later boutique pedals that reused or adapted Electra-derived circuits.

**Sources:**
- https://www.vintageguitar.com/wp-content/uploads/VGPRICEGUIDE2016_Sample.pdf
- https://www.electraguitar.com/pages/history
- https://pisotones.com/Electra/Electra.htm

## Honey Co. Ltd.
**Status:** builder-confirmed / candidate catalog-in-progress

Honey Co. Ltd. was an important late-1960s Japanese effects builder associated with the early Super-Fuzz family. First-party Shin-Ei historical material identifies the Honey FY-6 Baby Crying effect as an original 1967 release, and historical sources describe the larger Psychedelic Machine as a Honey multi-effect containing the fuzz circuit.

**Dirt products identified:**
- FY-6 Baby Crying
- Psychedelic Machine

Keep Honey separate from later Shin-Ei and Univox-branded versions. The product lineage can be retained as research metadata without collapsing the builder identities.

**Sources:**
- https://www.shin-ei.com/
- https://jhspedals.info/blogs/news/pedals-i-wish-i-made
- https://www.effectrode.com/knowledge-base/history-of-vibrato/

## Research Notes

- JDM materially expands current boutique coverage with a large first-party dirt catalog.
- Univox adds another distinct U.S. brand identity covering multiple dirt products in the late-1960s/1970s period.
- Electra fills a 1970s effects branch that includes both standalone dirt pedals and modular Electra MPC dirt effects.
- Honey strengthens the earliest Japanese Super-Fuzz lineage and must remain a separate recognized builder identity from Shin-Ei and Univox.
