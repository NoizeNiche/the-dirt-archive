# Builder → Pedals Research Block 043

## Builder
**Amptweaker — James Brown / Jack Thompson**

## Verification basis
Amptweaker's official history identifies legendary amp designer **James Brown** as the founder and original designer/builder of the company's overdrive, fuzz, and distortion pedals. The same official history says **Jack Thompson bought the company from James Brown** and signs the page as President/Owner. Current Amptweaker product pages continue to identify James Brown as the designer of the core Tight-series pedals. The census therefore records the builder relationship as Amptweaker — James Brown / Jack Thompson rather than attributing every historical/current product to one person alone.

Primary manufacturer pages were used wherever available. For discontinued historical models whose original product pages are no longer exposed in the current catalog, contemporaneous manufacturer press releases reproduced by established guitar publications, manufacturer-originated manuals/videos, and authoritative historical pedal databases were used only to establish product identity and dirt category. Cosmetic editions and artist/limited editions are not split unless the manufacturer presents a materially distinct pedal identity.

## Product notes
- **Tight Drive:** current Amptweaker page explicitly categorizes it as Overdrive and describes its range from a nuanced amp push through heavy overdrive/distortion. It is retained under Overdrive, not duplicated into Distortion solely because the gain can reach distortion-like territory.
- **Tight Drive Pro:** contemporaneous Amptweaker press material identifies it as an overdrive and describes a high-gain mode alongside transparent cleaner boost/drive operation. It is retained under Overdrive.
- **TightBoost:** historical manufacturer-originated material describes it as the lower-gain, higher-volume version of the TightDrive, providing cleaner boost and boosted overdrive tones. It is retained under Overdrive rather than treating boost as a separate target category.
- **Tight Rock:** the Amptweaker manual explicitly says it can be used as a distortion pedal and, at lower gain/higher volume, to overdrive a lead channel. It is therefore retained in both Distortion and Overdrive.
- **Tight Rock Jr:** the manufacturer-announced Jr series identifies it as one of the smaller versions of Amptweaker's distortion pedals. It is retained under Distortion.
- **Bass TightRock Jr:** Amptweaker's own 2016 Bass Jr announcement identifies it as a bass version of the TightRock Jr and describes the series as ranging from mild dirt to high-gain saturation. It is retained under Distortion.
- **Big Rock Pro / Big Rock Pro II:** Amptweaker describes these as high-gain/"big gain" rock pedals and places them in the Pro/Rock product family. The manufacturer documentation describes the internal drive as distortion-oriented, so they are retained under Distortion. The earlier Big Rock Pro identity is kept separate from the later Pro II rather than collapsing the two.
- **Tight Metal / Tight Metal Jr:** Amptweaker's current Tight Metal page explicitly says it uses the same distortion circuitry as the Tight Metal Pro II. The historical Jr announcement identifies TightMetal Jr as part of the Jr Distortion series. They are retained under Distortion.
- **Bass TightMetal Jr:** Amptweaker's official Bass Jr announcement identifies it as the bass counterpart to the TightMetal Jr and describes the trio as distortion/drive pedals. It is retained under Distortion.
- **Tight Metal Pro / Tight Metal Pro II:** Amptweaker's Pro-series material describes these as high-gain metal distortion/preamp pedals. The later Pro II is kept as a distinct product identity because the manufacturer presents it as a revised product with additional processing/features.
- **Fat Metal / Fat Metal Pro / Fat Metal Pro II:** Amptweaker material describes these as low-end-focused high-gain distortion pedals for black metal, doom, and extended-range players. The current Fat Metal Pro II page explicitly identifies low-end distortion. They are retained under Distortion.
- **Fat Rock:** contemporaneous Amptweaker press material identifies it as a warmer variation of the TightRock distortion pedal. It is retained under Distortion.
- **TightFuzz:** manufacturer-originated material explicitly identifies it as a fuzz pedal, with Germanium/Silicon switching and an auto-biasing Fuzz control. It is retained under Fuzz.
- **Bass TightFuzz:** Amptweaker's manufacturer press release and manual identify it as a bass-tweaked version of TightFuzz and explicitly describe its fuzz circuit. It is retained under Fuzz.
- **TightFuzz Pro:** Amptweaker's own 2015 announcement calls it a Pro fuzz pedal based on TightFuzz. It is retained under Fuzz.
- **TightFuzz Jr:** historical manufacturer-originated product material identifies the Jr as a compact fuzz version with Germanium/Silicon selection, 60s/70s/Now voicing, and auto bias. It is retained under Fuzz.
- **Bass TightFuzz Jr:** Amptweaker directly identified the Bass TightFuzz Jr in company communications and described its bass-oriented dry-low implementation and fuzz controls. It is retained under Fuzz.
- **BluesFuzz / BluesFuzz Limited:** Amptweaker's historical product material identifies the BluesFuzz family as a lower-gain germanium fuzz design. The limited 49-piece BluesFuzz is treated as the same core product family rather than a separate census identity.
- **BluesFuzz Jr:** product documentation identifies it as a fuzz pedal based on the BluesFuzz Limited, using a low-gain germanium transistor, boost, mid EQ, and automatic bias. It is retained under Fuzz.
- **Bass BluesFuzz:** contemporary product discussion and manufacturer-originated material identify the bass version as a lower-gain germanium BluesFuzz variant with mid EQ. It is retained under Fuzz.
- **Bass BluesFuzz Jr:** Amptweaker's own communication identifies this as the bass Jr version using lower-output germanium transistors and a mid EQ. It is retained under Fuzz.
- **Misery Index TightMetal Jr:** this was a limited artist/artist-collaboration edition of the TightMetal Jr. Because the manufacturer describes it as the TightMetal Jr platform with custom graphics rather than a materially distinct dirt circuit, it is retained only as a product note within the TightMetal Jr identity and is not treated as a separate census pedal. The name is included above only to preserve the historical lead; the categorized relationship remains TightMetal Jr → Distortion.

## Explicit exclusions / restraint
- **Depth Finder:** current Amptweaker catalog product, but it is a tone-shaping/depth utility rather than one of the three target dirt categories.
- **PressuRizer:** excluded from the core dirt census because it is a utility/preamp/tone-shaping product rather than a clearly manufacturer-classified OD, distortion, or fuzz pedal.
- **SwirlPool Tremolo Vibe:** excluded as modulation.
- **Limited artist/cosmetic editions:** not split when the manufacturer presents them as the same underlying dirt product, including the Misery Index TightMetal Jr and the BluesFuzz Limited relationship to BluesFuzz.
- **Clean boost functions:** not counted as a fourth category. A pedal is retained under Overdrive only when Amptweaker or authoritative manufacturer-originated material identifies an overdrive function, not merely because the pedal can push another device.
- **Amp/preamp operation:** does not create an additional pedal identity. The target census is the physical dirt pedal/product identity.
