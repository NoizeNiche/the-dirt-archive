# Builder → Pedals Research Block 106

## Builder
**Dr. Scientist — Ryan Clarke / Tanya Clarke / Neil Graham**

## Verification basis
Dr. Scientist's official site identifies the company as a boutique pedal builder that began in 2005 and describes its pedals as hand-made in British Columbia, Canada. The current catalog directly identifies The Elements as an overdrive/distortion, Dungeon Crawler as a distortion, and Frazz Dazzler as a fuzz. Current official product pages were used as the primary source. Older multi-effects material was reviewed for restraint, but multi-effect products were not promoted into the core dirt census when dirt was only one component of a broader effect system.

The archive follows Dr. Scientist's named product identity rather than treating every gain setting as a separate category. A product is placed in more than one target category only when Dr. Scientist explicitly gives it those identities or distinct dirt functions.

## Product notes
- **The Elements:** Dr. Scientist explicitly calls The Elements a dual-channel overdrive/distortion with a wide gain range. It is therefore retained in both Overdrive and Distortion. Its three-band EQ, clipping options, and clean/equalizer role remain part of the same named pedal and are not counted separately.
- **Dungeon Crawler:** Dr. Scientist explicitly identifies Dungeon Crawler as a MOSFET-based distortion designed for extreme metal tones. It remains Distortion only even though it includes a boost stomp and broad gain control.
- **Frazz Dazzler:** Dr. Scientist explicitly identifies Frazz Dazzler as a dual-channel aggressive fuzz. The pedal's voltage-starve control can create glitchy and sub-octave textures, but these remain behavior inside the named fuzz identity rather than separate dirt categories.

## Explicit exclusions / restraint
- **BitQuest:** excluded because Dr. Scientist presents it primarily as an eight-effect digital multi-effects pedal. It includes a built-in digital fuzz mode, but fuzz is only one selectable part of a much broader multi-effect platform.
- **BitQuest Eurorack Module:** excluded because it is the modular-synth version of the BitQuest multi-effects concept, not a standalone dirt pedal.
- **BoostBot:** excluded because Dr. Scientist presents it as a clean boost and display/utility pedal rather than Overdrive, Distortion, or Fuzz.
- **Sunny Day Delay, Dusk, Phreak, Spectrum Analyzer, and other current effects:** excluded because their primary identities are delay, filter, ring modulation, analysis, or other non-target effects.
- **Frazz Dazzler gain range:** lower-gain operation and clipping changes do not create a separate Overdrive or Distortion identity because Dr. Scientist names the product as a fuzz.
- **The Elements gain range:** despite being capable of very clean and very high-gain sounds, both Overdrive and Distortion are explicitly part of the product identity, so both categories are retained.
- **Version/reissue handling:** normal revisions of the same named product are consolidated unless Dr. Scientist presents them as a distinct product identity.
