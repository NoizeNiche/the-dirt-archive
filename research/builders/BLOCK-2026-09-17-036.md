# Builder → Pedals Research Block 036

## Builder
**Dr. Scientist Sounds**

## Scope
Builder → Pedals only. This block records Dr. Scientist's documented overdrive, distortion, and fuzz identities. Non-dirt effects are excluded.

## Builder identity
Dr. Scientist Sounds is a Canadian pedal builder whose official site describes its pedals as designed and handmade in British Columbia, Canada. The current official catalog includes The Elements, Frazz Dazzler, and Dungeon Crawler among its dirt-focused products.

## Current dirt pedals

### Overdrive / distortion
- **The Elements** — official page classifies it as a dual-channel overdrive/distortion with a wide gain range and active three-band EQ. It has selectable low- and high-gain distortion channels and switchable clipping modes.

### Distortion
- **The Elements** — dual-channel overdrive/distortion; retained under both relevant categories because the manufacturer explicitly uses both classifications.
- **Dungeon Crawler** — official page identifies it as a MOSFET-based distortion designed to take a clean amplifier from high gain into extreme metal tones. It uses three high-gain MOSFET stages, six gyrator EQ stages, a silicon-rectifier Grind control, and a noise gate.

### Fuzz
- **Frazz Dazzler** — official page identifies it as a dual-channel aggressive fuzz with a wide gain range, three-band EQ, voltage-starve system, and glitch/sub-octave behavior at low headroom settings.
- **BitQuest** — official page identifies a built-in digital fuzz mode alongside its other digital effects. It is retained as a dirt-bearing multi-effect identity, but it is not treated as a conventional standalone fuzz pedal.

## Verification notes
1. Dr. Scientist's official homepage currently highlights The Elements as an equalizer/overdrive/high-gain distortion, Frazz Dazzler as an aggressive dual-channel fuzz, and Dungeon Crawler as an extreme-metal distortion product.
2. The Elements official product page explicitly uses the term "dual-channel overdrive/distortion" and documents low/high gain distortion channels and selectable clipping.
3. Dungeon Crawler's official page explicitly identifies the circuit as MOSFET-based distortion and describes its extreme-metal purpose.
4. Frazz Dazzler's official page explicitly identifies it as a dual-channel fuzz and documents its voltage-starve system and high-gain channels.
5. BitQuest is included only as a dirt-bearing multi-effect because its official documentation explicitly provides a digital fuzz mode. It is not promoted into the standalone fuzz category as though it were a dedicated fuzz circuit.
6. BoostBot is excluded because the builder classifies it as a clean boost. Other Dr. Scientist products such as BitQuest Eurorack Module, Phreak, Sunny Day Delay, Dusk, and Spectrum Analyzer are excluded because they are not overdrive, distortion, or fuzz products.
7. No unsupported historical pedal names were promoted into the census in this block. The accessible official catalog was sufficient to document the qualifying dirt products without relying on uncertain third-party legacy lists.
