# Block 089 — Danelectro

## Builder
**Danelectro**

## Verification basis
Primary and authoritative sources reviewed:
- Danelectro official pedal pages and current pedal catalog
- Danelectro official history material
- Danelectro official product pages for Nichols 1966, The Breakdown, Eisenhower Fuzz, 3699 fUZZ, and Roebuck
- authoritative Effects Database catalog coverage for Danelectro Cool Cat, FAB, mini, Paisley, Wasabi, Billionaire, and other stompbox lines
- historical Danelectro catalogs / dealer catalog material for FAB and earlier stompbox products
- current and historical manual archives for Danelectro dirt products
- active-repository search for “Danelectro” before addition

The active repository contained no prior Danelectro match before this block was added. Danelectro is treated as the builder/brand identity for Danelectro-branded standalone dirt pedals. The census does not turn modeled effects, amplifier products, or non-dirt accessories into separate identities.

The reviewed material supports a broad Danelectro Overdrive, Distortion, and Fuzz catalog spanning current vintage-series products and several historical stompbox families.

Verification notes:
- **The Breakdown** is presented by Danelectro as a vintage-style drive/boost product. The company describes the Break Up control as progressively increasing drive into the amplifier; authoritative catalog coverage places it in Overdrive.
- **Daddy O**, **Cool Cat Drive**, **Cool Cat Transparent Overdrive**, **FAB Overdrive**, **Pastrami Overdrive**, **Blue Paisley PureDrive**, **Wasabi Overdrive**, **Cash Cow Overdrive**, and **Pride of Texas Overdrive** are documented as standalone Danelectro overdrive products in authoritative catalog coverage.

## Fuzz
Current / manufacturer-supported identities:
- Eisenhower Fuzz
- 3699 fUZZ
- Nichols 1966

Historical / catalog-supported identities:
- Cool Cat Fuzz
- FAB Fuzz
- French Toast

Verification notes:
- **Eisenhower Fuzz** is explicitly presented by Danelectro as an octave fuzz pedal.
- **3699 fUZZ** is explicitly presented by Danelectro as the current revival of Steve Ridinger's FOXX Tone Machine-era fuzz design, with octave and EQ changes.
- **Nichols 1966** is explicitly described by Danelectro as a fuzz/distortion hybrid. It therefore remains in both Fuzz and Distortion.
- **Cool Cat Fuzz** is documented as a Danelectro fuzz pedal; its V2 revision is treated as the same product identity rather than a separate design.
- **FAB Fuzz** is documented in Danelectro's FAB catalog and historical dealer material.
- **French Toast** is documented by authoritative catalog coverage as an octave-fuzz/octave-distortion product. The archive records it under Fuzz and Distortion rather than inventing additional identities for the octave function.

## Category-overlap decisions
- **Nichols 1966**: Distortion + Fuzz. Danelectro explicitly describes it as a fuzz/distortion hybrid. Published material also demonstrates smooth overdrive behavior, but the builder's own product description does not make Overdrive the named product category, so it is not promoted into the primary Overdrive set.
- **The Breakdown**: Overdrive only. Its six-position Break Up control reaches aggressive drive, but Danelectro does not name it a separate Distortion product.
- **Roebuck**: Distortion only. Secondary descriptions may discuss overdrive-style use, but Danelectro's own product identity is Distortion.
- **French Toast**: Fuzz + Distortion. It is an octave-fuzz/octave-distortion design; octave is a secondary function and does not create a separate product identity.
- **Eisenhower Fuzz**: Fuzz only. The octave feature is secondary to the named Fuzz identity.
- **Cool Cat Drive V2 / Transparent Overdrive V2**: consolidated with their respective underlying Cool Cat Drive / Transparent Overdrive identities rather than creating duplicate production identities.

## Historical / variant handling
- **Cool Cat Drive V2** remains the same product family as Cool Cat Drive for the initial census. **Cool Cat Transparent Overdrive V2** is likewise consolidated with the original Transparent Overdrive.
- **Cool Cat Fuzz V2** is retained as a revision of the Cool Cat Fuzz identity rather than a separate design.
- FAB product numbers such as D-1, D-2, and D-7 are supporting identifiers, not separate products.
- The historical mini “food” pedals remain separate named product identities because they were individually marketed stompboxes.
- Current vintage-series revivals such as **3699 fUZZ**, **Eisenhower Fuzz**, **The Breakdown**, and **Roebuck** remain distinct named products even when their inspiration traces to earlier designs.

## Unpromoted / cautious leads
- Danelectro's historical product families include additional dirt-adjacent mini and specialty products whose builder-level category wording is inconsistent across surviving catalogs. Where the available primary material did not clearly establish a target category, the product was not promoted beyond the supported category.
- **Nichols 1966** is intentionally treated as a category-boundary product rather than forcing it into a single historical taxonomy because Danelectro itself describes it as a fuzz/distortion hybrid.

## Exclusions
- Back Talk, Dan-Echo, Spring King, tremolo, chorus, flanger, vibe, wah, EQ, compressor, tuning, and other non-dirt products are outside target scope.
- Honeytone Mini Amp and other amplifier products are excluded.
- Billionaire boost-only products are excluded, except dirt products explicitly categorized as Overdrive such as Cash Cow and Pride of Texas.
- Modeled or multi-effect products are not mined into separate dirt identities.
- Cloning, schematics, components, internal construction, and reverse-engineering material are outside scope.
