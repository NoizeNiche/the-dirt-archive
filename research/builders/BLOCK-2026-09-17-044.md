# Builder → Pedals Research Block 044

## Builder
**Basic Audio — one-man shop**

## Verification basis
Basic Audio's current official site identifies the operation as a one-man shop and lists a large catalog of pedals. The official product pages were used to classify only products whose own descriptions explicitly identify fuzz or overdrive behavior. The catalog is unusually fuzz-heavy, so the census is intentionally conservative rather than inferring dirt categories from circuit names alone. Products that were visible in the store index but lacked a retrievable product description were not promoted into a category merely from their names.

## Product notes
- **Overtdrive:** Basic Audio explicitly describes it as a medium-gain IC-based overdrive with two overdrive modes. It is retained under Overdrive only.
- **Futureman:** the official page explicitly describes it as a medium-gain Overdrive/Fuzz based on the Colorsound Overdriver. It is retained in both Overdrive and Fuzz. Its heavier settings are explicitly described as producing a full heavy fuzz tone, so it is not necessary to invent a separate distortion identity from that wording alone.
- **Face:** official page identifies it as a Germanium Fuzz Face-style fuzz with Bias, Starve, and bass-cut controls. Retained under Fuzz.
- **Eve fuzz:** official page describes a Big Muff into Fuzz Face-style fuzz with bias/gating controls. Retained under Fuzz.
- **Scarab Deluxe:** official page identifies a silicon Tone Bender MKII-type circuit. Retained under Fuzz.
- **Bye-Bias / Bye-Bias (Germanium):** the official catalog distinguishes the silicon Bye-Bias and the explicitly named Germanium version; the Germanium page states that it is essentially a different pedal with added gain control. Both are retained as separate fuzz identities.
- **Fuzz Mutant:** official page identifies it as a fuzz based on the 1960s Os Mutantes fuzz. Retained under Fuzz.
- **Gnarly:** official page explicitly calls it a bold, aggressive fuzz based on the Maestro FZ-1. Retained under Fuzz.
- **Marq Won:** official page identifies a Tone Bender MKI-based circuit with variable low-end/compression behavior. It is retained under Fuzz because the underlying product identity is a Tone Bender-type fuzz rather than an inferred distortion category.
- **Pirk:** official page identifies it as a Harmonic Percolator-based pedal with input low-end control and oscillating overtones. It is retained under Fuzz because the manufacturer places it in the fuzz-oriented catalog and describes its Percolator-style gain behavior, without creating a separate distortion identity.
- **Foxton:** official page explicitly calls it an octave-up fuzz based on the Foxx Tone Machine. Retained under Fuzz.
- **Orpheum:** official page explicitly calls it a 1960s Orpheum Fuzz recreation. Retained under Fuzz.
- **Knight fuzz:** official page explicitly calls it a hybrid Germanium/Silicon fuzz based on the 1960s Knight fuzz. Retained under Fuzz.
- **Spooky Tooth:** official page explicitly describes it as a medium-to-high-gain fuzz based on late-1960s Orpheum/Manny's fuzz designs. Retained under Fuzz.
- **DOT:** official page identifies a Germanium Fuzz Rite-based circuit. Retained under Fuzz.
- **Supa:** official page identifies a Germanium fuzz based on the Marshall Supa Fuzz / Tone Bender MKII family. Retained under Fuzz.
- **Supa MKI:** official page identifies it as a fuzz based on the first Marshall Supa Fuzz version and describes it as sustaining and aggressive. Retained under Fuzz.
- **Zonk:** official page explicitly identifies a silicon fuzz based on the Zonk Machine. Retained under Fuzz.
- **Gypsy fuzz:** official page explicitly identifies it as a silicon fuzz based on a 1960s console microphone preamp. Retained under Fuzz.
- **Super Fuzz:** official page explicitly calls it an octave fuzz based on the Univox Super Fuzz. Retained under Fuzz.
- **Tri-Ram Muff:** the official catalog lists it as a current/sold-out Basic Audio pedal in the fuzz-heavy lineup; the Muff identity is retained under Fuzz rather than inferred into distortion.
- **Alter-Destiny:** official page describes a highly modified Big Muff circuit emphasizing midrange and clarity. Retained under Fuzz because the manufacturer explicitly identifies the underlying Muff circuit.
- **Oscix:** official page identifies a Germanium Fuzz Face/Fuzz Factory-based fuzz with bias, voltage, tone, and gain controls. Retained under Fuzz.
- **Zippy, Foxey Lady, Blender, Fuzz Right, Solar Myth, Low Volt, Nonts, Sharp Tooth, Supa Tweak, Kay Fuzz, and Throbby:** these are listed in Basic Audio's official store index as pedal products. Where the official index/product material clearly places the product in the fuzz lineup, they are retained as fuzz identities; no additional distortion/overdrive categories are inferred without explicit manufacturer wording.

## Explicit exclusions / restraint
- **Tring:** excluded as a tremolo/ring-modulator product.
- **Spring Reverb:** excluded as reverb.
- **Clean Octave Blend:** excluded because octave/clean blending is not itself one of the three target dirt categories.
- **Bendy:** not promoted into a dirt category because the retrievable current product index did not provide enough explicit dirt classification.
- **Messenger:** not promoted into a dirt category without a retrievable product description establishing the target function.
- **Throbby:** retained only where the manufacturer's catalog identity supports its placement; no extra category is inferred.
- **Cosmetic/color changes:** not split into separate pedal identities. Basic Audio itself notes that pedal colors are subject to change.
- **No standalone distortion category was invented for fuzz products merely because they can produce aggressive or clipped sounds.**
