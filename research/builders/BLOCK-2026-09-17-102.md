# Builder → Pedals Research Block 102

## Builder
**Fairfield Circuitry — Fairfield Circuitry**

## Verification basis
Fairfield Circuitry's official site says the company has been making pedals since 2008 and that manufacturing is done in Hull, Québec. The current catalog and FAQ explicitly identify products such as Barbershop as overdrive, ~900 as fuzz, and 20% More as distortion. Fairfield's own FAQ and current product pages also document the relationship between the original Barbershop, the Millennium Edition, and Modèle B, plus the discontinued Four Eyes fuzz lineage behind ~900.

The archive uses Fairfield's own named product identity. A separate named pedal is retained when Fairfield sells/presents it as its own product, while ordinary revisions of the same product are consolidated. Gain-range descriptions are not automatically turned into extra dirt categories.

## Product notes
- **Barbershop:** Fairfield explicitly calls this the Barbershop Overdrive. The FAQ says the V1 and Millennium Edition share the same circuit topology, so those revisions are consolidated into one Barbershop identity.
- **Modèle B:** Fairfield explicitly describes Modèle B as the same Barbershop circuit in a smaller enclosure and markets it as an “Always On Barbershop.” It is retained as a separately named product identity, while its category remains Overdrive.
- **20% More:** Fairfield's current catalog explicitly labels 20% More “Slew Limiting Distortion.” The product description also refers to synthetic distortion artifacts and JFET clipping. It is retained under Distortion only.
- **~900:** Fairfield explicitly labels ~900 a “Four Knob Fuzz” and a ferocious fuzz pedal built from two cascading JFET gain stages. The product page says its lineage traces back to the discontinued Four Eyes Fuzz. It remains Fuzz only.
- **The Unpleasant Surprise:** Fairfield explicitly labels this an “Experimental Fuzz/Gate.” It is retained under Fuzz; the gate/experimental character is not treated as another dirt category.
- **Four Eyes:** Fairfield's current ~900 page explicitly identifies Four Eyes as the discontinued fuzz from which ~900 traces its lineage. It is retained as a historical Fuzz identity because Fairfield itself names it as a prior product.

## Explicit exclusions / restraint
- **Accountant:** excluded because Fairfield explicitly identifies it as a compressor. Makeup gain does not create a standalone Overdrive identity.
- **Meet Maude:** excluded because it is an analogue delay.
- **Randy's Revenge:** excluded because it is a ring modulator.
- **Shallow Water / Roger That:** excluded because their primary identities are modulation and FM modulation/demodulation.
- **Hors d'Oeuvre?:** excluded because Fairfield identifies it as an active feedback loop rather than one of the three core dirt categories.
- **Long Life:** excluded because Fairfield identifies it primarily as a parametric EQ/filter with gritty extras, not a standalone dirt pedal.
- **Board Member:** excluded because it is a CV-controlled voltage-sag utility; its ability to create unexpected fuzz sounds is not enough to establish it as a target dirt product.
- **Barbershop V1 vs. Millennium Edition:** consolidated because Fairfield states the circuit topology was not changed and describes the difference as a smaller enclosure plus control changes.
- **Gain-range inflation:** Barbershop/Modèle B are not expanded into Distortion merely because their drive can be increased, and ~900 is not expanded into Overdrive merely because its gain range is broad. Categories follow Fairfield's explicit product labels.
