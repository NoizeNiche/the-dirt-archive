# Block 061 — Skreddy Pedals — Marc Ahlfs

## Builder
**Skreddy Pedals — Marc Ahlfs**

## Verification basis
Primary manufacturer sources reviewed:
- Skreddy Pedals About page
- Skreddy Pedals current Overdrive category
- Skreddy Pedals current Fuzz category
- Skreddy Pedals current all-products index
- Skreddy Pedals Big Muff variants index
- Skreddy Pedals ToneBender variants index
- individual manufacturer product pages/manuals for selected overlapping or ambiguous dirt identities

The manufacturer identifies Skreddy Pedals as Marc Ahlfs, with Cynthia Ahlfs and occasional helpers, and says they have been designing and building guitar effects since 2004. The current manufacturer taxonomy lists 13 Overdrive products and 33 Fuzz products. The site does not maintain a separate current Distortion category, so Distortion entries below are included only where the manufacturer's own product/index/manual language explicitly identifies the pedal as distortion rather than merely describing high gain, clipping, or fuzz-like behavior.

The current manufacturer Overdrive category explicitly lists these 13 identities. The Skunk Drive manual further describes it as an overdrive and the product page calls it vintage cutting fuzz/overdrive. Serenity's manufacturer page explicitly describes its range from cleanish boost through overdrive to fuzz and identifies it as overdrive/distortion/fuzz. Hybrid Fuzz Driver is explicitly described by the manufacturer as boost, overdrive, and light fuzz. Lunar Module Mini Deluxe is included because the current manufacturer Overdrive category lists it there even though its product description emphasizes silicon fuzz.

Verification notes:
- Serenity Fuzzdrive: manufacturer explicitly calls it Class A overdrive / distortion / fuzz.
- ROVER Fuzz: manufacturer describes the pedal as capable of meaty distortion and hot overdrive as well as fuzz.
- Top Fuel: manufacturer index calls it distortion with hot mids; the official manual describes its sustain control as controlling the intensity of the distortion.
- Supa Tone: manufacturer index explicitly describes it as grinding distortion.
- Cognitive Dissonance MkIV: manufacturer index explicitly describes sustain/distortion.
- Perestroika: manufacturer index explicitly describes early-90s Russian muffy distortion.
- 1971: manufacturer index explicitly describes vintage amp-like lower-gain muffy distortion.
- Pink Flesh: manufacturer index explicitly describes smooth, intense, sustaining distortion.

Conservative exclusions from Distortion:
- ZERO is not promoted as Distortion solely from its manual's references to distortion character. Its current manufacturer product identity is Fuzz, and the manual's primary description is raw/aggressive/sustaining fuzz.
- Rust Rod, Mayo MkIII, Mayonaise MkIII, BC109 Fuzz, BC239 Fuzz, etc. remain Fuzz unless the manufacturer explicitly presents the identity as a distortion product rather than describing distortion behavior within a fuzz circuit.

## Category-overlap decisions
- **Serenity Fuzzdrive**: Overdrive + Distortion + Fuzz. Manufacturer explicitly supports all three.
- **ROVER Fuzz**: Fuzz + Distortion. The manufacturer also describes hot overdrive capability in the product text, but the current product identity is Fuzz and the current Overdrive category does not list ROVER, so it is not promoted into the archive's Overdrive category.
- **Skunk Drive**: Overdrive + Fuzz. The current manufacturer category places it in Overdrive and Fuzz, while the manual says it is definitely in the overdrive camp.
- **Cosmos**: Overdrive + Fuzz. The manufacturer places it in both categories and calls it a Fuzz-Tone sound in an overdrive.
- **Klipper**: Overdrive + Fuzz. The current manufacturer categories place it in both.
- **Lunar Module Mini Deluxe**: Overdrive + Fuzz. Both current manufacturer categories include it.
- **Hybrid Fuzz Driver**: Overdrive + Fuzz. The manufacturer explicitly calls it boost, overdrive, and light fuzz.
- **Supa Tone** and **Cognitive Dissonance MkIV**: Fuzz + Distortion because the manufacturer explicitly labels the products as fuzz-family products while also describing them as distortion.
- **Top Fuel**, **Perestroika**, **1971**, **Pink Flesh**: Fuzz + Distortion because the manufacturer lists them in the Big Muff/Fuzz family and explicitly describes their sounds as distortion.

## Exclusions
- LZ-129: boost-only/dirty boost, not promoted into the target dirt taxonomy.
- Dynamic Mids Enhancer: preamp/clean-gain utility, not promoted as Overdrive/Distortion/Fuzz.
- Direct In: analog amp emulator, not promoted as a dirt pedal.
- Dual Loop: switching utility.
- Echo, Echo Mini, Echo Infinity, New Echo Vintage: delay products.
- Little Miss Sunshine, Swirl: modulation products.
- Galaxy Cluster: multi-effect/custom platform, not split into duplicate dirt identities here.
- Non-dirt utility products are excluded from the builder→dirt census.

## Verification notes
- Builder identity is directly supported by Skreddy Pedals' About page.
- Current product taxonomy is directly supported by the manufacturer's Overdrive, Fuzz, and all-products pages.
- Distortion relationships were treated more conservatively because the current site does not expose a separate Distortion category.
- The archive uses product identity/category relationships rather than inferring a category from generic words such as "gain," "saturation," "crunch," or "clipping."
