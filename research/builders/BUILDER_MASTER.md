# The Dirt Archive — Builder Master Research Census

**Purpose:** Research-stage master list of guitar dirt-pedal builders and their associated dirt products before building the public library.

**Target span:** 1960s–current

**Current research focus:** Establish builder identity and collect their dirt-pedal names. Exterior photographs and concise pedal information come later. Detailed variants, colorways, enclosure changes, components, manufacturing relationships, and deeper histories are deferred to pedal-specific deep dives.

**Governing workflow:** Builder → Pedals → Photos → Information

**Important naming rule:** Different recognized builder/product pairings remain separate. For example, Dallas-Arbiter Fuzz Face and Jim Dunlop Fuzz Face are separate builder/product entries. Within the same builder/product identity, minor alternate names or cosmetic variants can be grouped under the most recognizable name for the first pass.

## Research Status Legend

- `candidate` = discovered builder, not yet sufficiently checked
- `builder-confirmed` = builder identity supported by usable sources
- `catalog-in-progress` = builder confirmed; dirt catalog being mapped
- `cataloged` = initial dirt catalog sufficiently mapped
- `needs-review` = identity/catalog issue remains

The official site identifies EarthQuaker as an Akron, Ohio builder. Sources: https://www.earthquakerdevices.com/devices and https://www.earthquakerdevices.com/about

### Wren and Cuff
The official current products page exposes numerous fuzz, distortion, and drive products, including multiple versions of Caprid, Eye See '78, Garbage Face, Pickle Pie, Russian-derived products, Your Face variants, Ace Octave Fuzz, and others. Source: https://www.wrenandcuff.com/products

## Initial Operating Rule

Do not build the public site from this file. This is research-stage material.

For each builder, the immediate job is:

1. Establish recognizable builder identity.
2. Find the dirt pedals they produced.
3. Prefer builders with multiple dirt pedals.
4. Record the names and source links.
5. Avoid deep historical rabbit holes.
6. Do not split the same builder's product into records merely for colorways, enclosure changes, components, or cosmetic variations.
7. Do not merge separate recognized builder/product identities merely because they share a product name.
8. Do not seek internal pedal photographs.

Only after the builder census is sufficiently mature should the public catalog architecture be built.
