# Builder → Pedals Research Block 039

## Builder
**Tech 21 NYC — B. Andrew Barta**

## Verification basis
Tech 21's official About page states that B. Andrew Barta launched Tech 21 in 1989 and introduced the SansAmp, and identifies him as the designer behind the company's products. Tech 21's official support/archive pages and individual product pages were used to establish dirt-pedal identities. This block is intentionally conservative: it records pedals where Tech 21's own material explicitly identifies overdrive, distortion, or fuzz behavior. Amp-emulator/SansAmp products that merely have a Drive control are not automatically counted as standalone dirt pedals.

## Product notes
- **Boost Overdrive:** Tech 21's official archive describes it as an overdrive pedal with a dedicated Sparkle control and independent post-boost.
- **Boost Distortion:** official product page identifies it as a distortion pedal and describes its driven range as distortion/dirt/grit/fuzz/overdrive.
- **Boost Fuzz:** official product page and manual explicitly identify the unit as a fuzz pedal based around classic germanium fuzz behavior.
- **Hot-Rod Plexi:** official page explicitly describes Drive as overdrive and the circuit as generating tube-amp distortion characteristics. It is therefore retained in both Overdrive and Distortion.
- **Double Drive:** official archive describes separate Class A and Class A/B power-amp distortion stages and cascading distortion. The Class A stage is also described in terms of overdrive-like behavior, so it is retained in both categories.
- **Double Drive 3X:** official archive describes Class A and Class A/B power-amp distortions, cascading operation, and a clean-boost range at minimum drive. It is retained under Distortion and Overdrive.
- **XXL Original / Guitar / Bass:** official Tech 21 material explicitly calls the XXL distortion, while also describing minimum-Warp operation as overdrive-like and extreme settings as capable of vintage fuzz tones. Those category overlaps are preserved rather than forced into one label.
- **Red Ripper:** official Tech 21 page calls it an all-analog distortion effect and explicitly describes its range from vintage fuzz through distortion and octaver-fuzz territory. It is retained in Distortion and Fuzz.
- **Richie Kotzen Signature OMG:** official Tech 21 archive calls it a signature overdrive pedal and describes its asymmetric distortion behavior. It is retained in Overdrive and Fuzz because Tech 21's later RK5 documentation explicitly identifies the OMG section's Fuzz switch as changing the overdrive into a fuzz-style tone.
- **Bass Boost Fuzz:** official Tech 21 archive identifies it as a Boost Series fuzz product. It is retained under Fuzz; no separate distortion category is inferred solely from generic dirt language.

## Explicit exclusions / restraint
- **SansAmp Classic and other SansAmp amp/preamp products:** excluded from the categorized dirt census when the official material primarily defines them as tube-amplifier emulators/preamp systems rather than standalone OD/distortion/fuzz pedals. A Drive control alone is not treated as sufficient classification.
- **Fly Rigs:** not counted as separate pedal identities when the dirt section is a derivative of an already documented Tech 21 pedal or when the unit is primarily a multi-effect/amp-emulation platform. Individual Fly Rig dirt sections may be used as corroborating evidence for underlying pedal classifications.
- **Character Plus products:** not promoted into the core census here despite containing explicit overdrive/fuzz sections, because they are multi-effect amp-character pedals and would substantially broaden the classification boundary. They remain useful corroborating material for Tech 21's dirt implementation.
- **Cosmetic/edition changes:** not split into separate base identities unless Tech 21 documents a materially distinct product identity.
- **Boost-only products:** excluded.
