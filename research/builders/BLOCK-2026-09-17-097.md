# Builder → Pedals Research Block 097

## Builder
**Fjord Fuzz — Daniel Thornhill**

## Verification basis
Fjord Fuzz's official site maintains a dedicated Fuzz section and currently lists multiple standalone fuzz products. The builder's own product pages and official Fjord Fuzz videos provide product-level descriptions; Guitar Pedal X and other authoritative pedal references were used only to corroborate historical/current identities where the official site no longer exposes an older product page prominently. This block is limited to physical pedals and the archive's three target dirt categories.

The builder is strongly fuzz-focused. The archive does not promote neighboring overdrive, preamp, distortion, or boost behavior into separate categories unless the product itself is explicitly identified that way. Normal revisions and enclosure/finish changes are consolidated into the underlying product identity.

## Product notes
- **Odin:** Fjord Fuzz's official product page explicitly identifies the core circuit as an octave fuzz and says the regular mode is a classic-style fuzz. It remains Fuzz only.
- **Midgard:** the official product page explicitly calls MIDGARD a warm fuzz pedal and describes its pickup-simulation/signal-translator section as an auxiliary feature. It remains Fuzz only; the translator/preamp functionality is not treated as a separate dirt identity.
- **Thor:** Fjord Fuzz's official 2025 video explicitly identifies THOR as a gated, bass-forward fuzz pedal. It remains Fuzz only.
- **Midnattsol:** the official product page explicitly describes three modes of fuzz plus vibe/filter-style expression. It remains Fuzz only.
- **Dovre:** Fjord Fuzz's official 2024 product video calls DOVRE a fuzz + reverb pedal and describes the fuzz as the primary dirt section. It is retained under Fuzz; the reverb is outside scope.
- **Hedda:** builder-level descriptions identify HEDDA as a vintage fuzz with high dynamic range and a chaos-oscillation switch. It remains Fuzz only.
- **Måne:** the official Fjord Fuzz video calls MÅNE a dual fuzz / dual filter pedal. It is retained under Fuzz only; the filter is not a target dirt category.
- **SOL:** Fjord Fuzz's published catalog materials identify SOL as a Fuzz & Vibe design. It is retained under Fuzz; the vibe function is outside scope.
- **Embla:** the official constructor description says EMBLA was designed as a fuzz that behaves like an overdrive. Because the manufacturer explicitly frames the design around both fuzz and overdrive behavior, it is retained in both Fuzz and Overdrive. The identity is historical/discontinued in the current range.
- **Bifrost:** Fjord Fuzz's official 2020 video describes a fuzz section that can clean up to preamp drive or clean signal inside a combined fuzz/delay pedal. It is retained under Fuzz only because the named dirt identity is fuzz; the delay is outside scope.
- **Gjallarhorn:** period builder-description material identifies it as a reworked FY-6 / Super-Fuzz style fuzz and notes that lower gain yields a less-saturated drive. The archive retains Fuzz only rather than inferring a second Overdrive identity from that gain range.
- **Berserk:** the product family is described as a combined take on classic Fuzz Face and Tone Bender approaches. V1/V2/V3 are consolidated as one Berserk identity; descriptive references to lower-gain grit do not create an additional target category.
- **Freia:** the builder's own 2022 video explicitly identifies FREIA as a fuzz created from the Uni-Vibe preamp and describes the range from clean preamp tones to thick gated fuzz. It is retained under Fuzz only.
- **Kvasir:** manufacturer-originated description reproduced by specialist references identifies Kvasir as a vintage-style transistor fuzz. It remains Fuzz only.
- **Mjølner:** historical Fjord Fuzz catalog coverage identifies Mjølner as a high-gain fuzz. It is retained under Fuzz.
- **Bjørn:** historical catalog coverage identifies Bjørn as a vintage dark gated fuzz. It is retained under Fuzz.
- **Bufo:** historical catalog coverage identifies Bufo as a croaky fuzz-drive design. The archive retains it under Fuzz because the documented product identity is fuzz-oriented and the drive wording describes behavior rather than a separately named overdrive product.
- **Fjord-Fuzz Super-Fuzz:** historical catalog coverage identifies this as a Super-Fuzz family pedal. It is retained under Fuzz.

## Explicit exclusions / restraint
- **Frei / Frei 2:** excluded because the product identity is Uni-Vibe/vibe modulation rather than a dirt pedal. The separate Freia is retained because Fjord Fuzz explicitly transformed that preamp into a fuzz product.
- **Loke:** excluded because it is a delay/tape-manipulation multi-effect. Its preamp drive capability does not create a standalone dirt identity.
- **FJÆR / Mime:** excluded because they are reverb products.
- **Njord / Frøy:** excluded because their primary identity is modulation/rotary/vibe rather than dirt.
- **Bifrost:** delay is not separately counted; only its explicit fuzz section contributes to the dirt census.
- **Version handling:** Odin II/III/IV, Berserk V1/V2/V3, and other normal revisions are consolidated unless the manufacturer presents a materially different product identity.
- **Gain-range inflation:** products described as reaching clean boost, overdrive, or distortion territory remain Fuzz-only unless the builder establishes that target function as a distinct product identity.
- **No standalone distortion identity:** reviewed material did not establish a sufficiently explicit Fjord Fuzz product whose primary product identity is Distortion.

## Status
**Verified research block.** Fjord Fuzz is a previously uncovered builder in the fresh census. The official catalog establishes a current core of fuzz products including Odin, Midgard, Thor, and Midnattsol, with additional fuzz identities documented through the builder's official videos and authoritative historical coverage. Embla is the clearest explicit Overdrive + Fuzz overlap. No standalone Distortion identity was established strongly enough to promote. Modulation, reverb, delay, and multi-effect functions were deliberately excluded.
