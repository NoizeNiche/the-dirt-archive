# Early Foundational Handoff 96

## Pass 48: 2007-2015 internet-era boutique expansion, DIY-to-brand transitions, and the rise of global micro-builders

Pass 48 extends the archive from the late-2000s boutique boom into the 2010-2015 second generation. The pass prioritizes builder-origin evidence, first-sale/batch dates, and physical production details that can separate early specimens from later versions.

### Newly documented or clarified

- **JHS Morning Glory V1**: JHS historical material places the first original Morning Glory production in summer 2009, with a plain powder-coated enclosure, rubber-stamped graphics and an early run of about 100 units.
- **JHS Colour Box V1**: JHS history dates the V1 debut to Winter NAMM 2014, preserving it as an early pre-expanded-family production state.
- **Origin Effects Cali76**: company history places the founding in 2012 and identifies the large-format Cali76 as the beginning of the product line; 2012 publication evidence independently records the pedal.
- **VFE Proto OD**: Peter Rutter identifies Proto OD as his first original design, sold for approximately $40-$60 on eBay before the formal VFE Pedals brand launch.
- **VFE brand transition**: Rutter places the VFE Pedals name around March 2010, separating earlier personal/eBay builds from branded retail production.
- **GFI System Bluescaster**: Indra Achmanto identifies the Bluescaster as his first pedal, an overdrive plus independent booster developed during the early professional-building period beginning in 2010.
- **KMA Fuzzly Bear**: KMA states that the business properly began in late 2013 with the Fuzzly Bear and that early units were built in a shared Berlin apartment.
- **KMA original specimen context**: the 2013 artwork and hand-built apartment production process are preserved as an early generation/specimen marker.
- **Tanabe.tv Zenkudo**: Toshihiko Tanabe states that he built the first Zenkudo for himself in 2007 and continued producing pedals personally by direct order, with natural abalone-topped enclosures giving each specimen a unique appearance.
- **Sonic Edge J&J Overdrive**: Premier Guitar's March 19, 2010 launch notice identifies the J&J as Sonic Edge's first pedal and inaugural product.
- **Dwarfcraft The Great Destroyer**: February and September 2008 public documentation establishes a strong contemporaneous circulation corridor for the oscillating fuzz/distortion pedal while leaving the exact factory launch date unresolved.
- **Electronic Audio Experiments Longsword**: John Snyder documents June 17, 2015 as the date orders opened for the first batch of the Longsword, EAE's first pedal.
- **EAE Longsword V1**: the first production batch is kept separate from later V2-V4 revisions and from later manufacturing-focused changes.
- **ThorpyFX Gunshot**: ThorpyFX dates its company launch to 2014 and the first commercial Gunshot release to April 2015.
- **Tone City builder origin**: Tone City dates its founding to the end of 2014 after years of pedal design and hand-building experience by J. Wong; product-level first-production dates remain open where primary evidence is insufficient.

### Key research findings

1. **The modern boutique ecosystem grows out of several overlapping routes.** JHS, VFE, GFI and KMA each show a different path from hobbyist or modification work into branded production.
2. **First-sale dates are increasingly recoverable.** JHS, EAE and ThorpyFX provide unusually clear first-production or first-order clocks, allowing the archive to distinguish prototypes, first batches and mature models.
3. **DIY and online distribution are historical evidence.** VFE's eBay phase and KMA's apartment production demonstrate that the marketplace and workshop context can be part of a pedal's identity.
4. **International micro-builders deserve equal treatment.** Indonesian GFI, Japanese Tanabe, German KMA, British ThorpyFX, and Chinese Tone City show the global spread of boutique production.
5. **Physical uniqueness matters.** JHS stamping, Tanabe's natural abalone faces, and KMA's original artwork/hand-built context provide non-electronic specimen clues.
6. **Later model sophistication should not overwrite first states.** Colour Box V1, Longsword V1 and early Morning Glory specimens need separate generation records from later versions.
7. **Some important dates should remain deliberately fuzzy.** Dwarfcraft's 2008 public circulation and GFI's early Bluescaster chronology are useful without inventing unsupported retail launch months.

### Evidence discipline

- Manufacturer and builder-authored histories remain preferred for product clocks.
- First-order, first-sale, and first-batch dates are distinct evidence classes and are labeled separately.
- Contemporary forum/blog evidence is retained as public-circulation or specimen evidence rather than universal factory chronology.
- eBay and direct-order history documents distribution channels, not automatically factory scale.
- Non-dirt products remain outside the central dirt count when they function only as builder chronology boundaries.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries, or cloning instructions are added.

### Current lineage position

The archive now connects the late-2000s micro-builder wave with the 2010-2015 global boutique expansion. JHS and VFE document DIY/modification-to-brand transitions; GFI and Tanabe strengthen the Asian independent network; KMA and ThorpyFX show new European hand-built brands; EAE marks the rise of a highly documented internet-era experimental builder; and Tone City represents the move from artisanal design experience toward scalable budget boutique manufacturing.
