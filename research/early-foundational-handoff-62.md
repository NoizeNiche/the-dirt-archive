# Early Foundational Handoff 62

## Pass 10: 1980s FX-family, regional BOSS, TS descendants, and rack-era RAT evidence

This pass continues the archive's move from early-1980s landmark dirt into the manufacturing and product-format transition that defined the mid-1980s through 1990s. Nine additional reconciliation records were added.

### Newly documented

- **DOD FX55B Supra Distortion**: anchored to DOD's 1986 catalog as a distinct FX-series distortion model.
- **DOD FX10 Bi-FET Preamp**: added to the original 1982-88 FX-series visual/lineage window.
- **DOD FX55 Distortion**: separated from the later FX55B and anchored to the 1984 catalog.
- **BOSS DS-1**: Japan production era and Taiwan transition kept as separate regional states, with the country field treated as specimen evidence.
- **Ibanez TS5**: Soundtank plastic-enclosure generation added as a later Tube Screamer retail state.
- **Ibanez TS7**: Tone-Lok generation added as a later Tube Screamer state with its Hot mode retained as an external product distinction.
- **ProCo R2DU**: rack-era dual-RAT unit documented with its important dating dispute preserved. The strongest commercial evidence located so far is an 1988 advertisement, while common reference dating remains 1984-88.
- **ROSS Distortion**: original Bud Ross / Keas lineage strengthened as a Kansas-built historical brand, while regional and enclosure differences remain specimen-level.

### Key research findings

1. **DOD's early FX catalog is a primary chronology anchor.** The 1984 and 1986 catalog records expose multiple dirt models before later graphic and ownership transitions, giving the archive a stronger backbone than undated marketplace listings.
2. **DOD's visual identity itself is a dating layer.** Specialist field-guide research divides the FX-series into an original 1982-88 design, a transition period, and a second 1989-98 design. This matters for identification because catalogs did not always update immediately when the hardware/graphics changed. cite-placeholder
3. **BOSS country changes should not be treated as a single date switch.** DS-1 research places the Japan-to-Taiwan move around 1989-90, while later circuit revisions supply additional generation markers. The archive should attach these observations to specimen evidence.
4. **Tube Screamer history is a sequence of retail families rather than one uninterrupted object.** TS9, TS10, TS5, and TS7 belong to visibly different enclosure/series eras, and their dates overlap adjacent Ibanez product generations.
5. **R2DU is a textbook uncertainty case.** Common secondary dating says 1984-88, but JHS's historical review argues that documentary evidence points to a commercial 1988 release. The archive will retain both hypotheses until a stronger dated ProCo document resolves the conflict.
6. **Rack-era dirt belongs in the catalog.** The R2DU shows that the history of distortion pedals includes rack products and remote switching systems, not just stompboxes.
7. **ROSS deserves continued specimen-led treatment.** The brand's Keas Electronics / Chanute, Kansas history is stronger than a simple “U.S. pedal” label, and later production states should remain separately documented.

### Evidence discipline

- **VERIFIED** is reserved for explicit catalog or manufacturer-level support.
- **RESEARCH** remains appropriate for specialist chronology, regional manufacturing transitions, and disputed dating.
- Country, enclosure, logo, hardware, and generation clues may be public when they identify a production state without exposing reconstruction-level circuit information.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot galleries, or cloning instructions are added.
