# 2010-2014 Micro-Boutique Dirt Audit 02

## Purpose

This batch extends the 2010-2014 boutique builder layer with documented small-run, cult, experimental, and regional builders. The research emphasis is chronology and builder identity first, then dirt-product enumeration. Product dates below distinguish indexed publication/appearance from confirmed first sale where the surviving evidence does not establish a sale date.

## Dwarfcraft Devices

Effects Database indexes 46 Dwarfcraft products and provides a product chronology that is unusually useful for the early catalog. The earliest dirt anchors in this pass are **The Great Destroyer** (published August 11, 2008), **HAX** and **Eau Claire Thunder** (both indexed July 30, 2009), and **Robot Devil** (December 6, 2009). The timeline also records **The Internet** and **The Ronald Raygun: We Will All Be Dirt** in 2010, followed by **Baby Thundaa / Baby Thunder** and **The Secret Destroyer** in 2011.

The archive treats Baby Thundaa as a separate production/model state rather than merely an enclosure-size footnote. The indexed record identifies it as a smaller version of Eau Claire Thunder and also documents a special nine-unit edition connected to The Ronald Raygun release. The Secret Destroyer is retained as a separate limited retail product because it was a FuzzHugger-exclusive deluxe Great Destroyer variant.

Sources: Effects Database Dwarfcraft category and individual product records.

## Smallsound/Bigsound

Brian Hamilton's Philadelphia operation is a foundational modern small-batch dirt builder. Effects Database currently indexes 14 products. The **Team_Awesome Fuzzmachine** is indexed October 21, 2008, establishing a strong early product anchor. **Year 2525** follows in 2010, **Fuck Overdrive** in February 2011, **Poly-Grace** in August 2011, and **Hawk** in June 2012.

The archive keeps non-dirt products such as No Memory outside the central dirt count while preserving them as chronology boundaries when useful. Contemporary coverage also explicitly described the early company as focused on dirt pedals, which supports treating the fuzz/overdrive layer as the core historical story.

Sources: Effects Database Smallsound/Bigsound category and individual product pages; contemporary Brian Hamilton interview coverage.

## Fuzzrocious Pedals

Ryan and Shannon Ratajski's history illustrates the DIY-to-boutique transition. Ryan describes beginning around 2008 with a Big Muff kit taught by his father, then building roughly 50 pedals for friends before shifting into adaptations and original designs. Effects Database indexes 43 products.

The **Grey Stache** is the key early dirt anchor. By 2010 it was already circulating as an identifiable Fuzzrocious production pedal. **Oh See Demon** is indexed July 4, 2011 and **Terrordactyl** appears in the September 2011 Effects Database additions. The catalog also includes Green Stache, Mail Order Muff, Ram The Manparts and Rat King. These remain separate retail records even where family names or modification options connect them.

The archive does not backdate all later Fuzzrocious products to the 2008 origin point. Builder origin and individual product chronology stay separate.

Sources: Effects Database Fuzzrocious category and interview; Effects Database Oh See Demon; Effects Database September 2011 weekly update; contemporary TalkBass coverage of Grey Stache.

## Crazy Tube Circuits

Crazy Tube Circuits is an Athens, Greece builder run by Christos Ntaifotis. Effects Database indexes 54 products across the lifetime catalog. For this historical corridor, **Black Magic** is the clearest early dirt anchor, published July 15, 2009 as a high-gain distortion/fuzz. **Pin Up Fuzz** follows in November 2011. The broader indexed catalog contains Motherload, Doctor Boogey, Heatseeker and later gain models.

Because the lifetime category is broad and exact first-sale dates are uneven, this batch promotes the builder to a canonical baseline but does not claim lifetime catalog completeness.

Sources: Effects Database Crazy Tube Circuits category; Black Magic; Pin Up Fuzz.

## FuzzHugger(fx)

Effects Database indexes 31 FuzzHugger products, making this an important small-builder universe rather than a single-pedal footnote. The 2010 corridor is particularly well documented. **Phantom Octave** has a January 2010 index and an associated January press release; **Phantom Ring** is indexed in April 2010; **Phantom Arcade** in July 2010; and **AB-Synth Dream** in September 2010.

These should be retained as distinct product families because the later products add new modes and signal paths rather than simply being cosmetic revisions. The archive records them as fuzz or fuzz-hybrid products and does not collapse octave/ring-mod hybrids into generic modulation entries.

Sources: Effects Database FuzzHugger category and product pages.

## Monsterpiece Fuzz

Monsterpiece is a particularly useful micro-builder example because the historical record explicitly describes one-person, limited-basis handmade production. **Fuzz MkIII** is indexed October 27, 2009. **Puma Fuzz** and **Ge Fuzz** are both indexed May 16, 2011, while **Fuzz Mk1.5** is indexed November 20, 2012.

The archive treats these as separate product states. The Mk1.5 is especially useful as a historical marker because its product description explicitly identifies the Sola Sound Tone Bender Mk1.5 family as the reference lineage while retaining the Monsterpiece implementation as its own builder product.

Sources: Effects Database Monsterpiece Fuzz MkIII, Puma Fuzz, Ge Fuzz, and Mk1.5.

## Anarchy Audio

Anarchy Audio provides the Australian regional bridge in this pass. The company states it was established in **2011** and identifies Perth, Western Australia as its base. Its current site states that pedals are designed, hand wired, and assembled in Western Australia. A 2016 builder profile likewise describes a workload split between production pedals and custom builds.

The present pass establishes Anarchy Audio as a canonical builder target but deliberately stops short of assigning exact 2011 launch dates to individual dirt models without stronger period catalog evidence. This keeps the chronology honest while preserving the builder for subsequent catalog completion.

Sources: Anarchy Audio official site; Australian Guitar Review builder profile.

## Dr. Scientist

Dr. Scientist is an important Canada-based builder whose commercial history begins earlier than the 2010-2014 corridor. Ryan Clarke's own timeline states that he began building effects in 2003, met Tanya in 2005, worked on an initial seven-pedal lineup through 2005-2006, and officially opened for business in **August 2006**. He also states that every Dr. Scientist pedal was built, tested and packed by him during the documented small-company period.

The **Elements** is retained as the key overdrive/distortion anchor. The modern official page describes it as a dual-channel overdrive/distortion, while the historical interview places the company's commercial start in 2006. This is a builder whose 2010-2014 presence is best understood as a continuation and scaling phase rather than a founding event.

Sources: Effects Database Dr. Scientist interview; Dr. Scientist official Elements page.

## Heavy Electronics

Heavy Electronics was founded in **July 2007** after prototype development. Its early products were made in Minneapolis with handmade construction and outsourced painting/silkscreening. **Red Eyes** is an early gritty overdrive documented in 2008 trade coverage, while **Radio Havana** became the builder's leading seller according to Sayer Payne's 2012 interview. **El Oso Bass Distortion** is explicitly dated to 2011 in the builder timeline.

The archive therefore treats Red Eyes, Radio Havana and El Oso as separate dirt products while keeping non-dirt products such as Rodeo Loop outside the dirt count.

Sources: Effects Database Heavy Electronics timeline and interview; Premier Guitar 2008 coverage.

## Black Arts Toneworks

The Pharaoh appears in Black Arts Toneworks archival material from **2010**, with January 11, 2011 production notes documenting an active batch and expansion into dealer distribution. The product description shows the Pharaoh spanning cleanish boost, light overdrive, and saturated fuzz, so it belongs in the central dirt layer rather than being treated as a pure fuzz-only edge case.

The current batch does not claim the complete BAT lifetime catalog. It establishes Pharaoh chronology and preserves subsequent catalog completion as a separate task.

Sources: Black Arts Toneworks 2010 archive; Black Arts Toneworks January 11, 2011 production post; official Pharaoh page.

## Red Panda and digital scope boundary

Red Panda is retained in the broader builder census because its company history begins in **2009**, but the documented early catalog is centered on DSP effects such as the ToneCore Bitcrusher, Subdivision, and later Particle. Red Panda's own support history separates 2009-2011 ToneCore modules from the 2011-2016 Particle-era products. This builder is therefore useful as a scope-boundary case: the archive should record experimental digital sound-design history without automatically inflating the analog OD/distortion/fuzz catalog.

Sources: Red Panda company page and historical technical support timeline.

## British Pedal Company / JMI transition

British Pedal Company is not folded into historical JMI. The company states that its team originally developed, designed and recreated pedals for JMI in the early 2000s, cut ties with JMI in **2011**, and launched the British Pedal Company range in **2014**. That makes BPC a distinct revival/manufacturer lineage with a documented predecessor relationship.

The archive should therefore keep JMI, the BPC builder team, and the 2014 BPC retail range as separate nodes linked by evidence rather than treating BPC as simply a renamed JMI.

Source: British Pedal Company company history.

## Archive interpretation

This corridor shows the modern boutique scene becoming a dense ecosystem rather than a handful of famous brands. The research value lies in preserving small-run builders, limited editions, product-family branches, and builder-to-product chronology while refusing to infer a complete catalog from a current storefront.

The strongest next work is catalog completion for the newly promoted builders, followed by OEM/relationship reconciliation. Individual specimen archaeology should come only after each builder's dirt universe is substantially mapped.
