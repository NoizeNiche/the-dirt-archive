# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 58: Rosac / Sierra / Sangil U.S. California Continuity

## Scope
Builder-first reconciliation of the Mosrite-adjacent California effects lineage around Ed Sanner, separating Sierra Electronics, Rosac Electronics, and Sangil rather than treating them as one uninterrupted company.

## Sierra -> Rosac

Effects Database historical notes state that Ed Sanner was developing the Sierra Electronics Nu-Fuzz after Mosrite's collapse and that the company changed its name to Rosac because another company already used the Sierra Electronics name. Tone Machines Blog's specimen research identifies Sierra-labeled Sprague modules in early Rosac-era Nu-Fuzz, Nu-Wa Fuzz, and Distortion Blender units and documents November 1969 advertising for those products. This supports a transitional Sierra/Rosac manufacturing corridor, but the exact legal/name-change date should remain a documentary question rather than an assumed single-day event.

## Rosac dirt catalog

- Nu-Fuzz: fuzz product with tone control, directly tied to Ed Sanner's post-Mosrite work. Effects Database places the Nu-Fuzz in the Rosac catalog and treats Sierra Nu-Fuzz as its immediate predecessor state.
- Nu-Wa: wah product, retained as a Rosac catalog boundary because it belongs to the same effects development program.
- Nu-Wa-Fuzz: combined fuzz-wah product. Effects Database identifies it as part of the Rosac catalog and ties its design to Ed Sanner.
- Nu-Fuzz Distortion Blender: fuzz/clean blend product in the Rosac catalog; model-level evidence connects it to the Nu-Fuzz family.
- Tremolo Unit: non-dirt Rosac effect retained as a catalog-boundary record, not counted toward central dirt totals.

Rosac historical notes also describe the company as a compact Bakersfield operation where cabinet work, electronics, assembly, shipping, repair, and inventory were carried out in one facility. The surviving secondary record places the company around the late 1960s through the 1970s, with closure reported around 1978-79, but the exact dirt-production endpoint remains unresolved.

## Sangil

Around 1970 Ed Sanner and Ken Gilstrap left Rosac and formed Sangil. Surviving historical accounts identify Sanner as the designer/electronics builder and Gilstrap as the sales-side manager. Sangil therefore becomes a distinct builder/company node rather than merely another Rosac brand state.

The Iron Butterfly Fuzz Wah is the key surviving Sangil-era dirt record. Effects Database and auction-derived provenance describe a short-lived Sangil effort around 1970, with only a handful of Iron Butterfly units made and the proposed endorsement/licensing arrangement never becoming a normal production run. Treat this as a limited/failed-commercialization product state, not as evidence of a broad Sangil catalog.

## Evidence boundaries

- Sierra/Rosac relationship: evidence-qualified.
- Rosac physical operation in Bakersfield: evidence-qualified through historical interview-based secondary material.
- Rosac exact corporate endpoint: unresolved.
- Sangil formation around 1970: evidence-qualified.
- Iron Butterfly Fuzz Wah as a Sangil-era limited product: evidence-qualified but with small surviving quantity and incomplete commercial documentation.
- Do not infer that every Mosrite-looking or Sanner-associated fuzz was made by Rosac/Sangil without model-specific evidence.
