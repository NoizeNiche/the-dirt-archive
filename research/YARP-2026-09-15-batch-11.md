# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 11

## Focus

This batch returns to two high-priority British builders already present in the census, **Marshall Amplification** and **Selmer UK**, and performs a catalog-completion pass rather than deep specimen archaeology. The important historical result is the separation of early outsourced production from later in-house Marshall production, plus the clear manufacturer transition inside the Selmer Fuzz-Wah family.

## Marshall Amplification: dirt catalog corridor

### Supa Fuzz
The Supa Fuzz is retained as an early Marshall dirt family with a documented production handoff. Fuzzboxes' historical research separates an early Sola Sound-supplied period from Marshall's own manufacturing beginning around **August 1968**. The earliest documented Supa Fuzz variant is the short-lived "MKI" state, followed by the better-known Tone Bender MkII-derived form. This means "Marshall Supa Fuzz" is not one homogeneous manufacturing state.

Archive treatment: one retail family, multiple manufacturing/production-state nodes: Sola Sound-supplied and Marshall-manufactured.

### Guv'nor
A major 1980s catalog anchor. Aion FX dates first release to **1988** and identifies Marshall's return to stompboxes after the late-1960s Supa-Fuzz/Supa-Wah period. Sweetwater's historical article corroborates the 1988 release and documents the amp-in-a-box concept plus the original effects-loop feature. Early Guv'nor specimens have UK manufacture; later production moved offshore, so UK and Korea/Taiwan states should remain separate in future specimen work.

### Drivemaster
The 1991 successor/repackaging of the Guv'nor is a distinct retail model but closely related product state. Aion FX says the Drivemaster removed the original Guv'nor effects loop while otherwise retaining the same basic circuit lineage. This is best represented as a successor/model-state relationship, not as an independent design lineage.

### ShredMaster
Introduced in the 1991 Marshall trio with Drivemaster and Bluesbreaker. Sweetwater describes it as the highest-gain member of that trio. It is a separate model and must remain independently searchable despite later Jackhammer lineage.

### Bluesbreaker
The original Bluesbreaker overdrive pedal launched in **1991**. It is a distinct low-gain Marshall amp-inspired model, not merely a relabeled Guv'nor. A later Bluesbreaker II belongs to the 1998 compact/offshore generation and must remain separate.

### 1998 compact generation
Sweetwater's catalog history identifies three distortion/overdrive models launched in the 1998 compact line: **Bluesbreaker II, Guv'nor Plus, and Jackhammer**. The same historical source notes that Jackhammer is the renamed successor to the ShredMaster concept, while Aion FX notes that the Guv'nor Plus and Bluesbreaker II introduced substantial changes rather than being exact reissues.

### Modern vintage-reissue generation
Marshall's current official pedal page lists the **DriveMaster Vintage Reissue, Guv'nor Vintage Reissue, ShredMaster Vintage Reissue and Bluesbreaker Vintage Reissue** as current reissue products, alongside a new **1959 Overdrive, JVM Overdrive, JCM900 Overdrive, JCM800 Overdrive and DSL Overdrive** series. The current catalog is therefore now explicitly connected to the historical Marshall dirt tree without being mistaken for original production. The 2023 vintage reissues are a revival/production state, not a continuation of the original 1988-1990s production run.

## Selmer UK: Fuzz-Wah production transition

The first Selmer Fuzz-Wah is independently documented as a **1968-c.1971** UK-designed and produced pedal. Vintage Hofner's archival page quotes John Weir describing his 1968 design work and the combined Fuzz/Wah/Repeat concept, with two toe/heel-operated switches. The same page documents a **second version from 1971 onward** and preserves the historical statement that Selmer eventually found it cheaper to import pedals from the Far East.

Archive treatment: one retail family, at least two production states, with the first state UK production and the later state probably Far East. The exact later factory remains unresolved.

## Cross-builder lessons

1. Marshall's Supa Fuzz demonstrates why a marketed brand can retain one product name across a real physical-manufacturer transition.
2. Marshall's Guv'nor/Drivemaster relationship demonstrates why a close successor can share a design lineage without being the same retail model.
3. Selmer's Fuzz-Wah demonstrates why a manufacturer transition should be represented at the **production-period** level rather than forcing the entire model into either UK or Far East origin.
4. Modern reissues are separate production states even when the manufacturer explicitly intends them to replicate the historical product.
