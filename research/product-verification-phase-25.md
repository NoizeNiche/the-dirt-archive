# Product Verification Phase 25 — P1 Lineage Verification

## Status

This phase converts the strongest Phase 24 product-depth candidates into explicit verification records. It is still a research layer, not automatic public promotion. The goal is to establish what can be stated confidently, what requires collector-language labeling, and what still needs specimen/production-period evidence.

## Verification standard

A product may be promoted when the archive can establish, at minimum:

1. a stable retail model/family identity;
2. a builder/manufacturer relationship;
3. at least one authoritative historical or current source;
4. meaningful production-period/version distinctions where the builder documents them;
5. no unresolved naming conflict that would cause collector shorthand to be mistaken for an official model or generation.

Serial thresholds and dated builder statements are retained as exact claims. Community labels remain explicitly labeled as collector terminology.

## 1. Fulltone OCD — VERIFIED LINEAGE CANDIDATE

### Stable identity

**Retail family:** Fulltone OCD

**Primary builder/manufacturer relationship:** Fulltone Musical Products for the original historical production; Fulltone USA for the current relaunch-era production.

### Evidence-backed chronology

- Fulltone states that OCD began in **2004**.
- Fulltone explicitly says the commonly repeated "V2 / V3 / V4" naming is misleading. Its own documentation describes one OCD lineage with small value changes designated **V1.0 through V1.8**.
- Fulltone states that **serial 205811 and higher** are the less-compressed revision commonly referred to by users as **"v2.01"**.
- Fulltone USA currently sells OCDv2, OCD-Germanium and Custom Shop OCD v1.4. These are current Fulltone USA offerings and should not be backfilled into the original 2000s production chronology without separate era fields.
- Fulltone USA's Custom Shop OCD v1.4 is a modern reproduction/limited production event and should be represented as an edition/production event tied to the historical v1.4 specification.

### Archive treatment

Use **OCD** as the stable family/model identity. Store V1.0–V1.8 and serial 205811+ as production-period/version claims beneath it. Store collector "v2.01" terminology in an alias/terminology field, not as an official generation name.

### Confidence

**High for family identity and documented version terminology.**

### Remaining research

- establish exact dates/serial ranges for V1.0–V1.8 where available;
- document enclosure/graphics changes as identification clues only when source-backed;
- distinguish original Fulltone Musical Products production from Fulltone USA relaunch production in the production-relationship graph.

Sources:
- https://www.fulltoneusa.com/products/ocd-v2
- https://www.fulltoneusa.com/collections/ocd/products/custom-shop-ocd-v1-4
- https://www.fulltoneusa.com/products/ocd-germanium

## 2. Fulltone Full-Drive 2 — VERIFIED FAMILY CANDIDATE

### Stable identity

**Retail family:** Full-Drive 2

The model name persisted across multiple substantially different production periods. It should therefore not be treated as one homogeneous SKU.

### Evidence-backed production structure

Fulltone USA's current documentation explicitly distinguishes the historical Full-Drive sound across:

- early Full-Drive 2 / Comp-Cut behavior;
- later Flat-Mids behavior;
- MOSFET-era Full-Drive 2 variants;
- later Full-Drive 2 v2 production.

The current Fulltone USA catalog also treats **Full-Drive1**, **Full-Drive2 v2**, and **Full-Drive3** as separate current retail products. Full-Drive1 is positioned as a recreation of the late-1990s/early-2000s Full-Drive sound rather than as the original historical product itself.

### Archive treatment

Create one **Full-Drive family graph** with distinct model/generation nodes for Full-Drive 2 historical production, Full-Drive 2 v2, Full-Drive 3 and the modern Full-Drive1 revival. Do not flatten these into one record merely because the Full-Drive name persists.

### Confidence

**High for family continuity and major documented production distinctions.**

### Remaining research

- obtain exact serial ranges/dates for the early, Flat-Mids, MOSFET and FD2 MOS periods;
- determine which enclosure/graphics identifiers can safely serve as practical specimen clues;
- document the relationship between Full-Drive 2 MOS and later Full-Drive 2 v2 without importing collector shorthand as official nomenclature.

Sources:
- https://www.fulltoneusa.com/products/full-drive1
- https://www.fulltoneusa.com/products/full-drive2-v2
- https://www.fulltoneusa.com/products/full-drive3
- https://www.fulltoneusa.com/pages/manuals

## 3. EarthQuaker Devices Hoof — VERIFIED HISTORICAL CANDIDATE

### Stable identity

**Retail family:** Hoof

### Evidence-backed history

EQD's official history identifies the first production Hoof sale as **March 18, 2007**. EQD places the Hoof in its early basement-production period and describes it as a germanium/silicon hybrid Muff-style fuzz inspired by the Russian Muff family.

The Hoof is therefore a strong anchor product for the archive's EQD timeline and for the builder's transition from basement production into a larger operation.

### Archive treatment

Hoof should be represented as its own family/model. Later Hoof-related products should link to it through explicit relationships rather than being treated as automatic revisions.

### Confidence

**High for first-production date and family identity.**

### Remaining research

- identify documented early-production enclosure/graphic changes;
- establish whether specific germanium/silicon sourcing changes can be dated strongly enough to become production-period clues;
- separate original Hoof production from later reissues/current production where evidence supports it.

Sources:
- https://www.earthquakerdevices.com/about
- https://www.earthquakerdevices.com/blog-posts/fuzzcyclopedia

## 4. EarthQuaker Devices Hoof Reaper — VERIFIED COMBINATION PRODUCT

### Stable identity

**Retail product:** Hoof Reaper

### Evidence-backed history

EQD says the Hoof Reaper was initially intended as a limited-run product. Its Fuzzcyclopedia history records that approximately **25 units were made for Tone Factor for a 2010 Black Friday sale** before demand led EQD to reintroduce it.

The current official page describes the product as the Hoof and Tone Reaper fuzzes in one enclosure with an analog octave-up circuit. It is currently marked **no longer in production**.

### Archive treatment

Model Hoof Reaper as a **combination product**, not as a Hoof revision. Preserve its 2010 limited-run origin and later reintroduction as separate production events.

### Confidence

**High for combination-product identity and 2010 origin.**

Sources:
- https://www.earthquakerdevices.com/hoof-reaper
- https://www.earthquakerdevices.com/blog-posts/fuzzcyclopedia

## 5. Death By Audio Fuzz War — VERIFIED FAMILY CANDIDATE

### Stable identity

**Retail family:** Fuzz War
**Builder:** Death By Audio

### Evidence-backed history

Death By Audio's official history states that Oliver Ackermann founded DBA in **2002** and that the company moved from an early web-based customization service through the Williamsburg Death By Audio factory/venue/studio before moving the pedal workshop to Queens after the venue closed in 2014.

The current Fuzz War documentation establishes the product as a core DBA offering. The archive should preserve production/graphic periods separately when evidence permits rather than treating all Fuzz War specimens as identical.

### Archive treatment

Fuzz War is a stable family/model. Related DBA products such as Soundwave Breakdown, Supersonic Fuzz Gun, Octave Clang and Apocalypse should remain independent families with relationship links.

### Confidence

**High for builder relationship and current family identity. Medium for detailed historical production chronology.**

### Remaining research

- locate earliest dated Fuzz War documentation;
- establish meaningful graphic/enclosure periods;
- distinguish factory/venue-era production from later Queens production only where specimen evidence or builder statements support it.

Sources:
- https://deathbyaudio.com/pages/about-timeline
- https://deathbyaudio.com/collections/1/products/fuzz-war

## 6. Electronic Audio Experiments Longsword — VERIFIED VERSION CHRONOLOGY

### Stable identity

**Retail family:** Longsword
**Builder:** Electronic Audio Experiments

### Evidence-backed chronology

EAE's current documentation provides unusually strong version evidence:

- V1: initial release.
- V2: added diode-clipping selection and Gain 1/2 features.
- V2.1: removed the Range toggle and fixed the range at the chosen intermediate setting.
- V3: added 9–18V operation and replaced Gain 1/2 with Boost.
- V4: six-knob layout, surface-mount PCB, external Boost control and relay bypass.
- V4.5: January 2021, serials **#362–3252**.
- V4.6: updated June 2026 beginning at **serial #3253**, adding intelligent momentary/latching footswitch behavior and reducing Boost switching pop.

### Archive treatment

Longsword is a model with explicit builder-defined generations/revisions. These should be first-class production-period records rather than aliases.

Finish variants such as Acid Etch, Rose Gold, Camouflage, Purple, Teal and the 2025 10 Year Edition should normally be represented as editions/finish variants unless independent evidence establishes substantive production differences.

### Confidence

**Very high for V1–V4.6 chronology.**

Sources:
- https://www.electronicaudioexperiments.com/pedals/longsword
- https://www.electronicaudioexperiments.com/blog/2025/6/17/a-decade-of-studying-the-blade

## 7. Electronic Audio Experiments Halberd — VERIFIED VERSION CHRONOLOGY

### Evidence-backed chronology

- V1: Fall 2019; BB enclosure; NOS germanium transistor in one gain stage.
- V2: August 2021; 125B enclosure; NOS germanium removed; lower current draw; electronic bypass; Voice switch; protection circuitry.
- V2.5: January 2025 beginning at **serial #1684**; revised Pre and Depth tapers and increased maximum gain.

EAE also documents Halberd's genealogy as a standalone iteration of the preamp from Sending, originally released in summer 2018.

### Archive treatment

Halberd should be a stable family with V1, V2 and V2.5 production generations. The Sending relationship should be represented as a design-lineage relationship, not as an assertion that Halberd is simply a Sending variant.

### Confidence

**Very high for version chronology.**

Sources:
- https://www.electronicaudioexperiments.com/pedals/halberd
- https://www.electronicaudioexperiments.com/s/Halberd-V2-Manual-R1.pdf

## 8. Electronic Audio Experiments Sending — VERIFIED GENERATIONAL SPLIT

### Stable identity

**Retail family:** Sending

### Evidence-backed chronology

Sending V1 was an analog tap-tempo delay with a discrete preamp from EAE's early bedroom-DIY period. EAE says the difficulty of fulfilling V1 preorders helped drive a manufacturing redesign.

Sending V2, announced in 2023, is explicitly described as a near-total redesign. It retains the concept of a discrete preamp driving the delay while introducing two MN3005 BBDs, a 32-bit processor, presets, MIDI, LFO modulation and an insert loop.

### Archive treatment

V1 and V2 are separate production generations under one Sending family. V2 must not be represented as a minor revision of V1.

### Confidence

**High.**

Sources:
- https://www.electronicaudioexperiments.com/blog/2023/6/20/sending-v2-coming-july-2023
- https://www.electronicaudioexperiments.com/pedals/sending
- https://www.electronicaudioexperiments.com/legacy

## 9. Black Arts Toneworks Pharaoh — VERIFIED FAMILY CANDIDATE

### Stable identity

**Retail family:** Pharaoh
**Builder:** Black Arts Toneworks

### Evidence-backed structure

BAT's official material presents Pharaoh as a fuzz-rooted dirt design with boost, overdrive and saturated fuzz territory. BAT's archive separately identifies the **Pharaoh Supreme**, dated **January 17, 2015**, as an expanded Pharaoh product combining the Standard and Primitive Pharaoh capabilities with an adjustable pre-input saturation control.

### Archive treatment

Pharaoh should be a stable family. Standard Pharaoh and Pharaoh Supreme should not be merged into one undifferentiated record. The Supreme is a distinct expanded product/edition with its own documented feature set and date.

### Confidence

**High for family relationship and Supreme date; medium for the complete historical chronology of Standard/Primitive production.**

Sources:
- https://www.blackartstoneworks.com/pedal/pharaoh/
- https://www.blackartstoneworks.com/pedal/page/4/

## 10. Black Arts Toneworks LSTR — VERIFIED RELATED FAMILY

### Stable identity

**Retail family:** LSTR

BAT describes LSTR as the Pharaoh's "unruly brother" and explicitly places it in the same BMP/Big Muff family territory while distinguishing its voicing and control behavior. BAT currently marks LSTR **out of production**.

BAT's archive dates the LSTR page to **January 17, 2015**, making that date useful as a documented archive-page anchor, but not necessarily as the product's original introduction date without corroboration.

### Archive treatment

LSTR should be a distinct family related to Pharaoh, not a Pharaoh revision. Sarcophagus should be represented separately as a combination product containing both Pharaoh and LSTR with switchable order.

### Confidence

**High for relationship and discontinued status; medium for original introduction date.**

## Cross-builder findings

### 1. Version naming is not universal

Fulltone's OCD documentation deliberately rejects the collector habit of treating every revision as a numbered version, while EAE openly documents V1/V2/V3/V4.x generations. The archive therefore cannot use a single generic "version" convention without preserving the builder's own terminology.

### 2. Serial thresholds are unusually valuable

EAE's Longsword #3253 and Halberd #1684 thresholds, and Fulltone's OCD serial 205811 threshold, are strong examples of identification-grade evidence. These should be stored as production-period boundary claims rather than merely as prose notes.

### 3. Combination pedals deserve their own product identity

Hoof Reaper and BAT Sarcophagus demonstrate that a pedal can contain established effect families without being a revision of either one. The archive should use relationship types such as `combines`, `contains`, or `derived_from` rather than forcing these products into a parent/child revision hierarchy.

### 4. Current catalog does not equal historical production

Fulltone USA's current catalog contains modern OCD, Full-Drive and fuzz products, while the historical Fulltone Musical Products production era has its own chronology. Current availability should therefore be modeled separately from historical production status.

## Promotion recommendation

The strongest immediate public-promotion candidates are:

**Tier A:** EAE Longsword, EAE Halberd, EAE Sending, EQD Hoof, EQD Hoof Reaper, Fulltone OCD.

**Tier B:** Fulltone Full-Drive 2, DBA Fuzz War, BAT Pharaoh, BAT LSTR.

Tier A has the clearest builder-supplied version/date evidence. Tier B is historically strong but still benefits from additional early-production and specimen-level research before publishing detailed chronology.
