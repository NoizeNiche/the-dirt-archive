# Early Foundational Handoff 98

## Pass 50: 2015-2020 boutique acceleration, finish/version archaeology, and the programmable dirt era

Pass 50 pushes the archive deeper into the 2015-2020 transition, where boutique builders increasingly exposed version boundaries through enclosure changes, finish changes, preorder windows, small first runs, and digitally controlled analog signal paths. The focus remains historical identification rather than circuit reconstruction.

### Newly documented or clarified

- **King Tone Duellist**: first released in 2015 with CNC-engraved polished-aluminum enclosures and recessed black artwork. By 2017 black and red powdercoat finishes appeared, and in 2018 King Tone changed from engraving to UV printing. These finish and artwork changes are strong external specimen clues.
- **KHDK Dark Blood**: late-2016 release-era documentation establishes Kirk Hammett's signature high-gain distortion as an early KHDK production product, with November and December 2016 public records preserved as distinct documentary anchors.
- **Strymon Riverside**: October 2016 announcement and November 2016 availability mark Strymon's move into dedicated overdrive/distortion products under its own brand, following the earlier Damage Control lineage.
- **Strymon Sunset**: 2017 release-era documentation establishes the dual overdrive as a follow-on to Riverside and another step into hybrid analog/DSP dirt.
- **Chase Bliss Brothers**: March 20, 2017 release with February preorder window; documented as Chase Bliss Audio's first drive pedal and a major expansion of digitally controlled analog gain-stage design.
- **Browne Protein**: David Brown states that the Protein launched at the end of 2018 and began shipping in 2019 as the first actual production run. The initial plan was for 100 units, making the first-run scale unusually clear.
- **Mythos Golden Fleece**: early-2017 public documentation captures the first production/public state of the one-knob fuzz/overdrive design. Later mini-enclosure versions should not overwrite the earlier state.
- **Coda Effects Black Hole**: December 2017 documentation shows the original prototype and a small series being prepared, preserving a French DIY-to-small-batch transition with unusually direct builder evidence.
- **Old Blood Noise Endeavors Excess**: November 2017 presale and December retail window document the original distortion-plus-modulation production state before the later Excess V2.
- **Jackson Audio Prism**: October 2017 review identifies Prism as the first release from Jackson Audio, establishing its early boost/overdrive and programmable-control identity.
- **Jackson Audio Broken Arrow**: 2019 documentation establishes the first production era for the programmable overdrive/boost before later V2/MIDI generations.

### Key research findings

1. **Exterior finish can become a real generation marker.** The Duellist provides a clean example where CNC engraving, powdercoat and UV printing form a documentary production sequence without requiring internal inspection.
2. **The boutique category starts absorbing digital control without abandoning analog audio paths.** Brothers, Riverside, Sunset and Jackson Audio demonstrate different ways of wrapping programmable control around gain stages.
3. **First-run size is sometimes recoverable.** Browne's 100-unit initial Protein run and Coda's small Black Hole series show why builder interviews and archived posts matter alongside dealer catalogs.
4. **Preorder, announcement, first availability and first shipment remain separate clocks.** ThorpyFX, Chase Bliss, Coda and Browne reinforce the archive's rule that these events should not be collapsed into one invented launch date.
5. **DIY-to-brand transitions continue well into the late 2010s.** Mythos and Coda illustrate how individual builders could move from experiments and one-off builds into recognizable, documented production families.
6. **Programmability changes identification requirements.** Later boutique dirt pedals may differ not only by enclosure and graphics but also by firmware/control architecture and preset systems. The archive should document those user-visible generation boundaries without publishing implementation recipes.

### Evidence discipline

- Manufacturer and builder-authored statements remain the preferred evidence for release chronology and production changes.
- Contemporary retailer and specialist records are used as public-circulation evidence when primary chronology is incomplete.
- Documentary dates are not silently converted into absolute factory-launch dates.
- Finish, enclosure, switching, control layout and documented manufacturing changes are identification clues only when supported by evidence.
- Circuit-analysis sources and cloning documents remain out of the public archive, consistent with the project's no-schematics/no-PCB/no-complete-BOM boundary.
- Boundary products such as Prism's boost/preamp emphasis are retained only when they clarify a builder's dirt-era chronology and do not distort the central dirt count.

### Current lineage position

The archive now reaches firmly into the programmable boutique era. King Tone gives us an unusually detailed finish chronology; KHDK captures the rise of artist-led high-gain boutique branding; Strymon brings hybrid analog/DSP architecture into mainstream boutique dirt; Chase Bliss makes digitally controlled analog gain a defining feature; Browne preserves a classic tiny-first-run growth story; Mythos and Coda preserve DIY-to-brand paths; OBNE continues its experimental distortion language; and Jackson Audio demonstrates the programmable control layer becoming central to dirt-pedal design.
