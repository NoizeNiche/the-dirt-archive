# Ibanez Soundtank / 5-Series Catalog Audit 05

## Scope

This pass isolates the original 1989 metal-enclosure Soundtank run from the later plastic 5-Series production states and reconciles the 1991-1992 catalog corridor with later 1990s catalog evidence. It deliberately avoids circuit reconstruction details.

## Strong catalog anchors

Ibanez's current official catalog archive lists a dedicated **1989 Soundtank** catalog, a **1991 Soundtank** catalog, and the 1980s/1990s catalog trail needed to place the series in its historical sequence. The official archive therefore supports treating Soundtank as a documented Ibanez product series, not merely a later collector label.

The surviving 1989 Soundtank catalog/dealer-sheet record is especially important because specialist historical sources identify the first run as a metal-enclosure group. ToneHome dates the metal-enclosure 5-Series/Soundtank group to **1989-1990** and distinguishes it from the subsequent plastic series. Gitarre & Bass independently describes the 1989 Soundtank launch and the later replacement of the original metal housings with plastic housings.

## Dirt products in the original Soundtank corridor

The core early dirt models are:

- **CR5 Crunchy Rhythm** - 1989 introduction corridor; early Soundtank dirt model.
- **TM5 Thrashmetal** - 1989 introduction corridor; early Soundtank high-gain model.
- **SF5 60's Fuzz** - early metal-enclosure fuzz state, documented for 1989-1990 in specialist historical coverage.

The later Soundtank catalog universe expands beyond those initial dirt products. The surviving 1991/1992 catalog trail and later 1997 catalog indexing show the continued presence of **FZ5, TM5, TS5, PL5, SP5, CM5, CD5, and other 5-Series models**, with non-dirt products intentionally retained only as boundary/context records.

## SF5 -> FZ5 relationship

The SF5/FZ5 transition should be represented as a **retail/model-state lineage**, not automatically as two unrelated fuzz families. Specialist evidence identifies the metal-enclosure SF5 as the earlier state and the later FZ5 as the renamed/relabelled 60's Fuzz identity. The exact date boundary should remain an evidence-qualified corridor rather than a falsely precise single day.

## Soundtank attribution caution

Aion FX explicitly states that the Soundtank pedals were **not designed or manufactured by Maxon**, despite the strong Maxon relationship in earlier Ibanez 8-Series/9-Series history. Effects Database likewise reports the series as probably associated with Daphon manufacturing, while noting uncertainty. The archive should therefore keep:

**Ibanez = marketed brand / product owner identity**

separate from

**physical manufacturer = unresolved or model-specific unless documentary evidence supports attribution**.

Do not inherit the Nisshin Onpa/Maxon manufacturer field from TS808, TS9, OD850, or related earlier Ibanez lines merely because the product is also an Ibanez pedal.

## 1990s catalog confirmation

The Ibanez 1997 catalog index explicitly lists the Soundtank dirt group including **FZ5, SP5, PL5, TS5, CD5, CS5, PH5, CP5, DL5, BP5, BC5, CM5, TM5, TL5**, while official Ibanez catalog archives also preserve the 1991 Soundtank catalog and surrounding 1990s catalog sequence. This confirms that Soundtank was an active, evolving product family rather than a one-year 1989 launch artifact.

The broader historical Soundtank index also identifies a 20-product 5-Series universe, including **BN5 Black Noise, CD5 Cyberdrive, SP5 Slam Punk, MF5 Modern Fusion, PL5 Powerlead, CM5 Classic Metal, TM5 Thrashmetal, TS5 Tubescreamer, and FZ5 60's Fuzz**. These records should be treated as catalog leads and chronology aids; model-specific manufacturer claims still require separate evidence.

## Archive decisions

1. Keep **SF5 and FZ5** linked as successive retail/model states of the 60's Fuzz family.
2. Keep **CR5, TM5, TS5, and other Soundtank models** under the Ibanez marketed-product entity until individual manufacturing evidence is established.
3. Do not label all Soundtank products as Maxon-made.
4. Treat **1989-1990 metal enclosure** and later plastic enclosure production as distinct specimen/prodution-state corridors.
5. Use the 1991/1992 and 1997 catalog records to expand the lifetime model census before deep specimen archaeology.
