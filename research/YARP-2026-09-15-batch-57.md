# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 57: Maestro / Gibson Vintage Fuzz Family

## Scope
Builder-first catalog completion for the original Maestro/Gibson fuzz line, separating FZ-1, FZ-1A, FZ-1B and FZ-1S generations from wah, ring-modulator, octave, reissue, and unresolved Canadian production states.

## Builder identity
Maestro was Gibson's effects brand during the Chicago Musical Instruments (CMI) era and later Norlin ownership. Gibson's current historical timeline identifies the FZ-1 as the brand's 1962 introduction and places subsequent FZ-1A, FZ-1B and FZ-1S products in the same broad historical family. The archive should therefore treat Maestro as the marketed effects brand and Gibson/CMI/Norlin as the corporate manufacturing layer, while preserving any model-specific manufacturing exceptions.

## Enumerated dirt catalog

### FZ-1 Fuzz-Tone
Introduced in 1962. Gibson's current Maestro timeline gives the 1962 introduction, while Effects Database records substantial 1962-1967 shipments and identifies Kalamazoo production. The FZ-1 was the original commercially successful Maestro fuzz generation.

### FZ-1A Fuzz-Tone
The FZ-1A is a distinct 1965-1968 revision rather than merely a late FZ-1 enclosure state. Secondary historical documentation places its start after the 1965 demand surge and notes production beginning in Kalamazoo before moving to Lincolnwood, Illinois.

### FZ-1B Fuzz-Tone
The FZ-1B is a 1968 redesign associated with Robert Moog, with a different appearance and silicon-transistor generation. Secondary sources describe multiple internal production revisions. Exact first-shipment and end-of-production dates remain a deeper archival task.

### FZ-1S Super-Fuzz
The 1970s FZ-1S is a substantially different-looking and sounding successor. It belongs in the Maestro dirt catalog as a separate family member rather than being treated as another FZ-1 generation.

### FZ-2 Fuzz-Tone
A late-1960s Canadian FZ-2 is reported by secondary historical sources as limited production by Turner Musical Instruments. This is not yet strong enough to fold into Gibson's canonical manufacturer record. Archive status remains discovery/unresolved pending primary evidence.

## Non-dirt and boundary records

- BG-1 Boomerang Wah, 1967: separate wah product.
- RM-1A Ring Modulator, 1969 onward: Norlin-era non-dirt effect.
- Vintage Maestro Octave Box: fuzz-adjacent octave family, retained as a boundary/catalog lead until production chronology is deeper.
- 1990s FZ-1A reissue: revival layer, not vintage production.
- 2022 Fuzz-Tone FZ-M and newer Maestro line: modern Gibson revival, separate from vintage CMI/Norlin production.

## Historical significance

Maestro is a critical builder/brand node because its Fuzz-Tone family provides a clean multi-generation timeline from 1962 through the 1970s. The archive now treats the product family as a sequence of historically distinct generations instead of one generic "FZ-1" object. The move from Kalamazoo to Lincolnwood in the FZ-1A period also creates a useful future specimen-reconciliation axis without requiring circuit reconstruction.

## Evidence state

**Verified/evidence-qualified:** FZ-1 1962; FZ-1A 1965-1968 corridor; FZ-1B 1968 redesign; FZ-1S 1970s family; Gibson/CMI/Norlin corporate/brand continuity; separate modern reissue layers.

**Unresolved:** exact FZ-1B revision chronology; exact FZ-1S introduction/end dates; full FZ-2 Canadian manufacturing documentation; complete non-dirt Maestro catalog for any additional dirt-adjacent hybrids.
