# Early Foundational Handoff 61

## Pass 09: FY-2 label backfill + 1980s dirt corridor

This pass continues the archive's descent from the late-1960s/1970s Japanese OEM network into the early-1980s transition period. It adds sixteen reconciliation records covering both obscure FY-2 branded states and a deliberately narrow set of major 1980s overdrive/distortion lineages.

### Newly documented

- **J.H. Experience FY-2 Fuzz Box**: cataloged as a 1970s OEM-branded Companion FY-2 state. Evidence remains research-level.
- **Kimbara FY-2 Fuzz Box**: added as a distinct retail identity in the FY-2 network.
- **Suzuki FY-2 Fuzz**: regional Mexican-market specimen evidence, treated as specimen-level rather than a universal Suzuki production claim.
- **Zenta FY-2 Fuzz Box**: added as another FY-2 retail identity.
- **Companion FY-2 Fuzz Box**: records the surviving early-germanium versus later-silicon specimen distinction without assigning a universal cutoff.
- **DOD FX50 Overdrive Preamp / FX50B Overdrive Plus**: transitional early-1980s DOD family, with the FX50B's initial and later boost-related states separated.
- **Ibanez TS9 / TS10**: original 9-series and later 10-series Tube Screamer states added as distinct retail generations.
- **BOSS SD-1 / OD-2**: SD-1 anchored to BOSS's own 1981 history; OD-2 kept research-level pending stronger primary catalog evidence.
- **ProCo RAT 2 / Turbo RAT**: RAT 2 enclosure-era evidence and Turbo RAT's separate 1989 lineage added.
- **ROSS Distortion**: regional production transition flagged as specimen-dependent.
- **Colorsound Jumbo Tone Bender**: late-production chronology retained separately from the earlier Supa Tone Bender naming and lineage.

### Key research findings

1. **FY-2 remains a family tree, not a single-box story.** Specialist and catalog evidence supports numerous branded retail states. The archive should keep each label visible while preserving uncertainty around importer, factory, and exact contract relationships.
2. **Germanium/silicon is an identification layer, not a blanket date.** Surviving FY-2 evidence demonstrates technology changes across specimens. Future entries should attach transistor-era statements to dated examples whenever possible.
3. **The DOD 250 -> FX50 -> FX50B corridor is worth treating as a lineage.** The FX50B was introduced in 1986 and received a reported functional revision around 1987. These are useful generation markers even though the archive will not publish circuit reconstruction material.
4. **The Tube Screamer line is best modeled by retail series.** TS9 and TS10 are materially different archive objects, and their production eras overlap the broader Japanese OEM transition into increasingly distributed manufacturing.
5. **The RAT family needs enclosure-led specimen identification.** RAT 2's early flat enclosure and later sloped housing create strong visible generation markers. Turbo RAT is a separate model branch beginning in 1989.
6. **ROSS is another reminder that country is a specimen attribute.** A 1970s U.S. ROSS Distortion and a later Taiwan-built specimen should not be silently collapsed into one manufacturing state.
7. **Colorsound naming chronology is messy by design.** Supa Tone Bender and Jumbo Tone Bender overlap in lineage while retail naming changed during the 1970s. The archive should retain both model names and their chronology rather than normalize them away.

### Evidence discipline

- **VERIFIED** is reserved for claims with strong manufacturer or similarly authoritative historical support.
- **RESEARCH** remains the default for collector/specialist chronology, OEM family assignments, marketplace-derived dating, and regional production claims that need primary-source confirmation.
- Exact component details may be used privately for identification, but the public archive remains focused on external evidence, generation markers, provenance, chronology, and object-level distinctions.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot galleries, or cloning instructions are added.
