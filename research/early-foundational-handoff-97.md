# Early Foundational Handoff 97

## Pass 49: 2009-2017 boutique maturation, first-run variability, and the modern production transition

Pass 49 continues the archive from the early internet boutique era into the period when builders increasingly documented their own version histories, production changes, and first-batch stories. The emphasis remains on externally useful identification clues and evidence-class discipline.

### Newly documented or clarified

- **JHS Morning Glory transitional V1/V2 corridor**: JHS explicitly documents experimental 2009 builds whose toggle, logo, enclosure, and custom features blur the nominal V1/V2 boundary. This is retained as a specimen corridor rather than a false hard cutoff.
- **JHS Morning Glory V3**: JHS dates the move to the smaller enclosure to summer 2010, followed by a fall-2010 Roman-candle artwork change and a move to printed-circuit-board production.
- **JHS Morning Glory 2012 stamp state**: the V3 family remained in production in 2012 while the exterior stamp was redesigned, providing a cosmetic specimen boundary without implying an electronic redesign.
- **JHS Muffuletta**: first released September 15, 2015, establishing a dated fuzz-family expansion distinct from earlier JHS versions.
- **Empress Heavy**: the original two-channel Heavy launched in 2013 and remains distinct from the later Heavy Menace compact redesign.
- **Bondi Effects Sick As**: Bondi's official history states that the first Sick As was sold in 2012, establishing the builder's first branded overdrive anchor.
- **Origin Effects SlideRIG and Cali76**: 2012 founding/product chronology is retained as a compressor boundary that explains the British studio-pedal lineage without entering the central dirt count.
- **ThorpyFX Gunshot**: first commercial pedal released April 2015, with contemporaneous company launch material confirming the opening of the product line.
- **ThorpyFX Warthog**: December 2015 preorder and planned February 2016 shipment establish a useful preorder-to-first-shipment production corridor for an early distortion model.
- **Electronic Audio Experiments Dagger V1**: archived manufacturer documentation dates V1 to 2017-2019 and explicitly notes intentional early-batch variation in clipping devices, EQ voicing and drive amount.
- **KMA Fuzzly Bear**: 2013 first production state reinforced from KMA's own history, including apartment-based manufacturing and original artwork context.
- **Coda Effects Black Hole**: 2014 Coda brand-origin anchor preserved alongside the early handmade Black Hole product family, with exact first-sale dating left provisional.

### Key research findings

1. **Nominal version boundaries can be porous.** JHS's Morning Glory history demonstrates that early custom work can overlap named revisions, so physical identification should use a cluster of clues rather than one control or logo change.
2. **Production-method changes can be historically significant.** The Morning Glory's shift from hand-built variation toward PCB production is a useful manufacturing boundary even when the audible design remained stable.
3. **First shipment and preorder windows are becoming easier to document.** ThorpyFX shows how website posts can preserve exact launch stages, while the Dagger shows how later manufacturer retrospectives can recover first-version variability.
4. **Modern boutique histories increasingly preserve variability rather than hiding it.** EAE's Dagger V1 is a strong example where different early units were intentionally not guaranteed to match exactly.
5. **International boutique chronology remains broad.** Australia, Germany, France, Canada, the UK and the USA all contribute meaningful early-production histories in this pass.
6. **Scope boundaries still matter.** Origin Effects' early compressor lineage is kept as builder chronology, while core dirt indexing remains focused on fuzz, overdrive and distortion.

### Evidence discipline

- Manufacturer and builder-authored sources remain preferred for production dates, version changes and first-sale events.
- Contemporary posts and later retrospective pages are retained as distinct evidence classes.
- Preorders, first shipment, first sale, first release and first design remain separate historical states.
- Product variability is recorded when explicitly acknowledged by the manufacturer rather than generalized from individual specimens.
- Non-dirt products remain chronology boundaries and do not inflate the central dirt count.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries or cloning instructions are added.

### Current lineage position

The archive now has a robust bridge from the first internet-era micro-builders into the middle of the 2010s. JHS illustrates the messy overlap between custom builds and named revisions; Empress, Bondi and Origin broaden the professional boutique network; ThorpyFX preserves exact preorder and launch clocks; EAE documents early-batch variability; KMA maintains a hand-built European lineage; and Coda shows how the DIY pedal-building community continued to generate new brands.
