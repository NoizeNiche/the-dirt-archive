# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 25

## Focus
Independent American fuzz makers that sit outside the better-known mass-market 1960s catalog: InterFax Electronics and Ridinger Associates.

## Findings

### InterFax Electronics / Ed Giese
InterFax Electronics was a Milwaukee, Wisconsin electronics repair business operated by Ed Giese. Historical sources identify the **Harmonic Percolator HP-1** as a Giese-made fuzz/distortion pedal from the 1970s, produced in very small quantities. ToneHome identifies the maker as Ed Giese in Milwaukee and records the pedal as a 1970s product; Reverb's historical account places the origins in an early-1970s Milwaukee repair business and describes direct/local-music-store sales. The archive therefore treats InterFax as a canonical small American builder with a single strongly indexed dirt anchor, while keeping exact start/end production dates unresolved.

### Harmonic Percolator HP-1
The HP-1 belongs in the central dirt catalog because its identity as an original 1970s InterFax fuzz/distortion product is well supported. Sources disagree on exact chronology and on some production-state details, so the archive uses a broad 1970s corridor rather than a fabricated exact launch year. Later reproductions and boutique interpretations are explicitly excluded from the original-builder count.

### Ridinger Associates / Steve Ridinger
Steve Ridinger states that he began making the **Liverpool Fuzz Tone** around 1966, initially selling handmade units to classmates, then dealers in the Hollywood area from June 1967, followed by U.S. distribution. Effects Database preserves his first-person chronology and a later assembly-company production phase, estimating roughly 500-1,000 examples. Tone Machines Blog independently documents the product's November 1968 Billboard exposure and December 1968 Beat Instrumental coverage, while the period Beat Instrumental scan identifies the Liverpool Fuzz-Tone as a Ridinger Associates product.

The archive therefore promotes Ridinger Associates to a canonical California independent-builder lineage and treats Liverpool Fuzz Tone as its core historical dirt product. The later fOXX identity remains a successor/related builder lineage, not a reason to rewrite the Liverpool record as a fOXX product retroactively.

### Evidence boundary
The Liverpool chronology is stronger than the exact factory name used for the later assembly phase. The archive records the hand-built-to-assembly transition without inventing an OEM factory identity. No schematic, PCB, BOM, gutshot or cloning material is included.
