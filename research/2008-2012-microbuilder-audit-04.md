# 2008-2012 Microbuilder Audit 04

## Scope

This pass expands the 2008-2012 boutique dirt corridor with four builders whose surviving evidence supports more than a one-pedal mention: Catalinbread, Blackout Effectors, Spaceman Effects, and Wampler Pedals. The archive treats builder identity, marketed product identity, edition, generation, and exact production state as separate layers.

## Catalinbread

Catalinbread's own historical summary, preserved in the Effects Database catalog, places a major expansion in 2008-2011. The company says the Dirty Little Secret was released during 2008-2009, while SFT, Formula No. 5, Merkin and other products occupy the same growth corridor. The documented 2010 transition from Lexan faceplates to two-color silkscreen graphics and from plastic knobs to machined aluminum knobs is historically useful because enclosure appearance can otherwise be mistaken for circuit-generation evidence.

The Dirty Little Secret is a particularly good specimen-archaeology target because the surviving record contains a v1 "Usual Suspects" prototype, later MkII, and several edition/color states. The archive therefore records the prototype and generation relationship without treating every visual variant as a different pedal. The Merkin is separately anchored to Aug. 3, 2010. The company's chronology places Manx Loaghtan, DLS MkII, CB30 and Naga Viper in 2011.

## Blackout Effectors

Kyle Tompkins states that Blackout Effectors began in early 2008 with the release of Fix'D Fuzz. Effects Database independently places the company in Vancouver in 2008 and notes a move to Asheville, North Carolina in 2009. This makes Blackout a clean example of a small builder whose geography changed early enough that photographs and labels from different periods may describe the same company but different production contexts.

The Fix'D Fuzz and Musket are both anchored to the 2008 corridor. The lifetime Effects Database index contains 17 Blackout products, of which a meaningful subset are dirt: Fix'D Fuzz, Fix'D Fuzz Deluxe, Musket, FUBAR, Fuzz Bear, Mantra, Dual Gain Mantra, Twosome variants and Blunderbuss. This pass intentionally records the catalog universe without claiming the lifetime catalog is complete or every first-sale date is resolved.

## Spaceman Effects

Spaceman's current company history states it was founded in 2009 and began as an artisan, hand-built, strictly limited pedal operation. The strongest early evidence here is the Spacerocket, first indexed in July 2010 as a limited 70-unit silicon fuzz. Crucially, an Aug. 16, 2010 Spaceman update documents a design change that replaced the Fuzz control with Tone and moved Fuzz to a three-position toggle. That is retained as a real production-state boundary because it is direct builder evidence, not inference from photographs.

Gemini III is anchored to NAMM 2011 / Jan. 15, 2011 and combines separate germanium and silicon fuzz paths in parallel. The Wow Signal is anchored to 2012 and has a documented 30-unit copper First Edition made with The Dandy Warhols. Saturn V is anchored to Nov. 19, 2012 as a harmonic booster / low-gain overdrive. Later Sputnik, Gemini IV, Saturn VI and Spacerocket II products are not back-projected into this early catalog window.

## Wampler Pedals

Wampler's current company history describes the business as growing from Brian Wampler's earlier DIY and pedal-modding activity, while his 2019 retrospective explains that the business was called IndyGuitarist in the early phase and began using outside manufacturing as it scaled. This is important for the archive because "Wampler" describes the design/business identity while physical production may have occurred elsewhere. The file therefore avoids equating brand owner with factory.

The documented 2007-2010 dirt sequence includes PlexTortion, Pinnacle, Plexi-Drive, Ecstasy Drive, Hot Wired, Super Plextortion, Triple Recstortion/Triple Wreck and Paisley Drive. Effects Database publication dates provide clean chronological anchors for many of these entries. The Triple Recstortion is particularly valuable because the surviving description states that only 100 units were built before the later Triple Wreck brought the concept back with expanded controls. That is exactly the kind of limited-run to regular-product transition the archive should preserve.

The Ecstasy Drive / Euphoria relationship is also retained as a product-state lineage rather than treated as unrelated pedals. Euphoria's 2012 record explicitly presents it as the evolution of the earlier Ecstasy concept, while still describing a distinct retail product.

## Evidence discipline

- Primary builder/company statements are preferred for founding dates, design changes, manufacturing arrangements, and explicit release chronology.
- Effects Database is used as a historical secondary index and date anchor, not as automatic proof of factory attribution.
- A later reissue is not counted as the original production state.
- Limited editions with documented unit counts remain linked to the parent model rather than inflating model counts.
- OEM or outside-manufacturing claims require separate evidence and should not be inferred merely from enclosure similarity.
