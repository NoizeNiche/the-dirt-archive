# Sola Sound / Colorsound Tone Bender Catalog Audit 02

## Scope

This pass tightens the 1968–1976 Sola Sound / Colorsound dirt chronology, with special attention to the Tone Bender Mk III/Mk IV transition, the OEM corridor, and the Supa/Jumbo Tone Bender split.

This is a historical catalog record only. No schematics, PCB layouts, BOMs, or circuit-reconstruction instructions are included.

## Confidence / chronology notes

### Tone Bender Mk III

- Strong historical consensus places the Mk III in the late-1960s, commonly dated to **1968**.
- Sola Sound built Mk III family pedals for multiple marketed brands, including **Vox, Park, and Rotosound**.
- Evidence from surviving examples indicates that the label alone is not sufficient to determine one exact internal production state. The Mk III corridor evolved over time, and different branded examples can contain different production variants.
- The Sola Sound-branded Mk III appears to be substantially scarcer than the Vox-marked examples.

### Tone Bender Mk IV

- The Mk IV appears around **1970** and should be treated as its own retail/enclosure generation rather than merely another name for every late Mk III.
- Early Mk IV examples used the new pressed-steel enclosure and distinctive graphics while remaining closely related to the preceding Mk III family.
- The two enclosure generations overlapped for a period. Therefore, a simple statement such as “Mk III ended when Mk IV began” is too rigid for the archive.
- Around **1971**, the Sola Sound graphics shifted toward the better-known **Tone-Bender Fuzz** presentation. The archive should retain “Mk IV” and “Tone-Bender Fuzz” as historically distinct retail/graphic states linked within one family.
- Surviving examples demonstrate that the production family changed internally over the 1970s. Exact internal variants belong at specimen/generation level rather than being generalized across the whole model name.

### OEM / rebadge corridor

Sola Sound continued to operate as an OEM supplier through this period. The archive therefore keeps the following as separate marketed identities linked to a Sola Sound manufacturing relationship when evidence supports it:

- Vox Tone Bender Mk III / related Vox-marked variants
- Park Fuzz Sound
- Rotosound Fuzz Box / Fuzz Box Mk II family
- Carlsbro Fuzz
- Later B&M / Champion and other export-market identities associated with the broader Colorsound/Sola Sound production corridor

The key archive rule is **do not count every branded enclosure as a separate builder**. The builder/manufacturer relationship belongs above the retail brand node.

### Colorsound Power Boost → Overdriver

- The **Colorsound Power Boost** belongs to the early-1970s Colorsound dirt/boost family.
- The product was subsequently marketed as the **Colorsound Overdriver**, with evidence placing the rename in **1973**.
- The archive should preserve Power Boost and Overdriver as separate retail-name states within a linked family, not as unrelated products and not as one flat SKU.
- Period examples show production evolution, including later requests/modifications such as the addition of a master-volume control. These should not be projected backward onto all original Power Boost units.

### Supa Tone Bender

- Strong sources place the **Colorsound Supa Tone Bender** in **1973**.
- It is a major break from the earlier germanium Tone Bender lineage. The archive should therefore keep it as a new family branch rather than assuming “Tone Bender” means one continuous circuit/product architecture.
- Period sources describe the Supa Tone Bender as related to the contemporary Electro-Harmonix Big Muff family, while still treating the Colorsound product as its own marketed pedal.
- The wide enclosure is a strong specimen-era discriminator and should be retained in image/specimen records.

### Jumbo Tone Bender

- Evidence places the **Colorsound Jumbo Tone Bender** around **1975–1976**, after the Supa Tone Bender had established the large-format Colorsound fuzz line.
- The Jumbo should be represented as a distinct retail model/family branch, not merely as a cosmetic Supa Tone Bender revision.
- Multiple secondary histories describe the Jumbo as using a simplified Big Muff-derived architecture. Because this is a circuit-level historical statement, it should remain secondary metadata and not become the primary identity key.
- Late-1970s OEM/export identities associated with the Jumbo include **B&M/Champion Fuzz**, **B&M Fuzz Unit**, **CMI Fuzz Unit**, **G.B. Fuzz / Fuzz Unit**, **Pro’Traffic Fuzz Unit**, and **Eurotec Black Box Fuzz Module** in surviving documentation. These are candidates for separate marketed-product records linked to the Sola Sound manufacturing corridor, subject to specimen-level confirmation.

## Important chronology distinction

The archive should avoid the collector-friendly but misleading single-line chronology:

> Mk III → Mk IV → Supa → Jumbo

That sequence is useful as a first approximation but hides simultaneous production, OEM overlap, enclosure changes, and parallel retail names.

A better representation is a **family tree with overlapping production windows**:

- Tone Bender family
  - Mk III, late 1960s
    - Sola Sound
    - Vox
    - Park
    - Rotosound
  - Mk IV / Tone-Bender Fuzz, c. 1970–1976
    - Sola Sound
    - Carlsbro
    - Park
    - related OEM/export states
  - Supa Tone Bender, c. 1973 onward
  - Jumbo Tone Bender, c. 1975–1976 onward

This structure preserves ambiguity where the evidence genuinely overlaps.

## Evidence hierarchy used

1. Period advertising / catalogs where available.
2. Manufacturer or current Macari/Sola Sound historical material where available.
3. Surviving dated specimens and specialist historical databases.
4. Specialist secondary histories for chronology cross-checking.
5. Forums are treated as leads, not final proof.
