# The Dirt Archive — Completeness Audit Phase 16

## Scope

This audit formalizes the next major workstream: **complete builder-level enumeration before deep individual-pedal research**. A builder record is considered incomplete until its documented catalog has been compared against at least one broad index and, where practical, the builder's own historical/current material.

## Dense catalogs requiring full enumeration

### JHS Pedals
Effects Database currently lists 98 products. This includes collaborations, utility products, fuzzes, overdrives, delays, modulation and discontinued/legacy items. The catalog includes, among others, 424 Gain Stage, 73 Rams Head Replica, 808, Angry Charlie, Artificial Blonde, Astro Mess, Bonsai, Bun Runner, Cheese Ball, Colour Box, Coyote, Double Barrel, Firefly Fuzz, Fumble, Haunting Mids, Moonshine, Morning Glory, Panther, Pulp 'N' Peel, SuperBolt, 3 Series and Legends of Fuzz products. The full 98-product list must be retained rather than reduced to dirt products.

EDB also exposes a substantial first-party media trail for JHS, including the company's own historical videos and a 2021 video specifically about the rarest JHS pedals. This makes JHS a high-value candidate for later product-by-product research, variation tracking and historical quotations.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/jhspedals

### EarthQuaker Devices
Effects Database currently lists 88 products. The list spans the company's entire effect range and includes a particularly useful group of dirt/fuzz products: Acapulco Gold, Barrows, Bellows, Bit Commander, Black Ash, Blaster Fuzz, Blumes, Bows, Chelsea, Cloven Hoof, Crimson Drive, Dirt Transmitter, Dream Crusher, Dunes, Erupter, Fantastic Beast, Fuzz Master General, Hoof, Hoof Reaper, Hizumitas, Monarch, Plumes, Speaker Cranker, Special Cranker, Spires, Stealth Fuzz, Terminal, Tone Reaper, Tusk and Zoar, among many non-dirt products.

EDB contains a detailed first-person company timeline beginning with Jamie Stillman's 2005-2007 experimentation and including the first Hoof sale in March 2007, later dealer growth, and the transition to full-time production. This should eventually feed the builder history record.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/earthquaker

### Keeley Electronics
Effects Database currently lists 125 products. The list includes full-size pedals, mini versions, custom-shop and signature products, compressors, drives, fuzzes, modulation, delay, reverb and utility products. Fuzz/drive items include Black Glass OC44, Black Glass OC81D, Black Silk OC75, Blacktop, Charing Cross, Clipper Fuzz, Electric Mudd, Fuzz, Fuzz Bender, Fuzz Head, Fuzzy Pizdets, Germanium Monterey Special, Germanium Super Phat Mod, Holy Fuzz, Java Boost, Katana Blues Drive, and many more.

The Keeley catalog needs a dedicated variant audit because several product families contain multiple versions or revisions that should not be casually treated as separate unrelated pedals.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/keeley

### Mr. Black
Effects Database currently lists 94 products. The catalog contains fuzz, distortion and overdrive products alongside delay, reverb, modulation and mini pedals. The full enumeration should be retained even though the Dirt Archive's founding scope emphasizes dirt.

The current EDB list includes JD-255x, JD-800 Lead, Jalapeño Overdrive, JDS-50, Orro, Snake Drive, Sour Cream & Onion, Super-Extra Five Thousand, Thunderclaw, Vintage Overdrive, White Widow and numerous non-dirt designs.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/mrblack

### Electro-Harmonix
Effects Database currently lists 133 products in the main EHX manufacturer index. This is a critical canonical builder and should eventually receive one of the deepest individual-product research passes because the catalog includes multiple generations and reissues of historically important dirt products, including Big Muff Pi, Deluxe Big Muff Pi, Little Big Muff, Little Muff, Muff Fuzz, Double Muff, Fuzz Wah, Hot Tubes, LPB-1/LPB-2 and multiple related products.

The current EDB index also includes modern collaborations and a long tail of obscure vintage EHX products. The archive must distinguish an actual product generation from a later reissue, collaboration or renamed version.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/eh

### BSM
Effects Database lists 35 products. BSM's historical value is unusually high because it is a defunct specialist builder whose products were tailored to specific players and periods. The builder record should eventually document model-by-model artist/tone target, production period, hand-built construction era and component-family changes at a high level.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/bsm

### VFE Pedals
Effects Database lists 42 products and gives a useful first-person chronology: BYOC discovery in 2008, modified eBay pedals in summer 2008, first designed PCB in May 2009, formal founding in January 2010, full-time operation in June 2010, relay switching in October 2010 and silkscreen graphics/retail distribution in April 2011.

Those production milestones should later be used as cross-check points when researching individual VFE variants.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/vfe

### Blackout Effectors

The archive already knows the core fuzz lineage but should enumerate all 17, including A Seriously Special Twosome, A Very Special Twosome, Blunderbuss, Crystal Dagger, Fix'D Fuzz, Fix'D Fuzz Deluxe, FUBÄR, Fuzz Bear, Musket Fuzz, Twosome variants and non-fuzz products.

Blackout is a strong later case study for artwork/finish variants, collaborations and production changes.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/blackout

### Dwarfcraft Devices
Effects Database lists 46 products. The builder's humorous timeline should be treated as builder voice rather than literal chronology, while the product catalog itself should be fully enumerated. The builder is historically important to the experimental/boutique branch and has documented collaborations with other small builders.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/dwarfcraft

### Chase Bliss Audio

### Heavy Electronics
Effects Database lists 11 products. The builder's first-person chronology is useful for tracking production-stage changes from painted/stenciled units to vinyl and later silk-screened graphics, plus the addition of assemblers and a move to a larger location. Full product enumeration is required.

### G.S. Wyllie
Effects Database lists 10 products. All ten should be preserved. The builder's historical notes are especially valuable for documenting early home-built fuzz history and the eventual reduction in germanium use due to component consistency issues.

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/gswyllie

## Photography backlog

Effects Database currently exposes a dedicated `pictures needed` category containing 198 products. This is a useful external image-research queue, not a license to copy those images. The Dirt Archive should use it to identify products that need:

1. first-party photographs
2. public-domain or openly licensed photographs
3. permission-cleared collector photographs
4. eventual owned specimen photography

Source: https://www.effectsdatabase.com/incomplete/images

## Photography rule for the eventual archive

Every product record should have an image status independent of the product's research status:

`No known image`
`Image lead found`
`Source image found; rights unresolved`
`Licensed / permission-cleared`
`Public-domain / open license`
`Owned / first-party`

Do not display a marketplace image merely because it is the easiest image to find.

## Important implementation note

The complete-list target applies to **every builder we discover**, including one-person workshops, dead brands, OEMs and current large manufacturers. The archive should not stop at the famous examples.

For the current project, the working sequence is:

`Builder discovered -> builder catalog enumerated -> product records created -> variants separated -> photos sourced -> individual product research -> canonical promotion`

This ordering prevents the common failure mode where five famous products receive excellent research while dozens of obscure products from the same builder never make it into the archive.
