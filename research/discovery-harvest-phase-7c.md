# The Dirt Archive — Discovery Harvest Phase 7C

This pass prioritizes small, one-person, hand-built, discontinued, and unusual builders whose product histories are easy to lose. Effects Database is used as a discovery backbone. These entries are discovery leads unless independently promoted elsewhere in the archive.

## High-value small-builder records

### G.S. Wyllie
Effects Database currently lists 10 products. The builder profile identifies Glenn S. Wyllie as an American one-person workshop in North Carolina who built effects, string instruments, and visual art. Documented effects include Fuzzmite, IronStone, Moonrock, Over-Tron, OZO Dynamic Alien Octaver, Rising Sun, Wylo, Wylo Delux, X-Fuzz and Xylo. The profile is especially valuable because Wyllie's work includes a named Moonrock model and a Rising Sun associated with Shin-ei Super Fuzz lineage, while still representing an individual builder's own production history.

**Archive treatment:** Builder deserves a first-class record. Preserve individual model names and do not collapse his output into generic clones or reproductions.

### DenTone Electronics
Effects Database currently lists 19 products. It describes Dennis Menard's operation as a one-man "mad scientist" workshop in Barre, Vermont. Product names include 900 Pound Violin, Alien Hate Fuzz, BFF Biggest Fattest Fuzz, Black Hole Generator, Bolthead, Buzzaround, CigBooster, Demented Circus Monkey, Freaktone Fuzz, Fuzzrite, FY-2 Companion Fuzz, Jupiter Rocket, Pinhead, Pinhead 2, Purple Octavepus, Rangemaster, Rocket, SteamRoller and SupaFuzz.

**Archive treatment:** This is an exemplary small-builder catalog. Preserve the complete product-name history and any documented version/edition distinctions. Avoid reproducing technical build recipes.

### T. Jauernig Electronics
Effects Database currently lists 22 products and describes the company as a one-person operation from Wausau, Wisconsin. Timothy Jauernig's timeline says the first Luxury Drive was made in early 2004, followed by a small initial commercial run after a local shop encouraged him to build more.

Products include F-Bomb, 1-800-GAIN, Amalgam, Atomic FET-Boy, Bombastortion, DGTM Diabolical Gristle Tone Manipulator, Double DGTM, Gristle King, Jubilee, Kollmanation, Luxury Drive, Luxury Overdrive, Orateur, Parallel Dementia, Plexian Herb, Sweet POI Overdrive and Viscosilator, plus non-dirt effects.

**Archive treatment:** Preserve builder origin story, individual product names, artist collaborations, and chronology.

### Moody Sounds
Effects Database currently lists 9 products. It identifies Albin Roslund as the main one-person operator in Sweden, with occasional assistance from collaborators. Timeline entries document the sale of an early Baby Box Noise Machine and Moody Fuzz in October 2005, a move into pedal kits in 2006, and the first Moody Mushroom pedal in 2009.

Products include Baby Box / Noise Box, Carlin Compressor, Carlin Phaser, Carlin Ring Modulator, Hjärt Müller Bass Drive, Moody Tremolo, Moody Vibes, Mushroom Echo and Zeygot Fuzz.

**Archive treatment:** Strong candidate for preservation because it combines commercial pedals, kits, workshops, and small-scale European production.

### Spiral Electric FX
Effects Database currently lists 14 products and preserves interviews/videos involving Tom Cram. Dirt-oriented product names include Allora High-Gain Darlington Fuzz, Black Spiral Fuzz, Brute, Demhe Fuzz, Ge Fuzz, Private Reserve Premium Fuzz and Starbird Fuzzstortion. There are also collaborations such as the x Shoe Pedals Red Spiral.

**Archive treatment:** Separate original models from collaborations. Tom Cram's later association with DOD/DigiTech is a useful builder-career relationship, not proof that all Spiral products share a manufacturer.

### Buffalo FX
The Germanium Fuzz page documents at least three versions. V1 was a limited run of 50 units using Matsushita 2SB175 germanium transistors; later V2/V3 versions used other germanium transistor sources. This is a particularly good example of a boutique production-period distinction that matters historically without requiring a full schematic or BOM.

**Archive treatment:** Record V1/V2/V3 as production variants with the 50-unit V1 quantity only where the cited source supports it.

### RPS Effects
Effects Database currently lists 6 products and describes RPS Effects as a Pittsburgh boutique manufacturer producing original, hand-built designs. Current discovery names include Arcade Machine, Bit Reactor, Red Sun distortion, Tremulus Maximus, Vitamin C and Warp Drive.

**Archive treatment:** Builder page plus individual product records; preserve dates when discoverable.

### Spellcaster FX
Effects Database currently lists 3 products. Products include Hellmüt Fuzztortion, King Wizard Germanium Fuzz Driver and The Boostmaster. Recent media entries indicate an active Portland, Oregon hand-built operation.

**Archive treatment:** A good modern small-builder snapshot. Keep current status separate from historical/defunct status.

### Metal Pedals
Effects Database currently lists 24 products. Dirt records include American Overdrive, Amp Blaster, Big Rocker, Black Gravity, Blue Crunch, Bomb Shell, Demon Drive, Dirty Bitch, Fuzstortion, Green Box, Hard Core, Hard Core XXX, JH-3 Johnny Hiland, Mad Bull, Pro Country, Pushover, Raw Hide, Screaming Whore, Screaming Whore II, Snake Bite, The Rancher, Ultra-Boost and Ultra-Man.

**Archive treatment:** Preserve artist-signature and model/version relationships rather than flattening the catalog.

### Effects Pedal Boutique
Effects Database lists 8 products and identifies Dave and Joy Wireman as the builders in Watkinsville, Georgia. Dirt-relevant discovery names include Athana Signature Overdrive, Bluzking, Forrest Lee Jr. Double Overdrive, MIB Distortion Pro, SRB-808, SRB-808 Plus, SRB-808 Plus 2 in 1 and SRB-808 Plus Tri-Plex.

**Archive treatment:** Preserve the builders as named individuals where appropriate and document artist/signature relationships.

### MI Audio
Effects Database identifies Michael Ibrahim as owner/operator in Sydney, Australia. His timeline says he began making Tube Zone overdrive pedals in 1995, selling roughly 25 before the company was formally founded in 2002, and worked as a one-person operation from home until 2005. The company later expanded substantially.

**Archive treatment:** The 1995 pre-company period should be modeled as early builder history rather than as if the formal company existed from the beginning.

### Coopersonic
Effects Database documents hand-built pedals from Nottingham, including Valveslapper. The record describes high-voltage valve distortion and identifies the pedal as hand-built. This is a useful example of a small UK builder making a specialized gain product outside the usual Fuzz Face/Big Muff/Tone Bender canon.

**Archive treatment:** Preserve the builder and product even if the catalog remains small.

## Important historical micro-catalogs

### Sunda Fuzz Sound
Effects Database documents two Fuzz Sound versions associated with Gary Sunda and Sunda Amplifiers of Westminster, California. The source describes a 1966 two-knob Bakelite version with roughly 10 units and a 1967 single-knob metal version with probably fewer than 50 units. The page also includes Gary Sunda's later recollection that the design was his own. Because the evidence comes from a retrospective account, archive dates/quantities should retain the approximate wording and not be upgraded to exact census numbers without further corroboration.

**Archive priority:** VERY HIGH. This is exactly the kind of tiny American fuzz production that a broad catalog can preserve.

### Jersey Devil Pedal Company
Effects Database currently describes the company as founded in July 2025 by Sam Deo in Pequannock, New Jersey, with products including FP 826 Fuzz Blaster, Treblemaker and The Creature. The category counter currently shows zero products despite the profile text naming three models, which is a good example of why discovery and normalized counts must be kept separate.

**Archive treatment:** Record the named models as discovery leads, but retain the source-count discrepancy as a data-quality note rather than guessing.

## Additional current/modern preservation candidates

- Beetronics — Effects Database lists 16 products and describes the brand as designed in Brazil and hand-built in California.
- Electronic Audio Experiments — 21 products, including Dagger, Dude Incredible, Halberd, Longsword and collaborations with Mask Audio Electronics, Benson Amps, Obstructures and others.
- Origin Effects — 30 products, including several drive-family products such as Halcyon Blue, Halcyon Gold, Halcyon Green, DCX Boost and DCX Bass.
- Hamilton Effects — 7 products currently indexed, including Core Embers, Embers, Faded Embers, Faded Twin and Twin Boost.
- Daredevil Pedals — The Chicago Way is a numbered, signed artist collaboration from the first run, combining fuzz and overdrive in a single unit. This should be modeled as a collaboration/edition relationship rather than as an ordinary undifferentiated model.
- Lovepedal — Dragon Fuzz has multiple named edition variants including White Dragon, Black Dragon and Gold Dragon. These are candidates for separate edition records if the physical/product evidence supports distinct releases.

## Editorial rule reinforced by this harvest

A two-pedal builder is still historically important if those two pedals are otherwise undocumented. A 50-unit run is not "too small" for the archive. A product that existed only briefly can be more valuable to preserve than a current mass-market model with abundant documentation.

The archive should therefore preserve:

**builder existence + product name + approximate era + geographic context + source + relationship notes**

first, and only then add deeper production-period or specimen research.
