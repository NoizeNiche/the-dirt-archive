### Findings

- LIMMCO is now independently anchored as a New York music-industry distribution and wholesale company through a 1968 Billboard/NAMM exhibitor listing, complementing the later Bob Seidman oral-history research.
- Kimberly Buzz Box chronology remains the strongest LIMMCO production case: initial warehouse manufacture, subsequent outside production, then later Applied and UMI-linked states. The exact intermediate contract remains unresolved.
- Nomad remains a cross-supplier label. Banshee and Rogue are Applied-linked, Tone Booster is UMI-linked, while the early Super Fuzz remains an unresolved Mosrite/Fuzzrite-related case.
- Gracin's Fubar remains East Coast family evidence without a factory identification. Gracin's & Towne Wa & Volume remains separately Foxx-made.
- Goya Panther Box remains the strongest currently researched Goya/Applied model; weaker Goya product assignments are not promoted by association.
- The U.S.-Japan sourcing relationship associated with Bob Seidman is useful historical network context but is not sufficient to assign a Japanese factory to individual LIMMCO labels or pedals.
