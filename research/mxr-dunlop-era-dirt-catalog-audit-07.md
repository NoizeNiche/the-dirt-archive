# MXR / Jim Dunlop Era Dirt Catalog Audit 07

## Scope
Post-original-company MXR dirt products and the MXR-branded revival era. The archive keeps original MXR Innovations products separate from later Jim Dunlop designs, signature products, collaborations, limited editions, and special colorways.

## Historical transition
Jim Dunlop states that after MXR closed its doors, Dunlop acquired and resurrected the MXR brand. Dunlop identifies its first MXR releases as the Phase 90, Distortion+, Dyna Comp Compressor, and Blue Box Octave Fuzz. These are therefore revival/continuation products under later ownership, even where the retail names deliberately preserve classic MXR identities.

## Critical naming traps

### “'78” does not mean 1978
The M78 Custom Badass '78 Distortion uses 1978 as a stylistic/historical reference. Dunlop describes the product as a modern factory-modded pedal. It must therefore remain in the Dunlop-era catalog rather than being indexed as an original 1978 MXR model.

### Distortion III is not “the third original Distortion+”
Dunlop's own description says the M115 was developed from the ground up. It is a later design, not evidence of an uninterrupted three-generation original-company Distortion+ line.

### Signatures remain signatures
RR104/RR104C, YJM308, EVH5150 and WA44 carry artist relationships that matter historically. They should not be silently folded into a generic MXR dirt family merely because their control sets or product names resemble other MXR pedals.

### Custom Shop is a separate product corridor
Il Torino, Gran Torino, Shin-Juku, Il Diavolo, Timmy and Hybrid Fuzz are best modeled as Custom Shop / collaboration / later-era products, with distinct identities and evidence status.

## Current manufacturer/source observations
Dunlop's current MXR catalog and MAP listings continue to show classic M103/M104 alongside later M75/M77/M78 and collaboration/signature products. Current product pages explicitly identify some models as legacy/no longer in production, providing useful negative-current-state evidence even though those pages are not substitutes for historical first-sale documentation.

## Research conclusion
The MXR brand now needs to be modeled as multiple historical layers:

1. **Original MXR Innovations (1970s-1984)**
2. **Dunlop revival of classic MXR products**
3. **Dunlop-era original designs sold under MXR**
4. **Custom Shop / collaboration models**
5. **Artist signatures and special editions**

This layered structure prevents the current MXR catalog from being projected backward into the original company period.

## Public-archive boundary
No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries, cloning instructions, or circuit-reconstruction material are included. The archive records product identity, ownership era, model relationships, chronology, and evidence status only.
