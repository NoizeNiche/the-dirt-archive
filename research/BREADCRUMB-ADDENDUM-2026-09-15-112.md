# YARP Breadcrumb 112

## Purpose
Foundational Japanese and Italian builder pass, with explicit separation of true manufacturers from OEM/marketed identities.

## Cross-reference / dedupe decisions

- **Ace Tone / Ace Electronic Industries Inc.** is added as a genuine Japanese manufacturer lineage, distinct from later Roland/BOSS. Ikutaro Kakehashi founded Ace Tone in 1960 and later founded Roland in 1972; this corporate-personnel continuity does not justify collapsing the manufacturers into one entity.
- **Ace Tone FM-1, FM-2 and FM-3** are treated as distinct model generations within the same manufacturer, while Twin Ace FW-1 remains a separate fuzz/wah product.
- **Montarbo** is retained as research-only pending stronger independent archival confirmation of the Sinfhoton chronology and broader effects catalog.
- **Lyle Distortion Sustainer** is preserved as an Electro-Harmonix OEM/marketed identity for L.D. Heater Music Co., not as a separate manufacturer.
- **Electronic Sounds / UFO Fuzzman / Dallas Arbiter states** remain a Gary Hurst-associated marketed/design lineage with the Italian physical factory unresolved; no speculative JEN attribution is promoted.

## Evidence notes

Ace Tone is independently documented as an Osaka manufacturer founded in 1960, with effects production beginning by the late 1960s. Effects Database indexes the FM-1 and FM-3, while recent archival research on the FM-2 places its introduction as early as March 1968 and documents catalog presence through at least 1979. This materially strengthens Ace Tone as a foundational builder entry rather than a mere Roland precursor label.

Montarbo's Sinfhoton is documented by Effects Database as an Italian fuzz/distortion product introduced in 1968. The archive keeps it research-only because the chronology and catalog breadth currently rest too heavily on a single secondary database record.

The same historical research trail confirms Lyle Distortion Sustainer as an Electro-Harmonix OEM version of the Big Muff V2, reinforcing the archive's OEM relationship model. Electronic Sounds products remain a separate historical relationship problem because the Italian factory attribution is still uncertain.
