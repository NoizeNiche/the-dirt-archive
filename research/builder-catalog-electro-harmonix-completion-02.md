## Canonical builder identity

Canonical builder: Electro-Harmonix. Preserve distinct manufacturing/ownership eras and keep marketed collaborations or revived products linked rather than treating every later product as the same physical production state.

## Big Muff family treatment

The archive must treat Big Muff as a family with distinct historical/production states rather than one undifferentiated model. Candidate family branches include Triangle, Ram's Head, V2/late-'70s variants, Op-Amp, Russian/Sovtek, Green Russian, Bass, Nano, Deluxe, and documented artist or anniversary editions.

The public catalog should expose a family relationship while each historically meaningful production state receives its own generation/edition record when evidence supports it.

## Hot Tubes treatment

Separate the 1978 original Hot Tubes from the later reissue and from unrelated later tube/compact product states. Do not infer that all products sharing the Hot Tubes name are the same production line.

## Boundary and research rules

- Boosters/preamp products may be retained as historical boundaries but should not inflate the central dirt count automatically.
- Filter/glitch/digital products should only enter the dirt catalog when their historical identity materially belongs to the dirt story.
- Reissues, artist models and modern compact reproductions remain separate states.
- Component claims are recorded only when supported by historical evidence and useful for identification. No schematics, complete BOMs or circuit reconstruction are published.
