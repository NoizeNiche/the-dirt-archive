# Early Foundational Handoff 100

## Pass 52: 2019-2022 pandemic-era micro-builders, limited runs, and physical-generation archaeology

Pass 52 moves the archive through the 2020-2022 transition while deepening the distinction between first runs, limited editions, regular production and later streamlined generations. The research priority is the physical historical state of the pedal and the documentary clock attached to it.

### Newly documented or clarified

- **OBNE Alpha Haunt original**: 2019 coverage preserves the large original enclosure and unusually dense control layout, giving the first generation a strong visual identity.
- **OBNE Alpha Haunt 2021**: February 2021 reporting documents the streamlined, pedalboard-friendly redesign while retaining the broad control concept. The 2019 and 2021 states should not be collapsed.
- **Chase Bliss / ZVEX Bliss Factory**: November 10, 2020 announcement documentation records a limited 1,000-unit collaboration combining the Fuzz Factory concept with Chase Bliss control, presets and additional routing.
- **Mask Audio Electronics / Collector//Emitter Part Garden**: 2020 public documentation establishes a collaborative dual-fuzz production state built around stacked fuzz stages that could move into overdrive territory.
- **Spiral Electric FX Summer Cyan**: Tom Cram documents a ten-unit 2020 Black Spiral Fuzz limited edition released August 15, 2020, with distinct cyan/brushed-aluminum cosmetics.
- **Pettyjohn Rail Fuzz**: 2020 coverage identifies the Rail as the first release in Pettyjohn's Core Series and a fuzz product that intentionally traverses from overdrive into heavier fuzz.
- **Caroline CROM**: the pedal first appeared as a limited Black Friday 2021 release and became part of the regular lineup in 2022, creating a clean limited-edition-to-standard-production transition.
- **Summer School Electronics Gus-Drive**: official builder history documents a COVID-era origin, an initial 12-pedal run, and 13 incoming orders that turned the experiment into a business.
- **Summer School Electronics Gladys**: the builder's second pedal arrived on Black Friday 2021, documenting rapid first-year expansion.
- **Summer School 2022 expansion**: six additional models arrived in 2022, turning a two-pedal startup into a broader catalog and giving the archive a useful builder-growth boundary.
- **JHS 3 Series Fuzz**: the Fuzz was part of the original October 2, 2020 seven-pedal $99 launch rather than a later addition.
- **JHS 3 Series Screamer**: JHS dates the dedicated Screamer addition to April 2022, providing a later model-level boundary inside the 3 Series family.

### Key research findings

1. **The pandemic created new production histories, not just interruptions.** JHS's 2020 releases and Summer School's COVID-origin startup show that builders actively changed product strategy, workshop practice and launch methods during shutdown conditions.
2. **Limited-run scarcity became a documentary feature.** Bliss Factory's 1,000-unit run and Summer Cyan's ten-unit run show why quantity, colorway and release window may be part of a specimen's identity.
3. **A product can cross categories without losing a stable family identity.** Part Garden, Rail Fuzz and CROM illustrate how modern dirt builders deliberately live between fuzz, overdrive and distortion.
4. **Enclosure changes remain among the safest external generation clues.** Alpha Haunt demonstrates how a large first enclosure can be separated from a later streamlined version without relying on internal inspection.
5. **Release clocks are increasingly multi-stage.** Announcements, limited drops, Black Friday releases, regular-line adoption and later expansion should remain distinct events.
6. **Small builders leave unusually valuable startup evidence.** Summer School's initial twelve units and thirteen orders are the kind of concrete numbers that help reconstruct how modern micro-brands actually formed.
7. **The archive now needs more collaboration modeling.** Bliss Factory and Part Garden show that modern pedal lineage can cross builder entities, so collaborative authorship should remain distinguishable from sole-brand production.

### Evidence discipline

- Manufacturer-authored histories remain preferred for launch dates, version changes and production transitions.
- Contemporary specialist reporting is treated as announcement/public-availability evidence where primary records are incomplete.
- Limited editions, colorways, first runs, and regular production are distinct states.
- A documentary release date is not automatically converted into a factory-production start date.
- Collaboration records preserve both participating builder entities where the history materially depends on both.
- Cosmetic and enclosure distinctions are recorded only when supported by evidence.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries or cloning instructions are added.

### Current lineage position

The Dirt Archive now has a documented bridge from the boutique boom through the pandemic-era modern pedal ecosystem. Earlier boutique brands are entering a phase of rapid revision and lower-priced product tiers, while new micro-builders are launching in tiny batches from home workshops. The historical object is increasingly defined by release mechanics, limited-run status, enclosure generation, artwork, collaboration, and manufacturing scale as much as by the named model.
