# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 09

## Focus

This batch continues the Japanese OEM/export census by splitting the **FY-2** network from the better-known FY-6/Super Fuzz network and by upgrading several obscure FY-6 export labels from a single consolidated index into model-specific records. The goal is breadth-first catalog reconciliation, not specimen-level archaeology.

## Evidence rules applied

- A marketed label is not promoted to a physical manufacturer merely because a family relationship is strong.
- `INDEXED_RELATIONSHIP` means a model-level relationship is supported by a specialist/database record, while the physical OEM may still require stronger primary-source documentation.
- `DISCOVERY` is retained when the only available evidence is a consolidated family index, a seller description, or an isolated specimen with insufficient production-history context.
- Dates attached to individual specimens are treated as specimen evidence, not automatically as the complete production window.

## FY-2 network: current census upgrades

### Companion FY-2 Fuzz Box
Canonical marketed/house-brand node under the Shin-Ei lineage. Effects Database maintains a dedicated Companion FY-2 record and indexes it as the base family for Avora, J.H. Experience, JAX, Kimbara, Suzuki, Tele-Star, Tempo, Thomas and Zenta variants. A 2024 Tone Machines video indexed on the model page documents a **1970 Germanium Companion FY-2**, providing a useful early specimen corridor. Current Shin-ei also sells an explicit reproduction of the vintage Companion FY-2, so the modern revival state must remain separate from original production.

### J.H. Experience FY-2
Model-level attribution is now `INDEXED_RELATIONSHIP`. Effects Database explicitly describes it as a **1970s OEM edition of the Shin-Ei FY-2**, made in Japan, with the two-control Vol/Fuzz layout. This is a distinct Canadian-market marketed node, not evidence for a separate physical factory.

### Kimbara FY-2
Model-level attribution is now `INDEXED_RELATIONSHIP`. Effects Database lists the Kimbara FY-2 directly beneath the Shin-Ei Companion FY-2 family. A surviving dealer listing also describes a Kimbara FY-2 as late-1960s/early-1970s and notes an original box/manual, making the retail identity independently useful even though the exact factory contract remains tied to the broader OEM evidence trail rather than a surviving period manufacturer document.

### Suzuki FY-2
New obscure export node, retained as `DISCOVERY` pending stronger documentary attribution. Effects Database records a Suzuki-branded FY-2 sold in **Mexico during the 1970s**, with all-original Japanese parts claimed by the specimen owner. The page explicitly describes the unit as obscure and says there was little information on it, which makes it valuable for census breadth but not sufficient for a canonical factory claim.

### Tele-Star FY-2
New marketed/import node, retained as `DISCOVERY`. Effects Database's Tele-Star entity page explicitly lists **Tele-Star FY-2 Fuzz Box** alongside other Tele-Star effects and explains that Tele-Star distributed imported effects under its own label. This supports Tele-Star as an import/market identity, while the exact FY-2 factory remains unresolved in the current pass.

### Zenta FY-2
Model-level attribution is now `INDEXED_RELATIONSHIP`. Effects Database explicitly identifies the Zenta FY-2 as an OEM-branded version of the Shin-Ei Companion FY-2 made in Japan during the late 1960s/1970s. It is retained as a marketed/OEM relationship node, not a duplicate manufacturer.

### FY-2 family control and period caution
The accessible evidence shows that FY-2 was not one frozen transistor/controls state. Effects Database's current model index preserves 1970 Germanium and 1977 Silicon specimen references, while Guitar-Restos documents an early 1970 Shin-Ei Companion FY-2 with germanium transistors and a switched volume pot, contrasting it with later silicon versions that changed the faceplate/knobs and removed the switch on the volume potentiometer. These observations are sufficient to keep **generation/specimen state** separate from the umbrella FY-2 family. They are not being used here to publish circuit reconstruction details.

## FY-6/Super Fuzz network: model-level upgrades

### Avora FY-6 Super Fuzz
Effects Database has a dedicated model page identifying it as one of the Japanese-OEM-branded Super Fuzz variants and gives the full four-control layout: Expander/on-off, Balance, two-position Tone switch and footswitch. It is therefore upgraded from generic discovery to `INDEXED_RELATIONSHIP`, while the precise factory/date corridor remains model-specific and unresolved.

### Mica-Tone Fuzz Master
Effects Database has a dedicated model page and explicitly identifies Mica-Tone as a rebranded **FY-6 Super Fuzz** made by a Japanese OEM and distributed under different names. Upgraded to `INDEXED_RELATIONSHIP`.

### Oscar FY-6 Fuzz Master
Effects Database has a dedicated model page identifying the Oscar unit as part of the OEM-built Super Fuzz family, with the characteristic Expander/on-off, Balance, Tone switch and footswitch arrangement. Upgraded to `INDEXED_RELATIONSHIP`.

### Hohner MF-50 Master Fuzz Control
Effects Database now supplies a direct model page under **Hohner by Shin-Ei**, identifying the MF-50 as a Hohner-branded version of the Super Fuzz family. Tone Machines Blog further reports a Music Trades announcement in **March 1974**, narrowing the retail introduction corridor to late 1973 or early 1974 according to its research. This batch upgrades Hohner from discovery to `INDEXED_RELATIONSHIP`, but retains the 1974 date as a reported introduction window, not a factory-wide production start.

### Mayfair Model No. 9540 Fuzz Master
Current evidence remains mixed but materially stronger than a bare family index. A surviving Mayfair 9540 specimen is listed as **1970-72, Made in Japan**, and its seller describes the familiar Super Fuzz controls and a six-transistor silicon design. Because this is a single modern seller/specimen record rather than a period catalog or manufacturer document, the archive retains `DISCOVERY` for the relationship and uses the 1970-72 period only as specimen evidence.

### Mana FY-6 Fuzz Master
Tone Machines Blog reports a surviving **August 1972** Mana FY-6 specimen and places Mana in the Shin-Ei OEM run. This is strong specimen-level discovery evidence, but the blogger explicitly says little is known about the brand. The archive therefore records a `DISCOVERY` marketed/export entity with a dated specimen corridor instead of assuming a complete production history.

### Zenta FY-6 Fuzz Master
Still retained as `DISCOVERY` for FY-6 specifically. The consolidated FY-6 index lists the model, but the stronger model-specific page recovered in this pass concerns **Zenta FY-2**, not FY-6. This distinction prevents the FY-2 evidence from contaminating the FY-6 record.

### Clear Sound Super Fuzz
Dedicated Effects Database page exists, but the accessible page supplies little model-level historical detail beyond the family relationship. Retained as `DISCOVERY` pending a stronger period source or specimen trail.

### Factone Fuzz Machine
Tone Machines Blog identifies Factone as one of the rarer Super Fuzz examples and suggests it may have been a **Japan-only** affair, possibly involving remaining Honey deadstock after the 1969 bankruptcy. Because the deadstock interpretation is explicitly a hypothesis, the archive keeps Factone as `DISCOVERY` and records the deadstock idea only as an unresolved research note.

## Cross-family findings

1. **FY-2 must remain a separate family node from FY-6.** The accessible model indexes and specimen evidence clearly distinguish Companion FY-2 from Companion FY-6, and the OEM names overlap without implying identical product models.
2. **Shin-Ei's house-brand phase is historically important.** Tone Machines research places Companion's FY-6 introduction around 1970 and its transition to a "Shin-ei Companion" naming state in 1973, while current Shin-ei reproductions preserve the historical product names as separate modern revival products.
3. **Date-code evidence is more useful for production-state work than for blanket catalog dating.** Tone Machines' date-code research identifies March 1970 as its earliest documented Shin-ei date-stamp style, which supports careful separation of early Honey-era specimens from later Shin-ei production rather than retroactively dating every OEM label.
4. **The export network is geographically wider than the U.S. alone.** The Suzuki FY-2 specimen is documented as a Mexican-market example, while Tele-Star's entity record supports a U.S. import/distribution role. The archive should continue searching Canada, Mexico, Australia, Europe and regional distributor catalogs for additional Shin-Ei private-label names.

## New unresolved queues

- Period catalogs/ads for Avora FY-2, JAX FY-2, Kimbara FY-2, Tele-Star FY-2, Tempo TR-2 and Thomas FY-2.
- Period Hohner catalogs to corroborate the MF-50 introduction and clarify whether Hohner's Shin-Ei contract covered the same enclosure/control state throughout production.
- Primary or period documentation for Mana, Mayfair, Mica-Tone, Oscar, Clear Sound, Zenta FY-6 and Factone.
- Additional export-country labels that may sit outside the common U.S./UK collector databases.

## Scope result

This batch increases the **relationship breadth** of the Shin-Ei OEM census without artificially inflating physical-builder counts. FY-2 and FY-6 are now more cleanly separated, obscure marketed names are preserved as searchable nodes, and uncertainty is carried forward instead of being converted into false certainty.

No schematics, PCB layouts, BOMs, gutshots, or cloning instructions are included.
