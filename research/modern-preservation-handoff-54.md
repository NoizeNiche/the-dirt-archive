## Important historical findings
- Effects Database's own 20-year retrospective describes multiple waves of boutique builder growth, including the wave around 2007-2008, and emphasizes that many early small builders first existed through eBay and forums before gaining broader distribution.
- Effects Database's 2009 Winter NAMM census is especially rich, naming Sweet Honey Overdrive, Fuchs Noxious, Suhr Shiba Drive and Riot, ZVEX Distortron and Mastotron, Visual Sound Open Road, Voodoo Lab Sparkle Drive MOD and many others. Its Summer 2009 census adds Fender Sub-Lime Bass Fuzz and AboveGroundFX.
- The 2008 NAMM census captures the Danelectro Cool Cat series, DigiTech HardWire series, Mad Professor, Maxon, MI Audio and revived Way Huge line in one contemporary source. This is a useful snapshot of the broadening pedal market immediately before the 2010s explosion.
- The 2007 NAMM census documents the emergence of Mad Professor as a named series alongside Visual Sound and the expanding budget/mass-market dirt field.
- Xotic's BB Preamp has unusually clear dating: Effects Database published the model record September 26, 2005, while later Xotic material states the original BB Preamp first hit the market in 2005.
- A contemporary May 2005 OCD review documents an early Fulltone OCD production object. The archive preserves V1 as a specimen state rather than assigning an unsupported exact first serial boundary.
- Catalinbread states that the company was born in 2003 and that Super Chili Picoso was its first branded pedal, originally produced for ToneFactor. This is one of the strongest early-2000s builder chronology anchors.
- DigiTech's DF-7, announced at NAMM 2005, is retained because it demonstrates the important transition to a single object containing multiple modeled distortion/overdrive/fuzz voices.
- Early-2000s records remain mostly Research because surviving web evidence is much more fragmented than the later NAMM census. The archive therefore preserves model identity and lineage while avoiding false precision on launch dates.

## Data rule reinforced
The early-2000s layer uses a deliberate **era-window strategy** where exact launch years cannot be proven. A record can therefore carry a 2000s or 2005-era production window while retaining a null `introduced_year`. The archive should not manufacture exact dates merely to make the timeline look complete.

Foundational lineage records such as Fuzz Face, Tone Bender, Tube Screamer, RAT, Klon Centaur and Big Muff are retained only where they help explain the later boutique ecosystem. They are not being relabeled as 2000 launches.
