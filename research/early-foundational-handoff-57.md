## Major findings
- Gibson's current Maestro timeline dates the FZ-1 Fuzz-Tone to 1962, establishing a firm landmark before the British Tone Bender wave.
- Sola Sound Tone Bender MKI is preserved as a 1965 Gary Hurst product, with its early wedge enclosure treated as a specimen clue.
- The community term MK1.5 is preserved as a useful discriminator for the 1965-1966 rounded-enclosure two-transistor state, while acknowledging that the original case did not necessarily use the later numerical label.
- The Professional MKII becomes a distinct 1966 three-transistor production family and a documented OEM platform for Vox, Marshall and Rotosound.
- Marshall Supa Fuzz is explicitly split between Sola Sound-supplied 1966-1968 production and later Marshall manufacturing.
- MKIII and MKIV are linked as a related family but retained as separate external production states because enclosure and control presentation changed while forms overlapped.
- WEM Project V is preserved as a non-Tone-Bender British fuzz benchmark with a 1968-1969 evidence window and strong surviving advertisement/specimen documentation.
- EHX Muff Fuzz and Big Muff Pi are kept as separate product families/precursors. EHX/Mike Matthews documentation supports a 1969 Big Muff release claim with 1970 advertising evidence.

## Evidence rule
The archive should resist neat single-date mythology where surviving examples show overlap. Enclosure, control layout, branding, OEM relationship and dated advertisements are all valid identification fields, but none should be treated as an absolute serial cutoff without evidence.
