# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 22

## Focus
Early American OEM transition around Guild Foxey Lady, AUL Instruments, Manny's, and the pre-EHX Mike Matthews lineage.

## Evidence upgrades

### AUL Instruments
Effects Database documents an AUL Instruments fuzz made for Mike Matthews in 1967, during the effort to replace Mosrite production for Guild. Kit Rae's historical work likewise places AUL in Long Island City and identifies the AUL-built gray folded-metal Foxey Lady predecessor as a transitional production state before Electro-Harmonix production.

The important archive distinction is **manufacturer vs marketed brand**: AUL is retained as a physical manufacturing entity, while Guild remains the marketed brand and Mike Matthews remains the commercial/entrepreneurial relationship node. The surviving chronology is not treated as a single uninterrupted Foxey Lady build.

### Guild Foxey Lady
The Guild Foxey Lady is now explicitly split into at least three manufacturer states for future catalog and specimen work:
1. Mosrite-built Foxey Lady, derived from the Mosrite Fuzzrite and constrained by surviving date-code evidence to mid-1968 or later.
2. AUL Instruments-built Foxey Lady / early Matthews fuzz, associated with the gray folded-steel enclosure.
3. Electro-Harmonix-built Foxey Lady after Matthews formally launched EHX in late 1968.

This is a high-value lineage because the same retail name spans multiple physical manufacturers. It is exactly the kind of relationship the archive hierarchy is designed to preserve.

### Manny's / early Matthews fuzz
Tone Machines Blog research and surviving examples indicate that some AUL-built fuzzes resurfaced with Manny's stickers, consistent with Mike Matthews supplying Manny's Music as well as Guild. The archive keeps Manny's as a **marketed/distribution relationship candidate**, not as a canonical physical manufacturer.

### Historical caution
The AUL/Guild chronology contains conflicting recollections from Mike Matthews and later secondary accounts about which party first initiated the relationship. The archive records the manufacturing relationship with high confidence but leaves the precise commercial sequence open where the evidence conflicts.
