# The Dirt Archive — Discovery Harvest Phase 9E

## Purpose

This pass continues the discovery layer using Effects Database as a discovery backbone, prioritizing obscure vintage and OEM-linked fuzz records that are easy to lose. The goal is **existence preservation first**, not exhaustive technical reverse engineering. A discovery record may remain minimal until corroborated by stronger sources or surviving specimens.

## Source backbone

Effects Database currently exposes roughly 4,200 fuzz records, with the exact index count varying slightly as the site changes. Its fuzz index explicitly includes unknown-brand, unknown-country, dead-builder, and obscure entries, making it especially valuable for finding names that would otherwise disappear from the archive. citeturn239600search0turn997074search4

The Dirt Archive should treat those records as **discovery leads**, not automatically as canonical truth. Preserve the source URL and uncertainty, then promote only after normalization and corroboration.

## Harvest A: Lectrolab / Allied Fuzz Buzz

### Builder / brand
- Lectrolab
- Allied (marketing/distribution association)

### Model
- Fuzz Buzz

### Period
- Mid-1960s; documented examples have potentiometer date codes from 1966.
- A 1968 Radio-Electronics ad and 1968 Allied catalog are preserved in the Effects Database record.

### Historical significance
The Fuzz Buzz is an important early U.S. fuzz record because it shows how quickly the emerging stompbox market generated products closely derived from the Maestro FZ-1/FZ-1A concept. The surviving evidence is strong enough to preserve the model as a standalone historical record, while the relationship to Maestro should be described as derivative rather than as an OEM identity claim.

### Distinguishers worth recording
- Compact wedge-shaped enclosure
- Foot-controlled operation
- Single-battery operation
- Documented 1966 pot-date examples
- Three-transistor construction reported by EDB

### Research note
Do **not** reproduce the complete circuit or schematic. The identification value lies in the model, period, enclosure, branding, and documented relationship to the early Maestro design family.

### Sources

- Lectrolab historical page with 1966 pot-date example: https://lectrolab.wordpress.com/guitars-etc/lectrolab-fuzz-pedal-yes/

## Harvest B: Applied Audio Products OEM cluster

Applied Electronics / Applied Audio Products deserves a cluster in the discovery database rather than being treated as a single pedal entry. The surviving Effects Database material connects a number of obscure late-1960s U.S. branded effects to this manufacturing lineage.

### Applied Rogue Fuzz
- Brand: Applied
- Model: Rogue Fuzz
- Approx. period: 1967–1973, with one surviving owner's statement that the pictured example was purchased new in 1973.
- Country: USA
- Historical note: wedge-shaped cast-metal enclosure, also seen on the Hohner MF-50 Tri Dirty Booster, though EDB reports the internal design is different.

The exact corporate/OEM chain should remain cautiously worded. The safe archival formulation is that the pedal is **associated with Applied/Kadent/Nomad-era U.S. OEM production**, with exact relationships requiring specimen-level corroboration.

Source: Effects Database. citeturn997074search15

### Applied Banshee Fuzz
- Brand: Applied
- Model: Banshee Fuzz
- Variant/branding note: sometimes branded Nomad (by Applied), according to EDB.
- Approx. period: late 1960s–early 1970s.
- Historical significance: very rare obscure American fuzz with a distinctive slanted enclosure and close visual relationship to the Nomad Rogue family.

The current EDB description makes stronger construction claims than the archival evidence necessarily warrants. For The Dirt Archive, keep the public record conservative: **rare Applied/Nomad-associated fuzz; late-1960s/early-1970s; distinctive slanted enclosure; suspected relationship to the period's Shin-Ei-influenced fuzz market.** Avoid presenting a complete circuit description as settled fact.

Source: Effects Database. citeturn239600search4

### Lou Rose Combo-Fuzz
- Brand: Lou Rose
- Model: Combo-Fuzz
- Approx. period: around 1968
- Manufacturer attribution: Applied Audio Products Inc., according to EDB
- Place in history: an unusually useful named-store/OEM example because it links a retail/music-store brand to a period U.S. effects manufacturer.
- Functions: fuzz + treble boost

The record is especially valuable for demonstrating the archive's **brand ≠ builder/manufacturer** rule. Lou Rose should be a brand/store association; Applied Audio Products should be the manufacturer attribution, with the relationship marked according to evidence strength.

Source: Effects Database. citeturn997074search13

### Conrad Fuzzy Fury / Conrad cluster
Effects Database currently lists four Conrad effects, including Fuzzy Fury and Sky Hawk - Fuzz and Treble Booster. EDB identifies Conrad as a house brand distributed by David Wexler & Co. of Chicago and associates its late-1960s fuzz products with Applied Electronics. The exact OEM chain should stay separate from the brand record.

Known listed effects:
- Conrad Fuzzy Fury
- Conrad Sky Hawk - Fuzz and Treble Booster
- Conrad Buzz Box & Volume Expander
- Conrad Bass Hawk

The Fuzzy Fury has a preserved 1968 catalog image and is related in EDB's research tree to the Applied Big Fuzz / Goya Fury Box family. This is high-value discovery evidence even before the full manufacturer chain is resolved.

## Harvest C: Unknown / unresolved Japanese records

### Kurosawa? The Fuzz
- Working brand: `(unknown, Japan, Kurosawa?)`
- Model: The Fuzz
- Approx. period: late 1970s–1980s according to EDB
- Evidence marker: circuit-board marking reportedly says `Kurosawa` and `PRAT-7` on the documented specimen.
- Historical significance: rare Japanese Super-Fuzz-derived product with an unusual two-mode control concept.

This should **not** be promoted to a definite Kurosawa-manufactured record yet. Preserve the EDB attribution as a hypothesis linked to a specific surviving specimen. The archive should distinguish:
1. printed/observed specimen markings,
2. inferred brand/manufacturer,
3. later database attribution.

## Harvest D: Unknown British / international records

### Rangemaster Fuzzbug
Effects Database preserves an unresolved UK Fuzzbug record with separate Mk1.5 and MkII specimen groupings. The record is connected in EDB's relationship tree to the Gary Hurst Tone Bender MkI lineage, including RotoSound Fuzz Box MkI and Sola Sound Tone Bender MkI.

Discovery treatment:
- Brand/manufacturer: unresolved
- Model: Rangemaster Fuzzbug
- Country: UK
- Variants: Mk1.5 and MkII as **research labels**, not assumed factory nomenclature unless specimen documentation proves the names were contemporary.
- Controls: Volume, Sustain
- Historical value: potential bridge between early British fuzz/Tone Bender-era products and the later proliferation of Fuzzbug-branded units.

Source: Effects Database. citeturn997074search11

## Harvest F: Conrad / OEM architecture recommendation

The Conrad cluster reinforces a core structural rule for The Dirt Archive:

**Brand / seller / distributor / manufacturer must remain separate entities.**

A single historical pedal may legitimately have:
- a consumer-facing badge,
- a store or distributor association,
- an OEM manufacturer,
- a related enclosure family,
- and a circuit/family relationship to other models.

For example, the Lou Rose Combo-Fuzz and Conrad Fuzzy Fury are not best represented by collapsing everything under “Applied.” Instead:

`Brand -> Manufactured by -> Manufacturer`

and separately:

`Model -> Related to -> Family / other models`

This will prevent the archive from turning uncertain OEM history into false certainty as the database grows into the thousands.

## Archive philosophy reinforced by this pass

The most valuable records are not always the famous pedals. They are often the awkward little survivors: an unknown Japanese box, a store-branded fuzz, a distributor badge, a 1966 pot code, a weird enclosure shared across unrelated circuits, or a pedal whose manufacturer is still unresolved.

The Dirt Archive should preserve these records **before** certainty arrives. Uncertainty itself is historical information.

That means a record can legitimately say:

> Manufacturer unresolved; current attribution is provisional.

That is preferable to deleting the pedal from history because the paperwork has not survived.
