# Early Foundational Handoff 80

## Pass 32: HBE mid-2000s catalog spine, MI Audio prototype lineage, and D*A.M. late-2000s state evidence

Pass 32 continues the 1998-2010 boutique corridor while tightening the distinction between documentary presence, prototype history, commercial production and later retrospective descriptions. The emphasis is on records that can help identify surviving objects or explain how a builder's product family evolved.

### Newly documented

- **Homebrew Electronics Hematoma - Bass Overdrive**: February 22, 2006 specialist publication marker plus independent January 2006 period owner evidence establish a mid-2000s circulation window.
- **Homebrew Electronics Doomsday Device**: October 10, 2008 documentary marker for the Gary Holt signature overdrive product.
- **Homebrew Electronics Big D**: June 2005 period specimen evidence records the physical enclosure form and the pedal's broad OD/distortion control architecture.
- **Homebrew Electronics Dos Mos**: July 2005 period production evidence documents the dual-channel boost/preamp/buffer as a real HBE catalog object and useful scope-boundary companion.
- **MI Audio Blues Pro / Blues Prototype**: specialist historical material preserves a notable prototype-to-product story in which unused Blue Boy PCBs were repurposed into bare-aluminium Blues Prototype units sold at cost, followed by unexpectedly strong demand.
- **MI Audio Blues Pro**: the later 2006-2008 commercial family is now kept separate from the earlier prototype state.
- **D*A.M. Fuzzaround**: dated November 2008 demonstrations provide late-2000s specimen evidence independent of the October 2009 database publication marker.
- **D*A.M. Grease Box GB-83**: June 8, 2010 specialist documentation establishes a named version state and records David Main's own retrospective description of its short-lived sales run.
- **Black Arts Toneworks Revelation**: May 2010 manufacturer-era documentation establishes the SuperBass/SuperLead family as part of the same early heavy-dirt catalog snapshot as Pharaoh, LSTR, Black Forest and Ritual.
- **Black Arts Toneworks OATH**: chronology is intentionally held open because the available OATH-specific publication evidence is later than the May 2010 manufacturer snapshot; its relationship to the Revelation family is preserved without backdating.

### Key research findings

1. **HBE's catalog is becoming unusually well anchored in 2005-2008.** Period evidence now places Big D, Dos Mos and Hematoma in active circulation, while the UFO and Power Screamer already have independent period records. The archive can therefore treat HBE as a coherent boutique catalog rather than a collection of isolated names.
2. **Prototype history matters.** MI Audio's Blues Prototype is a particularly useful example of an unintended product lineage: an obsolete PCB investment became a low-cost experiment that turned into a sought-after model family. The archive separates the prototype state from later Blues Pro production rather than flattening them into one record.
3. **D*A.M. has another genuine generation-like state in Grease Box.** The GB-83 designation and David Main's own comments provide stronger version evidence than a generic later marketplace listing would, while still leaving production-volume questions open.
4. **Late documentary dates are not launch dates.** Fuzzaround demonstrations from November 2008 predate its 2009 specialist database publication, illustrating why the archive stores specimen evidence and publication evidence independently.
5. **Black Arts Toneworks' early catalog is broader than its flagship Pharaoh.** Revelation's SuperBass/SuperLead split gives the 2010 snapshot a meaningful amp-pusher family distinction, while OATH is deliberately held in a later context until earlier evidence surfaces.
6. **Boundary records remain useful.** Dos Mos is not counted as a primary dirt product, but documenting it preserves HBE's surrounding product ecosystem and helps explain the builder's contemporary catalog structure.

### Evidence discipline

- Specialist database publication dates remain publication markers unless independent period evidence establishes earlier production presence.
- Period owner reports are treated as specimen/circulation evidence, not factory release announcements.
- Prototype-to-product transitions are recorded as their own lineage state where the builder's history supports the distinction.
- Signature products remain distinct from general catalog families.
- Later retrospective pages do not override earlier documentary boundaries.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, full BOMs, gutshot libraries or cloning instructions are added.

### Current lineage position

The 2004-2010 boutique corridor now has a stronger multi-builder structure: D*A.M. demonstrates highly individualized small-run design and version states; HBE shows a dense working-musician-oriented catalog; MI Audio shows how prototypes can become commercially important products; and Black Arts Toneworks documents the formation of an explicitly heavy, fuzz-forward boutique identity.
