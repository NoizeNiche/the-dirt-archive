# Early Foundational Handoff 101

## Pass 53: 2022-2024 global boutique expansion, pandemic-born builders, and the return of the small-run artifact

Pass 53 advances the archive through 2022-2024 while deliberately widening the geographic lens. The central object remains the physical and historical state of the pedal, but the source strategy increasingly has to account for pandemic-era startups, limited editions, collaborations, artist brands, and regional builders whose first public documentation may be brief or fragile.

### Newly documented or clarified

- **KMA Chief Disruptor**: January 2022 announcement evidence establishes a new KMA Muff-style fuzz/distortion product with three gain modes and unusually extensive external tone shaping.
- **KMA Fuzzly Bear 2**: April 2022 coverage documents the return of KMA's first production pedal in a more pedalboard-friendly enclosure with top-mounted jacks and relay switching. The original graphics remain recognizable, making this a strong V1/V2 external boundary.
- **Heather Brown Electronicals Sensation Fuzzdrive**: May-July 2022 release evidence establishes a deliberate fuzz/overdrive hybrid from Heather Brown, with the maker explicitly resisting a single-category description.
- **Aclam The Woman Tone**: July 2022 launch documentation places the Spanish builder's Eric Clapton/Cream-inspired overdrive in a clearly dated modern production period, with switchable Woman Tone functionality and distinctive third-party artwork.
- **Messiah Dandelion and Quiggly**: October 27, 2022 coverage records these as Messiah Guitars' first two effects pedals, creating a clean builder-origin event with one fuzz/drive and one octafuzz product.
- **Messiah Billy**: January 26, 2023 coverage identifies Billy as the builder's first overdrive pedal, separating it from the earlier Dandelion fuzz/drive.
- **Balam FX Itzamna**: a 2022 Mexican-focused profile documents a pandemic-era independent fuzz maker whose lockdown experience led to online pedal production. Exact first-sale dating remains intentionally provisional.
- **GalahcoreFX**: an Australian builder profile dates the official builder launch to May 7, 2022 after COVID-lockdown experimentation. This is retained as builder chronology until stronger individual dirt-product clocks are recovered.
- **Sabbadius Toro Argentino Overdrive**: the maker documents a 2022 Argentina-themed limited edition with only 20 handmade units produced in Córdoba. This is a high-value edition-level record because quantity and event tie-in are explicit.
- **Neural Devices Eager Fuzz V2**: a Mexican builder overview lists Eager Fuzz V2 among Neural Devices' 2022 releases, preserving the revised generation separately from the earlier Eager Fuzz state.
- **Neural Devices Gentlemen Analog Gain Stage**: the same 2023 overview lists this dedicated drive model among the builder's 2022 releases, expanding the Mexican dirt catalog with a documented production-year anchor.
- **Krozz Devices Royalbreaker Distortion**: the Brazilian builder's official history dates Royalbreaker to 2024 and frames it as part of the company's continuing independent expansion.
- **Third Man Hardware Fuzz-a-Tron**: January 2024 NAMM coverage documents the $75 build-at-home fuzz kit. It remains a scope-boundary event showing how boutique/DIY culture had become part of an artist-brand's mainstream product strategy.

### Key research findings

1. **The geography of boutique dirt is broadening in the archive.** Mexico, Argentina, Brazil, Spain and Australia now join the earlier US/UK/Germany/Canada/Japan/Indonesia network with documented 2022-2024 events.
2. **Pandemic-era builder formation continues to surface as a distinct historical phenomenon.** Balam FX and GalahcoreFX show how lockdowns created time and impetus for new one-person effects businesses.
3. **Modern production histories often depend on edition mechanics.** Sabbadius' 20-unit Toro Argentino is a clear specimen-level artifact rather than a generic model-year record.
4. **V1/V2 boundaries are increasingly recoverable from the outside.** KMA's Fuzzly Bear 2 demonstrates how enclosure size, jack placement and switching method can distinguish generations without internal documentation.
5. **Builder first-product chronology and model chronology should remain separate.** Messiah's Dandelion/Quiggly/Billy sequence and GalahcoreFX's builder launch show why company-origin events should not be mistaken for first sales of every subsequent model.
6. **Category boundaries continue to blur.** Sensation Fuzzdrive, Dandelion, Itzamna and Chief Disruptor reinforce the archive's practice of preserving maker language alongside archive classification.
7. **Mainstream artist brands increasingly recycle boutique production ideas.** Third Man's Fuzz-a-Tron provides a useful boundary between boutique identity and mass-market/DIY-kit strategy.

### Evidence discipline

- Manufacturer and builder-authored pages remain the preferred evidence for model dates, generation changes and production quantities.
- Trade coverage is retained as public announcement or availability evidence when first-party chronology is incomplete.
- Exact launch days are not invented from later product pages or retailer listings.
- Limited runs, first production batches, later standard production and builder-origin events remain distinct historical states.
- Regional builder coverage is expanded without lowering confidence standards; provisional dates stay provisional.
- Scope-boundary products are retained only when they illuminate the broader dirt/pedal-production ecosystem.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries or cloning instructions are added.

### Current lineage position

The archive now reaches from classic boutique maturation into a genuinely global pandemic-era micro-builder landscape. KMA demonstrates how an early boutique classic can be rehoused and refreshed without losing its graphic identity; Heather Brown and Messiah blur category boundaries; Aclam and Sabbadius give the archive strong Spain/Argentina edition evidence; Balam FX and GalahcoreFX capture lockdown-born builders; Neural Devices extends the Mexican digital/analog boutique story; and Krozz adds a documented Brazilian 2024 expansion.
