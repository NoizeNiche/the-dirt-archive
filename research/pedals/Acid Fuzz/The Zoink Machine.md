# Acid Fuzz — The Zoink Machine

## PRP identity
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz
- **Identity:** A compact Acid Fuzz recreation of the 1960s John Hornby Skewes Zonk Machine, developed at the request of Doyle Bramhall II.

## What this pedal is
The Zoink Machine is a smaller, pedalboard-friendly Zonk Machine clone. Its core circuit uses a trio of NOS 1960s germanium transistors and is deliberately tuned for a clear, articulate fuzz that keeps individual notes audible in chords and fast lead playing. [1][2]

## Colorways
- The archive does not currently have enough reliable documentation to establish a complete factory colorway list.
- Surviving examples appear in different finishes, but finish alone is not being treated as a verified production variant.

## Versions / options
- **Standard Zoink:** original Acid Fuzz Zonk-style voicing.
- **DBII tuning:** a documented Doyle Bramhall II-oriented tuning that is more gated and pinches the note more. It cleans up less from the guitar volume control than the standard tuning. [3]
- Three-position tone/voicing switch:
  - Left: added mids.
  - Middle: original Zonk Machine voicing.
  - Right: approximately full-frequency guitar voicing. [1]
- True bypass, LED, 9V jack, compact enclosure.
- Typical transistor sets use one NOS Texas Instruments TI A02, 2G374 or 2N1379 and two black-glass Mullard OC75 or OC71 devices. [1][2]

## Version changes
The core Zoink design stays tied to the Zonk Machine circuit, while the documented DBII tuning changes the feel and cleanup response for Doyle Bramhall II. The tone switch also broadens the usable range beyond the very bright original-style middle position. [1][3]

## Transistor
- **Type:** Germanium.
- **Typical documented sets:** one NOS Texas Instruments TI A02, 2G374 or 2N1379, plus two Mullard OC75 or OC71 devices. [1][2]
- Acid Fuzz specifically notes that it has used the original Texas Instruments TI A02 type found in original Zonk Machines. [1]

## Diode
- Separate clipping diode: **Not publicly documented.**

## Sound
The Zoink is tight, articulate and unusually good at keeping chords intelligible for a vintage-style germanium fuzz. The standard voicing is bright and cutting, while the tone switch can add mids or open the frequency range; the DBII tuning pushes the response toward a more gated, pinched-note character. [1][3]
