# Analog Man — Astro Tone

## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** Astro Tone

## What this pedal is
Astro Tone is Analog Man's modern interpretation of the rare 1960s Sam Ash Fuzzz Boxx/Astro-Tone family. Mike Piera says the earliest prototype was too thin, so Analog Man retained the basic historical character while redesigning the circuit to restore usable low end and tone control; more than 3,000 were made with the original Fairchild silicon transistors before a later transistor source change. [1][2]

## Colorways
- Standard examples include the original production finish.
- A **black version with gold graphics** was documented by Analog Man beginning in late summer 2011.
- The black version is a cosmetic edition, not a separate circuit/version. [1]

## Versions and factory production changes
### Astro Tone — original production
- Volume
- Fuzz
- Tone
- true bypass
- bright LED
- top-mounted input/output jacks
- standard negative-ground power jack
- original-type silicon transistors [1]

### Later transistor production change
- Analog Man states that in **2023** it began using newer transistors of the same general type/shape after the original Fairchild supply was exhausted.
- This is documented as a **component-generation production change**, not a named V2. The archive does not invent a new version number.
- The pedal's intended silicon-fuzz identity remains the same. [1]

## Version changes
The most important historical changes are the redesign that fixed the original prototype's low end and the later 2023 transistor-source change. The black/gold run is recorded as a cosmetic production option only and does not become its own variant page. [1]

## Transistor
- **Technology:** Silicon.

- **2023 onward:** newer silicon transistors of the same general type/shape, after the original Fairchild supply was depleted.
- Exact later part number is not published on the current product page. [1]

## Sound
Astro Tone is a relatively open, less-compressed silicon fuzz that sits between a classic fuzz and a distortion rather than producing an ultra-thick Fuzz Face-style wall. Analog Man specifically describes it as clear and dynamic, with enough low-end correction and tone control to make the vintage concept more practical while retaining a raw, responsive character. [1][3]
