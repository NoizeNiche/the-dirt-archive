# Analog Man — King Of Tone

## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** King Of Tone
- **Builder:** Analog Man
- **Catalog types:** Overdrive / Distortion
- **Introduction:** 2003-era development; Ver4 production developed in late 2005 and followed as the long-running standard architecture.
- **Identity:** One parent record containing the builder's documented Ver2, Ver3 and Ver4 generations. Ver4 is the current standard product, but the older revisions remain part of the same King Of Tone lineage rather than becoming unrelated pedals.

## What this pedal is
King Of Tone began with Analog Man's work modifying the Bluesbreaker used by Jim Weider, but the resulting pedal was extensively redesigned. Ver4 was developed in late 2005 after requests for independent control of the two sides; the modern pedal is effectively two independently adjustable overdrive circuits in one enclosure, each with OD/Clean/Distortion mode options selected by internal DIP switches. [1][2]

- The standard enclosure has remained visually consistent across the major revisions.

## Versions
### Ver2
- Earlier King Of Tone production generation.
- Preserved here as a documented historical revision within the parent record.
- Analog Man provides separate Ver2 information because its internal architecture/control details differ from later production. [1]

### Ver3
- Later revision preceding the present Ver4.
- Preserved as a historical production generation rather than silently rewritten into Ver4. [1]

### Ver4
- Developed in late 2005 and became the modern standard production design.
- Two independent overdrive circuits.
- Three modes per side: OD, Clean, Distortion.
- Internal DIP switches for gain/voicing options.
- External four-knob control per side.
- 9V battery or 9–18V power options. [1][2]

## Factory options / modifications
Analog Man offers builder-selected options on King Of Tone units, including higher-gain configurations and trim/control options depending on the order/version. These are factory configurations, not aftermarket modifications, and should remain subordinate to the King Of Tone parent record. [1]

## Version changes
The major documented revision path is Ver2 → Ver3 → Ver4, with Ver4 adding the two-channel independent-control architecture that defines the current pedal. The archive will keep the builder's version names rather than creating new parent pedals for each revision. [1]

## Transistor
- **Type:** No discrete transistor technology is clearly specified as a defining King Of Tone component in the builder material reviewed.
- Do not infer a germanium/silicon transistor designation from the Bluesbreaker lineage.

## Diode
- Analog Man describes the pedal as using JRC op-amp technology and "more open" diode clipping in its documentation, but the exact diode part/type is not established in the reviewed builder material.
- **Archive status:** general diode clipping documented; exact diode type unknown. [1]

## Sound
King Of Tone ranges from fairly transparent overdrive and clean boosting to thicker, more compressed distortion, with the two sides allowing different gain/voicing settings within one pedal. The three-way mode structure and internal options make the pedal unusually flexible for low- to medium-gain rock, blues and amp-pushing applications. [1][2]
