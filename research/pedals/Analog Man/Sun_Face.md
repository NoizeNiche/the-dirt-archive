# Analog Man — Sun Face

## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** Sun Face
- **Builder:** Analog Man
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz
- **Introduction:** early 2000s; the Sun Face became its own named Analog Man pedal around 2002.
- **Identity:** One parent with many builder-supplied transistor and hardware options. The archive does not split each transistor option into a separate parent pedal.

## What this pedal is
Sun Face is Analog Man's long-running germanium/silicon fuzz platform, with the exact character intentionally varied by transistor selection and optional bias controls. Analog Man documents numerous factory transistor choices and has changed the enclosure size and jack layout over the years while maintaining the Sun Face identity. [1][2]

## Colorways and hardware periods
- Enclosure size changed in the mid/late 2000s.
- A smaller enclosure/top-jack arrangement became part of later production; optional power jacks and other hardware configurations have also been offered.
- These physical changes are production/hardware periods, not automatically separate versions.
- Cosmetic finish differences do not create variants unless a functional distinction is documented.

## Factory transistor variants
Analog Man has documented multiple factory transistor choices over the Sun Face's history, including:
- NKT-275 and other germanium options
- Russian germanium options
- 2SB171 / 2SB175 germanium
- GE 2N germanium
- BART germanium options
- BC108 / BC109 / BC183 and other silicon options

These are builder-selected factory builds within the Sun Face parent identity, not separate pedals. Exact availability changes over time, and not every option was continuously offered. [1]

## Versions / factory options
### Sun Face — standard platform
- Volume
- Fuzz
- internal bias trim
- true bypass
- germanium or silicon transistor options depending on ordered build

### Sun Dial option
The external **Sun Dial** exposes the same bias function that exists as an internal trim. It is a factory option/add-on and not a separate circuit or separate parent pedal. [1]

### Custom builder configurations
Analog Man has built custom Sun Face units around specific transistor choices and artist requests. These remain factory/custom-builder configurations, but the archive should only create a separately named variant where Analog Man documents a meaningful distinct identity.

## Version changes
The documented Sun Face history is best represented as hardware and transistor-production periods rather than invented V1/V2 labels. Enclosure size, jack placement, optional power hardware, and transistor availability changed over the years, while the parent Sun Face identity remained continuous. [1][2]

## Transistor
- **Technology:** Germanium and Silicon factory options, depending on build.
- Exact transistor identity is a deliberate part of many Sun Face builds and should be preserved when the builder documents the specific unit.
- Because the available options changed frequently, the archive should list the specific transistor as a factory build option rather than claiming one universal Sun Face transistor. [1]

## Diode

## Sound
Sun Face spans a wide range because Analog Man deliberately selects transistors for different degrees of gain, compression, cleanup and sputter. Germanium builds can be warm, touch-sensitive and highly interactive with the guitar volume, while silicon options tend toward more stable, brighter and more aggressive fuzz behavior; the exact result is build-dependent. [1][2]

### Verified version references

### Verified transistor/device terms
- BC109B.

### Verified sound evidence
This trim pot acts just like turning down the VOLUME knob on your guitar, to clean up the fuzz.
It can also be used to kill Radio Frequency Interference, which may be a problem on high gain pedals in some areas, by turning it down a hair.
