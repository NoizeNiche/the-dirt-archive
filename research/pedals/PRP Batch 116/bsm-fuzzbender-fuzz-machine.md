# BSM — FuzzBender Fuzz Machine

## PRP identity
- **Builder:** BSM
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz
- **Identity:** Handmade germanium fuzz combining switchable Fuzz Face and Tone Bender-style voices.

## What this pedal is
The BSM FuzzBender is a handmade germanium fuzz designed to move between two classic British fuzz voices. BSM describes the left switch position as a warm, fat Fuzz Face-style sound and the right position as a tighter, more aggressive Vox/Sola Sound Tone Bender Mk1.5-style sound. [1][2]

## Colorways
No single factory colorway history was reliably established from the surviving manufacturer documentation.

## Versions and factory options
- Volume control
- Fuzz control
- Lever switch selecting the two circuit characters
- 9V battery operation
- Approx. 600 µA current consumption
- Positive battery terminal tied to ground
- Average output level documented as -3 dBm
- Maximum output voltage documented as 1 V with hard picking [1]

A later Japanese distribution listing documents a **FuzzBender → FaßBender** model-name change and explicitly states that the contents were unchanged. The archive therefore treats FaßBender as a documented naming successor, not evidence of a circuit revision. [3]

## Version changes
No numbered factory hardware revision was verified. The documented name change to **FaßBender** did not change the internal design according to the Japanese distributor notice. [3]

## Transistor
- **Technology:** Germanium.
- **Exact transistor/device:** Not publicly documented in the manufacturer product description. Independent Bonedo coverage also identifies the unit as using selected germanium transistors. [1][4]

## Diode

## Sound
In Fuzz Face mode, the FuzzBender emphasizes a warm, fat low end and responds strongly to guitar volume, making it suitable for softer vintage fuzz textures and Hendrix-style cleanup. In Tone Bender mode, the sound becomes leaner in the lows with more aggressive upper frequencies, giving the pedal a more cutting mid-60s British fuzz character. [1][2][4]

### Verified sound evidence
As an OEM-product, Sola Sound built Hurst's circuit, slightly modified and in a large production number, now called Tone Bender (Mk1.5) for the fellow British company Vox.
Once developed and done, Sola Sound offered a minor number of the Tone Bender circuit under their own flag and brand.
In fall 1966 Arbiter, the giant in the British music business, introduced a Fuzz unit under the name Fuzz Face, which looked unique, but was actually just a modification of the Vox/Sola Sound (Mk1.5).
