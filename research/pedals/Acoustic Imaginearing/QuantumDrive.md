# Acoustic Imaginearing — QuantumDrive

## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** QuantumDrive
- **Builder:** Acoustic Imaginearing
- **Catalog type:** Distortion / Overdrive
- **Identity:** Dual-channel overdrive/distortion pedal built around the maker's claimed quantum-tunneling process.

## What this pedal is
QuantumDrive combines independent Overdrive and Quantum Drive channels that can be used separately or chained together. Acoustic Imaginearing describes the Quantum Drive side as using quantum-mechanical tunneling and a negative-resistance region as the basis for its distortion process, while the Overdrive side covers subtle sweetening through TS-style drive and heavy crunch. [1][2]

## Colorways
- The documented production presentation is a black enclosure with space-image artwork and silver controls.
- A separate “Champagne” 10th Anniversary edition was also produced. [1]

## Versions and factory options
### Original QuantumDrive
- Independent O.D. and Q.Drive channels
- Gain, Tone, O.D. and Q.Drive controls
- True bypass
- Designed for guitar and bass [1][2]

### 10th Anniversary Quantum Drive
- “Champagne” edition
- OverDrive Wave Shaper
- Quantum Position
- 3-Mode Tone Selector
- Limited edition of 7 units [1]

## Version changes
The 10th Anniversary edition added the Wave Shaper, Quantum Position and three-mode tone selector and was explicitly limited to seven units. It is documented as a named special edition rather than a completely new product. [1]

## Transistor
- **Exact transistor/device:** Not publicly documented in the sources reviewed.

## Diode
- Acoustic Imaginearing specifically contrasts its claimed tunneling process with standard diode distortion, but it does not publicly document a production clipping diode type for the pedal. [2]

## Sound
The Overdrive channel is described as handling light enhancement, TS-like drive and heavier crunch while retaining playing dynamics. The Quantum Drive channel is voiced as a more extreme distortion with strong harmonic content, and the maker says the pedal can retain clarity on complex chords even at higher distortion settings. [1][2]
