## What this pedal is
The **DOD FX50-B Overdrive Plus** is an analog overdrive designed to produce a range from mild overdrive to thicker, more sustained distortion. The surviving DOD manual describes a three-control interface built around **LEVEL, TONE, and DRIVE**, with the circuit intended to emulate the natural clipping character of a tube-type amplifier. [1][2]

The controls are:
- **LEVEL:** overall output volume.
- **TONE:** shifts the balance of low and high frequencies, with clockwise movement emphasizing treble and counter-clockwise movement emphasizing bass.
- **DRIVE:** controls the amount of gain/distortion and increases sustain as it is advanced. [1]

The unit uses a footswitch and LED indicator. Secondary reproductions of the DOD documentation describe **FET switching** for clickless effect insertion and standard 9V battery/external-adapter operation. [1]

## Historical identity
A DOD brochure reproduced by Effects Database dates the FX50-B to the **1990** FX-series period and describes it as a classic overdrive intended to work across amplifier volumes. [2]

The current DigiTech/DOD manual index still lists an **FX50B** manual, confirming the model remains part of the manufacturer's preserved documentation archive even though the pedal is a historical FX-series product. [3]

## Controls and operating behavior
DOD's manual guidance starts with LEVEL: match the engaged effect volume to the bypassed signal, then set DRIVE for the desired amount of distortion and use TONE to shape the frequency balance. [1]

This makes the FX50-B notably different from later DOD high-gain designs in the same broad family. Its documented purpose is conventional overdrive range and sustain rather than a specialized, heavily voiced distortion architecture. The archive retains **Overdrive** as the catalog classification.

## Circuit and design notes
The reviewed sources establish the tube-emulation design goal but do not establish a complete first-party schematic or production BOM for every FX50-B revision.

A 2007-era DIY Stompboxes discussion documents attempts to trace and modify the FX50 circuit, demonstrating that schematics and board-level information circulate in the repair/modding community. Because those sources are user-traced rather than DOD factory documents, the archive does not convert individual component values into universal production specifications. [4]
