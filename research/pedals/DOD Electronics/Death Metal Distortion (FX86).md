# DOD Electronics — Death Metal Distortion (FX86)

## Surface catalog record
- **Builder:** DOD Electronics
- **Pedal:** Death Metal Distortion (FX86)
- **Catalog type:** Distortion
- **Research level:** Deep
- **Deep research status:** Verified
- **Identity basis:** First-party DOD/DigiTech documentation confirms the FX86 Death Metal Distortion in the DOD FX Series. The original FX86 is documented separately from the later FX86B revision.

## What this pedal is
The **DOD FX86 Death Metal Distortion** is a fixed-gain, high-gain distortion pedal built around a four-control interface: **R.I.P., GUTS, PAIN, and SCREAM**. The DOD owner-manual material explains that the circuit has no conventional distortion/gain control. Instead, the distortion stage is fixed at a very high setting and the front-panel controls shape output level and the three main EQ bands. [1][2]

The four controls are:
- **R.I.P. (LEVEL):** sets the effect's output level relative to the original signal.
- **GUTS (LOW):** controls low-frequency content.
- **PAIN (MID):** controls midrange content.
- **SCREAM (HIGH):** controls high-frequency content. [1][2]

The manual describes the FX86 as capable of oscillation when R.I.P. is turned up and explicitly markets it toward death-metal and grindcore sounds. The DOD documentation also suggests lower guitar/bass tunings as part of the intended use case. [1]

## Historical identity
The original **FX86 Death Metal** is distinct from the later **FX86B Death Metal Distortion**. JHS Pedals' history of the DOD "Lamb Series" places the original FX86 release in **1994**. Contemporary/historical catalog sources likewise identify the FX86 as a DOD FX-series pedal from the mid-1990s. [3][4]

The later FX86B is documented as a subsequent model. A product-history source identifies the FX86B as replacing the original FX86 in **1998**, while contemporary manual material refers to the later FX86B by that designation. [3][5]

The archive therefore should not merge FX86 and FX86B into a single hardware identity. They share the same basic four-control concept and family name, but the suffix is meaningful historical/version evidence.

## Controls and operation
DOD's documentation describes the design as follows:
- **R.I.P.** matches the distortion level against the source signal by changing the effect output. At high settings it can also participate in oscillation behavior.
- **GUTS** increases or decreases low-frequency content.
- **PAIN** changes the midrange. Reducing PAIN makes the low and high controls more dominant in the resulting voicing.
- **SCREAM** increases or decreases high-frequency content and can emphasize pick attack. [1][2]

This architecture explains an important characteristic of the original FX86: the user is primarily **sculpting the frequency balance and output of a fixed high-gain distortion stage**, not dialing a conventional gain knob from clean to saturated.

## Historical design/context
The FX86 was part of a DOD period that included unusual, strongly themed dirt pedals. JHS Pedals identifies it as the third pedal in the DOD Lamb Series and describes its design in the context of the mid-1990s death-metal/grindcore market. [3]

The original manual's named artist references include **Napalm Death, Carcass, Brutal Truth, and Pungent Stench** as sonic/genre associations used by DOD in the product copy. Those are marketing references from the manual, not evidence that every named act officially endorsed or used the pedal. [1]

## Circuit information
The reviewed sources support describing the FX86 as an analog fixed-gain distortion, but they do not provide a complete factory schematic in the first-party documentation reviewed here.

Audiofanzine's technical reference reports **two 4558-type op-amps** and provides board/schematic references for the FX86, while a DIY/audio hardware discussion documents a partial 1994 FX86 schematic and board photographs. These are useful technical leads, but the archive does not promote an exact semiconductor BOM as universal across every FX86 unless tied to a specific board revision. [4][6]

## Variants and revisions
### FX86 Death Metal
- Original model.
- Dated to **1994** by historical DOD/Lamb-Series references.
- Four controls: R.I.P., GUTS, PAIN, SCREAM.
- Fixed-gain distortion architecture with no user gain control. [3][4]

### FX86B Death Metal Distortion
- Later successor/revision.
- Historical sources place its introduction in **1998**.
- The four-control layout remains R.I.P., GUTS, PAIN, SCREAM.
- The later manual explicitly calls the unit FX86B. [3][5]

The archive keeps the un-suffixed **FX86** record tied to the original identity rather than silently importing FX86B-specific construction or production facts.

## Manufacturing / physical evidence
A surviving 1994 FX86 example is documented with **Made in U.S.A.** packaging and is identified as a first-version FX86. This supports USA-made examples for the original production period, but the archive does not infer that every later FX86-family unit had identical manufacturing provenance. [7]

The DOD manual documents a rugged die-cast metal case, LED status indicator, battery access, AC-adapter operation, and a rubber non-skid bottom for the FX86B-era hardware. Those physical details are not automatically back-projected onto every original FX86 unit without model-specific confirmation. [2]

## Sonic behavior
The DOD manual describes the FX86 as an extreme high-gain distortion whose core identity is controlled with the three-band EQ. The low, mid, and high controls allow strongly different voicings while the fixed high-gain stage remains the foundation. [1]

Historical secondary references characterize the pedal as unusually aggressive and specialized, with the lack of a gain control being a defining part of its design. Those descriptions are historical/critical observations rather than an objective measurement of "best" or "worst" performance. [4]
