## What this pedal is
The **DOD FX59 Thrash Master** is a late-1980s/early-1990s DOD distortion pedal built around three front-panel controls: **Level, Presence, and Distortion**. DOD's manual presents it as an intentionally aggressive, high-distortion effect with a sharp, heavy voicing. [1]

The control functions are:
- **LEVEL:** sets output level.
- **PRESENCE:** shapes the upper-frequency character and attack.
- **DISTORTION:** controls the amount of distortion. [1]

The manual also documents FET switching and LED status indication, along with battery and DOD AC-adapter operation. A surviving DOD manual copy is copyright **1990** and explicitly states **Printed in USA / Made in U.S.A.** [1]

## Historical introduction and production
Independent exact-model research places the FX59's introduction in **early 1990**, alongside several other DOD distortion pedals from the same generation. Serial-number research suggests that a small number of units may date to late **1989**, making 1990 the safer documented launch-era year. [2]

The same research estimates that production probably ended in **late 1994**, with some examples possibly sold into early 1995. That is serial-based historical inference rather than a DOD-announced discontinuation date, so it is recorded as approximate. [2]

## Circuit and technical evidence
The FX59 shares its basic circuit-board platform with the **DOD FX56 American Metal**, but uses different component values and voicing choices. Secondary board research documents multiple IC configurations across surviving examples:
- Early examples reported with **three LM1458N** op-amps.
- Later observed examples reported with **one TL022CP plus two LM1458N** devices. [2]

This is useful revision evidence, but it does not establish that every FX59 ever produced used exactly one of those configurations. The archive therefore treats the two chip sets as **observed production examples**, not a universal BOM.

The FX59's relationship to the FX56 and FX58 is also useful in the DOD historical lineage. The models share design-era and board-family relationships without being interchangeable catalog identities. [2]

## Design and sonic context
DOD's marketing copy framed the FX59 for extremely heavy playing and "thrash" styles, emphasizing overwhelming distortion and an edgy tone. This is manufacturer-era marketing language, not an objective measurement of the pedal's sound. [1]

Independent user documentation describes the Presence control as strongly affecting the upper register and attack. Those observations should be read as player reports rather than laboratory frequency measurements. [2]
