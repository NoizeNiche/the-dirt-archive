Dod Overdrive Preamp 250 X Review: A Deep Dive Into Its Circuit Design, Tone Stack, and Studio Integration | GearStrings GEARSTRINGS Home Blog Categories Authors About Home / Blog / gear reviews gear reviews Dod Overdrive Preamp 250 X Review: A Deep Dive Into Its Circuit Design, Tone Stack, and Studio Integration By Marcus Reeve July 19, 2026 Introduction: What the Dod Overdrive Preamp 250 X Actually Is The Dod Overdrive Preamp 250 X is not a pedal—it’s a rack-mounted, transformer-coupled, discrete-JFET preamplifier designed for studio and stage use.
Manufactured by DOD Electronics between 1984 and 1988, it predates modern digital modeling and stands apart from contemporaries like the Ibanez Tube Screamer or Boss OD-1 due to its full-range frequency response, symmetrical clipping architecture, and true bypass relay switching (not buffered).
Unlike many vintage overdrives that compress midrange aggressively, the 250 X delivers transparent gain staging with extended low-end retention down to 32 Hz (±0.5 dB) and a high-frequency extension to 22.4 kHz (–3 dB).
