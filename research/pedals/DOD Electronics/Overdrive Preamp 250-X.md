## What this pedal is
The **DOD Overdrive Preamp 250-X** is a modern evolution of the classic 250 design. DOD presents it as the **tenth evolution** of the 250 family and gives it a selectable clipping system with three overdrive modes. [1]

The archive keeps **Overdrive** as the primary taxonomy because DOD's product identity is Overdrive Preamp. The fact that some settings can reach heavier distortion does not create a separate Distortion catalog identity. [1]

## Controls and architecture
DOD's manufacturer material identifies a **Gain** control and three selectable overdrive/clipping modes. The mode selection is the principal functional difference that distinguishes the 250-X from a generic 250 entry. [1]

The archive does not infer exact diode part numbers or semiconductor materials from retailer descriptions or unrelated reviews unless the model-specific manufacturer evidence supports them.

## Historical/variant handling
The 250-X is kept separate from the standard **Overdrive Preamp 250** because DOD explicitly identifies it as a new tenth-generation evolution with a three-mode clipping system. This is a documented functional difference, not merely a cosmetic anniversary variation. [1][2]

A separate anniversary presentation of the standard 250 remains consolidated under the 250 identity in the archive's DOD historical reconciliation. [2]

## Technical evidence boundary
An earlier record contained a 2026 retailer/review scrape that claimed the 250-X was a rack-mounted transformer-coupled JFET preamp from 1984–1988. That description is inconsistent with the actual DOD 250-X product identity and has been removed from the archive record.

The archive makes no universal transistor, clipping-diode, or op-amp claim for the 250-X in this pass.
