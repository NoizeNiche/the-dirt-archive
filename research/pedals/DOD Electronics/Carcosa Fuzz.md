## What this pedal is
The DOD Carcosa Fuzz is an **analog silicon fuzz**. DigiTech/DOD describes it as a modern fuzz derived from 1970s-era fuzz ideas, with an exaggerated midrange/treble character, substantial output, true bypass, and a wide range of sounds produced by the interaction of the BEFORE and AFTER controls. The official product page explicitly identifies the circuit as a silicon fuzz. [1]

The core control architecture is:
- **BEFORE:** controls the amount of fuzz.
- **OUTPUT:** controls the overall output level and has enough level to function as a boost.
- **AFTER:** a bias control that changes the fuzz texture. The manufacturer documents lower/counter-clockwise settings as louder, more woody, volume-knob-responsive fuzz and higher/clockwise settings as more broken, splatty, horn-like, and sustaining.
- **HI-CUT:** reduces high-frequency detail and is intended to help match the pedal to different pickups, amplifiers, and extreme AFTER-bias settings.
- **DEMHE/HALI:** a two-position voicing switch. DEMHE adds bass and low-mid emphasis for clean-amp use; HALI cuts bass for dirty-amp use. [1][2]

The owner manual also documents the pedal as true bypass and specifies **9VDC power**, either from a 9V alkaline battery or the optional **PS0913DC** adapter, with a **4 mA** current draw. The official product page lists a **1/4-inch TS unbalanced input at 500 kΩ** and **1/4-inch TS unbalanced output at 1 kΩ**. [1][2]

## Circuit and design history
DOD/DigiTech designer **Tom Cram** says he began the Carcosa project after encountering a mint **Maestro FZ-1S** at Chicago Music Exchange in **2013**. He describes the FZ-1S as the starting inspiration for the then-unnamed DOD design, followed by breadboarding, tagboard prototyping, and approximately a year of changes. According to Cram, DOD added a clipping stage, changed the voicing of both modes, added power filtering, changed most component values, and changed pot tapers before settling on the final design. The Carcosa artwork came from a dream Cram sketched on a 3×5 card, with literary inspiration from Robert W. Chambers and Ambrose Bierce. [3]

This history supports describing the Carcosa as **inspired by the Maestro FZ-1S**, but not as a simple one-to-one reproduction. Cram explicitly describes multiple circuit and voicing changes during development. [3]

## Historical launch evidence
Contemporary coverage places the Carcosa's public launch in **2016**. MusicRadar reported the pedal on **July 19, 2016**, in connection with Summer NAMM and described it as a DOD release with BEFORE, OUTPUT, AFTER, HI-CUT, and Hali/Demhe controls. Effects Database records the product on **June 23, 2016** and tags the entry with Summer NAMM 2016. A December 2016 industry report stated that DigiTech and UK distributor Sound Technology were shipping the Carcosa that month. [4][5][6]

Because the first-party origin story dates the start of design work to 2013 while contemporary launch evidence is from 2016, the archive treats **2013 as documented design-development history** and **2016 as documented launch-era evidence**, rather than collapsing those into a single "release year."

## Sonic and operating behavior
DOD's manual describes the Carcosa as capable of both controlled, volume-responsive fuzz and extreme gated/sputtering textures as AFTER moves clockwise. Lower bias settings respond well to guitar volume changes and pick attack, while more extreme bias settings become less conventional and can produce broken or horn-like textures. [2]

The manufacturer explains the two voicing modes in system terms:
- **DEMHE:** bass and low-mid boost, intended to work especially well into clean amplification.
- **HALI:** bass cut, intended to work especially well into already-dirty amplification.
- **HI-CUT:** trims high-frequency detail and can compensate for tonal changes introduced by the AFTER bias setting. [1][2]

Contemporary review coverage independently describes BEFORE and AFTER as the central interaction, with HI-CUT providing substantial tonal control and the Hali/Demhe switch changing how the pedal sits against clean or dirty amplifier foundations. Guitar.com also records the published specification of true bypass, 9VDC operation, 4 mA current draw, and dimensions of **119 × 67 × 57 mm**. [7]
