## What this pedal is
The **DOD FX69B Grunge** is a historical DOD FX-series high-gain dirt pedal. DOD's current history places the Grunge among its 1990s experimental FX-series designs and describes the period as a time when DOD pushed the series toward unusually extreme, nontraditional controls and sounds. [1]

Historical exact-model references identify the original **FX69 Grunge** as the predecessor and the **FX69B Grunge** as the later final-series version. The archive keeps the suffix because the model designation provides useful version evidence. [2]

## Controls and product identity
Historical documentation for the Grunge family identifies the characteristic four-control architecture:
- **Loud:** output level.
- **Butt:** low-frequency EQ.
- **Face:** high-frequency EQ.
- **Grunge:** distortion amount. [2]

These controls are important to the product identity because DOD intentionally used themed names rather than conventional level, bass, treble, and gain labels during the 1990s experimental FX period. [1][2]

The archive classifies FX69B as **Distortion**, following DOD's historical product positioning and the later model's identity rather than treating the word “Grunge” as a separate technical category.

## Historical relationship: FX69 → FX69B
The original **FX69 Grunge** was introduced in the early 1990s and became one of the best-known members of DOD's experimental FX-series period. Independent historical references identify the later **FX69B** as the 1998 successor/final-series form of the Grunge. [2]

The suffix is therefore retained in the catalog. A surviving FX69B should not be assumed to have exactly the same component population or board layout as an original FX69 without unit-specific evidence.

## Sonic context
DOD's history describes the Grunge and related Lamb-series products as no-holds-barred experiments that emphasized extreme grind rather than restrained tube-like breakup. [1]

Historical descriptions of the original FX69 characterize it as a high-gain, aggressive circuit. The later FX69B retains the Grunge identity, but the archive does not collapse subjective adjectives such as “nasty,” “heavy,” or “aggressive” into technical specifications. [2]
