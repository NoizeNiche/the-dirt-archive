# Colorsound / Sola Sound - Tone Bender

## Research identity
- **Builder:** Colorsound / Sola Sound
- **Catalog identity:** Tone Bender
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz
- **Identity:** Historical Tone Bender catalog identity used across multiple Sola Sound/Colorsound generations.

## What this pedal is
This catalog label is historically broad. Sola Sound reused the Tone Bender name as the design evolved, so a surviving unit labeled only "Tone Bender" can represent materially different generations. [1][2]

The archive preserves the model-specific MKII and MKIII entries separately rather than treating every Tone Bender-labeled unit as one circuit.

## Controls
There is no single fixed control set for all historical units carrying the generic Tone Bender name.

## Colorways
Enclosure and graphics changed repeatedly across the Tone Bender family, including wooden, folded-steel, cast-metal, and pressed-steel examples. [1]

## Versions and factory options
Historical branches include early MKI/MK1.5 forms, MKII, MKIII, MKIV and later Colorsound-era derivatives. [1][2]

## Version changes
The circuit evolved from early modified Fuzz Tone designs through two- and three-transistor germanium/silicon versions and later 1970s silicon designs. [1][2]

## Construction
Original Sola Sound Tone Benders were produced in England for the Musical Exchange/Macaris operation. [1]

## Transistor
- **Exact device:** Generation-dependent.
- Early units use germanium devices; later units include silicon designs. [1][2]

## Diode
- **Exact clipping diode/device:** Generation-dependent and not asserted for the generic catalog identity.

## Sound
The broad family spans very different fuzz responses, from early woolly germanium saturation to tighter/more aggressive later silicon circuits.

## Photo provenance
A generic photograph should not be accepted without matching the label and enclosure generation. Model-specific MKII/MKIII imagery should remain with those records.
