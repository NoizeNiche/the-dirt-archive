# Colorsound / Sola Sound - Tone Bender MKII

## Research identity
- **Builder:** Colorsound / Sola Sound
- **Catalog identity:** Tone Bender MKII
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz
- **Identity:** Mid-1960s three-transistor Tone Bender Professional MKII lineage manufactured by Sola Sound and later supplied under other brands including Vox.

## What this pedal is
The Tone Bender MKII Professional is the landmark mid-1960s three-transistor Sola Sound Tone Bender. Sola Sound supplied MKII units for Vox and other brands, with the formally branded Vox version becoming common from about 1967 until the MKII was discontinued in 1968. [1][2]

## Controls
The classic MKII control set is:
- **Fuzz**
- **Tone**
- **Volume** [1]

## Colorways
- Early Sola Sound units used the earlier graphics and enclosure style.
- Vox-branded MKII examples are commonly found in silver Hammerite finishes with tall silver-topped knobs. [1]
- Branding/enclosure differences are historical production states rather than automatically separate circuits.

## Versions and factory options
The family includes early Sola Sound and later Vox-branded production. The core MKII circuit remained functionally the same through the Sola-to-Vox branding transition described by Fuzzboxes. [1]

## Version changes
The MKII was discontinued in 1968, after which Sola Sound developed the MKIII for the continuing Vox supply arrangement. [1]

## Construction
The original MKII is a hand-built British fuzz from Sola Sound's mid-1960s production. Historical examples use the period enclosure and point-to-point/tagboard construction methods of the era. [1][2]

## Transistor
- **Three-transistor architecture** is established for the MKII. [1][3]
- Surviving original units use germanium transistor variants such as Mullard OC75/OC81D in documented examples, but exact fit varies by production specimen. [2]

## Diode
- The MKII is associated with a no-bias/diode-variation fuzz stage, but the specific diode device should be treated as specimen-dependent unless documented. [3]

## Sound
The MKII is known for a high-gain, sustaining British fuzz voice with strong midrange and responsive cleanup from guitar-volume changes. [1][2]

Fuzzboxes and Effects Database provide model-specific MKII histories and imagery. [1][3]
