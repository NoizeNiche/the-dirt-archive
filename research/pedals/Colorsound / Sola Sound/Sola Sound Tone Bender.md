# Colorsound / Sola Sound - Sola Sound Tone Bender

## Research identity
- **Builder:** Colorsound / Sola Sound
- **Catalog identity:** Sola Sound Tone Bender
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz
- **Identity:** Generic Sola Sound Tone Bender identity covering the original British family before the later model-specific MKII/MKIII distinctions.

## What this pedal is
The name **Tone Bender** was used by Sola Sound across multiple generations. The original family began in the mid-1960s, with early units evolving from the first wooden/steel-box forms through later cast-metal and MK-branded designs. [1][2]

Because the bare "Tone Bender" label spans more than one historical circuit and enclosure, this archive record is treated as a family-level identity and the specific MK records remain separate.

## Controls
Control sets vary by generation, so the generic record does not assign a single fixed panel layout.

## Colorways
Early Sola Sound examples include wooden and folded-steel enclosures, followed by cast-metal and later pressed-steel forms. [1]

## Versions and factory options
The major historical branches include:
- original Tone Bender / retroactively called MKI
- MK1.5-style two-transistor variants
- MKII Professional
- later MKIII/MKIV and subsequent Colorsound-era descendants. [1][2]

## Version changes
The Tone Bender family underwent substantial circuit and enclosure changes over the 1960s and 1970s. The archive keeps generic "Tone Bender" separate from the model-specific MKII and MKIII entries to avoid collapsing materially different circuits into one record. [1][2]

## Construction
Sola Sound produced the original British Tone Bender family for Musical Exchange/Macaris in London. [1]

## Transistor
- Device count and type vary substantially by generation.
- Early examples include germanium transistor circuits, while later generations moved to silicon implementations. [1][2]

## Diode
- Exact clipping diode/device varies by generation and is not asserted for the generic family record.

## Sound
The family ranges from early germanium fuzz designs to the later more aggressive silicon versions. A generic description therefore should not be substituted for the model-specific MKII/MKIII records.

## Photo provenance
Only photographs matching a specific generation's enclosure, label, controls, and artwork should be used for a generic family record.
