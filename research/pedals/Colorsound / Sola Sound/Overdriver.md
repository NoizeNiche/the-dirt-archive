# Colorsound / Sola Sound - Overdriver

## Research identity
- **Builder:** Colorsound / Sola Sound
- **Catalog identity:** Overdriver
- **Catalog type:** Overdrive / Boost
- **Identity:** Early-1970s Colorsound/Sola Sound boost and overdrive derived from the earlier Power Boost, with a two-band tone section.

## What this pedal is
The Overdriver is the 9V evolution of the earlier 18V Colorsound Power Boost concept. Historical references place the Overdriver in the early 1970s and describe it as a preamp/boost circuit with strong bass and treble shaping that can move from clean boost into overdrive and, at higher gain settings, more aggressive saturation. [1][2][3]

## Controls
Original vintage examples are documented with:
- **Gain**
- **Bass**
- **Treble**

Original early units did not universally have a master volume. Later factory units and reissues added a **Volume/Master Volume** control, so front-panel photographs should be checked against the specific production state. [1][3]

## Colorways
- Vintage examples exist in gray/metallic Colorsound housings as well as later wide-body variants.
- Earlier Power Boost units used an orange enclosure, but that is retained as a related model identity rather than silently merged into Overdriver. [2]

## Versions and factory options
- The major historical change is the transition from the **18V Power Boost** to the **9V Overdriver**.
- Later units added a master volume.
- References also document component-value and transistor-type variation over the production life. [2]
No complete numbered Overdriver revision system was established.

## Version changes
The Overdriver is historically a revised continuation of the Power Boost circuit family. Sources disagree on exact release-year details, with dates clustered around the early 1970s. Kit Rae cites the 1971 factory schematic and a 9V Overdriver transition around that period. [2]

## Construction
Original units were manufactured by Sola Sound in London and used pressed-metal Colorsound enclosures. [2][3]

## Transistor
- Historical references identify **silicon transistor** use in the Overdriver lineage, with more than one transistor type used over the production life. [2]
- Specific transistor part numbers for an individual unit should be tied to specimen evidence rather than generalized.
## Diode
- **Exact clipping diode/device:** Not publicly documented as a single universal Overdriver value across production.

## Sound
At lower gain the Overdriver can function as a loud, open boost with broad tonal shaping. Increasing gain moves into gritty overdrive and eventually more saturated textures. The Bass and Treble controls provide unusually broad shaping around the drive response. [1][3]

## Photo provenance
ToneHome, Kit Rae, and historical Colorsound material provide model-specific Overdriver imagery and production context. [2][4]
