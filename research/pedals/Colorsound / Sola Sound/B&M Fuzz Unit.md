# Colorsound / Sola Sound - B&M Fuzz Unit

## Research identity
- **Builder:** Colorsound / Sola Sound
- **Catalog identity:** B&M Fuzz Unit
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz
- **Identity:** Barnes & Mullins private-label late-1970s fuzz from the Sola Sound/Colorsound silicon Jumbo Tone Bender family.

## What this pedal is
B&M Fuzz Unit is the label used on surviving Barnes & Mullins versions of the late-1970s Sola Sound/Colorsound silicon Jumbo Tone Bender family. Historical sources also document closely related labels including B&M Champion Fuzz Unit, CMI Fuzz Unit, G.B. Fuzz Unit, and Pro'Traffic Fuzz Unit. [1][2]

## Controls
Surviving examples document:
- **Volume**
- **Sustain**
- **Tone** [3]

## Colorways
- Orange is documented in surviving marketplace photography.
- Black examples also exist within the same historical product family. [1][3]
- Label wording varies between B&M Fuzz Unit and B&M Champion Fuzz Unit in surviving examples. [1]

## Versions and factory options
The family is known for period-to-period construction differences while retaining the same underlying design lineage. No complete official revision numbering was established.

## Version changes
Historical evidence points to variations in enclosure color, labeling, and individual component batches rather than a clean V1/V2 production split. [1]

## Construction
The original units were produced in England by Sola Sound/Colorsound for third-party distribution through Barnes & Mullins. The circuit belongs to the later silicon Tone Bender/Jumbo family. [1][2]

## Transistor
- **Typical documented family:** Silicon **BC184C** transistors are associated with common examples of this circuit lineage. [1]
- Exact transistor selection can vary by production specimen and should not be generalized to every unit.

## Diode
- **Exact clipping diode/device:** Not reliably documented in the checked sources.

## Sound
The B&M Fuzz Unit belongs to the more compressed, sustaining and woolly late-1970s silicon Tone Bender branch, with the basic circuit lineage described as a close relative of the Big Muff topology. [1][2]

## Photo provenance
Multiple Reverb listings provide clear exact-label B&M Fuzz Unit product photographs. [3][4]
