# Anasounds — sandman

## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** sandman
- **Builder:** Anasounds
- **Catalog type:** Overdrive
- **Identity:** Limited-edition dual overdrive combining the original Savage MkI circuit with the Ego Driver. Anasounds states that the Savage side is the original MkI circuit, not a modified version. [1][2]

## What this pedal is
Sandman puts two complementary Anasounds overdrives into one enclosure: the **Savage MkI** on the left and the **Ego Driver** on the right. It is designed to work as a double overdrive or as a stacked pair, with the Ego Driver capable of pushing the Savage input and adding mid presence. [1][2]

## Versions and factory options
### Savage MkI section
- **Out** output-volume control.
- **Tone** presence/tone control.
- **Gain** controls saturation while mixing clean and driven signal, giving the circuit a parallel-overdrive behavior useful for retaining low end.
- **Ge/Si** switch selects the clipping technology: germanium for the compressed vintage voice associated with the original transparent-overdrive concept, or silicon for the more dynamic Savage MkI implementation. [1]

### Ego Driver section
- **Out** output volume; when stacked into the Savage, this becomes the effective pre-gain control for the Savage.
- **Tone** adjusts high-frequency content and midrange focus.
- **Gain** ranges from light crunch to thicker overdrive.
- **Hi/Lo** switch selects lower-mid or upper-mid emphasis, with the middle position retaining the original mid-boost voicing. [1]

### Internal trimpots
- **High:** changes the treble range entering the Ego Driver clipping stage when Hi is selected.
- **Bass:** changes the bass range entering the clipping stage when Lo is selected.
- **Diodes/LED:** selects Ego Driver clipping between the original 1N914 silicon diodes and LED clipping for a more dynamic, less-compressed response.
- **T/M:** switches Savage voicing between a treble-focused T setting and a more modern midrange-focused M setting. [1]

## Version changes
Sandman is a new physical format for the **unchanged Savage MkI circuit** combined with the Ego Driver, rather than a numbered Savage revision. Anasounds positioned it as a time-limited edition and stated that the Savage's hard-to-source germanium diode was one reason for the limited production. [2]

## Transistor
- **Technology:** Not established for either circuit in the reviewed sources.
- The official Sandman documentation focuses on clipping devices and signal-path behavior rather than identifying a discrete transistor family.

## Diode / clipping
- **Savage MkI:** selectable **Germanium / Silicon** clipping through the Ge/Si switch. The builder identifies the germanium clipping element as the hard-to-find component associated with the original transparent-overdrive voice. [1][2]
- **Ego Driver:** internal selection between **1N914 silicon diodes** and **LED** clipping. [1]
- The Sandman also retains an unused Savage diode socket for experimentation with alternate clipping diodes. [1]

## Power / electrical behavior
- **Power:** only a **negative-center 9VDC** supply is specified by Anasounds. Mono jacks are required for proper power switching. A protection circuit cuts the supply when the wrong polarity is used. [1]
- The Savage section has approximately **27V of headroom/dynamic range**, described by Anasounds as matching the original circuit's behavior. [1]
- The pedal is built around two overdrive channels that can be operated independently or stacked. [1]

## Signal-path / control behavior
- The Savage can operate as a clean-ish preamp, an always-on coloration stage, or a higher-gain overdrive.
- The Ego Driver can push the Savage input when both are engaged.
- The pedal's internal high/bass trimpots shape the frequency content before the Ego Driver saturation stage, while the T/M trimpot alters the Savage voicing. [1]
- Anasounds states that clipping changes can alter perceived volume because different clipping thresholds require different signal amplitudes. [1]

## Sound
The Savage MkI is intended to preserve guitar and amplifier character while adding harmonics, thickness, and volume. The Ego Driver adds a pronounced midrange emphasis and can function as a gain/boost stage. Together, the two circuits can move from transparent, lower-gain drive to thicker stacked overdrive while retaining a wide range of mid and clipping options. [1][2]
