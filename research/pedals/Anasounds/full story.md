# Anasounds — full story

## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** full story
- **Builder:** Anasounds
- **Catalog type:** Distortion / Fuzz
- **Identity:** Modular distortion/fuzz platform using removable Tone Cards to reproduce or reinterpret multiple generations and circuit variants of a 1960s–1990s “big fuzz” lineage.

## What this pedal is
The Full Story is Anasounds' modular distortion/fuzz platform. Instead of fixing one historical circuit in the enclosure, the pedal uses removable **Tone Cards** containing part of the circuit so the player can change versions without buying another pedal. Anasounds developed several formats, including a collector's edition that retraces seven historical versions, a regular Anasounds version with two Tone Card slots, and an FX Teacher version with one Tone Card slot plus additional clipping customization. [1][2][3]

## Versions and factory options
### Collector's edition
- **Two Tone Card slots**.
- Includes a built-in **germanium boost** section inspired by a classic treble booster.
- The boost has a volume control and a four-position filter/voicing control ranging from full-range boost to treble-boost voicings.
- Includes a **guitar-pickup impedance simulator** so the modular boost can be placed later in the signal chain without the normal impedance interaction of vintage germanium boost circuits.
- Ships with the seven historical Tone Cards. [1]

### Regular Anasounds version
- **Two Tone Card slots**.
- Two independent fuzz channels, each with its own **Out, Fuzz, and Tone** controls.
- Each channel has an independent **Mid** switch.
- The two supplied Tone Cards are Anasounds-developed versions, with additional Tone Cards sold separately. [1][3]

### FX Teacher version
- **One Tone Card slot**.
- Adds a **Clip** switch with multiple clipping choices, including a no-clipping/SAG position, and a terminal block for component experimentation.
- Retains the mid-voicing concept with a more adjustable implementation.
- Offered as a DIY kit or assembled pedal. [1]

## Version changes
The major changes are architectural:
- The original collector format was designed to reproduce seven historical versions through interchangeable cards.
- The regular Anasounds version keeps two cards and two independent fuzz channels but removes the collector edition's built-in booster.
- The FX Teacher version uses one card, adds clipping customization, and is optimized for experimentation and DIY modification. [1][2][3]

## Tone Card lineage
Anasounds documents a broad historical set of Tone Cards:
- A **1969–1972 first-version** inspired card with large bass response and softer highs.
- A later **Ram's Head-era** style card with long sustain, warm/round tone, and softer highs.
- A later aggressive/bright **modern-era** fuzz card.
- An **op-amp-based** version distinct from the transistor cards and described as less mid-scooped and more distortion-oriented.
- Additional Anasounds cards and collaborative cards from **ALH Effects** and **Tampco**, including the Battersea, Pulse, Piggy, and a Tone Bender-inspired All-Bender. [1]
- Anasounds states the Tone Card concept is extensible, allowing future circuits and custom versions to be added without changing the main pedal platform. [1]

## Controls
For the regular two-channel Anasounds version:
- **Main channel:** Out, Fuzz, Tone.
- **Secondary channel:** Out, Fuzz, Tone.
- **Mid switch:** independent for each channel.
- Two footswitches select the two channels, and the channels can also be stacked in series. [3]

The FX Teacher version adds:
- **Clip** control/switch.
- A more flexible midrange component terminal.
- One Tone Card slot instead of two. [1]

## Transistor
- **Technology:** Not fixed at platform level.
- Tone Card circuit technology varies by card. Anasounds explicitly documents at least one Tone Card as **op-amp based rather than transistor based**, so the platform should not be classified as a single transistor technology. [1]

## Diode / clipping
- **Technology:** Version-dependent.
- The regular Anasounds format does not document one universal clipping device for all Tone Cards.
- The FX Teacher version explicitly adds a **three-position clipping system** with different diodes or no clipping at all, plus a terminal block for experimentation. [1]
- Because Tone Cards can contain different circuits, clipping should be treated as card-specific metadata rather than a single Full Story component value.

## Power / signal-path behavior
- The collector's edition includes a pickup-impedance simulator specifically to prevent vintage germanium boost sections from reacting badly to their position in the chain. [1]
- The regular Full Story is designed to work anywhere in the signal chain, including later-stage placement before the amplifier, according to the user guide. [2]

## Sound
Full Story is intended to cover a broad range from huge, mid-scooped fuzz/distortion through mid-forward lead sounds and more distortion-oriented op-amp voices. The Mid switches can add presence to otherwise scooped versions, while Tone Cards determine the underlying circuit and frequency/gain character. [1][2][3]

## Deep research verification
Verified against multiple current and historical Anasounds pages plus the Full Story user guide. The evidence supports the collector/regular/FX Teacher platform distinctions, removable Tone Card architecture, dual-channel regular version, mid switches, FX Teacher clipping system, historical Tone Card lineage, op-amp-based card variant, collector-edition germanium boost, pickup-impedance simulator, and the platform's role as a modular distortion/fuzz system. A single universal transistor or clipping technology cannot be assigned without specifying the installed Tone Card. [1][2][3]
