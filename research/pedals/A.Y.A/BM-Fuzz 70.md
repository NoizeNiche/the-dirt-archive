# A.Y.A — BM-Fuzz 70

## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** BM-Fuzz 70
- **Builder:** A.Y.A
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz
- **Identity:** A.Y.A Tokyo Japan BM-Fuzz 70, the 1970s-oriented member of A.Y.A's BM-Fuzz pair.

## What this pedal is
BM-Fuzz 70 is a compact Japanese-made fuzz built around the tonal idea of the **1970s Big Muff / Ram's Head family**. A Japanese gear reference describes it as a Big Muff-family fuzz and specifically identifies the 70 version with 1970s Ram's Head-style sound. Effects Database independently lists the exact model as a fuzz pedal. [1][2]

Effects Database published the exact-model entry on **April 3, 2006**. That is a database publication date, not a claimed factory release date. [3]

## Controls
- **Fuzz:** controls the amount of fuzz. [3]
- **Tone:** moves the center of a boosted frequency range from bass toward treble as turned clockwise. [3]
- **Level:** controls effect output volume. [3]

## Version relationship
BM-Fuzz 70 and BM-Fuzz 80 are cataloged as separate A.Y.A models, not as color variants.

A 2013 Japanese review states that BM-Fuzz 80 keeps the basic BM-Fuzz 70 concept but changes the transistor gain characteristics and internal wiring materials. The same source identifies BM-Fuzz 70 with a 1970s Big Muff basis and describes the two models as intentionally distinct voicings. [2]

## Components and construction
Effects Database describes the BM-Fuzz family as using:
- carbon-composition resistors,
- high-quality parts including Vishay, Sprague, AVX and Fairchild devices,
- BELDEN internal wiring,
- multiple KESTER solder types used with the aim of influencing tone quality. [3]

The exact transistor part number for a specific BM-Fuzz 70 unit is **not verified**. The surviving source wording mentions Fairchild transistor components but does not establish one exact production device for every unit. [2][3]

- **Transistor:** Exact part number unknown.
- **Diode:** Exact type/device not publicly verified.
- **Schematic/component values:** Not verified.

## Sound
The BM-Fuzz 70 is described as covering a range from a more conventional distortion-box sound into sharper/crushed fuzz while retaining the fat, strong character associated with the BM-Fuzz family. The 1970s/Ram's Head reference is the useful historical cue, but the archive does not infer a one-to-one circuit clone without schematic evidence. [1][2][3]

## Manufacturer history
A.Y.A's own history says the compact pedal line began with R-COMP, followed by BM-Fuzz, Drivesta, WarmChorus and FrontGateBassDrive. The maker identifies Makoto Kawai as the builder and explains that A.Y.A adopted decorative Japanese-themed artwork as part of the brand identity. [4]

## Artwork / enclosure
A.Y.A's history explicitly identifies the BM-FUZZ artwork as a **showa-era Japanese-house veranda scene**, while a contemporary Japanese review describes the pedal's visual design as part of A.Y.A's decorative approach. Cosmetic artwork is not treated as a separate circuit revision. [2][4]
