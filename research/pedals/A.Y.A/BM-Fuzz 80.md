# A.Y.A — BM-Fuzz 80

## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** BM-Fuzz 80
- **Builder:** A.Y.A
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz
- **Identity:** A.Y.A Tokyo Japan BM-Fuzz 80, the 1980s-oriented member of A.Y.A's BM-Fuzz pair.

## What this pedal is
BM-Fuzz 80 is the later-period counterpart to BM-Fuzz 70. A Japanese gear reference identifies it as a **Big Muff-family fuzz based on the 1980s, third-generation discrete-type style**, while A.Y.A's own documentation describes the BM-Fuzz family as capable of moving from distortion-box territory to crushed fuzz. [1][2]

Effects Database published the exact-model entry on **April 3, 2006**. This is a database publication date, not a verified factory release date. [3]

## Controls
- **Fuzz:** controls the amount of fuzz. [3]
- **Tone:** moves the center of a boosted frequency range from bass toward treble as turned clockwise. [3]
- **Level:** controls effect output volume. [3]

## Version relationship
BM-Fuzz 80 is cataloged separately from BM-Fuzz 70.

A 2013 Japanese review says the 80 version is based on the later 1980s/third-generation discrete Muff concept and is built with changes to **transistor gain and internal wiring material** while retaining the basic BM-Fuzz format. [1]

## Components and construction
Effects Database documents the BM-Fuzz family as using:
- carbon-composition resistors,
- high-quality parts including Vishay, Sprague, AVX and Fairchild devices,
- BELDEN internal wiring,
- multiple KESTER solder types used to control tone quality. [3]

No exact production transistor part number for BM-Fuzz 80 is verified. Surviving descriptions identify Fairchild transistor components but do not provide a single exact device designation for all units. [1][3]

- **Transistor:** Exact part number unknown.
- **Diode:** Exact type/device not publicly verified.
- **Schematic/component values:** Not verified.

## Sound
The BM-Fuzz family is described as having a fat, strong voice that can cover natural distortion-box territory through dirty/crushed fuzz. The 80 version specifically targets the later 1980s discrete Muff character, so the archive treats that voicing as distinct from the 70 version rather than presenting the two as one generic BM-Fuzz. [1][2][3]

## Manufacturer history
A.Y.A's official history records BM-Fuzz as one of the early compact pedals after R-COMP and before Drivesta, WarmChorus and FrontGateBassDrive. The company attributes its decorative artwork and branding to Japanese seasonal imagery. [4]

## Artwork / enclosure
A.Y.A's history identifies BM-FUZZ artwork as a Japanese showa-era veranda scene. A contemporary comparison notes that the 80 version differs visually from the 70 version in model labeling and knob color. Those cosmetic differences are not treated as independent circuit versions. [1][4]
