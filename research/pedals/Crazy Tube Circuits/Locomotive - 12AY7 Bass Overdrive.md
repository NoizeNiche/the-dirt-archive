## What this pedal is
Locomotive is a real-tube bass overdrive built around a **12AY7** vacuum tube, the same tube type associated with classic tweed bass-amp preamp designs. Crazy Tube Circuits designed it for a wide gain range while retaining bass fundamentals through a parallel clean blend.

## Controls
- **Gain:** drives the tube section from large clean boost through warm overdrive.
- **Mean:** controls the level of the driven signal.
- **Clean:** blends the unaffected input signal back into the output to preserve low-frequency body.
- **Tone:** shapes the treble/frequency response.

## Circuit and operation
- Real 12AY7 tube in the drive circuit.
- Internal voltage multiplier raises the external supply voltage to the level required by the tube.
- Low-impedance output allows the pedal to operate as a standalone bass preamp.
- True bypass.

## Power
- 9V DC external supply, center-negative.
- Published current draw is approximately **400 mA**.
- The internal voltage multiplier is responsible for generating the higher operating voltage required by the 12AY7.

## Versions and factory options
- The checked sources establish the production Locomotive as a 12AY7-based bass overdrive.
- Crazy Tube Circuits' published documentation also permits alternative compatible dual-triode tube choices such as a **5751** for tonal variation, but this is a tube-substitution option rather than a separate factory model.
- No separate circuit revision was established in the checked evidence.

## Version changes
No distinct production circuit revision was established from the checked sources.

## Transistor
- The drive stage is tube-based; no exact transistor component specification was established for the signal path.

## Diode

## Sound
Locomotive is designed to span from a substantial clean boost to warm, organic tube grind, with touch-sensitive response and the natural compression associated with a tube gain stage. The Clean control lets the player retain unaffected bass fundamentals while increasing the amount of driven signal.
