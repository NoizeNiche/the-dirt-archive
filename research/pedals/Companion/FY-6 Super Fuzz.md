# Companion - FY-6 Super Fuzz

## Research identity
- **Builder:** Companion
- **Catalog identity:** FY-6 Super Fuzz
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz
- **Identity:** Shin-Ei/Companion FY-6 Super Fuzz from the late-1960s Japanese production family.

## What this pedal is
The Companion FY-6 Super Fuzz is part of the Japanese Super Fuzz family that followed the Honey "Baby Crying" FY-6 and was marketed under Shin-Ei/Companion branding. Effects Database maintains a dedicated Companion FY-6 record, while Shin-ei's current documentation identifies the original FY-6 as a 1968 product and describes the Honey FY-6 of 1967 as its preceding form. [1][2]

## Controls
The characteristic FY-6 control set is:
- **Balance / Expander**
- **Tone 1 / Tone 2** two-position slider switch. [2]

Different branded versions can have labeling variations, but the underlying two-knob/tone-switch concept remains the defining layout.

## Colorways and private labels
- FY-6 examples appear under Companion, Shin-Ei, Univox, Apollo, Tempo and other related branding. [1][3]
- Branding differences are historical marketed states and should not be merged with materially different revisions without evidence.

## Versions and factory options
The family evolved from the 1967 Honey Baby Crying FY-6 to the 1968 Shin-Ei FY-6 Super Fuzz. Shin-ei states that the later Shin-Ei model retained the same basic circuit design/components as the original Honey form apart from the label and the addition of a 9V input and LED. [2]

## Version changes
- **Honey FY-6:** 1967 predecessor.
- **Shin-Ei FY-6:** 1968 branded successor in the same documented family.
- Later private-label versions should be kept separate when the marketed identity is distinct. [2][3]

## Construction
The original FY-6 family is a Japanese transistor fuzz design. Shin-ei's current reproduction uses modern selected components while explicitly aiming to reproduce the original circuit. [2]

## Transistor

## Sound
The FY-6 is associated with its distinctive upper-octave/harmonic fuzz response and the Balance/Expander interaction. The current Shin-ei reproduction states that it duplicates the original circuitry and describes the design as an intentionally faithful reproduction. [2]
