# Basic Audio — Marq Won

## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** Marq Won
- **Builder:** Basic Audio
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz
- **Identity:** Basic Audio's Germanium Tone Bender MKI-style fuzz.

## What this pedal is
Marq Won is based on the Tone Bender MKI. Basic Audio uses a three-position low-end-cut switch in the middle of the circuit, changing compression and how hard the second stage is driven, plus a high-cut tone control. [1][2]

## Colorways
- A **bright orange enclosure with a hand-painted blue/green multicolor face** is documented in Basic Audio's official photograph.
- Reverb documents hand-painted custom finishes and a 2018 example. [3]
- Basic Audio notes that colors are subject to change. [1]

## Versions and factory options
### Marq Won production
- Tone Bender MKI basis
- Germanium transistor technology documented in surviving examples
- three-position low-end-cut switch
- high-cut tone control
- center-negative 9V operation
- battery snap [1][2][3]

Basic Audio has made multiple enclosure finishes and individual examples over time, but no numbered factory revision was verified.

## Version changes
The documented variation is primarily tonal switching and enclosure/finish variation rather than a verified numbered redesign. Reverb examples also show socketed Germanium transistors in individual units, but the exact device type should not be generalized to every production pedal. [3]

## Transistor
- **Technology:** Germanium in documented production examples. [3]
- **Exact transistor/device:** Unknown.
- **Important note:** A Reverb example specifically documents socketed Germanium transistors; that is evidence for that individual unit, not proof of one universal production transistor. [3]

## Diode
- **Type:** Not publicly documented.
- **Exact diode/device:** Unknown.

## Sound
Marq Won covers the loose, warm and spitty side of a Tone Bender MKI family fuzz, with the low-end switch changing both compression and stage drive. The high-cut tone control can tame the top end while the different switch positions move the pedal between thicker, softer textures and more open, ragged attack. [1][2][3]
