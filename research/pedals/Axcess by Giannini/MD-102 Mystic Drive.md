# Axcess by Giannini — MD-102 Mystic Drive

## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** MD-102 Mystic Drive
- **Builder:** Axcess by Giannini
- **Catalog type:** Overdrive
- **Identity:** Compact analog overdrive with Tone, Drive and Power Level controls plus a dedicated Scoop switch, true bypass and 9V battery/adapter operation. [1][2]

## Controls
- **DRIVE:** Main gain/saturation control.
- **TONE:** Primary high-frequency shaping.
- **POWER LEVEL:** Output level / boost function.
- **SCOOP:** Alternate mid/high-frequency contour. [1][2]

## What this pedal is
Giannini's period description presents MD-102 as a versatile overdrive/distortion pedal intended to reproduce the gain and saturation behavior of a tube amplifier. A surviving retailer description specifically notes the Drive control's broad range and a highly sensitive Tone control. [2]

## Circuit / clipping
Giannini's period material describes a **multiple-stage clipping system** whose behavior changes as Drive is adjusted. The exact schematic and diode/transistor implementation are not published in the reviewed sources. [1][2]

## Electrical / hardware specifications
Giannini's catalog/manual documentation specifies:
- **9VDC** battery or regulated adapter.
- **Center-negative** DC input.
- **110 × 60 × 53mm** metal enclosure.
- True bypass and LED indication.
- P10 input/output.
- The 2015-16 catalog identifies the Scoop control as a special tonal function. [1]

## Transistor
- Exact active-device part number is not publicly documented.
## Diode
- Multiple-stage clipping is described, but exact clipping components are unknown.

## Version history
- Effects Database dates the catalog entry to the **Musikmesse Frankfurt 2012** period.
- No numbered electronic revision is established.
- Surviving examples may have cosmetic differences; those are not treated as circuit revisions. [1]

## Sound
Giannini describes MD-102 as able to provide numerous overdrive/distortion timbres with a strong dynamic response. The Scoop control changes the tonal emphasis beyond the normal Tone control. [2]
