# Coggins Audio / Dinosaural — Dan Solo Mid Booster / Drive

## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** Dan Solo Mid Booster / Drive
- **Builder:** Coggins Audio / Dinosaural
- **Catalog type:** Boost / Overdrive
- **Identity:** Dan Solo, the standalone version of the Cogmeister's output Solo section.

## What this pedal is
Coggins Audio states that Dan Solo is identical to the final **Solo** section of the Dinosaural Cogmeister. It was released as a standalone pedal after interest in the individual Cogmeister gain stages grew. [1]

## Versions and factory options
The source family documents the Solo section as:
- PEAK gain/drive control.
- LEV output level control.
- Mid-focused boosting.
- Standalone enclosure derived from the Cogmeister section. [1]

## Version changes
Dan Solo is a deconstructed standalone section of Cogmeister rather than a new unrelated circuit. Coggins Audio currently lists it as discontinued and calls remaining stock the last chance to buy. [1][2]

## Transistor
Cogmeister's Solo section is part of its all-discrete, Class-A silicon transistor architecture. [3]
## Diode
No diode-clipping stage is documented for the Solo section.

## Sound
The Solo section can provide a moderate clean flat boost and can move into a narrower-bandwidth midrange boost with increased distortion as PEAK rises. It is intended both to lift solos and to increase intensity/focus. [3]

### Verified description
Dinosaural Cogmeister is a single guitar booster/overdrive/distortion/fuzz pedal, containing a trio of compatible sections arranged in fixed series pattern.

### Verified color/finish evidence
- Since its introduction, it has gained popularity among musicians like Chris Turpin (Ida Mae, Kill It Kid) and Paul Stacey (Oasis, Noel Gallagher's High Flying Birds, the Black Crowes), who praise its versatility and ability to evoke classic rock tones.
- The central red LED is designed to dim noticeably, relative to the other two LEDs, whenever the battery gets too low and/or supply voltage drops.
- The 3 effects are marked by the two vertical white lines, and each effect has its own bypass switch.
