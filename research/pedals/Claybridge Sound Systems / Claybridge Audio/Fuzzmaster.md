# Claybridge Sound Systems / Claybridge Audio — Fuzzmaster

## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** Fuzzmaster
- **Builder:** Claybridge Sound Systems / Claybridge Audio
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz
- **Identity:** Claybridge Sound Systems Fuzzmaster, a rare Australian fuzz series dating to the mid-1960s.

## What this pedal is
Tone Machines Blog documents the Claybridge Fuzzmaster as one of the earliest known Australian fuzz series, associated with builder Ralph Bridges of Claybridge Sound Systems. The surviving history shows multiple forms of the Fuzzmaster family and later company-name changes to Claybridge Audio. [1]

A 1966 example documented by the same source uses a **three-silicon-transistor circuit with 2N3565 transistors** and a green hammerite-style enclosure with deep cherry-red knobs. [1]

## Colorways
Historical examples documented by Tone Machines include:
- Green hammerite with deep cherry-red knobs on an early 1966 example.
- Other later Fuzzmaster family enclosures include black/light-blue and silver hammerite finishes. [1]

The archive does not collapse every historical enclosure into one colorway unless the exact serial/model identity is established.

## Versions and factory options
The Fuzzmaster family included multiple forms. Tone Machines documents a broader sequence involving early Fuzzmaster, Wildcat, and later MKIV examples, while noting that some examples differed in transistor count and control layout. [1][2]

## Version changes
The exact historical chronology is still being reconstructed. The checked sources establish multiple distinct Fuzzmaster family forms and a later MKIV, but do not support a complete factory revision table. [1][2]

## Transistor
A documented 1966 Fuzzmaster example uses **three silicon 2N3565 transistors**. [1]

This is recorded as an exact documented example, not automatically generalized to every Fuzzmaster in the family.

## Diode
No exact-model diode specification was established.

## Sound
Tone Machines describes surviving Fuzzmaster variants as loud, aggressive, and raw, with later examples capable of very different gating/sustain behavior. These descriptions are source-specific and are not generalized across every Fuzzmaster variant. [2]
