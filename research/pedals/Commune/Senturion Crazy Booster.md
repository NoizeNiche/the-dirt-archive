# Commune - Senturion Crazy Booster

## Research identity
- **Builder:** Commune
- **Catalog identity:** Senturion Crazy Booster
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz
- **Identity:** Japanese bass-oriented fuzz/booster built around a wet/dry mix concept and unusually high output capability.

## What this pedal is
Senturion Crazy Booster is a bass-oriented fuzz from Commune. Despite the "Booster" name, historical and recent user documentation identifies its primary effect as aggressive fuzz, with the dry signal retained through a dedicated boost control. [1][2]

## Controls
- **ROAR BOOST:** adjusts the original/dry signal level.
- **CRAZY:** adjusts the fuzz amount.
- **FILTER:** shapes the fuzz component's tone.
- **OPERATE:** adjusts fuzz sustain. [1]

ROAR BOOST and CRAZY together allow the original and effected signals to be mixed. [1][2]

## Colorways and construction
- Surviving units use a clear top plate over pressed-flower artwork.
- The flowers and their arrangement can differ from pedal to pedal, so this is an individual cosmetic state rather than a circuit revision. [1]
- A secondary-hand market example documents a unit with the flower insert missing and separately confirms the same control set. [2]

## Versions and factory options
- **Power:** DC 9V center-negative; some surviving documentation also shows battery support, while later used-market units may omit battery operation. [1][2]
- No numbered factory revision sequence was established.
- Internal wiring in one surviving market description is identified as Belden wire, but this is specimen-level construction evidence rather than a universal production claim. [2]

## Version changes
No reliable date-based hardware revision chronology was established.

## Construction
The pedal is analog and was designed specifically for bass applications, with a parallel dry/effected concept and a rugged enclosed build. [1][2]

## Sound
The documented character is extremely forceful, low-heavy fuzz with very high available signal level. The dry-mix control can preserve bass fundamentals, although the effect can become so saturated that the dry component is less perceptually distinct at extreme settings. [1]
