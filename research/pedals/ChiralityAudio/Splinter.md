# ChiralityAudio — Splinter

## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** Splinter
- **Builder:** ChiralityAudio
- **Catalog type:** Overdrive
- **Identity:** ChiralityAudio Splinter, a high-headroom analog overdrive derived from a TS-808 concept but redesigned as an original circuit.

## What this pedal is
ChiralityAudio describes Splinter as a modern overdrive born from a respin of the legendary TS-808 concept, but explicitly not a clone. It is designed for guitar, bass, synths, drum machines, and line-level sources, with internally generated higher headroom from a standard 9V supply. [1]

The official manual documents a DRIVe, LEVEL, TONE control set plus clipping and bandwidth selectors. [2]

## Colorways
ChiralityAudio documents two electronically identical finishes:
- **Matte Black**
- **Natural Brushed Aluminum / Custom Shop** [1]

The builder states that the Custom Shop brushed-aluminum finish is manually etched and therefore each enclosure is visually unique. [1]

## Versions and factory options
Documented controls:
- DRIVE
- LEVEL
- TONE
- CLIPPING selector: I / II / III
- LOW CUT selector: H / M / L [2]

The clipping selector provides:
- I: aggressive/gritty clipping.
- II: silicon clipping.
- III: mild clipping. [2]

The pedal also provides true bypass, accepts +4 dBu input, draws up to 25 mA, and has input impedance over 300 kOhm. [1][2]

## Version changes
The official page documents the Custom Shop finish as cosmetically different but electronically identical to the standard black version. [1]

## Transistor
No exact-model transistor part number was published. The builder's technical documentation identifies **Burr Brown op-amps** but does not specify the exact part number in the checked product page/manual. [1][2]

## Diode
The clipping selector explicitly provides aggressive, silicon, and mild clipping configurations. Exact individual diode part numbers were not established. [2]

## Sound
Splinter covers clean boost through crunchy and higher-gain overdrive. Its bandwidth and clipping selectors let the player adapt the response to different instruments and input levels, while the higher internal headroom improves transient response and allows line-level operation. [1][2]
