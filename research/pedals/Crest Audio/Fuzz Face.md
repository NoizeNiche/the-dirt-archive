# Crest Audio — Fuzz Face

## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** Fuzz Face
- **Builder:** Crest Audio
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz
- **Identity:** The Crest Audio revival of the Dallas Arbiter Fuzz Face, produced during the mid-to-late 1980s.

## What this pedal is
Crest Audio brought the Fuzz Face back into production while Dave Fox was working at Crest. Fox later described finding original Fuzz Face and Trem Face parts in the Crest warehouse and developing the revival from that stock. Effects Database dates the Crest run to **1986–1990** and estimates roughly **2,000 units**. [1][2]

## Colorways
- Documented production finishes include **red and grey**, with a smaller blue run. [1]
- Cosmetic finish differences remain colorways rather than separate model identities.

## Versions and factory options
### Crest Audio Fuzz Face production
- Volume
- Fuzz
- Classic round Fuzz Face enclosure
- Dallas Arbiter-style lower label
- 9V battery operation
- Silicon transistor implementation [1]

Effects Database reports that some units used leftover original parts from the Crest warehouse, so component-level differences can exist between individual examples without creating a new catalog identity. [1]

## Version changes
The Crest Audio run is itself a distinct historical production era of the Fuzz Face family. Within that run, the available evidence does not establish a formal numbered V1/V2 sequence. The archive therefore keeps one Crest Audio Fuzz Face parent and records individual production variations as notes where documented.

## Transistor
- **Technology:** Silicon. [1]
- **Exact transistor/device:** **BC109C** is the typical reported device; **BC107** is also reported in some examples. [1][2]

## Diode
- **Type:** Exact production clipping/rectifier diode(s) were not established in the reliable sources checked.
- **Exact part:** Unknown.

## Sound
The Crest Audio version retains the broad, two-control Fuzz Face response while using silicon transistors for more consistent performance than many early germanium examples. Contemporary descriptions emphasize an aggressive, sustaining fuzz character with more predictable unit-to-unit behavior than vintage production. [1]
