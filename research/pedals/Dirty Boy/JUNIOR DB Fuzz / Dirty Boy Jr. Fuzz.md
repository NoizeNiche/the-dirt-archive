# Dirty Boy — JUNIOR DB Fuzz / Dirty Boy Jr. Fuzz

## PRP identity

- **Builder:** Dirty Boy
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz
- **Identity:** Dirty Boy Jr. Fuzz, also presented as **JUNIOR DB Fuzz** and sold in 2026 as the **Warm Audio Dirty Boy Jr. Fuzz**.
- **Circuit lineage:** Modified **Dirty Boy Afro Fuzz** circuit.

## What this pedal is

The **Dirty Boy Jr. Fuzz** is the compact production version of Dirty Boy's boutique Afro Fuzz design, created through a collaboration between Dirty Boy Pedals and Warm Audio. Warm Audio's exact-model product page describes it as an all-analog, vintage-style fuzz built from the original Dirty Boy “Afro Fuzz” circuit, with a six-position frequency selector added to the familiar Volume and Fuzz controls.

Sound On Sound's July 21, 2026 announcement likewise identifies the Jr. Fuzz as a modified vintage-style fuzz based on the original Afro Fuzz circuit, and describes the Jr. series as Warm Audio and Dirty Boy adapting three Dirty Boy designs for larger production runs.

## Controls

Warm Audio documents three user controls:
- **Volume:** output level.
- **Fuzz:** fuzz/gain intensity.
- **Frequency:** six-position rotary selector that reshapes the circuit's overall frequency response and changes the character of the fuzz.

The manufacturer describes the six frequency positions as moving from thick, low-end-heavy saturation toward sharper, more cutting textures. The archive records those positions as voicing changes rather than inventing six separate modes or circuits.

## Circuit / hardware evidence

Warm Audio describes the pedal as **premium all-analog circuitry** and states that it recreates the boutique-modified Dirty Boy Afro Fuzz circuit.

A September 15, 2026 GuitarPlayer review reports that the Jr. Fuzz uses **one germanium transistor and one silicon transistor**, rather than the dual-germanium arrangement of a classic Fuzz Face. That source describes the mixed-device arrangement as part of the design's effort to extend the fuzz range and reduce some of the temperature sensitivity associated with germanium-only circuits.

The archive treats the one-germanium/one-silicon statement as review-source evidence, not as a claim of a specific transistor part number.

## Power / switching

Warm Audio and retailer documentation identify standard **9V DC** operation. The exact-model retailer specification lists **center-negative** power and one 1/4-inch input plus one 1/4-inch output. The product is documented as **true bypass**.

## Sound

Warm Audio describes the Jr. Fuzz as delivering rich sustain, harmonic saturation, dynamic cleanup, and touch-sensitive response. Its six-position Frequency selector ranges the voicing from thick low-end emphasis through focused midrange toward sharper, cutting textures.

The 2026 GuitarPlayer review reports that the lower Frequency positions can produce heavier, more violin/cello-like weight, while the higher positions can become more scooped or treble-emphasized. A separate August 2026 review describes the pedal as responsive to guitar-volume cleanup and pick attack and notes that all six Frequency settings remained musically usable in testing. These are review observations and are retained as such rather than converted into objective circuit claims.

## Historical / production context

Warm Audio announced the Dirty Boy Jr. series in **July 2026** in collaboration with Dirty Boy Pedals. The Jr. Fuzz was introduced as a production-scale recreation of the original hand-built Afro Fuzz design. Warm Audio lists the product MSRP as **$169**.

The original Dirty Boy brand continues to list a separate **AFRO FUZZ** under its DB Core products, while the Jr. Fuzz appears under the DB Junior group. The archive therefore keeps the original Afro Fuzz and the Jr. Fuzz as separate canonical pedal identities, with the Jr. explicitly linked by design lineage.
