## What this pedal is
Maxon identifies the D&S as a classic 1970s transistor distortion/sustainer. The manufacturer says the original model debuted in 1974 and was designed to retain more of the player's original guitar tone and picking response than contemporary Muff-type distortion boxes. [1][2]

## Controls
- **DISTORTION:** amount of distortion. [1]
- **TONE:** active/interactive tonal shaping with a tighter frequency range than a Big Muff-style tone network. [1]
- **BALANCE:** blends the input signal with the distorted signal. [1]

## Circuit / architecture
Maxon documents a transistor-based design using **Toshiba 2SC1815 low-noise transistors** selected for gain and noise performance. The D&S uses a Hi-boost/Hi-cut tone structure intended to retain low end while adding note clarity. [1]
The bypass/output section is buffered with a JFET arrangement and low output impedance. [1]

## Transistor
- **Documented production device:** Toshiba **2SC1815** low-noise transistors. [1]
- Maxon describes the transistors as hand-selected for low noise and gain behavior.
## Diode

## Power and physical specification
- **Power:** 9V battery or special AC adaptor. [1]
- **Current consumption:** 6mA at 9V / 7mA at 10V. [1]
- **Input impedance:** 500kΩ. [1]
- **Output impedance:** 10kΩ. [1]
- **Dimensions:** 61 × 112 × 35mm body; 70 × 112 × 55mm overall. [1]
- **Weight:** 260g. [1]

## History and relationship
- **Original release:** 1974. [1][2]
- Maxon's historical model database also lists the D&S as **OD-801** in its 808-series numbering history. [2]
- The archive keeps the Maxon D&S record separate from the Maxon D&S II and from DOD-branded D&S records, because the cited evidence identifies them as distinct products. [2]

## Sound
Maxon characterizes the D&S as a high-gain distortion with long sustain and relatively low noise, spanning thick drive into more extreme fuzz territory while maintaining note definition. [1]
