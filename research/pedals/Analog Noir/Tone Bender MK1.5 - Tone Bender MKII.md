# Analog Noir — Tone Bender MK1.5 / Tone Bender MKII

## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** Tone Bender MK1.5 / Tone Bender MKII
- **Builder:** Analog Noir
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz
- **Identity:** Dual-circuit germanium fuzz pairing the two-transistor MK1.5 and three-transistor MKII.

## What this pedal is
Analog Noir's Dual Tone Bender MKII / MK1.5 combines recreations of the 1966 Sola Sound MK1.5 and MKII circuits in one enclosure. The builder's current pedal catalog lists the product as **Tone Bender MKII / Tone Bender MK1.5**. The MK1.5 is a two-transistor fuzz with lower gain, strong cleanup from the guitar volume control, and an external bias control. The MKII is a three-transistor development with an added input gain stage, altered bias/EQ, more gain and sustain, and a three-position voice switch. [1][2]

## Colorways
- Documented exact-model builds include **Hammered Silver**; prior archived catalog evidence also records **Hammered Copper**. [1][2]

## Versions and factory options
- Shared controls: **Volume, Fuzz, Bias**.
- MKII-specific controls: **Gain** and **Voice** switch with high/mid/low positions.
- The dual unit uses a right footswitch to select **MKII / MK1.5** and a left footswitch for bypass/engage.
- The standard product uses a **9V DC jack** and vintage PNP germanium transistors that require an isolated supply. The current exact-model listing reports approximately **10mA current draw**. [2]
- The documented MKII voice control provides three EQ positions described as **full range, mid forward, and upper mid**. [2]

## Version changes
This archive record represents a dual-circuit product rather than a numbered production V2. The two sides reproduce historically distinct Tone Bender topologies:
- **MK1.5:** two-transistor design with less gain than the earlier MKI, a smoother/sweeter fuzz response, and pronounced cleanup from the guitar volume control.
- **MKII:** three-transistor design heavily influenced by the MK1.5, with changes to bias and EQ plus an added input gain stage that produces more aggressive clipping, gain, and sustain. [2]

## Transistor
- **Technology:** Germanium.
- The exact-model Reverb listing documents a hybrid trio of vintage germanium transistor families: **1960s Mullard GET, 1974 Texas Instruments CV, and 1960s New Market NKT** examples. It also identifies the transistors as vintage **PNP** devices and states that isolated power is required when using the 9V DC jack. [2]

## Sound
The **MK1.5** side is described as having a sweeter, more nuanced fuzz response with strong guitar-volume cleanup. The **MKII** side delivers a more aggressive, higher-gain and higher-sustain voice, with selectable full-range, mid-forward, and upper-mid EQ emphasis. [2]
