## What this pedal is
MI Audio designed the G.I. Fuzz as the high-gain silicon counterpart to the earlier Neo Fuzz. The manufacturer describes it as a new design based around high-gain silicon transistors and a very wide adjustable fuzz range. [1][2]

## Circuit / architecture
- **Fuzz device class:** trio of high-gain silicon transistors. [2]
- The circuit exposes bias and pickup-loading parameters rather than relying only on conventional fuzz and volume controls. [1][2]
- The manufacturer describes a dedicated midrange Body control, a bidirectional Tone control, and a Brightness/Tame control for the high end. [1]

## Controls
- **FUZZ:** fuzz amount. [1][2]
- **LOAD:** variable input impedance that changes pickup loading. [1][2]
- **BIAS:** adjusts transistor operating point/symmetry and can move toward gated/pumping behavior. [1]
- **BODY:** midrange response. [1]
- **TONE:** balances low and high frequencies. [1]
- **VOLUME:** output level. [1]
- **BRIGHT/TAME switch:** high-frequency control. [1][2]
- **Internal GAIN trimmer:** limits/tames the overall gain range. [2]

## Transistor
- **Documented device:** three high-gain silicon transistors. [2]
- **Exact production transistor part numbers:** Unknown.
## Diode
- **Exact production clipping/rectifier diode:** Unknown.

## Power and switching
- **Power:** internal 9V battery or 9VDC external supply. [1]
- **Switching:** heavy-duty 3PDT true bypass. [1]

## Version history
- **v2** is explicitly identified in the manufacturer owner manual. [1]
- The manufacturer positions G.I. Fuzz as the younger, higher-gain silicon sibling to Neo Fuzz. [1]

## Sound
MI Audio describes the G.I. Fuzz as covering everything from fat, lower-gain fuzz/overdrive behavior through traditional fuzz, sustained high gain, and gated/synth-like textures as the Bias, Load, and other controls are moved. [1][2]
