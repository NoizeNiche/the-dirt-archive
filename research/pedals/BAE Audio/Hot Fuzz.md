# BAE Audio - Hot Fuzz

## PRP identity
- Builder: BAE Audio
- Catalog type: Fuzz
- Identity: BAE Hot Fuzz, a two-section high-frequency boost and 1970s-style fuzz.

## What this pedal is
Hot Fuzz combines an independent high-frequency boost with an English 1970s-style fuzz. The sections may be used individually or in series.

## Controls / architecture
- Independent gain control for each section.
- Fuzz section includes Juice, Bass and Treble controls.
- Two individual true-bypass footswitches.
- High-frequency boost is a single stage; fuzz is a three-stage circuit.

## Components / power
- All-solid-state design with four bipolar low-noise transistors.
- Single 9V battery or external center-negative supply.
- Maximum current draw below 6mA.
- Handbuilt in the USA.

## Deep research verification

This pass confirms the factory architecture and control details against BAE's current product documentation and the 2017 manual, with independent review context.

### Verified description

BAE Audio describes Hot Fuzz as two independently switchable sections in one enclosure: a **high-frequency boost** on the left and an **English 1970s-style fuzz** on the right. The two sections can operate independently or in series. BAE specifies a solid-state design with **four bipolar low-noise transistors**.

### Verified controls and hardware

- High Frequency Boost: individual Gain control; single-stage circuit extending to **5 kHz**.
- Fuzz: individual Gain plus **Juice, Bass, and Treble** controls; triple-stage circuit.
- Each section has its own true-bypass footswitch.
- BAE documents a single 9V battery or external supply through a 2.5 mm center-negative jack.
- Maximum current draw is below **6 mA**.
- BAE gives dimensions of 2.5 x 6 x 3.5 inches and describes the pedal as handbuilt in the USA.
- The fuzz section's manual describes warm/sweet fuzz at lower-to-mid settings and progressively higher-gain, more extreme fuzz at the ends of the control ranges.
- BAE's manual documents the high-frequency boost as emphasizing the upper-mid/high-frequency region, with measurable lift extending above the 5 kHz nominal section range depending on settings.

### Verified circuit / lineage

The manufacturer calls the fuzz an English 1970s-style fuzz. MusicRadar independently describes the fuzz as carrying **Tone Bender DNA** and the high-frequency boost as being in the **Rangemaster** family of treble boosters. That lineage is treated here as attributed circuit-family context rather than as a claim of an exact vintage clone.
