# CBS-Arbiter — CBS-Arbiter Doubler

## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** CBS-Arbiter Doubler
- **Builder:** CBS-Arbiter
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz
- **Identity:** Vintage CBS-Arbiter octave fuzz associated with Gary Hurst and the Electronic Sounds/UFO Octa-Fuzz design lineage.

## What this pedal is
The CBS-Arbiter Doubler is a vintage octave fuzz from the mid-1970s, documented by Effects Database as a Gary Hurst design developed during his work with CBS-Arbiter and Electronic Sounds. The circuit provides a conventional fuzz mode and a switchable upper-octave mode, and the model is closely related to the Electronic Sounds UFO Octa-Fuzz and Moreschi Octave Fuzz. [1][2]

## Colorways
- Surviving documentation shows large metal enclosures in white and silver finishes. [1][3]
- Finish differences are treated as cosmetic variants rather than separate models.

## Versions and factory options
### CBS-Arbiter Doubler
- Volume
- Sustain
- Filter
- Normal fuzz footswitch
- Octave footswitch
- 1/4-inch input and output
- 9V battery operation; later surviving examples may also have external 9V DC added [1][3]

No numbered factory V1/V2 sequence was established.

## Version changes
Circuit traces of two surviving examples document component-value differences, including different transistor types and some resistor/capacitor values. These differences are documented as individual unit/circuit variants rather than a verified numbered factory revision history. [4]

## Transistor
- **BC173:** listed by Effects Database as the original specification and also identified in a Reverb listing describing an all-original circuit. [1][3]
- Circuit-trace reports also document surviving units using BC109 and BC549 transistors, so the exact transistor population should not be assumed identical across every surviving Doubler. [4]

## Diode
- The exact factory diode type is **not securely established for every CBS-Arbiter Doubler** in the checked sources.
- A circuit-trace discussion of an Electronic Sounds Doubler reports 1N4148 diodes, while another traced unit's blue diodes were visually uncertain. That evidence is kept separate from a universal CBS-Arbiter specification. [4]

## Sound
The Doubler combines a thick, saturated vintage fuzz mode with a separately switchable upper-octave mode. The octave circuit emphasizes upper harmonics and can become especially pronounced on single-note and higher-register playing, while the Filter control shapes the overall tonal balance from darker and denser toward brighter, more cutting fuzz. [1][3]
