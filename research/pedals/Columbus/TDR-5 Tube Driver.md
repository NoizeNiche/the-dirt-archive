# Columbus - TDR-5 Tube Driver

## Research identity
- **Builder:** Columbus
- **Catalog identity:** TDR-5 Tube Driver
- **Catalog type:** Overdrive / Distortion
- **Identity:** Japanese late-1980s/early-1990s solid-state OEM pedal from the TDR-5 family.

## What this pedal is
The Columbus TDR-5 Tube Driver is documented as a Japanese-made overdrive/distortion pedal produced in the late 1980s and early 1990s. It belongs to a family sold under Columbus, Axtron, Profile+, Vision and Series 10 branding, with the shared TDR-5 design differing primarily by branding and cosmetics. [1][2]

Despite the "Tube Driver" name, the documented TDR-5 is a solid-state analog circuit rather than a vacuum-tube pedal. [1][2]

## Controls
- **Level:** output volume.
- **Drive:** gain/saturation.
- **High:** high-frequency adjustment.
- **Low:** low-frequency adjustment. [1]

## Colorways

## Construction
Effects Database describes the family as compact molded-plastic Japanese OEM pedals with buffered bypass and 9V DC/battery operation. [1]

## Transistor
- **Documented TDR-5 family device:** 2SC1815 / C1815 discrete transistors. [1][2]
- The archive treats this as family-level evidence and does not claim every Columbus specimen has an identical transistor batch.

## Diode
- **Exact clipping diode/device:** Not publicly documented in the checked sources.

## Sound
The TDR-5 spans warm, compressed overdrive through more aggressive distortion. The independent High/Low EQ controls provide broader shaping than many compact Japanese drives of the period. [1][2]
