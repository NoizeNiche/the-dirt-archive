# B.K. Butler — Tube Driver

## PRP identity

- **Archive parent:** Tube Driver
- **Builder:** B.K. Butler
- **Catalog type:** Overdrive
- **Identity:** B.K. Butler's tube-driven overdrive/preamp family, produced in multiple generations from the mid-1980s onward. The archive treats the name as a family identity and preserves major documented production generations separately in the research notes. [1][2]

## What this pedal is

The classic Tube Driver combines an op-amp gain stage with a **12AX7 preamp tube** operating in a low-voltage/starved-voltage environment. It is a four-knob overdrive intended to move from relatively mild drive into saturated, singing distortion. Later Tube Works / Genz Benz versions modify the circuit and, in some generations, add or remove controls. [1][2]

## Major documented generations

### 1985–1987 B.K. Butler / Chandler Tube Driver
- Four-knob Tube Driver.
- Op-amp overdrive stage followed by 12AX7 tube stage.
- Three distinct Butler circuit-board generations are documented.
- Early units have serial-number/date-code conventions that can help identify production period. [1][2]

### 1987 Tube Works Real Tube
- Five-knob evolution of the Tube Driver.
- Adds **MIDS** control.
- Uses the same broad op-amp + 12AX7 architecture but with component-value changes.
- Intended to extend the midrange shaping into more Tube-Screamer-like territory. [1][2]

### 1993–1998 Tube Works / Genz Benz 911 Tube Driver
- Four-knob version derived from the Real Tube architecture.
- Not electrically identical to the 1980s B.K. Butler/Chandler version.
- Multiple component-value changes affect the response.
- Later Taiwanese Genz Benz production is distinct from the original U.S. Tube Works run. [1]

### 2005–2008+ Butler reissues
- Butler revived the 911 design after the Chandler Tube Driver trademark issue had lapsed.
- Later reissues use a **Golden Dragon 12AX7** in documented examples. [1][3]

## Controls

The exact control set depends on generation:
- Classic four-knob versions: **Drive, Tone, Level, Tube**-related control layout documented by the respective manuals/examples.
- Real Tube: adds **Mids**.
- The archive does not force one control legend onto every Tube Driver generation because the family spans multiple circuit revisions. [1][2]

## Circuit architecture

- **1980s classic:** op-amp stage → 12AX7 tube stage.
- Butler's historical research identifies **MC4558** as the usual op-amp, with some examples using **MC1458** or **TL072**.
- Later Real Tube / 911 generations retain the broad architecture but change component values and, in some cases, control layout. [1][2]

## Transistor / active device

- **Primary active devices:** 12AX7/ECC83 tube plus an op-amp.
- The classic 1980s examples commonly use MC4558, with MC1458 and TL072 also documented.
- The archive does not assume those op-amp choices apply to every later 911/reissue unit. [1]

## Diode / clipping

- The defining distortion system is the op-amp/tube preamp architecture.
- Exact clipping diode part numbers are not established as the central clipping mechanism in the reviewed sources.

## Power / construction

The classic Tube Driver is an AC-powered tube pedal rather than a standard 9V stompbox. Historical versions were built for 120V or 240V mains, with transformer/rectifier arrangements changing across the serial range. [1]
Later revisions/reissues have their own documented power implementations; the archive does not generalize one mains specification to the whole family.

## Version history

- **1985–86:** Early B.K. Butler/Chandler Tube Driver.
- **1986–87:** Revised board/markings and later circuit-board generations.
- **1987:** Tube Works Real Tube / five-knob evolution.
- **1988:** Chandler-produced unauthorized copy is historically separate from Butler's design.
- **1993–98:** Tube Works / Genz Benz 911.
- **2005 onward:** Butler 911 reissues. [1][2]

The archive explicitly excludes the 1988 Chandler copy from the B.K. Butler production lineage.

## Sound

Kit Rae's historical research describes the 1980s Tube Driver as a strong overdrive platform whose sound can vary noticeably between early PCB versions. The later Real Tube adds midrange flexibility, and the 911 changes component values sufficiently that it should not be treated as tonally identical to the 1980s circuit. [1]
The builder/family is associated with harmonically rich, tube-driven saturation spanning mild overdrive through high gain.

## Deep research verification

Kit Rae's detailed historical Tube Driver research was cross-checked with Award-Session/period auction material. The evidence establishes the major Butler/Chandler, Tube Works and Genz Benz generations, 12AX7 architecture, multiple 1980s circuit-board versions and the distinction between Butler production and Chandler's later unauthorized copy.
