# B.K. Butler - Tube Driver
## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** Tube Driver
- **Builder:** B.K. Butler
- **Catalog type:** Overdrive
- **Identity:** Brent K. Butler's Tube Driver family and classic 1980s pedal platform.
## What this pedal is
Historical research traces the Tube Driver from Butler's early tube-overdrive experiments and late-1970s Butronics production to the popular 4-knob Tube Driver designed in 1983 and produced around 1985. The classic version uses an op-amp stage followed by a 12AX7/ECC83 tube stage; later production appeared under Chandler, B.K. Butler, and Tube Works/Real Tube branding.
## Colorways
- Early Butronics production is documented in blue.
- The classic 1985-87 Tube Driver form is associated with tan/black graphics.
- Later Real Tube productions used graphic changes; uncommon gold examples are documented as special runs.
## Versions and factory options
### Butronics-era
- Publicly marketed around 1979
- Early 3-knob form
- 6AV6 or 12AV6 documented by historical sources
### Mini Boogee / Mini Matrix
- 1980-era five-knob form
- Two 12AX7 tubes documented
### Classic Tube Driver
- Butler-designed 1983
- Produced around 1985
- Level, high EQ, low EQ, drive
- Op-amp + 12AX7/ECC83
### Bias-control versions
- User-adjustable bias control documented on some versions
## Version changes
These are materially different stages of the Tube Driver family, so early Butronics and Mini Boogee designs should not be assumed electrically identical to the classic 4-knob production.
## Transistor
- **Technology:** Classic documented active gain elements are a dual op amp and 12AX7/ECC83 tube, not a discrete transistor gain stage.
- **Exact device:** TL072 is commonly reported in traced/technical sources, but is not treated as universal across all revisions.
## Diode
- **Type:** No universal factory clipping-diode specification securely established. Traced schematics show rectifier/protection diodes, but not one confirmed arrangement for every revision.
## Sound
The classic platform combines op-amp overdrive with a tube stage that shapes and colors the signal. It can move from relatively open drive into thicker, more compressed sustain as drive and EQ are increased.
