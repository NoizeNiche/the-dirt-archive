# 1981 Inventions — DRV MOD 1

## PRP identity

- **Archive parent:** DRV MOD 1
- **Builder:** 1981 Inventions
- **Catalog type:** Distortion
- **Identity:** 1981 Inventions DRV MOD 1, the original V1 DRV configuration; White is a documented colorway of this model.

## What this pedal is

The **DRV MOD 1** is the original V1 DRV configuration. The currently documented **DRV MOD 1 (WHITE)** is a white-finish presentation of this same model, rather than evidence of a separate circuit generation. 1981 Inventions describes it as the original configuration for the circuit, technically a prototype, and says the current limited batch uses the original PCB and old-style through-hole construction.

The major functional distinction is its **zero-gain mode**. At minimum DRV control, the distortion section is taken out of the signal path almost entirely, leaving the always-on preamp active. This gives the pedal a boost/preamp mode that is substantially different from ordinary DRV operation.

## Colorways & Editions

- **White:** documented by 1981 Inventions as DRV MOD 1 (WHITE). The finish is treated as a colorway, while the V1 circuit, through-hole construction, original PCB, zero-gain mode, and socketed op-amp system define the MOD 1 identity.
- The current white release is a limited renewed batch and should be distinguished from earlier prototype-era units, but not treated as a separate circuit merely because it is white.

## History and production context

The manufacturer describes the MOD 1 as one of the earliest/original DRV configurations and says it is extremely rare. It was later brought back in extremely limited quantities.

Matthew Hoopes wrote a dedicated 2024 DRV MOD 1 deep dive describing a final run of roughly **100 units**, making this configuration substantially more collectible than ordinary DRV production.

The current manufacturer page says the MOD 1 batch was hand assembled in Nashville. It uses a slightly updated, lighter aluminum enclosure while retaining original-style knobs, so the modern limited batch should not be assumed to be physically identical to the earliest prototype-era enclosure.

## Controls

- **DRV:** gain/drive and the control responsible for engaging the zero-gain behavior at minimum.
- **CUT:** tone/high-frequency control.
- **VOL:** output level.

The basic DRV interface remains a three-control design.

## Circuit / architecture

The MOD 1 retains the original V1 DRV circuit concept, including the DRV's preamp + distortion architecture.

The manufacturer specifically describes the zero-gain mode as removing the distortion section from the signal path almost entirely while leaving the always-on preamp and volume path available.

### Swappable op-amp options

This is one of the most thoroughly documented technical distinctions of MOD 1.

In his 2024 first-person deep dive, Hoopes documents three socketed op-amp options:

- **TL072:** described by Hoopes as the standard and his favorite overall, with a warm/soft low end and clear response.
- **NE5532:** described as adding a different midrange character, tightening the low end, and making palm-muted playing more punchy.
- **Burr-Brown OPA2134:** presented as a "Hi-Fi" option with a lower noise floor. Hoopes says this version is smoother through the midrange, warmer/fatter overall, and noticeably cleaner in zero-gain mode.

The current manufacturer product page confirms the OPA2134 implementation and says the original op-amp is also supplied so the socketed part can be changed without soldering.

These component options belong specifically to the MOD 1 configuration and should not be back-propagated into the base DRV record.

## Construction

The current MOD 1 product page specifies:

- original-style **through-hole components**
- the **original PCB** design
- socketed op-amp access
- current-batch hand assembly in Nashville
- a lighter aluminum enclosure in the renewed limited batch
- original-style knobs

The current builder description emphasizes the labor involved in assembling this V1-style configuration and notes that the original PCB had not been produced for more than two years before the limited return.

## Power

The current manufacturer page specifies standard **9V Boss-style power** and recommends an isolated supply. It does not list a battery clip or power supply as included.

## Versions and factory options

The archive should keep the following concepts separate:

- **Base DRV:** core production record.
- **DRV MOD 1:** original V1/prototype-oriented configuration with zero-gain mode and socketed op-amp options.
- **DRV MOD 2:** later modified generation.
- **DRV2:** later redesigned DRV-family generation.

The manufacturer explicitly states that the DRV2 redesign was engineered by John Snyder of Electronic Audio Experiments, whereas the original DRV circuit was engineered by Jon Ashley of Bondi Effects.

## Version changes

Compared with a standard DRV, the documented MOD 1 differences include:

1. original V1 circuit configuration
2. zero-gain mode
3. socketed interchangeable op-amp system
4. TL072 / NE5532 / OPA2134 options
5. different tonal behavior depending on selected op-amp
6. limited-production/prototype-era positioning

The archive does not currently have a complete component-level chronology showing exactly when every MOD 1 feature first appeared in production.

## Sound

Hoopes' own descriptions make the MOD 1's most distinctive sound behavior its **zero-gain preamp mode**.

With DRV fully down, the distortion path is largely removed, leaving the preamp. Hoopes describes this as dark, clean, thick and unlike a conventional boost, with CUT manipulation needed to restore additional treble.

With the DRV turned back up, the pedal returns toward normal DRV operation while remaining warmer, thicker and slightly clearer than the standard configuration according to the manufacturer's description.

The three op-amp choices then provide a documented second layer of tonal variation:

- TL072 = warm, soft low end, clear
- NE5532 = tighter low end and more pronounced/punchy midrange behavior
- OPA2134 = smoother, warmer/fatter and lower-noise behavior, especially clean in zero-gain mode

These are designer descriptions, not independent laboratory measurements.

## Builder and designer history

The MOD 1 traces back to the original DRV collaboration between **Matthew Hoopes** and **Jon Ashley of Bondi Effects**.

The current manufacturer makes a clear distinction between this original circuit lineage and the later DRV2 redesign by **John Snyder of Electronic Audio Experiments**.

## Historical context

The MOD 1 is important because it preserves an early configuration of the DRV rather than representing merely a cosmetic special edition.

The manufacturer's description calls it the original circuit configuration and technically a prototype. The 2024 Hoopes deep dive also frames the renewed run as a highly limited collector-oriented release.

This makes MOD 1 a useful bridge record between the original DRV development story and the later DRV2/modified family.

### Qualifications

The MOD 1's **V1/prototype status, zero-gain mode and op-amp options are unusually well documented directly by Hoopes/1981 Inventions**.

The exact factory transistor and diode bill of materials remains unresolved.

The current renewed MOD 1 batch's aluminum enclosure should not be mistaken for proof that the earliest prototype units used aluminum; the manufacturer explicitly describes the current enclosure as a slightly updated/lighter design.
