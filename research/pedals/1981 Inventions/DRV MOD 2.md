# 1981 Inventions — DRV MOD 2

## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** DRV MOD 2
- **Builder:** 1981 Inventions
- **Catalog type:** Distortion
- **Identity:** 1981 Inventions DRV MOD 2, the higher-low-end hybrid-clipping modification of the DRV circuit. [1]

## What this pedal is
DRV MOD 2 is a modified DRV distortion circuit with a substantially larger input capacitor for additional low end and a hybrid clipping section combining BAT41 and LED clipping. 1981 Inventions describes the result as warmer, louder and more aggressive than the standard circuit. [1]

## Colorways
- The cited builder page documents this MOD 2 in black with bright teal print. [1]
- Other MOD 2 artwork editions are preserved elsewhere in the archive and are not assumed to be identical cosmetic runs.

## Controls and architecture
- DRV/CUT control layout follows the underlying DRV platform. [1]
- Larger input capacitor increases low-end content.
- Hybrid clipping uses **BAT41 diode** and **LED** clipping. [1]
- Internal switch can select true-bypass operation. [1]
- 9V or 18V standard pedal power is documented for this version. [1]

## Versions and factory options
- **DRV MOD 2 TEAL** is a documented presentation of MOD 2. [1]
- The MOD 2 circuit is positioned by the builder between the standard DRV circuit and its bass-oriented modification. [1]

## Version changes
The defining MOD 2 changes are the larger input capacitor and hybrid BAT41/LED clipping arrangement. The builder describes the version as having more low-end thump, more aggression and a somewhat less tight low end on palm mutes than the standard DRV. [1]

## Transistor
- Exact production transistor/device part numbers: Unknown.
## Diode
- **BAT41** and **LED** clipping devices are explicitly documented for this modification. [1]
- Exact additional clipping-device specifications are not established beyond that description.

## Sound
The builder describes MOD 2 as huge, warm, loud and punchy, with more midrange grit than the LED-only clipping arrangement. It has substantial low-end thump, can clean up at low gain, and becomes increasingly aggressive as gain rises, reaching textures approaching fuzz. [1]
