# Aether Electronic — Lenore

## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** Lenore
- **Builder:** Aether Electronic
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz / Overdrive
- **Identity:** Aether Electronic Lenore, a modernized fuzz/overdrive based on the late-1960s Schaller Fuzz circuit. [1][2]

## What this pedal is
Lenore is explicitly described by its builder as a fuzz/overdrive based on the Schaller Fuzz, a relatively overlooked German circuit from the late 1960s. Aether Electronic states that the circuit was modified for modern, easier-to-source components and that some original controls were replaced with more interactive/practical options, so it is not presented as a faithful one-to-one recreation. [1][2]

The surviving product documentation specifies three knobs, Gain, Starve, and Volume, plus a two-position Mode switch. Mode 1 is Overdrive and Mode 2 is Fuzz. Construction is described as point-to-point on tagboard in a powder-coated 125B aluminum enclosure with top-mounted Switchcraft jacks, an Alpha pot set, and a 3PDT switch. [1][2]

## Version changes
No numbered factory revision was verified. The defining design choice is the builder's adaptation of the Schaller Fuzz circuit for modern components and the replacement of some original control functions with the Gain/Starve/Volume and Mode arrangement. [1][2]

## Sound
Aether Electronic describes Lenore's range as running from edge-of-breakup boost and classic grit into thick overdrive, raspy textures, ripped-speaker sounds, and grinding, splatty decay. The builder's mode notes are more specific: Mode 1 is a dense, boomy overdrive with definite pick clarity, while Mode 2 is a super-buzzy, textured fuzz with gating and relatively short sustain. [1][2]

The Starve control is part of the pedal's instability/texture toolkit, making the transition toward more broken-up and gated fuzz behavior a central part of the design. [1][2]

## Historical documentation notes
Aether Electronic announced Lenore in March 2017 and described it directly as a Schaller Fuzz-derived design. Effects Database published its Lenore entry on March 11, 2017 and records the same core control and construction details. [1][2]
