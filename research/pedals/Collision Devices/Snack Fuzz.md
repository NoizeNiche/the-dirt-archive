- **Identity:** Collision Devices Snack Fuzz, a compact three-mode digital/analog hybrid fuzz and distortion pedal from the Snack Series.

## What this pedal is
Collision Devices describes Snack Fuzz as a compact distortion/fuzz with three modes: **Soft**, **Mid**, and **Hard**. The series also provides three presets, an assignable expression input, a Magic control, and standard or momentary activation. [1]

## Modes
- **Soft:** analog-style saturation with a filter; a slight flutter can be introduced for added character.
- **Mid:** dephased parallel fuzzes with controllable phase behavior, covering a broad range of digital-fuzz textures.
- **Hard:** aggressive filtered fuzz with a gated, highly square character. [1][2]

## Controls and features
- Volume.
- Gain.
- Texture.
- Filter.
- Magic.
- Three-mode program selection: Soft / Mid / Hard.
- Three presets.
- Assignable expression control.
- Standard or momentary activation.
- Mono I/O.
- Buffered bypass.
- Analog dry-through.
- Full-wet operation. [1][2]

## Power and construction
- +9V DC, negative center.
- Maximum current draw: 130 mA.
- Input impedance: 1 MOhm.
- Output impedance: 10 KOhm.
- 130 x 50 x 60 mm.
- 150 g.
- Lifetime warranty.
- Top-mounted audio, power, and expression connections. [1][3]

## Circuit / processing
The manufacturer presents Snack Fuzz as a multi-mode design rather than publishing a discrete transistor/diode schematic. The available documentation does not establish exact transistor or diode part numbers. Effects Database describes the pedal as combining digital and analog-style processing with preset storage and expression control. [1][3]

## Versions and finish
Snack Fuzz is part of the first three-pedal Snack Series release alongside Snack Pitcher and Snack Delay. The manufacturer uses a new compact enclosure design shared across the series. Current manufacturer imagery shows the core light/white enclosure presentation with black controls and the red/black Collision Devices mark. [1][4]

## Sound
The three programs cover analog-style saturation, dephased parallel fuzz, and an aggressively gated bit-fuzz character. The Magic control introduces mode-specific variations, including subtle movement, randomized glitch behavior, or LFO-like modulation depending on the program. [1][2]
