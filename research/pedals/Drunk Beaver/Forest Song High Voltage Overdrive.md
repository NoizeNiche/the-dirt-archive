## What this pedal is
The **Drunk Beaver Forest Song** is a high-voltage overdrive built around a nonstandard resonant EQ section and multiple selectable clipping options. Contemporary coverage identifies the 2024 release as the **Forest Song High Voltage Overdrive**, with a control set of **Volume, Resonance, Drive, and Mode**. [1][2]

The distinctive **Resonance** control is described as two peak-filter centers, approximately **500 Hz and 800 Hz**, with cut/boost behavior. The **Mode** control selects among several clipping approaches. Contemporary coverage lists **Boost, LED, MOSFET, Silicon, Germanium, and Asymmetric Silicon** options and reports operating voltages around **18 V and 30 V** for the high-voltage architecture. [1][2]

## Development and versions
Guitar Pedal X describes the 2024 production release as the **third major iteration**, while noting an earlier V1 prototype. The same coverage says the earlier prototype's Germanium mode was weaker and the later production version refined the clipping implementation. [1]

This evidence supports a development history with multiple iterations, but it does not establish a complete factory revision numbering system. The archive therefore records the reported V1/prototype and V3 production history without inventing intermediate revision details.

## Circuit and technical boundary
The high-voltage architecture and multiple clipping modes are well documented in contemporary coverage. The reviewed sources do not provide a complete factory schematic or universal component BOM, so the archive does not assert individual semiconductor part numbers or claim that every surviving unit has identical component values.

The term **high voltage** refers to the pedal's internal operating architecture, not to the required external supply being a dangerous high-voltage source. External power requirements should be taken from the manufacturer's current documentation for the exact version.

## Operating concept
The Forest Song is presented as an intentionally different take on the Tube Screamer/Screamer-derived overdrive format. The Resonance section creates selectable frequency emphasis/cut around the 500/800 Hz regions, while the clipping Mode changes the character of the saturation. [1][2]

Those descriptions come from product/review coverage and should be treated as functional and listening observations rather than laboratory measurements.
