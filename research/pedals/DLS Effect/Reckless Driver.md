# DLS Effect — Reckless Driver

## PRP identity
- **Builder:** DLS Effect
- **Catalog type:** Overdrive
- **Identity:** DLS Effects' **Reckless Driver**.
- **Historical naming:** DLS also produced a later **RD2 Reckless Driver** with documented circuit and control enhancements. This record follows the archive's unqualified **Reckless Driver** identity and does not silently merge the two versions.

## What this pedal is
The Reckless Driver is a two-channel analog drive pedal that DLS describes as a **Distortion/Overdrive**. Its Normal and Boost channels each have independent Volume, Gain, and clean-Mix controls, with the Normal stage feeding the Boost stage. DLS created the design in 2013 after dealers asked the company to develop an overdrive, and the pedal was formally announced in February 2014.

The archive keeps the catalog classification as **Overdrive** because that is the canonical record identity, while the manufacturer and manual explicitly describe the circuit as covering both overdrive and distortion.

## Controls
The original Reckless Driver documentation establishes:
- **Normal Volume, Gain, Mix:** sets the Normal channel's output, drive, and clean-signal blend.
- **Boost Volume, Gain, Mix:** sets the Boost channel's output, drive, and clean-signal blend.
- **Bass:** full-range bass control with dynamic response.
- **Treble:** wide-range treble control with dynamic response.
- **Attack:** two-position voicing control. Right/high-attack adds a little more attack and brightness; left is warmer and smoother.
- **Boost footswitch:** selects the Boost channel. DLS notes that the Boost selector can be switched even while the effect itself is bypassed.
- **On/Off footswitch:** true-bypass effect switching.

DLS explains that the Normal channel drives the Boost channel, so the Boost volume behaves in relation to the Normal channel's level in a way the company compares to a master-volume relationship.

## Circuit / hardware evidence
DLS' original datasheet documents an **internal voltage charge pump that raises the operating voltage to about 13V** for higher headroom and a clear but warm overdrive/distortion response.

The enclosure contains two internal adjustments:
- **Input-stage / Drive Gain pot:** clockwise increases gain.
- **RT2 distortion-input-stage pot:** counterclockwise gives more distortion, bite, and straight-drive character; clockwise gives less distortion and a smoother drive.

DLS specifies high-quality 1/4-inch Amphenol input and output jacks, a die-cast aluminum enclosure, and a 3P3T true-bypass switch.

No exact production transistor or semiconductor part number is identified in the model-specific documents reviewed for this pass.

## Power / dimensions
The original DLS datasheet specifies **9V DC to 12V DC input at about 20mA**, using a standard 2.1mm jack and stating that input polarity does not matter. The same document gives dimensions of approximately **3.7 in. wide × 4.7 in. long × 1.7 in. high** and a weight of **1.9 lb**.

## Sound
DLS positions the pedal as covering clean boost, overdrive, and more aggressive distortion by combining the channel Gain and clean-Mix controls.

In its 2014 review, Premier Guitar found the Normal channel capable of low-to-mid-gain, edge-of-breakup sounds and described the pedal as strongly touch-sensitive. The review reported that harder picking increased the perceived rasp of the overdrive while lighter playing produced a smoother response. With both stages driven hard, the reviewer described the distortion as aggressive but still articulate, with useful note definition and picking dynamics.

Premier Guitar also reported that the Attack switch changes the feel and brightness of the response and that the shared Bass/Treble section gives substantial control over how the pedal interacts with different amplifiers. Those are review observations, not claims of an objectively fixed frequency response.

## Historical / production context
DLS owner and designer David Sestito states that the Reckless Driver was conceived in **2013** after several dealers encouraged DLS to develop an overdrive. He describes the original design as a Normal stage feeding a Boost stage, with clean-Mix controls added to let players retain more of the instrument's original signal.

Premier Guitar announced the new **Reckless Driver Overdrive/Distortion** on **February 26, 2014**. DLS' own history identifies the Reckless Driver as a 2013 development and says artists including Frank Hannon of Tesla, Quinton Gibson of Darius Rucker, Kerry Marx, and the Gibson Las Vegas studio adopted it.

DLS later introduced the **RD2 Reckless Driver** as an enhanced version. The RD2 documentation describes more Volume and Gain range in both channels, wider clean-Mix controls, increased sustain and fullness, enhanced/purer distortion, and revised EQ and Attack behavior. The later RD2 document also describes higher-voltage operation for additional headroom. The archive should treat RD2-specific enhancements as revision evidence rather than back-projecting them onto every original Reckless Driver.

## Deep research verification
This pass upgrades the record from a surface stub to a model-specific researched entry. The DLS 2013 datasheet establishes the original pedal's two-channel architecture, control set, internal gain trims, true bypass, high-headroom charge-pump design, power requirements, dimensions, and enclosure details. DLS' company history independently places the design work in 2013, while Premier Guitar documents the February 2014 release and provides hands-on observations about touch sensitivity, gain range, dynamics, and interaction with different amplifiers.

The later RD2 documentation is retained as revision evidence only. It establishes that DLS subsequently enhanced the Reckless Driver with broader control ranges and other changes, rather than proving those later specifications were present in the original model.

No exact transistor or diode part numbers are assigned.
