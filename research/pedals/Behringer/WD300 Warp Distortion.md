# Behringer - WD300 Warp Distortion

## Surface catalog record
- **Builder:** Behringer
- **Pedal:** WD300 Warp Distortion
- **Catalog type:** Distortion

## What this pedal is
The **WARP DISTORTION WD300** is Behringer's historical compact distortion pedal for thick, tube-like high-gain distortion and extended sustain.

## Versions and history
Behringer documentation identifies the WD300 as a compact pedal from the historical range. A Behringer price list from 2007 lists the WD300 as a new product at that time. No verified revision split was recovered.

## Sound
The manual describes super-thick, tube-like distortion with long sustain and hard-rock/heavy-metal applications. The WARP control and SUB switch provide substantial frequency-shaping options around the core distortion.

## Deep research verification

This pass uses the surviving Behringer manual plus a contemporaneous 2007 Behringer price list.

### Verified controls and hardware

- **SUB:** activates a low-frequency boost around 90 Hz intended to simulate the resonance of a 4 x 12-inch cabinet.
- **LEVEL:** output level.
- **WARP:** changes the midrange response. At minimum it boosts roughly 250 Hz to 1 kHz; at maximum it cuts around 500 Hz while adding presence around 3 kHz.
- **GAIN:** input sensitivity and resulting distortion amount.
- LED indicates effect/battery status.
- Footswitch activates or deactivates the effect.
- 1/4-inch TS input and output.
- 9 V DC power input.
- 9 V 6LR61 battery.
- The manual lists 500 kOhm input impedance, 1 kOhm output impedance, regulated 9 V / 100 mA supply specification, and approximately 30 mA current consumption.

### Verified history

A Behringer price list dated August 2007 lists **WARP DISTORTION WD300** among new products, establishing the model in the company's catalog by that date.

### Verified sound evidence

Behringer describes the WD300 as producing super-thick, tube-like distortion with endless sustain for hard-rock and heavy-metal sounds. The WARP and SUB controls substantially alter the frequency balance rather than simply increasing gain.
