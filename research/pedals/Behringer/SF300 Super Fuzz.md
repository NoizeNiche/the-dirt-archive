# Behringer — SF300 Super Fuzz

## PRP identity
- **Archive parent:** SF300 Super Fuzz
- **Builder:** Behringer
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz / Distortion / Boost
- **Identity:** Three-mode analog pedal widely documented as closely following the Boss FZ-2 Hyper Fuzz, itself a modified Univox Super Fuzz/FY-6-family design. [1][2]

## Controls / modes
- **LEVEL**
- **GAIN**
- **TREBLE**
- **BASS**
- **FUZZ 1**
- **FUZZ 2**
- **BOOST**
- Three-position mode system selects **Fuzz 1 / Fuzz 2 / Boost**. [1]

## Circuit lineage
- **Primary lineage:** Boss **FZ-2 Hyper Fuzz**.
- The FZ-2 itself is documented in circuit-analysis communities as a modified Univox Super Fuzz / Shin-Ei FY-6-family design.
- Freestompboxes tracing identifies the SF300's octave pair as **T4/T5**, phase-splitter as **T6**, and discusses its relationship to the FZ-2 schematic. [2][3]
- A later tracing/modification report states that changing three components can make an SF300 closely match the FZ-2 circuit, while noting component substitutions. This is independent tracing evidence, not Behringer's own clone claim. [3]

## Transistor
- The independent SF300 trace documents a multi-transistor discrete circuit, including the identifiable octave pair **T4/T5** and phase splitter **T6**.
- Exact transistor part numbers are not established as universal from the reviewed sources.
- Do not import FZ-2 transistor part numbers as though they were guaranteed SF300 production parts.
## Diode / clipping
- The independent trace identifies a dedicated octave/clipping diode section and specifically discusses the factory diode pair in comparison with 1N34A, 1N4001 and 1N4148 alternatives.
- Exact factory diode part number is not treated as universal because the traced sources do not establish one clean manufacturer BOM. [3]

## Power
- Standard Behringer pedal power/battery arrangement.
- Exact current draw not asserted without model-specific manual evidence.

## Version history
- No numbered factory electronic revision established.
- The three sound modes are core production features, not separate model revisions.

## Sound
Fuzz 1 is the denser classic Super Fuzz/FZ-2-style voice; Fuzz 2 is fuller and more scooped with pronounced upper harmonics; Boost bypasses the octave-fuzz generator and provides a cleaner gain-boost behavior. Independent circuit analysis specifically notes that the Boost mode bypasses the octave-fuzz-generator portion of the circuit. [2]
