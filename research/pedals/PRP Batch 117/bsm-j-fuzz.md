# BSM — J-Fuzz

## PRP identity
- **Builder:** BSM
- **Catalog type:** Fuzz
- **Identity:** Handmade recreation of the late-1960s Jennings Fuzz.

## What this pedal is
BSM describes J-Fuzz as a faithful recreation of the original Jennings Fuzz from the late 1960s. The original Jennings design used two silicon transistors and was noted for a milder, rounder response than many contemporary silicon fuzzes. BSM specifically sourced NOS Japanese Micro Electronics transistors from the early Jennings version for its J-Fuzz. [1]

## Colorways
No single factory colorway was reliably established from the surviving manufacturer documentation.

## Versions and factory options
- NOS Japanese Micro Electronics transistors
- Polystyrene capacitors
- 9V battery or external 9V power supply
- Approx. 2 mA current consumption
- Average output level: 5 dBm
- Maximum output voltage: 0.4 V under hard picking
- Negative battery terminal is ground [1]

## Version changes
BSM's product description distinguishes the early Jennings circuit from a later Jennings version that substituted a European transistor type and revised the circuit. J-Fuzz specifically follows the earlier Jennings version using the Japanese Micro Electronics parts. [1]

## Transistor
- **Technology:** Silicon.
- **Device:** NOS Japanese Micro Electronics transistors, sourced by BSM for J-Fuzz. [1]

## Diode
- Exact clipping diode/device: Not publicly documented.

## Sound
J-Fuzz is voiced as a surprisingly warm and full silicon fuzz, deliberately avoiding the sharper, more penetrating character associated with many other silicon designs. The BSM version is intended to capture the early Jennings response, with the guitar pickup and volume control playing a major part in the final texture. [1][2]
