# Ampeg - A-1 Distortion

## PRP identity

- **Builder:** Ampeg
- **Catalog type:** Distortion
- **Identity:** Ampeg A-1 Distortion from the early-1980s A Series.

## What this pedal is

The **A-1 Distortion** was one of Ampeg's Japanese-made A-Series effects, produced in **1982 and 1983** during MTI ownership. Effects Database records it as a dedicated three-control distortion pedal.

## Controls

- **Level:** Output level when the effect is engaged.
- **Distortion:** Amount of distortion.
- **Brilliance:** Tone control, covering bass-to-treble emphasis.

## Power / switching / hardware

- **Power:** 9V battery or Ampeg AC adaptor.
- **Switching:** Ampeg's "Noiseless" solid-state switching system.
- Dimensions: **68 x 53 x 120 mm**.
- Made in Japan.

## Circuit / lineage

A separate TalkBass historical discussion identifies the A-Series as a Japanese MTI-era product family and notes that the pedals were made by **Guyatone** and also appeared under Rozz, Westbury and Tora branding. This establishes the OEM family context, but no exact A-1 component complement is assigned without a model-specific schematic.

## Historical A-Series scope

The documented A-Series consists of:
- A-1 Distortion
- A-2 Compressor
- A-3 Over Drive
- A-4 Phaser
- A-5 Flanger
- A-6 Chorus
- A-7 Multi Octaver
- A-8 Analog Delay
- A-9 Parametric Equalizer
- SB-5 System Board

The Dirt Archive's dirt-box census is intentionally retaining the A-1 as the dirt-bearing entry while the other A-Series effects remain outside this dirt-focused catalog unless independently represented by a qualifying dirt circuit.

## Sound

The exact historical page identifies the A-1 as a distortion but does not provide a detailed modern tonal analysis. The archive therefore keeps the description at the documented function level.

## Deep research verification

This pass adds only claims supported by the reviewed exact-model and closely related OEM-family sources. Earlier archive research is retained unchanged.

### Verified description

Effects Database places the A-1 in Ampeg's Japanese-made A Series, with the exact model documented as a three-control distortion pedal from **1982 and 1983** during the MTI ownership era. The exact-model record documents **Level, Distortion, and Brilliance** controls, with Brilliance serving as the tone-shaping control. The same record documents 9V battery or Ampeg AC-adaptor power, Ampeg's "Noiseless" solid-state switching system, and a compact 68 x 53 x 120 mm enclosure.

### Verified version references

- No numbered factory revision or separate A-1 production version was verified in the reviewed sources.
- Effects Database's A-Series record places the A-1 alongside A-2 Compressor, A-3 Over Drive, A-4 Phaser, A-5 Flanger, A-6 Chorus, A-7 Multi Octaver, A-8 Analog Delay, A-9 Parametric Equalizer, and the SB-5 System Board.
- The exact production window established by the reviewed sources is 1982-1983.

### Verified circuit / OEM-family evidence

- Effects Database explicitly links the Ampeg A-1 with the **Guyatone PS-001**, **Rozz R-1**, **Tora TS-001**, and **Westbury W-01**, providing direct OEM-family lineage evidence.
- Independent documentation for the related Guyatone PS-001 records a traced op-amp-based distortion circuit. Effects Database likewise describes the related Tora TS-001 as an OEM variant of the Guyatone PS-001 and notes that surviving units can contain **TL5448P or UA1458C**. Those component references belong to the related PS-001/TS-001 family evidence and are **not** assigned here as the exact Ampeg A-1 component complement.

### Verified transistor/device terms

- **Exact A-1 transistor/device:** Not established by the reviewed exact-model sources.
- No exact A-1 semiconductor part number is assigned from the related Guyatone/Tora family evidence.

### Verified diode terms

- **Exact A-1 clipping/rectifier diode:** Not established by the reviewed exact-model sources.
- No diode part number is assigned from the related family traces.

### Verified sound evidence

- The exact A-1 record establishes the pedal's documented function as a distortion effect but does not provide a detailed historical tonal description.
- A modern demonstration of the related Guyatone PS-001 describes its range as extending from crunch/overdrive into fuzz-like distortion. This is retained only as **OEM-family context**, not as a claim that the Ampeg-branded A-1 sounds identically.
