## Pass 30: deeper 1998-2010 boutique corridor and early Black Arts / D*A.M. production boundaries

Pass 30 continues the breadth-first boutique corridor with a stronger emphasis on production-state boundaries rather than simply adding more model names. The new material connects D*A.M.'s early original-design overdrive work, HBE's broad 2000s catalog, MI Audio's early versioning, T-Pedals' scope boundary, Black Arts Toneworks' 2010 manufacturer documentation, and a surviving Ooh La La specimen.

### Newly documented

- **D*A.M. Sonic Titan early corridor**: Effects Database preserves a 2004-2008 branch and later 2009-onward branch.
- **D*A.M. Sonic Titan later branch**: 2009-2010 period evidence provides a useful specimen-facing generation marker.
- **Homebrew Electronics Big D**: documented as a 2000s HBE overdrive/distortion catalog object through contemporary owner testimony.
- **Homebrew Electronics UFO Fuzz / Octave**: confirmed as a named HBE fuzz/octave catalog object in contemporary 2008 owner testimony.
- **Homebrew Electronics Hematoma Overdrive Pre-Amp**: preserved as a documented HBE drive-family object, with chronology intentionally left open until stronger period catalog evidence appears.
- **MI Audio Crunch Box V2**: wider Tone sweep, revised Volume taper and added internal Presence control establish a meaningful version boundary.
- **T-Pedals Nuke Booster**: retained as a builder-catalog boundary record while remaining outside the main dirt count.
- **Black Arts Toneworks Black Forest**: manufacturer archive documents its presence alongside Pharaoh by May 20, 2010.
- **Black Arts Toneworks LSTR**: manufacturer archive documents the heavier Pharaoh companion in the same 2010-era catalog presentation.
- **Ooh La La Manufacturing Synth Mangler**: surviving serial-number specimen ties a later object to the Ooh La La / Effector 13 lineage without pretending the listing proves a complete production run.

### Key research findings

1. **D*A.M.'s Sonic Titan is a real generation problem.** The archive now treats the 2004-2008 and 2009-onward Titan states as separate historical branches rather than one timeless model.
2. **HBE needs catalog breadth before exact chronology.** Contemporary owner evidence already establishes named dirt products in the 2000s, but the archive will not invent launch dates simply because a pedal is mentioned in a period forum.
3. **MI Audio's version labels are historically meaningful.** Crunch Box V2 is not just a cosmetic revision: contemporary documentation records real control-response changes and an added presence adjustment.
4. **Black Arts Toneworks has unusually valuable manufacturer-era documentation.** The May 2010 archive page simultaneously presents Pharaoh, LSTR and Black Forest, giving the project an early catalog snapshot that can later be reconciled against dated listings and specialist publications.
5. **Ooh La La remains a relationship-and-specimen story.** The Synth Mangler record is useful because it joins surviving serial evidence to the licensed/manufactured Effector 13 family without collapsing Devi Ever, Ooh La La and later stewardship into one entity.
6. **Boundary records are intentional.** Boosts such as T-Pedals Nuke are documented to preserve the builder's real product universe, but the central archive count remains focused on fuzz, overdrive and distortion.

### Evidence discipline

- Manufacturer pages, specialist databases, contemporary community posts and surviving specimens remain separate evidence classes.
- Documentary presence is not automatically treated as exact launch chronology.
- Generation labels are promoted only where there is an identifiable specimen-facing distinction or explicit historical versioning evidence.
- Manufacturing, licensing and designer roles remain separate where evidence supports that distinction.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries or cloning instructions are added.

### Current lineage position

The archive now has a much stronger bridge through the 2004-2010 boutique expansion: D*A.M. moved beyond straightforward vintage recreation into original dirt concepts such as Sonic Titan; HBE demonstrates the broad small-builder catalog model; MI Audio shows versioned commercial growth; T-Pedals shows the overlap of retail and commissioned work; and Black Arts Toneworks provides a documented 2010 snapshot of a developing heavy-dirt identity.
