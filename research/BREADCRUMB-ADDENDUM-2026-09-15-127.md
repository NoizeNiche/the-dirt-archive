# YARP Breadcrumb 127

## Purpose
Pre-Maxon Japanese wedge-fuzz attribution pass focused on the Greco No. 35 / Sekova No. 59 corridor and the emerging Nisshin Onpa pre-Maxon OEM evidence.

## New research conclusions

- `VAR-115`: Greco No. 35 Haztone Fuzz Booster is a high-priority model-specific attribution lead. A November 1967 Japan Music Trades advertisement shows the No. 35 in the same distinctive wedge format associated with Sekova No. 59. Nisshin Onpa is strongly implicated by the broader Greco/Aria pre-Maxon relationship, but the 1967 ad alone does not prove physical manufacture by Nisshin Onpa.
- `VAR-116`: Current ToneHome research explicitly states that pre-Maxon Nisshin Onpa supplied unbranded fuzz and fuzz-wah products to Aria and Greco, with No. 57-59 and No. 83 families appearing under those brands. Preserve the physical-manufacturer and marketed-identity distinction.

## Cross-reference / dedupe decisions

- Do not create a new Greco manufacturer record from the No. 35 evidence. Greco remains a marketed/export identity within the broader Japanese OEM network.
- Do not promote the Sekova/Greco wedge factory to confirmed Nisshin Onpa solely from enclosure or control-layout similarity.
- Keep the confirmed pre-Maxon Nisshin Onpa relationships for Aria/Greco No. 57-59/No. 83 distinct from the still-unresolved earliest wedge-fuzz factory question.

## Evidence notes

Tone Machines Blog's 2025 investigation identifies a November 1967 Japan Music Trades advertisement for the Greco No. 35 Fuzz Booster and notes its direct visual identity with the Sekova wedge fuzz. The same investigation documents strong physical similarity across multiple wedge-fuzz variants but explicitly treats the Nisshin Onpa attribution as unresolved for the earliest 1967 forms. ToneHome's current first-series research independently identifies pre-Maxon Nisshin Onpa as an OEM supplier to Aria and Greco and links the No. 57-59/No. 83 families to those marketed brands.
