# The Dirt Archive — Discovery Harvest Phase 10

## Focus

This pass starts the transition from isolated discovery records into explicit historical family structures. The strongest examples are the Schaller West German fuzz network, the Jen Italian Vox branch, the Rosac/Sierra continuation of Mosrite, and the newly confirmed Kay/Uecks Japanese question.

The archive should not flatten these into one model. Each record needs a role: manufacturer, brand, importer, distributor, model, variant, or related family member.

## 1. Schaller fuzz family

Effects Database describes the Schaller Fuzz as a West German product sold from the mid-1960s through the 1970s, with OEM/rebrand appearances under Höfner, Kent, Van Hall and Blackfield. The site's current Schaller page also distinguishes earlier germanium examples from later silicon examples. citeturn500927search2turn500927search8

### Canonical structure

`Schaller -> Fuzz`

Related branded records:
- Höfner No. 539 Fuzz/Distortion
- Kent 6400 Distorter / Kent 6406 The Angry Fuzz
- Van Hall Fuzz
- Blackfield Fuzz

### High-value distinguishers
- Germanium vs silicon period
- enclosure construction/finish
- branded control labeling
- West German origin

The archive should record the germanium/silicon transition as an identification distinguisher, but should not publish complete component inventories.

### Fuzz-wah branch
Schaller also produced the Wha-Wha-Fuzz, documented at Musikmesse Frankfurt in 1971. Höfner's Wha-Wha-Fuzz is a rebranded Schaller product, and Effects Database relates it to the Kent 6406 The Angry Fuzz. citeturn500927search11turn626033search7

This suggests a distinct family branch:

`Schaller -> Wha-Wha-Fuzz -> Höfner Wha-Wha-Fuzz / Kent 6406`

That should remain separate from the basic Schaller Fuzz model even though the same manufacturer is involved.

## 2. Garen: early French domestic fuzz

The Garen Distortion / Chambre de Distorsion is an unusually important regional record. Effects Database dates it to around 1966 and identifies Garen Electronic Music Instruments MFG. as a Paris-based manufacturer otherwise better known for amplifiers. The pedal is documented with period 1966, 1968, 1969 and 1970? specimen/catalog material. citeturn500927search1

### Archive treatment
- Manufacturer: Garen Electronic Music Instruments MFG.
- Brand: Garen
- Model: Distortion / Chambre de Distorsion
- Region: France
- Approximate introduction: c. 1966
- Confidence: strong for existence and period; stronger claims about internal design should remain secondary unless independently corroborated.

This deserves promotion earlier than an arbitrary modern boutique pedal because it represents a rare domestically produced French fuzz in the first wave of transistor effects.

## 3. Jen / Vox Italian branch

The Vox V828 Tone Bender is documented by Effects Database as an Italian Jen-built germanium pedal made for Vox and distributed by Thomas Organ in the United States. The page explicitly places it in the late-1960s/early-1970s and relates it to the Sola Sound Tone Bender Mk1.5 and Dallas-Arbiter Fuzz Face family. citeturn396852search5

Jen-built combination pedals form another major branch. Effects Database documents the Vox/Double Sound and Luxor Double Sound as Italian Jen products, with Luxor specifically identified as an OEM-branded version. citeturn396852search3turn396852search8

### Family structure

`Jen Elettronica -> Vox V828 Tone Bender`

`Jen Elettronica -> Vox / Luxor Double Sound`

`Jen Elettronica -> other branded Italian fuzz/wah products`

The archive should preserve the Italian manufacturer even when the front badge is Vox, Luxor or Unicord. This is a classic case of a famous brand masking an important regional manufacturing history.

## 4. Elka Dizzy Tone

Effects Database maintains a dedicated Elka Dizzy Tone record and recent multimedia still shows the model circulating in current specialist vintage-fuzz research. The historical date commonly associated with the model is 1967–68, but the archive should retain specimen-based uncertainty until a period advertisement or dated component evidence is attached directly to a canonical record. citeturn500927search9

### Archive treatment
Preserve the model as a vintage Italian fuzz record, connect it to the broader Jen/Italian branch only when the source evidence supports the relationship, and avoid deriving a manufacturer merely from tonal similarity.

## 5. Rosac / Sierra continuation after Mosrite

Effects Database documents that Mosrite entered bankruptcy in February 1969 and that Sierra Electronics was started shortly afterward by five partners using Sierra Bag Company resources. Because the name Sierra was already in use elsewhere, the company changed its name to Rosac about a month later. Ed Sanner, designer associated with Mosrite's Fuzzrite, continued designing effects for the new company. citeturn500927search4turn500927search5

### Historical graph

`Mosrite -> bankruptcy (1969)`

`Mosrite personnel / Ed Sanner -> Sierra Electronics -> Rosac Electronics`

`Rosac -> Nu-Fuzz and other effects`

This is a particularly valuable lineage because it captures the personnel continuity behind a company change. The archive should use a relationship such as `successor / continuation` rather than treating Rosac as an unrelated new builder.

## 6. Kay F-1 and Uecks Fuzz Tone: unresolved manufacturer, stronger dates

Recent Tone Machines research establishes a much firmer chronological window for the Kay Fuzz Tone than older collector lore. The earliest printed mention found is October 1973, with surviving potentiometer date codes from 1975 and 1977 and local advertisements extending into 1978. The research therefore proposes a production window of approximately late 1973 to early 1978. citeturn674561search3

The same research identifies the Japanese-market Uecks Fuzz Tone and notes that Uecks was the brand name for Ueki Gakki. However, the actual pedal manufacturer remains unresolved. The author explicitly presents Ueki Gakki as a hypothesis rather than a proven attribution. citeturn674561search3

### Archive rule
This is a model example of how The Dirt Archive should handle uncertainty:

- Brand: Kay
- Model: F-1 Fuzz Tone
- Manufacturing country: Japan
- Production window: c. late 1973–early 1978
- Manufacturer: unresolved
- Possible domestic counterpart: Uecks Fuzz Tone
- Ueki Gakki relationship: hypothesis / provisional
- Related circuit family: Super-Fuzz-derived, but lineage should be described without a full circuit analysis

This record is strong enough to promote on dates and object identity while keeping the manufacturer unresolved.

## 7. Excetro Wau Wau Fuzz

Effects Database documents the Excetro Wau Wau Fuzz as a 1970s Japanese OEM fuzz-wah related to the Ibanez 58 Standard Wau Fuzz and Japanese Super-Fuzz-derived effects. The database also preserves an associated Wau Wau Machine record. citeturn396852search0

### Archive structure
Keep:
- Excetro Wau Wau Fuzz
- Excetro Wau Wau Machine

as separate models sharing an `Excetro family` or manufacturer relationship. The distinction matters because the model names represent different functional configurations.

## 8. Modern small-builder preservation: Shoe Pedals and Spiral Electric FX

Shoe Pedals is documented as a one-person operation created by composer CJM Venter. Its timeline gives unusually rich evidence for early prototypes, the Silver Apple and Pixel, the relationship with Deerhoof, the 2013 shift to PCB-based builds, and the continued evolution of the catalog. citeturn544444search1turn544444search5

Spiral Electric FX provides another useful modern example. Its Ge Fuzz was published in 2023 and later appears as a 2026 edition, with the product explicitly limited to 30 pieces in the original description. citeturn544444search2

These belong in the archive because the project is intended to preserve small builders and limited runs, not only historically famous brands.

## 11. Structural schema recommendation

The archive is now ready for explicit role-based relationships:

`Brand --manufactured-by--> Manufacturer`

`Brand --distributed-by--> Distributor`

`Model --sold-under--> Brand`

`Model --member-of--> Family`

`Model --variant-of--> Model`

`Manufacturer --successor-to--> Manufacturer`

`Model --related-to--> Model`

The critical point is that these relationships can remain provisional. A relationship may be `confirmed`, `probable`, `reported`, or `unverified` without deleting the underlying discovery record.

That is the architecture that will let The Dirt Archive grow into thousands of records without turning uncertain collector lore into fake certainty.
