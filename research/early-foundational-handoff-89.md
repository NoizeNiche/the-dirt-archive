# Early Foundational Handoff 89

## Pass 41: 2002-2006 boutique expansion, Scandinavian design lineage, and early-2000s production boundaries

Pass 41 extends the archive through the 2002-2006 boutique acceleration. The emphasis remains on product-era anchors and specimen-facing distinctions that help identify physical pedals, while builder-origin and non-dirt records remain explicit scope boundaries.

### Newly documented or clarified

- **Paul Cochrane Tim**: the larger-format original boutique overdrive remains a distinct family state preceding the 2004 Timmy.
- **Paul Cochrane Timmy**: the smaller, single-footswitch Timmy is anchored to 2004, separating the compact family from the larger Tim.
- **BJF Electronics Honey Bee Overdrive**: authorized BJFE material dates the product to 2002 and preserves its unusually small three-unit origin around the Stockholm guitar show.
- **Mad Professor Little Green Wonder**: first Mad Professor pedal, with company-history interview material and current Japanese documentation tying the original product to 2006 and Bjorn Juhl design.
- **Skreddy Zero**: January 2005 smaller-box redesign after the September 2004 Mayonaise.
- **Skreddy Screw Driver**: September 2005 first original Skreddy design, marking the transition from vintage-recreation work to an original product family.
- **Xotic BB Preamp**: official Xotic chronology dates the first BB Preamp to January 2005.
- **Xotic BB Bass boundary**: specialist historical work dates the Bass BB Preamp to 2006 and treats it as a separate model state.
- **Xotic Robotalk 1**: 1998 first pedal carrying the Xotic name, retained as builder chronology rather than central dirt.
- **Toneczar**: 2004 builder-origin lead retained as a scope-bound research record pending stronger product-specific first-production documentation.
- **Wampler modification era**: Brian Wampler's account places the start of his Boss DS-1 modification/testing phase around 2000; modified third-party pedals remain separate from Wampler-branded products.

### Key research findings

1. **The 2002-2006 period contains multiple clean transition clocks.** Tim to Timmy, Mayonaise to Zero to Screw Driver, and Honey Bee to later licensed production forms show why model names alone are insufficient.
2. **Scandinavia becomes a major design node.** BJFE and Mad Professor place Bjorn Juhl at the center of a lineage in which the same designer can appear across different manufacturing brands without those brands becoming identical production entities.
3. **Xotic's chronology is unusually useful for specimen work.** Robotalk 1 establishes the first pedal identity, while AC/RC/BB form a clearly dated early expansion and Bass BB becomes a separate branch.
4. **Modification culture still feeds original product design.** The Timmy, Honey Bee, and Wampler chronology all show builders moving from practical problems and modification work toward distinctive branded products.
5. **The archive should resist false precision.** Toneczar has a useful 2004 builder-origin lead, but not enough product-level evidence to backfill a specific 2004 dirt launch.
6. **A design lineage can cross manufacturers without being a clone-history claim.** BJFE, Mad Professor and later licensing relationships should be represented as designer/manufacturer relationships where supported, not flattened into one brand record.

### Evidence discipline

- Manufacturer and builder-authored chronology remains preferred for launch dates and production windows.
- Specialist databases and period-market material are used to corroborate or identify specimen corridors, not silently promoted to primary evidence.
- Product variants remain separate from modern reissues unless evidence supports continuity.
- Scope-bound records stay outside the central dirt count when the product is not fuzz, overdrive or distortion.
- No schematics, PCB layouts, complete BOMs, gutshot libraries or cloning instructions are added.

### Current lineage position

The archive now bridges the early-2000s boutique explosion from the first-generation U.S. transparent-drive builders into a genuinely international network: Cochrane and Wampler represent the practical DIY/modification-to-product path; BJFE and Mad Professor establish the Scandinavian design corridor; Skreddy documents the 2004-2005 vintage-recreation to original-design transition; and Xotic supplies one of the strongest dated product families of the period.
