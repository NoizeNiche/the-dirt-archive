# YARP 2026-09-15 Batch 114: Ideal Fuzz Master OEM Reconciliation

## Node
**Ideal Fuzz Master**

## Role
Marketed/distributed U.S. label on a late-1960s Japanese wedge fuzz. Treat Ideal as a brand/distribution node, not as the physical Japanese manufacturer.

## Evidence update
Effects Database documents the Ideal Fuzz Master as a late-1960s Japanese-made two-knob fuzz with hardwired input and output leads, an angled wedge enclosure, and an eyelet-board construction closely matching the Sekova No. 59 Distortion Box family.

Tone Machines' 2025 investigation adds an important caution: the broader Ideal guitar/effect import history intersects with Japanese OEM networks, and the exact factory behind this wedge-fuzz family remains unresolved. The article explicitly treats a common mystery Japanese manufacturer as plausible and notes that Apollo, Greco, Sekova, and related labels can occur in overlapping OEM networks. It also cautions that Apollo effects across different periods came from multiple manufacturers, including Honey/Shin-Ei, Maxon, and possibly Melos. Therefore an Apollo or Greco logo is not sufficient by itself to assign every model to one factory.

## Catalog state
- Ideal Fuzz Master: late 1960s Japanese OEM fuzz, two knobs, wedge enclosure.
- Factory: unresolved.
- Strong family relationship: Sekova No. 59 / eyelet-board wedge lineage.
- Probable shared OEM network with other Japanese export labels, but no universal factory assignment.

## Attribution rule
Do not presently promote Nisshin Onpa/Maxon from model-family hypothesis to definitive Ideal factory attribution. Earlier collector/reference sources commonly make that assignment, but the newer historical review leaves the manufacturer unresolved. Preserve Nisshin Onpa/Maxon as a candidate relationship requiring model-level evidence.
