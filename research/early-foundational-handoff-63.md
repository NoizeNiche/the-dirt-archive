# Early Foundational Handoff 63

## Pass 11: late-1980s compact hybrids, RAT descendants, and rack-era digital dirt

This pass extends the mid-1980s through early-1990s corridor into the point where traditional analog stompboxes, budget variants, and rack-based digital processors begin sharing the same historical space. Ten reconciliation records were added; one DOD FX67 entry is context-only and is not counted as a dirt record.

### Newly documented

- **DOD FX57 Hard Rock Distortion**: anchored to the dated 1987 FX-series catalog.
- **DOD FX87 Freestyle**: late first-generation FX-series catalog evidence retained at research status pending cleaner primary documentation.
- **BOSS OS-2 OverDrive/Distortion**: added as a distinct hybrid compact model rather than collapsing it into OD or DS families.
- **Ibanez ST9 Super Tube Screamer**: rare European-market state preserved separately from the general 9-series timeline.
- **ProCo Vintage RAT Reissue**: 1991-2005 reissue branch distinguished from the continuously produced RAT2 and Turbo RAT.
- **ProCo BRAT**: 1997-2001 budget RAT branch documented alongside the separate Guitar Center Roadkill identity.
- **ProCo You Dirty RAT**: 2004 standalone model traced to the Deucetone-era Dirty RAT concept.
- **Yamaha FX550**: rack-format digital guitar processor added because its dedicated digital distortion/overdrive functions place it directly inside the archive's dirt history.
- **Roland GP-8**: 1987 programmable 1U processor added as a bridge between BOSS-family analog dirt and rack-era systems.
- **DOD FX67 Flanger**: retained in the research TSV only as a context marker for catalog chronology, not promoted as a dirt record.

### Key research findings

1. **DOD's 1987 catalog is a major bridge document.** It demonstrates how broad the FX-series had become before the later transition to increasingly diverse formats and product families.
2. **Hybrid labeling matters.** BOSS OS-2 should remain its own archive object because its product identity explicitly straddles overdrive and distortion.
3. **Regional Tube Screamer history is worth preserving.** ST9 is a useful example of a market-specific Tube Screamer state during the 1985 transition between major Ibanez series.
4. **The RAT family became a genuine product ecosystem.** Vintage RAT, BRAT, Roadkill, Deucetone, You Dirty RAT, and later models should be linked as descendants without collapsing separate retail objects.
5. **RAT dating remains unusually uncertain.** R2DU still carries competing 1984-88 and documentary-1988 hypotheses, so the archive should keep the disagreement visible rather than choose by repetition.
6. **Rack-era digital dirt deserves equal historical status.** Yamaha and Roland processors show that distortion history cannot be contained inside footswitchable stompboxes. Digital processing, programmable presets, MIDI, and multi-effects architectures became part of the same guitar signal-chain ecosystem.
7. **The archive's public boundary remains intact.** External generation markers, product chronology, enclosure, region, and functional distinctions are documented. Schematics, PCB layouts, full BOMs, gutshot libraries, and cloning instructions remain excluded.

### Evidence discipline

- **VERIFIED** is reserved for explicit catalog or manufacturer-level support.
- **RESEARCH** remains appropriate for specialist chronology, regional production transitions, collector-derived dating, and disputed release windows.
- Context-only records may remain in research files when they improve chronology without being actual dirt products.
- No reconstruction-level technical material is added.

### Current lineage focus

The archive now has a continuous research corridor from the late 1970s RAT and Japanese Super Fuzz ecosystem through 1980s DOD/BOSS/Ibanez compact families and into 1990s RAT descendants and rack digital systems.
