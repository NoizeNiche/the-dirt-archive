Archive methodology

How the archive works.

A working census is only useful when the rules behind the census are visible. These are the rules used to keep pedal identity, evidence, photos, variations, and revisions separate.

Core principle

Document first. Infer last.

The archive records what can be supported by model-specific evidence. Similar circuits, shared enclosures, seller descriptions, or family resemblance are not treated as proof of identical construction.

Catalog identity

Builder → Pedal

The catalog's primary identity is the recorded builder plus pedal name. Variations and revisions can be attached to that identity when the evidence supports the relationship.

01 · Evidence hierarchy
First-party documentationManufacturer manuals, product pages, datasheets, catalogs, service material, and documented origin stories.
Contemporary reportingLaunch coverage, period reviews, trade reports, and other dated sources that describe the exact model.
Surviving-unit evidenceBoard photographs, traced schematics, serial-number research, packaging, and other physical evidence tied to a specific model.
Community technical researchRepair, tracing, and modification discussions can supply useful details, but those details are labeled as secondary evidence.
02 · Research status
Deep research

The research record contains model-specific evidence beyond the surface catalog identity.

Surface research

The record preserves the catalog identity and basic classification, with deeper verification still pending.

03 · Photos
Exact archive photoOnly an image stored under the archive's local pedal asset path counts toward the site's exact-photo coverage metric.
Reference imageryManufacturer, collector, historical, or marketplace imagery can help establish identity, but it does not automatically become an archive photo.
Image integrityThe photo layer rejects known generic artwork, donation/support graphics, broken assets, and other non-product imagery before an image is counted as archived.
04 · Variations and revisions
Colorways / editionsUsed when a documented model has meaningful finish or edition differences without becoming a separate primary catalog identity.
Hardware revisionsKept distinct when the evidence establishes a meaningful version boundary, such as a numbered successor or documented redesign.
Uncertain relationshipsWhen evidence is incomplete, the archive records the uncertainty instead of manufacturing a revision sequence.
05 · Technical claims

Component-level claims are only as strong as the evidence behind them. A traced board from one surviving unit may support an observed component configuration, but it does not automatically become a universal factory BOM. The same rule applies to transistor type, clipping devices, op-amps, power architecture, and board lineage.

Public technical filters expose only deliberately structured values that are explicitly supported in the research record. Missing values remain undocumented rather than being inferred from family resemblance.

Manufacturer language such as “tube-like,” “vintage,” or “Rectifier” is preserved as product positioning unless a source provides a more concrete technical explanation.

06 · Corrections and audit trail

The archive is intentionally a working census. When newer evidence conflicts with an older record, the preferred outcome is an explicit correction or identity flag rather than silently rewriting history.

The public archive describes its standards and sources at a high level, while internal research notes and maintenance states remain outside the visitor-facing interface.
